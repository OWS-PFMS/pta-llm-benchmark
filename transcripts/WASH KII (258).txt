1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda?

Interviewer:  Berhanu, let me start with you. In your personal opinion, what are what the major reasons impeding delivery of sustainable supply of drinkable water in the city? 
Respondent 1: In relation to water supply in Woliso City Administration, one of the problems is the steady increase of the city’s population from time to time. The work done previously in the area of water supply is not commensurate with the current size of the city’s population. So, one of the problems is the inadequate water supply infrastructure commensurate with the growth of the city’s population.
In my personal opinion, lack of coordination is a second problem. Increasing a city’s water supply is not the concern of a single sector of the administration and it requires coordination. I think these two problems are the major ones.
Third problem concerns the city’s resource capacity. In relation to budget, as the city’s size and its population increases, the concerned government authority lacks the means to provide the required supply of water.  I surmise lack of budget also plays a role.
These three are problems.
Interviewer:  Do you have anything to add?
Respondent 2: As Berhanu said, the size of the population used to be smaller. The water supply is not adequate enough to satisfy the demands of the growing size of the population. For example, there used to be a river called “Revu” which service the city’s water supply. There is a problem when the river is used by the nearby Woliso Woreda farmers union for irrigation in the dry season of the year. Lack of coordination between the city administration and water & sewerage service is also a problem. The roads are inaccessible in the rainy season and make it difficult to maintain the deep wells dug.  For instance, in the rainy season keble 01 was left without water supply for two months. I see a gap in coordination to improve the water supply problem.

Interviewer:   Excellent. Now let’s go to the solutions. One by one, what is your expert advice? When there is a problem, we always think about solutions even if we don’t have the means.  To solve the problem of imbalance between demand and supply permanently, who should do what?
Respondent 1: As you said, the solution is important. When we come to the solution to balance the demand and the supply, we have to remove the problems. [05:00]
Interviewer:  For instance?
Respondent 1: For instance, coordination problems, things demanded from the community, works expected from the leadership, and from professionals. If there is a lack of budget, how do we increase the revenue of the city and improve water supply. We need a clear and close discussion on the major problems of the city with the community. The community will also increase the understanding of its own problems. 
Second, what’s expected from the community? It is not only about money. The community is very helpful in providing ideas and material support. So, I have the opinion that if you go this way the problem will be resolved. 
Interviewer: In relation to providing solutions, you raised the issue of using drinkable water for irrigation purposes. This is a major problem as it is also raised by other governmental organizations. There is also a conflict. How could this problem be resolved in a sustainable manner?
Respondent 2: To resolve the conflict, the water should not be used for irrigation. The supply of drinkable water should also be increased. We also need a discussion with the local farmers. We need a schedule, for example, for 2 days of the week, the water could be used for irrigation without out totally cutting the city’s water supply. Teaching farmers that, 1/3rd of the water should always be available in the river. The water should also return to the river in the weekends. We can have a short term solution by discussing with the local farmers.  The city administration and the city’s water and sewerage service should plan together to dig deep water wells as underground water is aplenty in this locality. By discussing with the community, allocating the city’s budget and partnering with different nongovernmental organizations, we can increase the number of deep wells. If we are simply depending on the water supply from the river the problem cannot be resolved fully.
Again, by coordinating with zonal and woreda administration agricultural experts we need to teach the farmers about water conservation and to use drop irrigation. If the farmers are always dependant on the using the river for irrigation without a limit, the water will be wasted and city’s water problem couldn’t be resolved. So, I have the opinion that teaching farmers will help to resolve the problem.

Interviewer: WASH is not the concern of a single government entity. Let’s talk about the current state of coordination. What does the current state of coordination between the city’s departments look like? What needs to be improved?
Respondent 1: I describe the state of coordination as weak from what I observed. The water department acts on its own yearly plan unilaterally, and the city administration has its own water supply related tasks as part of its municipal service. The two entities should come closer to support each other in terms of idea or budget.
