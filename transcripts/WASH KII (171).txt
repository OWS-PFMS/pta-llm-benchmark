1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: As far as our schools based in rural areas are concerned, we have severe shortage of the service itself. Out of the available 341 regular schools, only 128 have access to water while in the remaining schools, either the water schemes are not functional or not developed yet. Even when I say, there is access to water in those 128 schools, it doesn’t mean that the service is available for 24 hours but it is to mean that they can at least have alternatives, some having mono pumps and others roof catchment systems. Accordingly, the main problems related to the service are existence of damaged schemes 
Interviewer: can you also add more challenges, you can also think out of the context of schools 
Respondent: when we see the rural community, especially those areas where the common livelihood is farming, people are usually using water from rivers for all services including drinking. 
In pastoralist areas, the problem is mainly difficulty to access water due to arid climate, so people in these areas are getting the service through tankers which will be provided by being transported from towns and other remote areas. If you go to Dasanech Woreda, for example, people are not accessing ground water for the whole year 
In the towns, the context is a bit different than those of rural areas. In Jinka town, for instance, the problem is mainly related to damaged and old pipes. In addition to that mismanagement is another aggravating factor 
Interviewer: What do you meant by mismanagement?  
Respondent: this is related to officers, they are not properly managing the shift system. People in the town don’t get water continuously, the service is arranged to be in a regular shift but because of either negligence of the experts or purposeful action to benefit some at the expense of others, the distribution is not equitable. There are villages and individuals with in the town that receive the water service for the 24/7 times while others are in a critical situation even to get water for drinking. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: let me remind you the problems you stated earlier and I wanted to start from the problem of accessibility as you said it there are only some schools receiving the service while majority are not, what do you think should be done to minimize this gap?
Respondent: I think you asked me a difficult question. When we think this in practical terms, it is difficult to solve as it is a long rooted problem related to shortage of finance on one hand and difficult climatic conditions on the other hand. Thus, it needs a collaborative action involing both the government, the NGOs and the community itself. Let’s see their role here; 
Government: This is a policy, budget and planning issues. So, we should think of proper policy supported by adequate budget to have sustainable and equitable water service in the zone. Besides this, we can also support the rural community to develop potable water simply from streams. They can protect the area by building a fence; add disinfectants regularly and use for drinking. 
NGOs are also contributing a lot by developing roof catchment systems and other schemes, for example, save the children, IRC low land WASH and others are doing a lot. This need encouragement from the government, otherwise it will be difficult to solve each and every problem by the government itself. 
Interviewer: you have also mentioned that there are water schemes that are not functioning properly, what should be done to maintain and sustain these types of schemes?
Respondent: I think local solution to the local problems would work. I said this because it has time and financial cost to send experts from towns to the remote and rural areas to fix such types of problems. Instead of that we can provide them a practical problem, including spare parts, how to fix and maintain minor issues on the spot. When there are issues that need senior technicians, they can report to the center. 
Interviewer: what about solutions to the specific problems related to pastoral and farming community?
Respondent: For the pastoral community, there was a strategy prepared by the government which is not implemented yet, it is pillarization. This is to try to change the way of life of those communities to live together in a village and adopt the system of sedentary life gradually. 
If this is successful, it would be easy for the government and other partners to provide the service at one place to benefit a large number of beneficiaries instead of doing it here and there. 
In the areas where mostly people engaged in faming activities are living, we need to provide continuous awareness programs and adequate disinfectants so that they can learn the negative consequences of drinking river water. 
In urban areas, avoiding unwise use of water and encouraging people and the experts working in the institute to use the service in a wise and equitable manner would solve the problem of sustainability. 
Interviewer: what about solutions to mismanagement of the schemes by experts working in the water office?
Respondent: I think the government should first fulfill necessary services and incentives for those workers engaged in the management of water schemes. This includes providing them with motor bikes, bikes, safety shoes and cloths so that they can move from place to place without challenge. Second, there should be clear program to dispense the service on shift bases and this has to be transparent and communicated to the public. It also needs to change the attitudes of some negligent workers who are careless about the service they are providing. Another issue is to collect information from the beneficiaries to see the gaps and satisfaction levels they are receiving from the water service and at the same time, the government should communicate problems it is facing to the people so that they can share the problems.  
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Oaky, I should see this from the three perspectives:
Within the farming community, providing them a practical training on how to simply develop stream water, preserve and protect for their own benefits is very vital solution. By doing so, we can teach them to use their own resources wisely and protect them from water borne diseases
In pastoral community, the main solution is pillarization/bringing them together. Once they are living in a closer environment, the government can provide all the services needed including water, education and health so that it would be easy to monitor and support them regularly 
In urban areas: attitudinal change is the first and most important thing to be practiced. Continuous awareness program can solve unwise use of the resource. This can also enhance the community to closely work with the government and fill some of the gaps the government is facing by themselves.
