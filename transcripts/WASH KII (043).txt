
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: I would say that the current interaction is good.  What requires is improvement on financing from the main financier, who is the county government and the other non-governmental organizations.  Financing the schemes, the community schemes.    

Interviewer:  My second question is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining rural water services in the county?

Interviewee: The biggest problem is poor management by the community committees.    

Interviewer:  Any other?

Interviewee: There is a very high cost of fuel, which makes the schemes not realize any funds for their further financing.  Otherwise, there are schemes with old equipment, which are more expensive to run and having breakages every now and then.  There’s also poor water quality, especially in some regions.  You find the underground water is so saline that it is more or less unsuitable for consumption.  There’s also a poor communication network at the schemes both road infrastructure and network coverage.   But with the current activities of the county government the communication is improving.  Otherwise, there is a high rate of misappropriation of the money collected at the schemes, by some members of the committees.  And there is lack of staff at scheme level.   

Interviewer:  And my third question is “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?”

Interviewee: We can train scheme personnel, say the committees as well as their staff.  We can also introduce green energy, especially the solar power, in the schemes.  This will reduce their use of fuel, especially diesel.  WE can also improve the road network, so that the schemes can be accessible by vehicles.  Especially with the misappropriation of revenue, we can introduce tough penalties to those who misuse the collected revenue.  Training the scheme staff on basic schemes.  

Interviewer:  My fourth question is, “of the solutions you have listed, which is the most important?”

Interviewee: The most important is to replace the fuel, especially the diesel, with some solar power.  

Interviewer:  Can you walk me through the next steps that would happen if this solution were to occur?

Interviewee:  You can reduce the cost of pumping water.  Because with less input, or less funds to pump water, the schemes would pump more water.  And then more water would be made available for livestock and domestic consumption.  And with much water, there will be more pipeline extensions, so that the distance to the water source can be reduced, and overall the living standards of the citizens will be improved.  

Interviewer:  From the increased access to water?

Interviewee:  Yes.
