Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: One challenge is the lack of enough revenue, that is financial resources to maintain the schemes. If you have no funds, mobility becomes a problem. We have no transport. If there were enough funds, we would get transport for every sub county to be mobile to monitor and report problems early enough. Secondly, we are poorly staffed at the county level. We have no staff on the ground up to the village level; our staff goes up to the sub county level so we have to stretch from the sub county up to the village, if we had staff up to the village level monitoring would be easy and we would be able to intervene before major breakdowns occur. Another challenge is due to climate change. Most of the water schemes have dried up and borehole yields have declined. So, the sources end up not being adequate to meet the demand.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: If the national government can increase allocation to the counties, this an enable the counties to allocate more money for operation and maintenance of the schemes. The money would also make it possible to employ more staff up to the village level. About climate change, what we can do is planting of trees and carrying out rain water harvesting – runoff rain water harvesting as well as roof catchment harvesting at the household level.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The important one is the financial aspect; if the finances are there things will flow. We will be in a position to be mobile because we will be having transport to do monitoring and support timely repairs. We will also be in a position to employ more staff to increase supervision and monitoring for the rural water schemes up to the village level and to the extreme up to the household level.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: One we have a rapid response unit team that moves fast when there is a breakdown, now as we are seated here, they are repairing a borehole in my sub-county and I am happy that it is a very cooperative team and they are doing well. We are also doing trainings for communities. We are collaborating with the business community to support with the repairs. Like myself when I went to work in the sub-county, I found 75% of the schemes were broken down and there was need for water. I went at a time when there were no funds, what could I have done? I couldn’t sit at the office and wait for people to die because there was no water. I had to go from office to office, from shop to shop because I already know the big business community members I told them “Look, this is your area, such and such people are suffering can you help with repairs of water systems?” and they have been doing it. It is all about improving services to the people and reducing downtime. I have also gotten into an agreement with a local supplier, of course with approval from the county, to get pumps on loan at the sub-county level. They provide pumps and are paid by the county later. With these kinds of interventions, I can say that at least 80% of all water schemes in my sub-county are operational.
