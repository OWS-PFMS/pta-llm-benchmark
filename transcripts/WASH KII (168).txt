
1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your woreda? 
Interviewer: What are the main water service sustainability problems? 
Respondent: The water reservoir we have is broken. Since the generator should not be on every now and again, the reservoir should be full and distribution is allowed from the reservoir only.
Interviewer: So, one problem is the reservoir is broken. Do not you have another one?
Respondent: No we do not have one. We have asked the woreda water office but they have not given us any yet.
Interviewer: What about other problems? 
Respondent: A water reservoir construction has been started by PCDP but now, it is terminated due to lack of budget. 
Interviewer: Why did not they finish it? 
Respondent: The budget allocated for the project was not enough. Additional budget was required but we could not get the required fund.
Interviewer: Does this hand pump function? Are people fetching water from it?
Respondent: Yes. But, often times community members misuse it. However, our main problem is lack of reservoir. Our reservoir is only 10,000 liter. We couldn’t turn the generator on whenever people come to fetch water.
Interviewer: What kind of generator do you have?
Respondent: It is diesel powered one.
Interviewer: Why do not you replace it by solar panel?
Respondent: This is up to the woreda and regional authorities. We have ETB 1,200.00 expense every two weeks for changing engine oil of the motor. 
Interviewer: Where do you get that amount of money?
Respondent: We charge one ETB for 25-liter water. But the ones that do not pay outnumber those who do. We most of the time take loan from the oil retailers.
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Whose responsibility is to complete the construction of the water reservoir?
Respondent: We should ask the regional water and energy bureau or non-governmental organizations. However, we have not asked yet. They have been supporting us. 
Interviewer: You have said that the water distribution point experiences frequent breakdown. What are you doing to address this issue? 
Respondent:  The problem is that the gate-valve is broken. 
Interviewer: But, even if you buy one it would again breakdown. What would be the permanent solution with for the problem?
Respondent: Currently we do not leave the gate valve for anyone to open it. Rather there will be one person in charge of turning the valve on and off at certain times so that it won’t break down easily. 
The water distribution point is also full of cracks and you can even find snakes in the cracks. 
Interviewer: Why do not you maintain the cracks? 
Respondent: We do not have the money to buy cement; because much of our expense is spend on fuel and engine oil. 
Interviewer: Do not you charge people when they fetch water from here?
Respondent: Yes we do. But it is just one ETB for a 25-liter water. Besides there are many elderly, sick and poor community members who can’t afford to pay for water. So, they use water for free. 
Interviewer: Do not you train the community members on how to use the gate valve?
Respondent: Yes we do. But we can train only people near the water point. But, many users come from far places and we cannot train them. Students also come here after school and play around there. The fee that we collect cannot afford anything beyond the fuel and engine oil. I am doing simple maintenance free of charge. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: Out of the solution that you have told me which one would solve the problems of water this kebele? What changes would happen if the problem is solved?
Respondent: If the water reservoir is completed, it can reach to most parts of the kebeles. Therefore, clean water will be accessible to many residents of the kebele.
