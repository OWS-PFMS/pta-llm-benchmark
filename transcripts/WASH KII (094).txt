1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability in your town? 
Interviewer: My first question is in what are the main problems related to sustainability sanitation in general and public latrines, communal latrines and waste disposal site in Debrebirhan town?
Respondent: The population of the town is increasing from time to time and the town is getting bigger and bigger. However, there is almost no communal latrine in the town. This led to poor environmental sanitation and waste management. On top of that, there is lack of vacuum truck to serve both the individual household latrines and public latrines. There are private-owned vacuum trucks, but it is a bit pricey for the community. Particularly, it gets worse during the rainy season because toilets get full fast. Further, there is no liquid waste disposal site. The biggest problem in Debrebirhan town is the absence of communal latrine. Individual households do not usually build high quality latrines. Many of them just dig pit without substructure that get full during the rainy season and overflow to the streets. So, even though there are private latrines, lack of building quality latrines is one of the problems. 
Interviewer: What about public latrines?
Respondent: We do not have more than one or two. But, they are not built properly and they have problems. We are discussing to conduct some maintenance. There is also a shortage of vacuum truck. Besides, we face problems in collecting money to pump the latrine when it gets full; many respond they cannot afford to contribute. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Let’s proceed to the solution. The first problem is communal latrines are few in number and are of poor quality. What do you think is a solution for this?
Respondent: The solution is building communal latrines in selected densely populated locations. This should be get due attention. Secondly, research should be conducted to identify poorer neighborhoods and build quality latrine. There are such practices in some kebeles and user communities also take care of the latrines. These kinds of latrine should be built in our kebele too. The biggest problem is in old neighborhoods and old houses. Communal latrines are most needed in those areas. In the recent ones latrine is included in the designs of the houses. 
Interviewer: Who should build the latrines?
Respondent: We should do our part. Partners should participate. The community has also its share and government bodies including kebeles should play their role. 
Interviewer: What about public latrines?
Respondent: There are some public latrines around the bus station. Yet, quality public latrines should be built near marketplaces. I think organizing youth around some marketplaces. It should be expanded to crowded places. 
There are bad practices such as dumping laundry kitchen discharges on the street. This is because there is lack of awareness in the community. Despite we have tried to create awareness and deter offenders by applying punishments. Yet, there is huge gap in community awareness regarding waste management. The health extension program has been ignored in keeping the environment clean and healthy for its residents. There are huge gaps regarding liquid waste management. We have tried to review and evaluate our health extension workers but nothing has changed. So, if we do our part on this issue, we will be able to solve it. 
Interviewer: Secondly, you have said that there is a problem in terms of quantity and quality of service. What do you think would be the solution for this?
Respondent: The town administration had had its own vacuum trucks; however, they are not currently functional for unknown reason. What the administration told us when we ask why is that there is no waste dumping site. I think there have been some initiations but it should be done without delay. The town has been growing in size and there should be multiple liquid waste disposal sites. Aside from that, the town has to purchase additional vacuum trucks. There are private-owned vacuum trucks but they are too pricey for the community; therefore, there should be options. 
Interviewer: So, what should be done in order for the private vacuum truck owner to ask a reasonable price for the services they provide to the community? As you may know, the cost of living is rising and paying too much for emptying out a latrine might be difficult. What should be done to solve this? 
Respondent: The thing that makes it difficult is that one latrine requires more than one trip to empty out. This has been costly and difficult to the residents. Besides, the government should discuss with the private vacuum truck owners and set constant cost for the services they provide. There should also be a directive on how they should serve the community. The government could also organize associations on this area other than just working by itself. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: Out of the solutions you have given, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Quality household level individual latrines, public latrines as well as communal latrines. The quality of these latrines should be strictly monitored by engineers during construction. The health task force in the town should also follow this issue up. Besides, latrines should be pumped out timely. In order to do so, we have to lay the ground; there should be affordable and enough vacuum trucks. Some hotels also inappropriately link their septic tank with rain water sewer pipes. This has been creating bad smell in the town streets. We should work on these issues. When buildings are built, their sanitary design and implementation issues should be strengthened. 
Interviewer: If quality latrines are built what changes would happen?
Respondent: If quality latrines are built, sludge from latrines will be contained in the septic tanks. If it gets full, the owners will get it pumped out; because the latrine will not be convenient for use. However, if the latrines are sub-standard, the sludge will infiltrate into the neighbors and it will have a detrimental effect on them.
