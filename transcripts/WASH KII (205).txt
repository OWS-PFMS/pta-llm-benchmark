
In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda? 



 [Shortage of water and its infrastructures] there is no continuous supply of water in the town. Since I am from Jinka town by birth, I know that there is a big difference between the existing water services and infrastructures at the early stages of Jinka and after its expansion, there is water service-population size incapability now. This is because the town’s population has increased drastically while the water infrastructure remains unchanged and very old. Moreover, the existing water schemes will disappear during the dry seasons adding another constraint to the existing water shortage. For this reason, town dwellers and other service sectors that need water are now receiving water services by shift system. The shift system is particularly common during the dry seasons where we receive water once a week and even it may take two weeks or a month, sometimes. [Lack of efficient use/management of existing water] this is because of un programmed /unwise use of at LEAST the existing water. This may be attributed to lack of adequate and skilled man power to provide efficient water delivery and to maintain the existing water services. For example when the pipes are damaged it takes long time to maintain because of shortage of technicians in the sector. [Weak public awareness] Some people think that they can use the water unwisely/as they need because of the philosophy that they are paying for the service. Many people don’t use the water services wisely; we see that taps are open throughout the days and nights, plus to that the cultures of using water from other sources like from rain water for washing and cleaning purposes is not common in our society.

What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

For the water shortage: finding additional water sources that will be added to the main water tank/bank is mandatory.  this may be from the nearby rivers (‘enzineri’ and ‘konte’) since they are flowing throughout the year and there is less probability of drying. So, we can reserve, treat and use them for drinking water. If this is not possible, we should increase water sources through digging additional boreholes. Regarding old infrastructures, they should be replaced by new and modern ones in a planned manner. While changing them, care should be taken install the pipelines free from connection of main roads, telecommunication and power poles. This process should take the leading role as it is the key to water service sustainability and starting from the water sources to the tankers. If you can see, Jinka town has expanded and It seems that the periphery of the town are not adequately receiving pipe water since there are no new water infrastructure developments parallel to the town’s expansion. Though our TVET issituated at the periphery, we are receiving water, but if you can see the newly established areas a bit far from the TVET, including the fiber factory, they are not receiving the service yet. I can say that nearly ¼ of the areas of the town are not receiving water through pipelines in Jinka. Thus, these people have two chances to get water, the first is going back to the center of the town to buy and use pure water in a relatively high price and the second option is using the water around them which is not treated well and will expose them to waterborne diseases. 

Regarding water management and expert issues: it is important to have skilled man power to use the water services more economically, to maintain pipes and lines when damaged and to respond to the demands of the users urgently. The employees in the sector should be well trained, but sometimes we observe that the water office hires people who have experience on water maintenance without having the actual certificates. Instead, they can hire young experts who are being produced from the TVET, either in a contract or formal employment forms.   

Concerning the public awareness issues, we need to teach the people in a campaign. We should introduce to them that each drop of water has value. We have to teach them that they have to use water wisely while cleaning the materials in a kitchen, washing cloths and the water they use for their toilet.  These campaigns can me made effective through schools, the water office itself or to maximum we can introduce rules and regulations that can punish people using water unwisely. This is because I personally observe that people usually leave water taps untapped and they are leaving them overnight.

Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? 

The first one, finding additional water sources, will be an absolute solution to bring about sustainable water services. This means increasing the current water availability in the water tanks through using additional water sources and also replacing the existing old infrastructures by a new and modern one. What next steps could happen…? I think it can solve the current water shortage problem up to 95 %. This is because when there is adequate water source and enough water in the tanks, we can address demand issues to all people and get away with the shifting system, especially during dry seasons. Without adequate sources of water, it would be ideal to think about skilled man power and other infrastructure issues. Thus, emphasis should be given to add water sources. 

In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water services? 

Lack of strong coordination is the cause of the major water service problems. I have told you previously that there is lack of skilled man power in the sector, which could have been solved with strong stalk holders’ coordination. For example, the TVET has trainees in the department of water pipeline supply and sanitation, both in a short term and regular schemes; hence one of the potential stalk holders that need to coordinate with water sector will be this institute. We have been working with the zone water, mine and energy department and the Jinka hospital so far through supplying young trainees in the form of apparent ship program but were not in a more coordinated and programmed way. The relation we have with the town water utility is not as such strong except the reaction to the request of material and technical supply, sometimes. Also, we could have potential to promote improved techniques of searching for and digging water holes, systems of water management and infrastructure maintenance techniques by using WASHcos and permission of the zone water department.  We have also the potential to give short term trainings using our fresh graduates and to some extent we have been practicing that up on the request, but in reality the coordination system is week and not as expected. This may be because every organization runs to accomplish its own plans and mission instead of discussing and working together and the other reason is that the trend of inviting our institute for capacity development or other intervention areas is very weak. What could be improved? I hope problems related to lack of skilled man power will be drastically solved if the zone water department takes the initiative to work with other stalk holders including our institute. This is due to the fact that we have better instruments and skills that are needed for water service sustainability in the zone. Instead of hiring people who have adopted techniques through experience, it would be better to hire our graduates.

In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the woreda. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?

First of all, all water stalk holders should come to the table, discuss major water sustainability problems and prepare common plans and strategies since coordination can possibly solve almost all water sustainability challenges I mentioned earlier. After that, they may share action plans bounded in a specific period of time and activities and come back again for discussions to evaluate on how the shared plans and activities have been implemented and what the major gaps have been observed. The zone water department or our TVET can take the initiative and the leading role for this program.
