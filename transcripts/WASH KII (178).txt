1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Interviewer: In your opinion what are the main challenges or constraints that affect the sustainability of WASH service in your woreda? When I say WASH service, it means water service. What are the main problems observed in this service?
Respondent: the problems related to ensure accessibility of drinking water or to maintain sustainable water service for the community; first, inability to prevail the problem of drinking water accessibility for the community fundamentally. Out of the woreda total population (which is around 176 to 177 thousand) clean water service provision was believed to be around 23 to 24 percent. Accordingly, community basic needs are not uphold. In my opinion, incompetence of government to deliver clean water service for citizen by coordinating different parties by itself isthe key and one of the problem.
Second, the community does not have sense of belongingness to care and manage properly the existing water facilities. More or less there are existing water facilities developed either by the government or NGOs. Some of them expect the government or other parties to provide maintenance and security for the water facility. On the other hand, absence of regular and continuous assistance for the existing water schemes from the stakeholders also has its own impact. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned the basic problems related to water and sanitation. Now let us discuss about the solutions to each problem. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: I understand that the provision for clean water service requires massive expenditures. It is important to build vast deep water well mainly through reconciling the public interest. The other main thing is to mobilize the society. It is important to create sense of belongingness with in the community for water schemes through training and awareness enhancement campaigns and other ways.
Interviewer: to mobilize or to create belongingness within the society, what do you think should be done?
Respondent: Once the water facility being implemented seeing the project as a one-time campaign is the big problem from our side; especially from the government side. In order to solve such problems, it is vital to establish an institution under the water resource sector in coordination with all relevant stakeholders. Predominantly, at the grass root level (at Kebele level). For instance, other than unions it is also essential to address the community, federations and Wereds’ decision makers. 

3.  Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: what do you think must be done to solve the problem of continuous assistance for the existing water schemes?
Respondent: first, it is important to mobilize the society and create sense of awareness of the existing water facilities. Thus, the community can contribute in kind in handling regular maintenance of the facilities. This in turn reduces the burden of maintenance cost and save the limited resources to other areas for the development of new schemes. 
Governmental and other non-governmental organizations that can support the water development should clearly identify the practical problems of the community to ensure sustainable water service. They must check the reality on ground, whether or not the society being the beneficiary from water schemes and find out a solution in collaboration with relevant stakeholders.
