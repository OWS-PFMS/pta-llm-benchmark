Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: What I can say are the main challenges mostly is the community management committees performance then the second one is the repairs and maintenance to those water schemes,
Interviewer: What about the first one?
Respondent: The first one about the committee performance? Majorly about the committees performance is how they handle the cash. Cash referring to the money resulting from the water sales. You may find that they have some financial mismanagement, challenges of record keeping and maybe for the large schemes whereby they collect large amounts of money, they may not have proper ways of handling the cash and when they collect it, it may dissappear even without helping them in any way.
Interviewer: The second one you said 
Respondent: The second one is on the repairs and maintenance to these water schemes. You find that even despite collecting the money from the water sales, the spare parts may not be available to them and whenever they are available you find that they barely meet the costs of those spare parts because the money maybe was not collected or it was collected and they cannot account for it and here now they need a sparepart they cannot afford, when it is available they cannot pay for it. Then it becomes a challenge in operating the scheme because the minor repairs, if they are not repaired, will lead to major repairs which will also guarantee maybe rehabilitation and when not done the project will stall.
Interviewer: What other challenge?
Respondent: I had majorly said they are two, then the others just result from those two.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Okay now, solutions to these problems, starting with the first one about the management, as an office we are trying as much as we can , we've development some monthly monitoring reports/logs to be filled by those management committees every month they'll be recording how water is being pumped for those which have got pumping mechanisms, they record the amount of water they are abstracting, the amount of water they are distributing, the money they are collecting, from the readings we will also be able to track the non-revenue water, and they also record the actual sales which they collected from there we also calculate the money which they did not collect then from there we now develop ways on how to approach those challenges because when we monitor how water is being sold, and how they are distributing it we will be able to develop even mechanisms to save the money they collect and then if we manage now the committees operations I know the repairs and maintenance we will be good enough to handle them because if we have the money we will be able to repair.
Interviewer: How do you make sure that the reports that they fill in is the right information?
Respondent: Okay we are engaging the county administration that is the village administrators and ward administrators and to an extend we also involve the chiefs that is to the schemes which have individual connections because maybe some people may deny access to the water meters in their compounds but if we involve the chief we will be able to record everything. Then the village administrators are with these committees every second week of the month they see whether they recorded the meter readings in the start of the month and at the end of the month they also go check again. Then when they are filling the forms they are being helped by the administrators then when they come to us we interpret.
Interviewer: What other recommendation?
Respondent: The other recommendation will be on the repairs to the abnormalities in the schemes. We are doing some partnerships with FundiFix, in Mwingi North. We are having schemes signing up repair and maintenance contracts with FundiFix whereby they'll be paying something little from the water sales then they will help them. They will avail the spare parts, they are also having mechanics and plumbers, they are responding to the smaller issues then when it comes to a major rehabilitation or replacing a major component in the scheme, the County Government plus some other actors who are now joining the county we are able to raise some funds and replace them. And again in repairs there are now those schemes which have not signed up maintenance contracts with FundiFix. We are engaging them together with the community around, we identify some young men and also women who are willing to be trained on how maybe to respond to the smaller issues like bursts. We show them how to deal with the bursts, engine servicing, that is the generators, changing the oil, the filters and all that. Then we are using our county staff. If it is the electrician, for the electricity nobody touches the electric bit other than the electrician. But if it is the bursts, we can calculate the materials for them, we buy for them we give them but we show them how to work or how to respond to those bursts.
Interviewer: Those are now the?
Respondent: The schemes which are not registered with FundiFix.
Interviewer: You mean you identify local artisans, train them?
Respondent: Yes. Local artisans we train them. We conduct that as an on job training. Like maybe in our monthly workplans we fix some dates like two days in a week we be in a certain ward. We identify like two schemes, we meet those two people from those two different projects we show them physically.
Interviewer: Are you doing this in collaboration with FundiFix
Respondent: Okay we are doing that to the schemes which are not targeted by FundiFix. Whereby they have signed up with FundiFix, FundiFix is doing everything for them. But for the schemes which are not willing, you know it's a matter of sensitizing them, they agree or disagree with you. For those which are not agreeing we are showing them the sense little by little but we show those local artisans how to do it.
Interviewer: What other ideas do you have?
Respondent: The other ideas I have is maybe the FundiFix if it can be upscaled they assist us in embracing these local artisans other than their artisans. Because you see there are even schemes which whereby they sign up then after like two years they are no willing to continue, they want pull out. So if we can embrace this idea of investing in those local artisans, it will be fine.
Interviewer: Why do they pull out?
Respondent: Maybe it's the operations. Because you see when they sensitize those communities to sign there is the maximum time they are allocating to respond to an issue and you find that if the downtime is extending, the limits they communicated to those people they will start issues. And you find that if it is a major component there comes a challenge because you have signed up with FundiFix let's say maybe the pump motor is burnt out, it is an expensive one. Like if the motor power rating is 3Kw and above, that will go to almost Ksh.80,000 and above. And if FundiFix is not able to replace that motor in the span of like four days, there will be an alarm. Then if now it comes that is the work of the government now and you find that maybe in our budgets that component is not fixed or you cannot just go and say you need such and such an amount to purchase a motor. It will delay, it will take time. So by the time you go back there it's like they are not willing. So I will say it's the operations. because I know if we are having young men and women, in these schemes who are able to respond to bursts, they can tighten gate valves and they can replace them we will not have this non-revenue water. 
So because we are now seeing to it that these management committees are streamlined and they save the money. We are also embracing that they open up bank accounts which will be monitored by the officers then all withdraws will be monitored, and they will be verified through minutes by those committees and we also track how they are saving money. And also remember, all these operations they are now channeled back to the community, they own up the scheme. And the more we  embrace these local artisans, the more we will even reduce some  vandalisms, communities are different around here, some may own up the project very quickly, others may take time to own it. But if they are fully involved in the operations and they be helped in the financial management and collection of those sales and savings. These schemes will be sustainable.
Interviewer: About the local artisans are they doing it under cost or are they doing it for voluntary? 
Respondent: They are not doing it on voluntary basis, they are doing it at a cost but since it is somebody from that community we are able to reach a consensus whereby they are being paid something little just to maintain them in the area. They are not charging that high.
Interviewer: What do you mean by that high?
Respondent: Because you see if you go for a maybe somebody who went for some plumbing classes, he is trained and has got certificates and he is there at home, then you go for him or her definitely you expect him to charge something high than that other local person which maybe you trained and he or she is working out of the experience which you have showed them. And again the local artisans they are doing the minor repairs like just doing repairing a burst and you see somebody with his or her certificates you cannot go and commit him to stay in a scheme just to be responding to a burst alone.
Interviewer: So these local artisans are only for small repairs?
Respondent: Yes small repairs and replacements.
Interviewer: How about the major repairs?
Respondent: The major repairs will come in now in two types, there are the more technical repairs and the major replacements now. The major replacements are been done by the government or even the partners. FundiFix is also helping us fundraise through some other partners through them. They buy us some pump motors then we replace them. The County is also giving out mechanics and electricians now to respond to the technical repairs which are needed. Like I told you if it is electricity nobody can touch those cables, it is the electrician alone.
Interviewer: How long does that take after you report to the county for them to come on the ground and respond?
Respondent: If it is an electrical fault, because when these committees report that maybe our scheme is not working we just ask them how is it behaving. We are maybe engaging the pump then the control pan is tripping, so quickly I just know that is an electric fault. I call the electrician, in Mwingi North we have one electrician. So it will take like maximum of two days. He is there, he rectifies that problem. But if needs maybe a component to be bought to be replaced, the committee has to buy it. If they cannot meet that cost, it's when now it will go for maybe more than a week as we look for funds to buy that component. But if it is maybe a fault which can be rectified there by there it will take a span of two days maximum.

Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: The interventions we are currently doing though we are almost winding up, we are doing some water pans, we've done six in the sub-county, distributed in the five wards. The other thing we are doing is distributing water tanks, 10,000L capacity to schools, to some market centers, then to these market centers we are targeting those water tanks maybe to be collecting, during rainy season they'll be collecting water from market shades then during the drier periods whereby there is no rainy water we normally do some water trucking so we put water to those tanks and the towns will be having water. The other tanks are going to the primary schools and secondary schools and hospitals.
Interviewer: What informed all those interventions?
Respondent: Mwingi North is dry but we have rains in two seasons. So we thought of if we can maybe increase storage of this water we can move an extra mile to reducing the water shortage. 
Interviewer; Who now thought?
Respondent: It was the officers plus the community because we requested the tanks through some community proposals. And the tanks are not only being brought by the county government but also partners like NDMA they are bringing tanks.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: Through the WASH Forums, we meet with different officers now from the other sub-counties and also other actors in Kitui County performing WASH activities, and you find that maybe an actor is working in Kitui South and we are here in Mwingi North but we exchange ideas on how they are doing. Like the last forum we had that was last year, it was Red Cross, they were buying tanks to boreholes to be fixed to some boreholes and these were the high yielding boreholes because you could find that the ideas we got from the WASH forums. The I had said that I learnt something whereby some water tanks were being distributed to the high yielding boreholes whereby the storage capacity was not enough or maybe it was mismatched with the pump capacity. Whereby you find that a pump can produce like 20 cubic in a day but the tank which was fitted there in the initial designs was 10. So they add up another. So the idea came, now increasing the storage will be an idea to reduce water scarcity.
Interviewer: How else did the WASH Forum influence your organization's interventions?
Respondent: I also learnt that the borehole water sometimes we do not maximize the borehole water because we have a borehole but maybe it's having only one kiosk or a point source. So by getting this water distributed to some individual connections will mean extra money to the management committee and when they have money that's where they also get an idea of maybe extending the water. Another Kilometer, they shorten the walking distance like that.
Interviewer: Anything else that you would want to add?
Respondent: Nothing else other than the local artisans, we should embrace that, we empower them.
