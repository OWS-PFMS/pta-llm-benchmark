1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Interviewer: what are the main problems of water service sustainability in Jinka town and in the zone? 
Respondent: As water schemes are capital intensive and resources are scarce in our town there is low coverage of potable water in the town, although I don’t exactly remember the percentage of coverage right now. 
There is huge gap between the surge in demand and the current supply.
The existing water schemes repeatedly break down which might be due to construction issues, misuse of the water facilities, lack of awareness, or lack of on time maintenance.
Interviewer: Who is responsible for these problems?
Respondent: These problems can be attributed to the water institute itself and user community. There is established reporting system and any problem should be reported timely. They should anticipate when some spare parts should be replaced, but they are trying to repair the spare parts after they are damaged. Repairing it after it is broken takes long time. There are even some that left without being repaired. 
Incompatibility between the service and beneficiaries: even the existing few schemes would be forced to serve huge number of users which overburdens the water schemes and thereby frequent break down.
Further, Spare parts are pricey and are beyond the capacity of the local community. These parts are not easily available in the local market whenever needed.
Cash collected from community and subsidy is not enough either to purchase spare parts or provide continuous maintenance. There is huge dependency on NGOs or government. 


2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: Everyone has the right to have access to clean water. So, government should allocate enough budgets based on local demands. New schemes should be built and the existing ones should also get maintained or just replaced. There is severe non-functionality rate among water schemes. Therefore, due attention should be given to maintenance of existing water schemes. The high dependency syndrome on NGOs and donors need to be revisited 
Feasibility studies and designs should be conducted properly. Stakeholders should base their budget allocation and activity breakdown on the current and future need 
Community should be the owner of the projects to protect the water schemes through WASHCO and other associations in the community. Local community should guard/fence and properly manage its water schemes.
In the long run, climate issues and reforestation should be addressed to increase availability of water.
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: you have mentioned a lot of solutions which is the most important one?
Respondent: Awareness creation is the most crucial one because stakeholders will develop sense of ownership and make their contribution. 
Interviewer: Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: If community members develop a sense of ownership, water schemes will be handled properly. In addition, simply building new water schemes will not cut it. The government should allocate sufficient budget for procurement of spare parts and maintenance accessories. For instance, maintenance of shallow wells require huge machineries such as cranes which are not available even at zonal level, thus, the region should make these inputs available for quick repairs of water schemes. 
Further, there is huge gap within trained technical personnel who are capable of repairing generators, water pumps and so on. Stakeholders should focus training technicians from TVET graduates. These could be effective remedies for the problems I have pointed out.
