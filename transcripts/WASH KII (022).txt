Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: One is model of management, two frequency of breakdown, three financial management, four high cost of spares and pipes. 
Interviewer: Any other?
Respondent: No
Interviewer: So could you explain each of those in details how they influence water service sustainability? We can start with the first one you've mentioned which is the model of management.
Respondent: You see, when we implement a project, when the County government or the National Government or a donor completes a project, the project is normally handed over to a management committee which is elected by the group members of the area where the project is serving. Then the community, normally they start very well and with time they start consuming the money that they collect as revenue. So at the end of the day you find that they cannot, if it is fuel they cannot fuel the generator, they cannot even repair a broken pipe, so with time that project breaks down. When it breaks down they normally come back to us, we go and repair and the problem always persists. So we train them, we retrain them but things are not getting any better but some of them are trying to cope up. There is one point that I left there, another one is political influence.
Interviewer: So let's go systematically, there is the second one that you had mentioned about frequency of breakdown.
Respondent: In most cases, especially boreholes, you find that things like motor, things like pumps, you know they are electrical things and their lifespan is normally one year to two years. And when a motor or a pump breaks down, you see their cost is very high and most of the management committees they normally don't have that kind of money and especially during this dry period when the county doesn't have money. When the government has not released the money it may end up during the dry spell, those people may end up not getting any water for that period until at such a time when the government releases the money is when the County Government or the water department will address that problem.
Interviewer: There is this other one, financial management.
Respondent: Normally, most of the people who are elected as the managers or the management committees they normally don't want to account for the money that they sell water. Actually even when we train them on how they should read the meter, how they should record what they have sold, how they should subtract you will always find that they don't follow that. So other places let's say like Tharaka, you find they have a manager who normally goes to the water kiosk and collect money, and he cheats the management committee that there are some breakdowns somewhere and he collects the money and he go and eat that money. You find that project loosing almost Kshs 200,000 within a span of six months. So chief will also go and collect the money, water officer will also go and collect the money. So you will find that in most cases those people they don't have any money to run the projects. Other projects the chairman will tend to chase the other committee members away and run the borehole alone. So you find that there is no transparency. When we ask, he tells us when he calls them for a meeting they don't come. So you find that the boreholes most of them are run by one person and in most cases some don't have even accounts.
Interviewer: You mean now the group, the management committee?
Respondent: Yes. So you find that it is a crisis. But I think the problem has been occassioned by that we don't have a proper bill. We don't have a proper act, County Water Act that can empower the sub-county water officers to tame that mismanagement.
Interviewer: The last one you mentioned about political interference. How is that affecting water services sustainability?
Respondent: In most cases, when we have election, after five years we normally elect MCAs(Members of County Assembly), when we elect the MCAs, every member would like to put those people who campaigned for them, would like to put them as the management as the managers of those projects in his ward.
Interviewer: So you mean they dictate who is to be elected?
Respondent: Yes, they normally have a lot influence so you might find that a committee actually was doing very well, running the water project very well but after when we have election the MCA would like to put the people who campaigned for him to be the managers of the water projects. So he might put people who cannot manage.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent:  Actually, the solution we can have with the sustainability of rural water supplies is to have a proper Water Act and a proper model of management.
Interviewer: Do you have suggestions of what you are calling a proper model of management?
Respondent: We have like FundiFix, we can give like an individual, somebody can be given to run a project and sustain it individually. And then we can also empower those groups who are managing their water projects well they can be empowered by actually capacity building them.
Interviewer: Any other suggestion?
Respondent: I think those are the key ones. Because when we don't have an Act, it is very hard to tame those who mismanage the finance. 

Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Actually the most important is to have the Water Act in place.
Interviewer: Because?
Respondent: Because the Water Act will have the by-laws that will enable the water office and the sub-county water office to have control of the water supplies in their sub-counties and therefore mismanagement will be minimized.
Interviewer: Any other thing that you would add to that point?
Respondent: I think that is the key one.

Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: What do you mean by interventions?
Interviewer: Interventions, what activities are you doing in Kitui?
Respondent: The activities that we are doing, we are trying to increase the water coverage by either extending the existing pipelines, or building some new dams, building some new sump-wells which can have good water and cover a very big area, we are also doing sub-surface dams, we are also drilling new boreholes to the areas that are not covered.
Interviewer: So what informed all those decisions?
Respondent: Actually one, is the distance to the water sources, two time taken to draw water, three the cost of water.
Interviewer: Can you say something about the cost of water and the other one that you have mentioned?
Respondent: During the dry spell, you'll find that cost of water will go Kshs 20 per 20 liter jerry can or Kshs 50 and when the times are normal water will cost at around Kshs 3-5 so you find that during the dry spell, people would buy water between Kshs 20 and Kshs 50 per 20 liter jerry can. Two, most of the dams dry during the dry season so you'll find that people will walk almost six to seven kilometers to get water. So that is what has informed us to do more water supplies.
Interviewer: So you mean the time that community takes to go to a water source? You mean the distances?
Respondent: Yeah. The distance, the time taken because when you go to a borehole you'll find that there are so many people. Over a thousand people. You might even end up sleeping there waiting for water.
Interviewer: So in such areas those are the areas you take your interventions to?
Respondent: Those are the areas we start with.

Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: Actually the WASH Forum has assisted us in so many ways because there before there were NGOs that were doing much about work and they were not reporting. So you would find that there is an NGO doing some water works somewhere and it is even not known. So when we started water forum, every NGO which is doing the water works, normally come and present what they are doing.
Interviewer: How else?
Respondent: We have learnt a lot about...there before we didn't have data of water coverage in every sub-county or in the whole county but now because of the WASH Forum, we have been informed and that we came with a decision of the survey of water. I don't know how to put it. There was a survey that was done of water coverage within the county. So that one actually came out of the WASH Forum.
Interviewer: So through the WASH Forums you mean you've been able to organize or to get data?
Respondent: Yeah we've been able to get data of water coverage within the county. 
Interviewer: So how about the data?
Respondent: The data has shown the areas that are covered and the areas which are not covered so it has informed us on areas that we should concentrate on.
Interviewer: How else has participating in the WASH Forum influenced your organization's interventions?
Respondent: Because when we meet there at the WASH Forum because we have now the data of the water coverage actually when we meet at the water forum, we would highlight the areas that are not covered that if a donor wants to do something he should go to such and such a place where it is not covered.
Interviewer: Anything else that you would like to add?
Respondent: No. There are so many things, I think those ones are enough.
Interviewer: Anything you feel maybe it is important to go to our survey?
Respondent: Actually it should be done regularly after three years. The data of water coverage should be collected after every three years.
Interviewer: You mean updating?
Respondent: Yes updating. Should be updated after every three years so that everybody could be informed of what has been done and the areas that have been left.
