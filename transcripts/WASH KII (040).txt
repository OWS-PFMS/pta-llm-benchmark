Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? 
Respondent:  From my experience, most of the water resources are not well maintained. Especially boreholes. I have no major issues with earth dams. The maintenance of boreholes is becoming a real issue. Sources are sometimes very crowded and being overused. This puts a strain on the boreholes and hence they keep breaking down. The crowding also causes a lot of conflicts because of the large number of people fetching water from one borehole.
Another issue is politics. Most of the boreholes done by the government are politically driven and when they are politically driven, some corruption sets in. There is an area where there is one borehole done by the Constituency Development Fund (CDF) in 2007. The contractor did not drill the recommended depth of the borehole because he was told that at as soon as he struck water, it was ok for him to stop drilling. The contractor then shared out the money that would have been used to drill to the correct depth and of course some community members got some money. The borehole does not have a casing so when you put a pump in, it does not take a while before breaking down-it will always break down. Some people will follow up, get another pump and it also breaks down. So, I managed to get someone who does hydrogeological surveys and asked what we could do to the borehole and if it can be dug deeper to achieve the recommended meters but he said he was not sure whether the person who drilled the borehole left some materials inside the borehole and he did not want to risk his equipment because if for instance there was something like a metal rod or some other component left inside the borehole, it could easily destroy drilling equipment. I found that heartbreaking because if there is borehole drilled somewhere, it should serve the people. Politics and corruption are an issue. Another issue is that when they breakdown the challenge is where to get the finances to do repair especially the government boreholes, once they break down, they take long before they are fixed.
Interviewer: Does that mean the boreholes by other organizations last longer or are functional for a longer period? 
Respondent: Yes
Interviewer: What are the reasons for that?
Respondent: Organizations operate differently from the government. How the non-governmental organizations (NGOs) work is not how the government works. You see, NGOs are highly answerable to the donors. We cannot report that we gave money to a specific project and it is not working. We cannot do a shoddy job if the donors are strict. The supervision is high and when they drill, we ensure they drill to the recommended depth, and install good quality materials, the casings, the pipes and we also want to show sustainability because we want more money for more projects. For the government, the projects are politically motivated. For example, if a governor goes to a place and they say their problem is water, then the Governor says their government will give the community a borehole, they will come and drill, but no one is supervising. So that element of supervision to me is an issue. 
Also, good will is a factor. If I am providing a solution to this community, I should really provide a solution. It’s not acceptable to have communities to fetch water for a year then the boreholes dry up. I know of boreholes that have dried up, but I also know that the government has an issue with technology. The hydrogeological surveys need some really smart equipment especially for this region. In Kitui south it is possible to drill a borehole only to find that is dry after you have invested money. The county government has drilled a dry borehole, CDF as well but we have never drilled a dry borehole. Actually, the technology and the hydrogeological survey should be the first major step. If you get it wrong you waste funds, if you get it right you get good water.
Interviewer: How do the boreholes drilled by organizations and handed over to community continue to function and give service to communities after the organizations leave? So, if they breakdown and the organization already left how does that differ from the government drilled boreholes?
Respondent: There are quite a number of differences because one, when we are drilling the borehole we are in partnership with the government because at the end of the day we cannot own a borehole. Water belongs to the government. The NGOs do good investment in training and quality materials because if you have quality there is more sustainability. Two, it’s partnerships. Like the scenario of Kilisa borehole which was drilled by another organization but our organization came in to offer a solution through installing a desalination system. So even if the organization that drilled it leaves, our organization, will still be there to make sure the people are still getting water. Then the other issue is with the community. There is that talk that the facilities we have set up belong to our organization and I think it is because of the high level of engagement that we do with them, we are always calling them and following up, whenever there is an issue we are always there to solve. The fact that we are so loud in that community, they also feel like they have a responsibility to take care of the borehole.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to the problems the mentioned?
Respondent: On maintenance, I know the sub county water engineer always goes around checking these boreholes. If they were well funded it would be easier but you hear that the government doesn’t have money so you wait until the government gets money, that is a challenge. As a solution, if the government talked to the existing organizations and then maybe we have a forum whereby we can agree that the organizations working in the region can take up the repairing of some of the boreholes that are still owned by the government. What happens in the community is that if a borehole was done by the county government and it breaks down, you will not see a proposal from them to NGOs requesting for support. They will always talk to the Members of County Assembly (MCAs) and the governor yet partnerships with NGOs and others can help. Some organizations are flexible. Our flexibility is limited, but if we took up the discussion, it might work. 
There is a certain community borehole where we wanted to change from generator to a solar pumping system and we approached Germany Agro Action because we wanted them to supply that water to a special school somewhere for free if we gave them the solar system but then they said they had already entered into an agreement with another NGO that would give them the solar system. So, partnerships are possible. Some organizations are open if they are approached and if the discussion is started to help with the maintenance. 
The other issue is on corruption, we have talked about good leadership-a lot of talk and less action because we are still voting for the wrong people and with time people are going to be enlightened on good governance. This change in government structure was something new to us so we did not know how to differentiate between technocrats and politicians because our minds are fixed on politicians. However, with enlightenment on good governance, we will be able to have good leaders who will make sure the little money that the government has, because for sure the government does not have money, they can deliver something good. About finances, of course some money will be spent on these boreholes so if we got genuine people, for example, if it is a member of parliament and there is a borehole being done, if he sees that 5 million shillings have been allocated to a borehole, he or she should stick to that and ensure that the 5 million goes to that borehole and in this way they will have given a quality borehole and sustainability is achieved but if you give half cooked solutions or use part of the money allocated for the boreholes through other ways, the borehole will constantly have issues.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: It will still be the finances because everything revolves around money. If there is adequate finance there will be quality of the materials used in the borehole, quality in the services. If it is drilling and it should be to a depth of 200 meters and the people who are supposed to supervise are given their per diems and are empowered to supervise, it will be better. The finances will also cater for the training of the community members running the boreholes and money for maintenance will also be available. Maintenance will not be an issue, if something breaks down, they repair within one month at most.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: We drill and equip boreholes. So far, we have drilled a number of boreholes in the last year, about four of them. However, one of the boreholes was very saline and so we did not equip it because we would not be providing good quality water. We are also doing pipeline extensions from the boreholes that we have drilled to the nearest villages so as to decongest the water points because one village can have like 800 jerricans in one water point which results in community members spending a lot of time fetching water. We supply plastic water tanks to schools. to harvest rain water and recently we have provided a desalination system at Kilisa borehole. 
Going back to the question on what informed selection of these interventions, I can give an example of Kilisa borehole and the reason for our intervention was a matter of providing a solution. We figured that 4 or 5 million shillings had been spent on it for drilling and the water is being pumped and there is a generator. So instead of letting all that water go to waste why don’t we just improve it to me make it good for the community. Sometimes we look at the investment made and instead of letting money go to waste we think of how to improve it. Unfortunately, we realized that the desalination system is an expensive plant. So that is why we did not go ahead and equip the saline one we drilled. 
The other issue about drilling boreholes, we go into a region and visit groups and check the distances from where they are fetching water, then when we see that there is really a need for more water systems, we come up with a proposal, present it to the founder of the organization, he looks for money, if he is given the money, we go ahead to drill boreholes.
About the pipeline extensions, we even did an extension to a borehole that we had not drilled ourselves. We provided water through the extensions to some of the schools that we have sponsored and of course some people got some points to fetch from, therefore decongesting the original points. So those are some of the things that we consider if we have schools around and there is an existing borehole, and we have not done the borehole then we can even pipe that water to another region.
