
In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in South Omo Zone?

The main challenges to water service sustainability in the zone are: First, less community participation. Mostly both governmental and non-governmental organization engage in the development of new rural water schemes. However, due to weak participation of the community in maintenance and management of the water schemes, they become non-functional within short period of time. Second, woreda water departments do not build the capacity of the community on how to repair water schemes. Rather they go to kebeles and repair damaged water schemes. But to make the water service sustainable the woreda water offices should train the community on how to use the water schemes properly and how to repair when they become broken.  Third, lack of spare parts to repair water schemes on time. There are no spare part sale outlet (stores) in the woreda towns. Fourth, there is no link between stakeholders in the sector. For instance, organizations develop rural water schemes and exit from the area without discussing with relevant stakeholders on the management of the water schemes. These are the main problems to water service sustainability in South Omo Zone. 

What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Regarding community participation, we should engage the community in every activities of water scheme development. That is we should participate the community from assessment work (identification of potential areas for water scheme development) to final implementation of the project. We should also engage kebele administration representatives in the construction and management of water schemes. Moreover, we should form water management committees (WASHcos) and involve them throughout the implementation process of water projects. If these WASH committees have practical exposure, they will properly manage the water schemes. In addition training should be given to the communities as well as to WASH committees on operation and maintenance of water schemes. By doing this we can make the water service more sustainable. We should also create water users associations for each water schemes. The importance of creating water users association is that members of the association will contribute some amount of money that will be sued for maintenance of the schemes. As a result the water scheme will sustainably serve the community.

Regarding problems related to capacity building of the community, there is community mobilization unit at woreda level. This unit is responsible to support WASH committees and water users associations. However, in practice they are not supporting the community related to operation and management of water schemes. Thus, I recommend that this community mobilization unit should have a plan to support the community on operation and management of water schemes. This unit should also collaborate with non-governmental organization in building the capacity of the community to ensure sustainable water service in the area.

Pertinent to the availability spare parts, there should be a revolving fund at woreda level that will be used to supply spare parts. To this end, we should form associations that supply spare parts to the community using the revolving fund. For instance, we can organize the woreda youths that are interested to engage on the supply of spare parts and provide maintenance service to the water schemes using the revolving fund. This would enable the community to access spare parts at its vicinity.

Regarding coordination, there are administrative bodies at kebele level and there is also water office at woreda level. So there should be regular information exchange between these entities when there is any problem related to water schemes. For example, when the water schemes become broken, the kebele administration should instantly report to the woreda water office. Moreover, there is information gaps between woreda stakeholders and zonal stakeholders, as a result there is duplication of effort. That is different organization goes to the same kebele to construct water schemes. Thus, there should be formal information exchange about plans and activities of different organizations. The information flow from kebele to zone should be uniform. There should also be asset inventory and should be communicated to all stakeholders in the sector (i.e. there should be information about which water schemes are functional and which water schemes are non-functional in the woreda).  Some NGOs also implement water projects without involving the woreda water office and other relevant stakeholders in the woreda. As a result the woreda stakeholders do not follow-up the functionality of these water schemes. Therefore, to ensure sustainable water service in the woreda there must be strong coordination between stakeholders in the WASH sector.  

Of the solution you listed, which is the most important?  Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs? 

Of the solutions I mentioned the most important is establishing water management committees for each water schemes in the woreda. This committees will involve the community in the operation and management of the water schemes. The other important solution is creating access to spare parts for the maintenance of water schemes. Spare part should be available at least at woreda level. Most of the time shallow wells and hand-dug wells become broken within a month time. This is because the community use the water not only for their household consumption but also for their livestock. Because of this the community over exploit the water schemes. To regularly repair these water schemes there should be shopping centers for spare parts in the area. 

In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved? 

In the first place there is no effort to create coordination between stakeholders. There is no collective planning and collective action in the zone. For the future there should be a task force that involve kebele administrators, woreda stakeholders, zonal stakeholders and NGOs. Particularly, we should form water coordination task force or WASH coordination task force in the area. The reason why I suggest this is that it avoids duplication of efforts and enhance the coordination between stakeholders in the area. It will also improve the information flow among stakeholders functioning in the area. 

In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?

Coordination has diversified role in ensuring water service sustainability. For instance, as I mentioned earlier WASH coordination task force can identify each and every WASH activities including rehabilitation of water schemes and drilling of new boreholes, and share the information to all stakeholders. It will also identify which water schemes are functional and which are non-functional, and set priority areas of intervention. It will also identify the gaps in water management committees. Therefore, the coordination task force enable us to systematically alleviate the problems related to water service sustainability.
