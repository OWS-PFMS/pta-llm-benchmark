Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: The main challenges to water service sustainability they are quite several, but majorly the issue of technical capacity of the water management committees which are in charge of the rural water schemes. I think that is where the main challenge is, because you realize that the distribution of the water to the communities is supposed to follow a certain model, but you realize that due to lack of technical capacity it is just like a trial and error way of doing things. Like in water distribution in some models we have distribution lines that are designated to serve designated areas. And those ones are not supposed to be interfered with like adding other connections to them so that the water can be delivered to the designated area but you realize that with the committees because of that lack of technical capacity and understanding they will just end up puncturing the whole pipeline, fixing private connections anyhow, anywhere, and that way they end up affecting the flow of the water in system. Therefore the challenge is about financial management. 
As you know these rural committees they collect some revenue through the tariffs which they have set or which we have set from the water consumers and so you realize that most of these funds they are not accounted for. So they are either misappropriated or they are either used but it is not easy to trace all the money how it has been used. And so this one has affected the sustainability because most of the schemes actually as we speak they are not able to meet their production cost, they are not able to meet their capital replacements like when motors burn, pumps get damaged and other major repairs. So that is a challenge and of course we have a few cases where they have been able to achieve this and they are able to partner with the government to achieve these repairs whenever they occur. All in all there is still a gap on this in terms of financial management and the financial holding capacity of the committees. And you realize that they are not able to follow the procedures that are supposed to ensure the money is properly utilized. Some of them they are not able to generate the annual budgets for their work, they are not able to come up with business plans so there is a gap in that, in terms of handling the funds. 
The other challenge when it comes to the sustainability of these water services, is the governance and this governance, I think one there is no proper policy even from the side of the government on how these committees are supposed to undertake their work. You realize that they are operating as social entities other than well-structured institutions. That can be gauged on legal grounds but as we speak, they are just social entities with no much legal orientation. So that one is a challenge and now you realize that even when we try to bring them on board and we try to make them achieve this sustainability it's also difficult even to us. Because there is that disconnect in terms of the policies. I think those are the main issues. Maybe the other issue that is now coming up is about the issues with climate change. You realize now the water levels are going down and most of the schemes when the drought bites they are not able to produce enough water to the consumers and so that one will affect even their financial capacity, it will also affect mainly the general water services at large.
Interviewer: Any other challenge?
Respondent: Those are the main challenges.
Interviewer: Could you speak something about governance. I know you have mentioned something about governance, could you expound on governance issues that you've said they are major challenges. You've talked about the policy, what else on governance?
Respondent: Governance is mainly about how the committees are constituted. You realize that after elections, after the committee is elected, democratically by the communities, it's quite difficult for them to stick together, and work together for a whole term. And mostly this disengagement comes in on the basis of financial issues and also other social problems among themselves. And the other issue is about interference from external actors like politicians; sometimes they infiltrate into the committees because of course their political interests. And so they cause disharmony among the committee members and as a result you find that the water services provision activities sometimes they collapse.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: The recommendation that I can give is these rural water schemes they need to at least have a person or one employee who has technical capacity on matters water distribution. So that they can be able to support the committee on these technical issues. They can be able to advise them on what to do with the system and what not to do. People who can understand how water flows and people who can diagnose challenges and then advice the committee on the intervention they are supposed to do. And this person is supposed to be able to also understand, is supposed to have financial capacity also so that they can also advice the committee on the same. So it's like if we have can have a model where we have a committee, well which is acting like a board, but we have another employee who is like a manager who is more knowledgeable so as to give technical advice to the committees so that they don't work out of ignorance or like what i was saying, through try and error. Because sometimes we have seen cases where rural water schemes are collapsing because of either ignorance or some capital mistakes which are committed as a result of lack of knowledge. 
Interviewer: Any other recommendation?
Respondent:  Maybe the other recommendation is because now, water is a social commodity and everybody will need water. Actually it is life. We say water is life so that means everybody must be able to get water. That means when we talk about financial capacity and we say sometimes committees are not able to meet their financial obligations, we cannot talk about increasing the tariffs per unit. But maybe we can look at other avenues where we can zero it, the operation and maintenance cost. Like now investing on solar energy, so that we move from electricity and also from diesel operated gensets. So i think for me  recommend that because that will easen the burden on expenditure and therefore even with a cost of Kshs2.00, a tariff of Kshs2.00 per 20L jerry can, then the scheme can be able to sustain its staff and also carry out the day to day operation and maintenance activities.
Interviewer: Any other idea?
Respondent: Maybe the other idea is now on continuous assessment of the committees to identify the gaps where they are and then do capacity building through on job trainings and also other forms of training. And then, the other thing is about...because the water sector is not a government business alone, the other partners like Rural Focus whom we know they have good skills, good ideas about the sustainability of this. We can also find ways of partnering with them so that they can be able to advice the committees through proper channels and the linkages; they can be able to work closely, with committees even in absence of the water officers. Committees can engage partners like Rural Focus and even get advice, they can also engage them on operation and maintenance issues and finally, is now like because there are also other private players who can also give an input in this, we also need to have a policy that can be able to bring all these partners who can bring an idea on how to make these schemes sustainable even if they are private. With a proper legislation, then I think it will work.  
Interviewer: Any other that you may wish to add?  
Respondent: Maybe for now we can go by that.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: You have talked about committees engaging a technical person, you've talked about adoption of solar energy, you've also talked about continuous assessment on the performance of the committees and capacity building, also talked about government partnering with other partners in water services provision. So which is the most important among those?
Respondent: For me the most important is to work on the technical capacity of the rural water management teams. Because on that, if we ensure that they have the right skills, right knowledge, I am sure the others will be able to follow.
Interviewer: Let me try to understand, what do you mean by getting technical skills to these people? You mean them employing a technical person, what do you mean exactly?
Respondent: They may not be able to have the financial capacity to employ a professional water engineer, but at least they can be able to get somebody with basic ideas on issues to do with water especially on plumbing works and also on rural water supplies. That means at least somebody who has gone to a college; they have somebody with a certificate maybe with a Diploma on water engineering. The reason why I’m saying that is because if we are able to get our systems right, then on thing we are guaranteed that the consumers will be able to get water and that way the issue of money will not be a big issue. But if we are able to get our infrastructure right, and we have understanding what our scenario in terms of water we have, our source, and our target communities. Then I am sure we will be able to have our schemes working. The reason I am saying that is because we have seen situations where we have the pipelines, we have tanks, but because of lack of technical capacity the committees are not able to do distribution of water to the people. And when you go and do assessment you find that it is purely lack of technical capacity and understanding of the water infrastructure and how they should work.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: In terms of the water service sustainability? 
Interviewer: Yes. And what informed the selection of those interventions?
Respondent: One, we are doing extensions of pipelines to reach the areas which are not served already and we are doing constant monitoring of course of the existing infrastructure to assess the gaps and also to give technical back up to the committees, that is mainly what we are doing. That is for existing. For the new areas we are doing capital investment on new supplies. 
Interviewer: What do you mean?
Respondent: Like drilling new boreholes and also doing pipeline extensions.
Interviewer: Anything else you are doing, and what really informed that?
Respondent: The other thing we are doing is we are really working on the technical capacity and also the financial capacity of the committees. Because previously we have had a lot of challenges because the committees will misuse the funds, or inappropriately use the funds and then when there is breakdown, they will get back to the government for support and so you find that it is really straining the government, particularly the department of water, because maintaining these schemes is quite expensive and so you find that a lot of money is getting into maintaining the existing infrastructure and to some extent this one is also denying the people who are not served an opportunity to get to be served. So we realize that if we can work on these issues of financial management or technical capacity, then we will be able to at least have operational and maintenance costs catered for by the revenue they collect from the community and now the government can focus on the production cost, the capital investment. And that one is really working because the number of phone calls that I was getting in last year I can say they have drastically gone down.
Interviewer: Calls for?
Respondent: From the committees reporting on minor breakdowns. So I'm getting very minimal calls from the committees and now you realize that at least they are able to address some of these problems.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: One, WASH Forum it was an eye opener. For me it was an eye opener because through it I was able to gain new ideas and also learn many other things concerning the rural water schemes, how to manage them and how to support them. So really it was a good platform for upgrading my understanding and my skills in this water sector.
Interviewer: How else?
Respondent: That networking, it was nice, it was good, and it was fruitful. We were able to know colleagues from other organizations, what other organizations are doing, sharing of materials and information which has been very very helpful on undertaking the water services provision.
Interviewer: Anything that you would like to add about that interview?
Respondent: Yes. The only thing that I can add is about these rural water schemes. We are increasing these infrastructure day in day out and you realize that to some extend they will get to a point and I can say they are getting to a point where the water department alone is not able to reach every point at every time and therefore, if now other partners who can support the communities with the same ideas can also come in and then with the proper legislation I think it will be a good milestone towards achieving this sustainability. So I would strongly encourage a partnership between the government, between the non-governmental organizations, between private players and also corporate into this business of water service provision.
