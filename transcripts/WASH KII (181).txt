1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: In South Omo Zone, there are three types or groups of society. The first one are farmers, the second group are pastoralist and the third group are semi-pastoralist and semi-farmers. In all the three areas there are health facilities but most of the health facilities do not have access for clean water. In other words, there is a serious problem of institutional WASH service. I can say only 20% of the health facilities have access for water supply. This water supply problem affected the health service in different areas. For instance, they cannot keep the latrines clean, they cannot provide proper maternal and childcare service, and the facilities cannot be exemplary for the community as the institutional WASH condition is very poor.
The other problem is that in pastoral areas due to the mobile nature of the community; we cannot introduce the use of latrines and minimize open defecation.
Interviewer: Are there any other problems related to water and sanitation service in the zone?
Respondent: Similar to the health facilities, the rural community has limited access for clean water. Most of them use unsafe river water and predisposed for various health problems.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You raised that almost 80% of the health facilities in the zone have no access for clean water and their WASH condition is very poor. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to this problem? 
Respondent: I think the budget requirement to develop water schemes in each health facility is beyond the capacity of the zonal administration thus we should mobilize potential partners that has institutional WASH development program. For instance, World Vision has developed borehole for Jinka zonal hospital very recently.  The government should also provide attention for institutional WASH problem in the area in terms of allocating budget and mobilizing potential partners.
Interviewer: You have also indicated that the pastoral community do not have a culture to use latrines and due to this fact, there is serious sanitation problem in these areas. What do you recommend to alleviate this problem?
Respondent: In my opinion, the first solution is that we have to settle the pastoral community in a village and develop communal latrines within the villages. Parallel to this we have to undertake awareness enhancement campaign on sanitation and hygiene. To this end, the zonal radio transmission center should play remarkable role by collaborating with the health extension program in terms of transmitting heath and sanitation education in different local languages.
Interviewer: You have mentioned that most of the rural communities have no access for clean water and forced to use unsafe river water. What do you recommend to solve this problem?
Respondent: I think the main responsible body to solve this problem is the zonal water office thus this institution in collaboration with other development partners should develop spring waters, treat river water and develop boreholes in different rural parts of the zone. Developing spring water and treating river water do not require large amount of budget unlike development of new boreholes.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: providing solution to the problems related to access for clean water for both health facilities and the communities is the most important solution. If there is sufficient access for clean water in all areas, we can reduce the occurrence of various water borne diseases. We can also reduce death of mothers and children significantly.
