
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: …and that is where I said the dialogue because that is where you get to know the issues that are affecting a project and what can be done to resolve all those issues. 

Interviewer: Which organizations would you say you have that level of dialogue with?

Interviewee: I think ADRA, the community and the development partners (the ADRA, ActionAid, Caritas).

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: To me, first is information. Information dissemination from the users to the technical department vary. That is one. Reporting there is a breakdown.

Second is skills. Those who are operating or using the facilities do not have enough skills to operate. Those are the major setbacks/issues which cause non-performance of the community water supplies.

I think also apart from the technical skills, there is also the aspect of management. Both financial and also O&M. Because you might find that a machine is working well, the communities are receiving the revenue from those projects but they are not ploughing back to maintain the machinery, so you find them just breaking down. 

Another thing is management. Some projects you find…you just ask them. As you go, you find that people are paying for that water but you do not know where…the money that is collected is not banked. So once they have a breakdown, they tend to come back to the department for assistance instead of sustaining or maintaining their project with the money they collect for revenue. 

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: Training. The main idea is training; capacity building the users. When you capacity build them, like with the training which was done by KEWI, we are trying to see some results, although very minimal. You can see now some of them have a clue of how a project can be managed. But it should be continuous, maybe after every quarter or after half a year. Continuous capacity building. Because sometimes the management committees do changes. This year we have the chairman, who was not there the previous year because there has to elections after every one year. So…changing of management…so the capacity building should be continuous.

The other thing I think is the identification of those to be trained. Sometimes I think the community plus part of the government should be able to get the right people to train because sometimes we can say we need to train people, but then there is political influence. Somebody…because somebody is my supporter, you propose them, but that person is not directly involved with the running of that project and the skills imparted on them will not have any assistance to that project. So I think that identification of those to be trained is also very key. Yes. 

Another solution is sense of ownership. Because you might find that an organization does a project. Once it is complete, it is handed over to the community. Instead of the community identifying themselves with that project, they keep on saying “this project belongs to maybe ADRA…belongs to …”; so they don’t actually take a lot of care for it because they think once something goes wrong, the people who build it will come maybe, and bail them out. So that ownership should be there.

Also, there is this issue of spare parts. Maybe the spare parts are not locally available and I think…if we can identify one stockist, for example in Mwingi we identify one stockist for spares for hand pumps so that we know we can get these spare parts…the spare parts are readily available from this stockist instead of…because that is one issue which causes the projects to fail because you… maybe a small spare part you have to go get it from Nairobi. It will take you a week or 3 days but if we can have a stockist nearby who can be distributing those spares to the community, it could be better to shorten the repair period.

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: They are all important; all these issues are very important. Management, capacity building, and spare parts, those are all key. 

Capacity building for management is the most important.

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: The water shortage would be minimized…drastically minimized. Economically, Kitui would be on a higher…because most of the time is spend on fetching water because of walking long distances and taking longer at the water sources, but if we can have those…you know, what makes them walk long distances is because the facilities near them are broken down and have not been repaired. So, if it can be repaired soonest possible, those man-hours could be saved for economic gains. So I think economic-wise, health-wise, it will change.
