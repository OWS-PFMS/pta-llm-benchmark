1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 

Respondent: Lack of community ownership since prior to construction the involvement of development agents is more dominant compared to the community and the government. 
 
Interviewer: Why do you think is that?
Respondent: This is because community members are not consulted about their own resources being built on their locality, starting from the assessment phase. 
The second problem is quality of materials to be used during construction of water schemes, which is causing a repeated break down and interruption of water supply. This could be attributed to poor feasibility study by the concerned stakeholders. Further, when feasibility study is conducted, there is a gap in selection of proper water source location that can sustain throughout the year. 
In addition, there is also awareness gap. 
Interviewer: Is it from the community side?
Respondent: Yes, it is from the community. Beneficiaries are expecting everything to come from the government, including maintenance of minor parts. This is due to lack of awareness and sense of ownership.  
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
     Interviewer: what do you think should be done to address these issues?

Respondent: To solve the challenge related to creating community ownership and awareness, close discussions and transparency is crucial with stakeholders and community members. Besides, after the scheme is completed, training WaSHCo members is important.
Interviewer: You have mentioned issues related to assessment and scheme quality. What should be done to solve that?
Respondent: Assessments should be properly conducted. This may include studies related to the current market value of inputs and equipment on one hand and assessments specific to water site. Quality materials should be selected for the civil work, mechanical work, and spare parts. There should be standards and strong quality assurance system with in the government and other development partners.


Quality should be ensured in the civil work and during procurement and purchase of spare parts, pumps, and pipes. Material quality and installation works should be carefully assessed.
Interviewer: Another problem you stated is related to selection of inappropriate water source. This is a fundamental problem I think. How do you think can this issue be solved?
In every assessment, we carry out it is imperative to use technology. Besides, since the local community has knowledge about nature of their neighborhood, stakeholders need to collect information from them before operation; for example, it is common to see that some water sources may get dry during summer, it is important during assessment that our selection should follow potentially permanent water sources. Before implementation, it is vital to utilize the latest available technologies, and if necessary, hiring consultants would be crucial. If there are projects whose assessment is completed but not implemented due to inadequate funding, updating the assessment after consulting the community and incorporating their inputs before implementation is beneficial. This is mainly due to the fact that there might be changes in the location where the water scheme is planned. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

Interviewer: You have mentioned a number of fundamental problems. My final question is of the solutions you listed, which is the most important one?  What would happen if the solutions materialize? 

Respondent: I think creating awareness is the most important one. 
Interviewer: Do you mean community awareness?

Respondent: Yes, community awareness and creating sense of ownership among local community is important from the very beginning of the assessment stage. If community members are engaged in the decision-making process of each phases of the project, they will develop sense of ownership as far as the sustainability is concerned. Issues related to communication with stakeholders and technology is also important. This will address the sustainability issue that has been prominent in many water projects in this area.
