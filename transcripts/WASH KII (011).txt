Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? 
Respondent: Kitui is water scarce and when you talk of enhancing water source development to improve coverage, the technology used will have to be expensive because in most of the areas what is available is possible is the exploitation of ground water and that means drilling boreholes. The cost of drilling and running boreholes is expensive. We don’t have other options. Kitui is semi-arid and the water sources are rare so we are driven to take up options which are expensive as a result of having no other options. We don’t drill boreholes because the community wants them, but because it is the only option and it is expensive. You can’t compare Kitui with Meru or Embu counties which have many surface water sources and all you need is pipes and once connected, the water flows by gravity. This is not as costly as drilling and running boreholes. Here you have to get it from the underground, get to the surface then to a storage tank. The costs make the difference and this is a challenge to achieving sustainability. You find that sustainability might be attained but it is costly. Secondly, the other issue is governance which is a key point. Governance means prudent use of money and effective management currently we don’t have any remedy which can be taken if you misuse funds from water sales. The Water Act is silent on the issue. Misuse of funds has not been cited as a criminal offense. 
What happens is that people misuse resources from water revenue with impunity and the only thing you can do is to dissolve the committee. You bring in others who might be more corrupt than the previous ones and the cycle continues. I think that is going to be curbed. We are developing Water Policy for Kitui County, a Bill and regulations and for some of these issues like misuse of funds I think we will be able to prosecute people and audit the water facilities also. 
During our experience here in Kitui, we have faced other challenges like here in Kitui East you find that the water is very saline; the salinity levels are very high and the cost of desalinization is high. Also, according to a sketch of ground water potential, you find that in some areas it is not worthy investing in a borehole because the yield is less 1m3/hr. You know when you drill a borehole, it is risky. You can drill a borehole and the yield is zero. We can say we don’t have a guideline where boreholes are viable and where they are not. For now, we take chances when drilling.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: One solution I would recommend is that we change the management of the rural schemes so that we inject skills and then good governance. We see the challenge of the cost of running facilities on diesel, and we need to roll out a program of renewable energy. We need to fast track the Water Policy, Bill and regulations so that we bring sanity to the water sector because the process was initiated as a result of these challenges observed in the sector. I think we are trying to address them. The moment we will have it approved by the county assembly and ascended to by the governor, then I think it will be work done well.
We can also think along the lines of harvesting water from the rivers instead of drilling boreholes. We can install infiltration galleries in river beds and these will be more useful than boreholes and we are sure that they will not be saline and their yield is very high. The one for Zombe Thua has a yield of around 40m3 /hr. In addition, we need to have a rigorous exercise of restoring our seasonal rivers. They are equally important. We can restore our rivers and then we have a series of sand dams so that they can act as sources of water as we look for alternatives to boreholes. We need a long-term program to restore our rivers.
It is also important to change the mode of investment because of salinity of ground water and focus on other sources other than boreholes. The saline ones are for animals but for the people, they walk up to 40km one way hence in total a return journey of 80km in search of water. 
In relation to reducing the risk of hitting dry boreholes, a solution could be ground water potential mapping though it is very expensive. It is a rigorous exercise. It might be lengthy and expensive. We hope one day we shall have a ground water potential map for the county.

Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? 
Respondent: The most critical one is governance. When we talk of governance, we need to look at how we manage our water supplies. Initially there used to be misuse of revenue from water, there were no consequences. The governance documents being developed address these matters so that we have remedies such that somebody can be prosecuted for this. On technical issues, I don’t know what is happening because of later I think some careers are becoming extinct with time, for instance plant mechanics. I often ask myself why is there only one in Kitui and he is already retired. So, I think that has to be looked into; the area of training. If these courses were there, they should be re-introduced in institutions that offer training on water in the country so that we have these people. They are very important. The other one, about renewable energy. As we said, Kitui is a water scarce county so you find that most of the investments are done for ground water and the cost of running a borehole is relatively high. So, when you introduce renewable energy you cut the cost by almost more than 50% because most of the cost is the cost of power and then we can use those funds somewhere else. It will improve sustainability.
Interviewer: Just to clarify, we are asking for the most important out of all the solutions that you are recommending.
Respondent: Governance is most important.
Interviewer: Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: If we can address governance, all the other things will fall into place automatically. As officers we will be able to review the management committees and identify the ones that are working, then find technicians who are competent in servicing generators, a big portion of the challenges in the rural schemes will be sorted. That is all. If you bring technical quality, all those things will come automatically.  
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: One of our partners in the sub-county has provided facilitation support and we have been able to have meetings with a number of committees; water committees for the major projects and the technical committees at the sub county water office. We have been analyzing the water schemes and have identified common challenges at the scheme level. Most of the committees don’t know how to service the infrastructure they have from the generators for boreholes, the lines up to the kiosks. So, what I did, because I have staff who have experience, I have pipe fitters, and water supply staff so I facilitated them and they have gone to more than 60% of the projects. So, we have been auditing them somehow. I go to every committee so that when I sit down with them, it is not the committee talking about the problems, it is my staff and with that we have seen success. We have also realized that to address some challenges we don’t need resources. It is just negligence or lack of knowledge. I can say that I have seen the need to understand the rural water schemes and also the need to procure for them technical services and experts like plant mechanics, electricians and pipe fitters. There should no longer be instances of people who do not have the technical expertise to repair facilities being the ones providing repair services.
Interviewer: What has informed these interventions you have talked about?
Respondent: When we started in May, there were very many boreholes that had been reported to be broken down. There was an outcry from the community and on top of it all, it has not rained in this area since May last year. If a water point is not working the best thing you can do is to revive it.
