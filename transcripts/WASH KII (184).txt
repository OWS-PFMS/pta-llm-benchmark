1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: Our zone consist of agrarian and pastoral areas. Most of the pastoral areas have no access for clean water. Particularly Hamer and Selmago Woredas have a serious problem of clean water supply. 
Interviewer: What is the reason for limited access for clean water in these areas?
Respondent: The main reason is that the areas are very arid and it requires huge capital budget to develop boreholes in the areas. The budget requirement is beyond the capacity of the woreda and zonal government.
There is also water supply shortage in the Zonal Town (Jinka) due to rapid growth of the population. To this end, the town municipality is trying to expand the water supply by developing additional water schemes in the town. However, this expansion project is delayed for long time due to capital budget shortage. Because of this, currently the town water utility office forced to supply water in shift (rationing) bases. That is the community access water every other week. 
Interviewer: Are there any other problems related to sanitation service?
Respondent: There are sanitation related problems particularly in rural areas of the zone. For instance, there is high prevalence of water borne diseases in different parts of the zone. Open defecation is very common practice as the pastoral community are not aware of the health benefits of latrines. 
As our office mandate mainly focus on administrative affairs of the zone, we have no detailed understanding of the problems related to sanitation service in our area. It is better for you to discuss with the zonal health office.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned that there is a serious water supply shortage in pastoral areas of the zone. In these areas, it is very difficult to get water due to arid climatic condition of the areas. The Zonal administration do not have capital budget to develop water schemes in these areas. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: Non-governmental organization should cooperate with the zonal government in terms of providing financial and technical support. The regional government should also allocate sufficient budget for the zone.
On the other hand, to develop water schemes that can serve the community for long period there should be in-depth study to identify areas that have potential water resource. Water projects should not be implemented without prior investigation of the water resource potential of the areas.
Interviewer: You have also indicated that there is a water supply shortage in Jinka town as well. The existing water supply do not meet the needs of the community. What do you suggest to alleviate this problem?
Respondent: To alleviate the water supply shortage of Jinka town, we have already implementing water service expansion projects but these projects are delayed due to budget constraint. Therefore, we have to mobilize funds for NGOs and the regional government to finalize the projects within short period. If these projects can be completed and become operational within short period, I think the supply shortage will be solved at least partly.   
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The water supply problems both in pastoral areas and in Jinka town are equally important and the government should provide equal attention in alleviating the problems in both areas. If we can solve this problem, we can achieve political stability. Currently the government is facing strong opposition from the community due water supply shortage. Thus, alleviating the water supply shortage will result in smooth relation between the community and the government.
