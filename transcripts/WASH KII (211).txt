
 [Limited water service and infrastructure coverage] We have been working in Mali woreda for five years especially in the Kolla areas without enough infrastructures. In this area there is a small service infrastructure coverage which amounts to less than 50%. But there are some recent improvements. We delivered humanitarian relief supports for the last three months in Bena Tsemay woreda. We worked in very remote areas which were almost without infrastructures. There were very few infrastructures and limited resources, sustainability, however, is another issue. This is because we can’t talk about water sustainability in areas where there is limited water resource and infrastructure. If you ask me whether water is available or not (for instance, reservoir, bore hole and shallow wells), the answer is yes there is. But it doesn’t meet the need of the community and its availability is also skewed to areas that are closer to towns. [Lack of WASHcos commitment and community participation] in areas where we work now (in Deso woreda) there is only one borehole which was constructed and given to the people by AMREF. But the lack of community participation and commitment and follow up by WASHcos caused such schemes to be unsustainable. [Lack of skilled man power] The other problem is lack of skilled manpower in the kebelles who are able to maintain the schemes and usually skilled people are called from Woredas. There may be only one person in the entire zone who will be working on maintenance everywhere in that zone. [Problems related to logistics], after the governmental or non-governmental institutions transfer the infrastructure for the people, there is no post-project management which is very pertinent in these types of activities. [Knowledge gap about WASH] within the community is the other problem. Some people would rather use water from natural ponds/ rivers. In areas like Mali people make and use their own water wells than paying 0.5 cents or 1 Birr to use the water from protected pipes. They ask why we should buy the water when we could make our own well or use the natural one. For instance, in Doyso water scheme the pump needs generator and some money is paid for the service so that fuel for the generator is bought with the collected revenue. However, due to the fuel’s unavailability in the area it is more expensive and people should pay one Birr for the water service. 5:00 For pastoralist or agro pastoralist areas like this, the community holds their money in items or assets instead of cash. Thus, one birr is expensive for them and they prefer using natural running water instead of using paid water. Another problem is lack of [accessories (spare parts)]. In order to find these spare parts such us gate valves, people should walk for 60 KMs to Jinka town just to buy a spare part. The best option to access spare parts is riding a motor bike, instead of walking on foot, which costs from 600 to 700 Birr for double trip. This is becoming hard to manage and cover the cost from the money collected by the people from available water schemes. 

What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

With regard to shortage of accessories and logistics, we installed a solar pump in Hembo Kebelle and when we handed it over to the community, we also donated some extra spare parts. There were also some people whom we trained for a short term to fix small technical problems. Additionally, small stocks of accessories can significantly contribute to the sustainability of these infrastructures. There is no store but stores are needed for keeping spare parts and accessories. Since there is no enough supply around here, it would be easier to purchase the accessories once from far places and store them. If possible make motorbikes available because there is no road suitable for other vehicles. Moreover, most people can ride them and it would make conditions better. We should apply technologies that use solar energy or new types of other technologies rather than building water schemes with fuel generators. Cistern system-is a system of collecting underground water and building a reservoir. It has also a pumping and cleaning process and can be used for at least three and up to six months. 

Regarding limited water coverage and infrastructure shortage, my recommendation is building water schemes where there is available water resource. Encouraging roof-catchment schemes could be alternative solution for this problem. In this zone, there are a lot of houses with tin roofs but people don’t collect water when it rains. During rainy seasons, there will be excess water that usually floods out to the resident’s farms. Especially, for lowland areas (kolla) there are schools and it is possible to construct water reserving wells in these schools and treat the water with cleaning chemicals to protect them from water born diseases. 

The other recommendation is about skilled man power issue, for example generators need maintenance and a lot of skilled individuals are needed on the spot. Facilitating that is one option and the other option is using automatic solar pumps which operate whenever there is solar power. If there is an overflow, then people can use it for their livestock and irrigation. Hiring individuals who are skilled and who have local language skills and know the culture of the community very well. The payment for these individuals could be taken from the money that the residents of the area contribute or collect.

My recommendation to the limited commitment of community and WASHcos is the need to provide them trainings (we have already started this) about the current government’s policy regarding WASH community management and structure. 10:00 In addition, I recommend creating of rural water and sanitation associations. We have recently started registering the people, pictures were collected for their IDs and this is a good initiative. For example, the community themselves collected from 27,000 Birr up to now. The essential thing in water schemes is engaging the community in the process. In our case, the community dug about 3.85 KM line during drought times, which was the worst scenario because they were being supported by food aid at the same time. We only offered only refreshments for them. In such cases, they wouldn’t allow for these infrastructures to be damaged because they feel responsible. 

We had also engaged the community in building the fences for the infrastructures as it is very important to engage them in small activities. Therefore, I highly suggest the engagement of the community in these types of activities and getting their feedbacks through discussions. It is also important for them to contribute some amount of money, for instance, in the solar project the community decided to collect 10 Birr per month from the residents. From the stalk holders side, it is very important not to stop follow ups after they handed their work over to the local governments. The society needs follow ups and continuous awareness creation from time to time. 

I also think that training health extension workers, teachers, women and elders about the usage of ponds and about water-borne diseases is essential. It is also important that these trainings need to be continuous rather than one time shot. If possible, making a women’s committee in line with the government’s 1:5 or 1:10 structural learning platform to share experiences and exchange ideas by meeting up to once a month with the people who are outside their community is very crucial. This will give them ideas about how other people use water and harvest. Even we are lagging behind in the practice of hand washing.

In order to increase awareness, there is a model- integrated functional adult literacy- which integrates water, agriculture and education. It supports health extension workers and includes representative women from the kebeles (in many areas from South Omo zone, women do most of the house work that is related with water and sanitation). Educating women is also educating children. Organizing events at every school level such as hand-washing days in schools and educating kids in such events is important. Also, centering the community leaders especially in Goda Wash (in Mali area) using such events and giving trainings and making discussions about the biggest needs of the community is very helpful. 

There is a story about what happened in Mali. The local people fed a lot of fresh honey to visitors (tourists) purposefully and made them thirsty, and as the visitors ask for water they told them that they didn’t have any water. Visitors understand the problem the moment they become thirsty and I think these people responded to the community water problem in some form of aid later. I think the community knows what they need and if people are assigned in every post to give trainings through adopting the way of life and living with the indigenous people, it would be very helpful.

Engaging the community and creating awareness are very essential. In your opinion, how effective is coordination for sustainable water service? 

I think need is high for instance, in our geography there is only one well getting dug. Once we had a crisis with money shortage because we couldn’t get exchange due to the nature of the economy, we went to the government and the government was also tight on exchange but eventually they helped us out. There is poor coordination and what made this lack of coordination is that when a project is needed to get implemented we act based on the emergency needs and our individual organizational missions.  There is low water infrastructure development and coverage in South Omo, I think the water coverage in the zone is less than 40% and we were applying water tracking in Mali, NaTsemay and Lemokentu towns. There was also water containing trucks in the town where people get water by their own small containers. This shows even the towns lack water and rural areas are even worse. There is high need for water in Jinka town but I think there is some improvement now. This is because there is a huge project to be implemented in Jinka in collaboration with IMF. It is 226 million Birr fund project and the water scheme is treated like the ones in AA. This is in fact one example for how coordination with other stalk holders alleviates the problem of water. 

What should be improved? In our area we haven’t even started the benchmarks for coordination; there is no invitation from other NGOs. The first thing should have been coming to the table for discussion, there is a big gap to led and take initiatives both from the line offices and NGOs side.  We can’t even use the inputs available because everything is at an infant stage. Moreover, there is lack of budget and whenever we want to do something we usually go to zone or woreda to collect information about what kind of relief work is needed but still there is lack of commitment among these bodies. They don’t even have something that is studied about the needs in the area. Therefore, these concerned bodies need to collect data and study about what kind of relief work is needed in their areas.
