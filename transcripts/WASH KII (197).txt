1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda? 

As it known, the zone water coverage, particularly, in rural areas is around 39 %. Nevertheless lots of activities have been made to increase water coverage; we couldn’t go beyond the current limit and satisfy the demand s of the community. With the involvement of both the government and other NGOs, we couldn’t address the sustainability issues. The first and major challenge is [lack of water resources] coupled with this is the limited government budget. We couldn’t cope up with the increasing demand of the water service as water scheme construction (developing the existing ones and digging deep shallow walls) needs long range plan.  
The second challenge is the [Limited contributions of the community] in assisting the development of small and large water scheme development programs. There is also a challenge related to basic infrastructure development plans, like electric and telecommunication poles. In this regard, we have seen gaps to bring about integrated infrastructure development that demands unreserved commitment in the years ahead. 
In some areas, we may also lack necessary equipment and spare parts after we make sure that availability of water has been identified. This may be related to lack of attention and detail investigation in the pre implementation stages. We have identified gaps in keeping the already excavated water schemes in some areas, these areas are drying and it is of course a particularly problem in some arid areas tied up with the desertification of the nature. As you know, in arid areas, there is basic problem to get the water access itself, to dig deep walls and to pump them to other distant areas.  Transporting water from the sources by itself is a challenge related to the resource issues I raised earlier. This also requires resourcing the water schemes through mobilization of all stalk holders (governmental sectors and NGOs). 

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Now, the main thing is helping the community access to pure drinking water which is the basic and best alternative part of our water service programs.  The first thing is setting a long term plan to investigate and locate large water resources. This should be based on research. There are big water projects being handled both around woredas and towns with the fund signed with World Bank. This is one of the first steps, among others, intended to solve the current water coverage problems and then to provide pure drinking water to the community through finding and working together with big international and national ‘chains’. The other option is focusing on rivers, as it has been started particularly in some woredas in the zone, and treating the water with appropriate chemicals being together with NGOs and our line offices, is the second alternative solution to cope up with the limiter water resource. This is among our major water plans which state ‘using the available water in modern approaches and applying the appropriate technologies to increase the current level of water coverage in the zone.’ 
We are also working with WASH and other water service stalk holders on digging immense water schemes, roof catchment activities, particularly around desert areas, and collecting and reserving all water sources as much we need. When we do this, we are also benefiting the agriculture sector, supplying water not only for people but for animals as well. In humid areas, we have relatively better water sources and our focus is to collect all the water we have there on big reservoirs so that we can use it as we need, both for our livestock and the community. We have also small water streams that are planned to be developed from the government side and then NGOs will follow the track line of the government. 
The other main solution is mobilizing the community though encouraging them participate in the water scheme management and other development processes. Particularly, mobilizing them in the maintenance of the available water infrastructures through providing continuous training is very important.  This is will be practically possible through the established WASHcos by helping them transform to formal water user associations which are more structured and directly involved to protect and manage water sources. The strategy from the government is to give the responsibility to these associations to collect the money from the services and recycle it to the maintenance of the damaged ones. When we develop the capacity of theses associations, we are building the capacity of water institutes indirectly. Though little developments have been made before, we are working hard to strengthen the available water structures and associations in the coming years so that we can achieve our WASH development goals from the GTP 2 plan (second growth and transformation plan). We should also develop and provide proposals for other stalk holders involved in WASH sector so that we can use all water sources to the optimum level. 
What could be done to increase community awareness? To increase awareness, we should tell teach the fact that water is life and we can’t service otherwise. Practically, we have learned a lot last time when unexpected climate change and drought affects the community, one of the basic problems was getting water. During that time, both government and NGOs have showed a fully-fledged support to provide water from all possible sources, including transporting it from remote areas.   From this point of view, we should provide awareness to the community to take care of it, though protecting the water sources, forming and working with WASHcos and other similar associations. The second thing, people should participate in accessing all water sources because working without popular participation will not be successful. We should also provide information on specific techniques of water management, to use water wisely, not to leave water points untapped and sanitation works. For this to happen, the WASHcos should be capacitated through trainings in one side and be supplied with adequate materials/supplies on the other hand. 
This is because we should find ways that this committee is to be an institute independently by transforming itself to formal water user associations and raising the required fund from the association itself.
