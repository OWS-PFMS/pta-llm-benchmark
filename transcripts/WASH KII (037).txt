Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? 
Respondent: Some of the main challenges we are facing in sustainability of our water supplies are governance, limited water sources, and poor implementation in some cases. Regarding the limited water resources, you know Kitui being an arid and semi-arid region, most of our water sources production goes down with time. You find that this becomes a challenge during the drought period. By poor implementation, I am talking about failure to adhere to project designs which results to poor implementation. Another factor is lack of good framework in monitoring and evaluation systems and another one is capacity of those who are managing the systems especially rural and Water Service Providers (WSPs) still have capacity gaps and another challenge is low funding which results to not implementing the projects to the required standards.
We can say in terms of governance, when you look into how the rural and urban water supply should be managed, within the water supply in the urban centers mainly the major towns the management of the water supplies is by the WSPs, the companies. In terms of governance of the companies, our companies being under public trust, are still not adhering to the governance policies within the companies. You find that if the companies are not autonomous per se because it is entrusted through the public, then there are issues like, if it is about the management boards, they do not function independently. There is still some external management which results to them being lenient in implementation of some issues. When it comes to employment, we still have challenges in the top management of the companies because management is also influenced politically sometimes. Due to unemployment, the employment of people with high skills and qualifications then the payment the remunerations of the staff, that has a linkage in demoralization of the staff so in this case you find that governance in general under WSPs has various challenges. 
When you come to the rural water supplies you find that the water users associations or the management committees, within the rural we have not been able to come up with the best management models but now we are working on the both County Water Policy and the County Bill and we are focusing to address management of rural water schemes which finally we hope with the different proposed management models the governance might improve because it is still an issue of management by committees. Even if they are given capacity, still the rate of turnover by communities or resource functions maybe lack of accountability, transparency most of them you find that there is a lot of turnover in the top management, this results to misappropriation, we can say so although some of the complains are not justifiable, but you find that after training a management committee this year, next year they are replaced by another committee, so it becomes a continuous problem. Sometimes if you don’t get somebody who feels attached to the scheme, or if the scheme is not generating adequate funds, people also feel that…the public feels that those who are managing should give free services. So, when they start getting some allowances, it also becomes a challenge. In this case where we have people or the community being very strict in monitoring the accountability and where they don’t want to allow either payment of allowances in a transparent manner is where we have people leaving and saying they cannot waste their time in all this. That’s why we are thinking maybe the proposed management model will address these challenges of governance of rural water supplies. 
Interviewer: You have talked of the community not being able to monitor the schemes and how they are managed for accountability purposes. How do you think this can be addressed? Do they know their rights, do they know the kind of services they should be getting from the schemes?
Respondent: The communities know. Sometimes I can say the failures come from the principal because as a county, we should have very good structured monitoring system and evaluation. Although we have a structure which we have from the county level, we have given the mandate to the sub county offices and even linked up with the county administration and coordination office in terms of the administrators but still the perception of the public is that the officers should be facilitated externally.  So, unless now we have a system whereby the officers can also be facilitated by the revenue generated within the system so that they can come up with their schedule and are aware that when they go to monitor and evaluate schemes their transport and probably allowance will be catered for by the proceeds from the facility it is only then when we will be able to have frequent and consistent provision of those services. This is something we need to capture clearly in the Policy because what we have seen is that when we wait for the officers to be facilitated by the principle to address these small rural schemes it has become a challenge and what we should think about is to link the Water Act 2002 and Water Act 2016 and draw up some of the strengths which were there. The Water Act 2002 allowed payment of the lease fee and the intention of the lease fee was to meet the cost of supervision, allowances, studies where you can improve specific facilities although it is not there within the new Act but maybe if it can be reconsidered it can strengthen the arm of monitoring and evaluation of the county. Also within the rural can we think of bringing in a certain levy which can be used specifically for schemes which register with small WSPs can be subscribing to this kind of a system like the proposed Kitui County Trust Fund, if it can bring out that element it can easily say that facilitation of monitoring and evaluation whereby rural schemes are subscribing even if it is an annual fee can be used to monitor, supervise and evaluate them and this can improve their performance because currently we are  depending on when the government facilitates which is not continuous so you find that these communities are left for a long time without being supervised and monitored so we only address issues when problems or breakdowns arise.
Interviewer: How about the issue of poor implementation?
Respondent: The issue of implementation we can say sometimes arises from the designs…they could be good and because the water sector has so many partners we have some who are not able to bring their design documents to be approved by the County due to the scarcity we have, sometimes they are allowed to continue so finally you find that whatever kind of designs they have, they may not be appropriate within the specific areas they are implementing so when the designs are not appropriate it means still the implementation will be jeopardized in the process. Secondly, if supervision is not strictly adhered to and we don’t follow up the project contract implementation standards, it’s like we have not been keen to follow specific standards, we have international standards, Kenyan standards and local standards which are not documented so you find most of the contractors who are also local they end up implementing local standards where strict supervision is not carried out. So that is how poor implementation comes up.
Interviewer: So you also talked about the capacity of the people who manage the schemes where you broke that into, the rural and the urban WSPs, how is that a challenge?
Respondent: Low salaries do not attract professionals to work for WSPs, so finally they end up recruiting people with no expertise just to be trained on the job. When it comes to the rural, we normally engage the community who are just capacity built and due to the issues of running the schemes they are not able to generate funds which can employ somebody who might have been trained in that field. So you find that probably this is a major challenge because sometimes to be able to meet cost for operation and maintenance for both rural and urban, what is required is adjusted tariffs but Kitui being an arid and semi-arid area where poverty is also a major issue when the tariffs go up it means that the community will revert back to the traditional sources so striking the balance in between there has been a complicated issue. So, the issue to consider is whether the government can think of a subsidy in all this if we want to achieve professionalism in the management of these systems.
The other element which might be very critical and we might not have mentioned is the destruction of our environment because this has to do with depletion of the sources and therefore, we have to bring strongly issues of catchment protection and protection of our rivers by defining the riparian land. That is key if we want to improve the water sources.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: For the solutions, I would say the County government to come up with probably guidelines which we are currently doing because we are developing our water policy and bill which we want to align with the national government guidelines. The other solution is to come up with a very strong monitoring and evaluation system because that is a major challenge. We can come up with so many guidelines, policies, good plans but if they are not subjected to monitoring and evaluation and which is a weak area within the systems, it will still remain a challenge. Then the other is continuous capacity building of the County staff, WSPs and even the community management committees, and this should go together with continuous sensitization. The other solution is incorporating a business model. We bring in the private sector within the management of water systems. The other thing is we think of renewable energy as a way of reducing operation and maintenance costs and more so we can focus on the solar powered systems. Due to an increasing population we need to think of developing major water sources. Despite the fact that we say we are within an arid and semi-arid region, but still if we analyze the amount of rainfall we receive in a season, if we can be harvesting the runoff with major harvesting structures this can be a solution.
Interviewer: How about the issue of funding and the one on design standards?
Respondent: In regard to the designs, all those undertaking water projects, it is appropriate for the designs to be approved by the directorate of water. When it comes to supervision, then let’s start adhering to the international standards. Although it may be challenging to our contractors, but we should now be thinking of adhering to the standard contract management principles.  
For low funding, the national government can probably consider increasing the allocation to the devolved governments because this is where the major services are required. We should also develop more proposals which we can share with our donors because if you analyze, we are still a developing nation and most people living in arid and semi-arid regions are subjected to more problems than those within the potential areas. 
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The most important is monitoring and evaluation because if we do projects and do not monitor, we will just be going around a revolving circle. Monitoring has so many things because you have to map out all your facilities then you have to subject them to the key performance indicators and therefore you have to be producing monthly reports, evaluating and recommending solutions to the same. Actually this will lead to overall sustainability because what we believe is that, because when we are talking of sustainability we are talking about facilities which are generating revenue and we want to know at the end if there is need for a subsidy to a facility if the same generated funds cannot address the challenge. In most cases we say, financial management especially within the rural sector has been a major challenge. As it is, within the WSPs two issues arise. We have the commercial loses and the physical loses and in analysis, commercial loses are higher than the physical loses because sometimes the physical loses can be addressed during implementation if you adhere to the contract implementation standards. Commercial losses which are internally generated can easily be addressed through a monitoring system.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: The major interventions we are doing to enhance sustainability are focusing on hybridization, that is the use of solar systems combined with other energy source systems for pumping of water from boreholes. Another one is that we are focusing on water harvesting in a holistic manner whereby from the homestead we sensitize communities to have water tanks for roof catchment harvesting, and we are also sensitizing them on surface runoff harvesting. That covers micro water harvesting. We are also exploring the possibility of having major water harvesting structures. That is something we are focusing on.
This kind of thinking has been brought about by the virtue of the amount of rainfall received in this region versus the storage we have currently. So, you find that we lose a lot when the rains come compared to what we store and this becomes a challenge during the drought seasons. 
We are also thinking of bulk storage within some sources and we are also thinking of pipeline extensions where after we have major sources we now do pipeline extensions to enable management of a system rather than have so many small facilities where we have a location can be served by one supply source. This can be more appropriate even to enhance sustainability. When we talk of hybridization, we link it to bulk storage and this is where we say, due to fluctuations in the way the solar systems operate, if we can have major storage they can enhance storage even when the weather is not favorable for pumping with solar. We can also say bulk storage enhances mitigation when there is a breakdown. So, within a period of 2 to 3 days before any problem is fixed people can still have water.
We are also doing borehole drilling and pipeline extensions. Pipeline extensions are done in areas where we have reliable sources. We think about whether the sources can be expanded to cover a larger area and the main focus is to bring water close to the communities either through a kiosk which the community can access within half a kilometer or we still have a target of enhancing individual connections or water supplied at the homesteads. That is the importance of the pipeline extensions.
In general, in terms of selecting what interventions to carry out, we can say that we consider the appropriate technologies within the arid and semi-arid regions then there is also mapping out of the potential within the region then probably now what we share in the Forums in terms of the key indicators of service provision…if it is accessibility, coverage, functionality and reliability. Those are the key issues which bring us to that. We also do public participation so that when they give their proposals, we can now see if it is applicable within that specific region.
Interviewer: You have talked about mapping out of potential, have you done this? Is it for ground or surface water? How is that done?
Respondent: Both the ground water and surface water because now when you talk about surface water we are geared towards harvesting. We have already mapped out our major streams and rivers where we want to put up the sand dams. Out of the experience of the boreholes we have been doing, we know the potential of ground water in specific areas, but we have not been able to specifically map out what we call the aquifer zones.
