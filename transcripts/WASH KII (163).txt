1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation/Water service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability? 
In our region WASH program was established five years ago. It has now ended its first phase and we are on the eve to start the second phase. When we evaluate the first phase regarding its achievements, it is almost poor. We didn’t achieve as it was planned. 
Thus, in our region one of the biggest challenges was the procurement process. The procurement was based on the existing guidelines of the World Bank which has resulted in many ups and downs regarding the progress of the projects. Afar regional water bureau is one of the offices that receive huge amount of budget in the region. Different projects have been conducted by the water bureau so far including developing small and very large boreholes and establishing the civil work. 
In each of the water projects, procurement teams are formed from the regional water bureau and the federal water, irrigation and electricity bureau. This has its own side effects as it takes a very long process to approve the procurement process. Had this mandate been operated by the region, projects would have completed on time. In this process, construction bidders who have little/no idea about Afar Region, its temperature, the hard rocks  and the specific area of the projects are invited to participate in the bidding. And they are the once that propose the lowest finance for the project and commonly the winners. The projects will then found to be failed from the very beginning which needs to broadcast another bid and waiting for new bidders to submit their proposals. 
We have been repeating this process for the last five years. As to me, federal ministry of water should give a special support and delegate to Afar Region as it has a number of special cases compared to other regions in Ethiopia. In this regard, due to our continuous appeal, little improvement has been made since last year. This change includes giving mandate for the regional offices to procure and approve bidding processes. 
 Until recently, a regional benefit from WASH Program is very low. The beneficiaries from the program are not more than 2000 or 3000 people, but the target was much more than this number. 
In the region, the WASH program is mainly operated by a synergy of three major sectors: education, health and water. In principle, they have to work in a close coordination regarding budget, plan, and other resources. Education sector creates awareness on the benefits clean water, washing hands in schools. As I visited the work of health and education sectors in person, they have built many toilets in different schools and institutions but the problem is that these toilets are not supplied with water service. This has resulted in the outbreak of communicable diseases in different woredas. All of these problems are related to the poor performance of the regional water bureau. 
Interviewer: Why do you think that water bureau is failing to execute its duty?
High turnover of the staff, particularly the top level managers is the first major problem.  Due to the increasing political instability in the region, a staff can stay for about six months, on average.  A new staff should take some time to learn what has been done in the sector and move to decision. This has its own effect on project life time and efficiency.  
The other problem is the fact that regional state has given lesser attention to WASH program. Because of the current volatile political issues, the regional state has become busy in other security and administrative problems. Water is the biggest priority for Afar region, but the sector lacks adequate attention both from the region and the federal actors.
Lack of adequate human resource is the other problem particularly serious in the regional bureau of finance and economic development. This problem has resulted in the inefficiency of different activities in the region, including a delay in financial report. Every time, we are kindly requesting woreda finance offices for their timely financial report but there has always been delay to get the financial report from the thirteen WASH program woredas. As it is well known by the federal government, Afar region is always at the back to submit compiled financial report. One of the contributing factors for is the long distance between WASH woredas and absence of internet and computers to send the reports timely. 




2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
The first thing I suggest is revising the process of the procurement so that it can take a very short time. This includes providing mandates to the regional and district level water offices with a minimum supervision from the federal bureau. The regional water bureau should also take the designing and follow up tasks as it has a better knowhow of the different woredas in the region. 
Interviewer: Do other regions follow up the procurement process by themselves?
Yes, in the four major regions, Amhara, Tigray, Oromia and SNNPR, the regional water bureaus have the mandate to follow up the procurement process by themselves. The same process has to work out in this region. Many of the problems that we are facing today are results of the bureaucratic processes of procurement
Delegates from the World Bank group do come to our regions sometimes but they couldn’t solve the problem of the procurement even if they are the major sources of the fund. It requires continuous appeal to the regional state and the ministry of water so that they can give attention to the problems we are facing. 
Interviewer: what should be done to solve the delay of financial reports from the woreda?
We have requested ministry of finance and economic cooperation (MoFEC) for additional budget to hire technical experts at the woreda levels so that they can work out and send reports on time. MoFEC didn’t accept our request. We know that other major regions have adequate experts and there has not been a delay in financial reports. 
Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
As I have mentioned it several times, the major challenge that should be given priority is problems related to procurement. If we have a simple and hands on procurement process, we can make that the three major sectors act on the process. This will improve the level of coordination they have now, focus on long and short term regional targets and bring changes sooner.
