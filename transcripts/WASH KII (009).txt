Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? 
Respondent: One is limited resources although it is a bit broad, resources like financial allocation. You know, the government has various competing departments which they need to fund, and then water is key in this county, it is a very big challenge. So, if enough resources are not allocated, we can’t achieve much. Since this area is rocky, most of the materials we use for the pipelines are very expensive so if you allocate just a small amount of money, you are going to achieve very minimal in relation to water access. Also, this area, in this county the water sources are very few because we can’t say it has many sustainable underground water sources. You can only rely on a few boreholes and if you look at the yield, it is not of good volume that can be extended to other areas. If you look at the only river that we are relying on, you have seen on the television the pollution that is happening. It requires thorough treatment thus putting the County in a very awkward situation in terms of water access. 
Poverty is also another challenge. It would be ideal in some areas where we have worked in, for sustainability of the project, we normally involve the community and tell them that we are doing the pipeline but that they must contribute so that they feel attached to the project. If you look at the poverty index of this area, what they might contribute might not be much. You know, if it is a construction of a building you can say they can bring hardcore because it is available but now for water projects, it becomes a challenge for them because they are poor and if you tell them they need to do trenching, trenching in this area you need an excavator. It can’t be done by hand. That is also another challenge.
There are areas where you find partners coordinate well. So, I won’t say lack of coordination but the level of coordination has not been to such a big extent. I’ll give you an example of that project we did with county. If we were left alone, we would not have done much and if the county were left alone, they would not have done much either. What we could have done is probably a smaller scheme which would not serve many people. So, if we coordinate well and pool resources together then we can do mega projects and we can serve many people. Currently, it is like that coordination element is not so much enhanced. If it can be enhanced at the county, at the implementation level leave alone at the meetings when we meet at the WASH forums. At the WASH forums, everybody will showcase what they are doing, but now after that are we taking any action to see if these partners are coordinating because I think that the essence of that forum is to enhance coordination. At the meeting I attended I never heard any feedback. I expected to hear someone say that from the previous learning through the forum they had been able to achieve coordination with certain partners, and give examples of what they had done…this partner and that have been able to pool resources together and work on something…such things. I think the leaders who are coordinating this need to move a step higher to enhance that coordination. 
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: First, on the resources, that would go direct to the county government. They need to allocate more and make water their key priority. The moment they allocate more and they come up with policies that will ensure that a lot of money is allocated in water, then I think that can somehow be addressed to some extent. Other partners can also do more in probably writing proposals and seeking funds to do mega schemes if they are able. But the key here is the government itself.
When we talk about the few sources, it will still come back to these resources. Since the sources are few, then we need to pool resources together and ensure that for the few sources we have, we can do mega schemes. For example, if we want to abstract from the river, we pool resources together and we can do a mega scheme. They are all tied together. 
When you come to poverty, poverty is very dynamic and even talking about how to eradicate it might be very difficult. Poverty to some extent is a mindset, so if we can have a change in mindset things can change. You see, animal farming is doing well in this sub-county (Kitui South). If people here can focus on animal farming just for business, the mindset of saying that they are very poor might not be there. Also, this area is very rocky and rocks are resources maybe if we can explore it then we can get some revenue. Poverty to me might be a mindset and the government needs to come in and see the need to educate the people on the need to exploit the available resources because everywhere, I think God planted resources that can be used and earn a revenue for people.
In terms of coordination, the government has to take the key role. Coordination should not be left to the partners. Like in the last WASH forum meeting, the discussion was that the meeting was delayed because of funding; the partner who funds the forum had not provided the funds needed. So, assuming that for the next meeting the say that they do not have funds, does it mean that we will not be able to have such a forum? So, if the government can take the lead on these coordination issues and come up with policies that say, for example, in certain areas scale water projects should not be set up…and that partners intending to set up small scale projects should partner with the county government and upon such an agreement, the partner and the county should pool resources and instead set up a huge project. If each partner comes and does projects according to the resources they have, we will be having very small projects that are not helping the community. So, the government needs to do a lot.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Could you walk us through the next steps if the solution were to occur?
 Respondent: The most important, although they are almost at the same level and you see that the government is cross-cutting, is the higher financial allocation. Now, on that issue of resources, the moment the government injects a lot of resources, the resources will come in handy  even in educating the people because you know the officers are in Kitui and the people are all over the county. If the officers don’t have resources, they can’t travel to educate the people. So, the moment they have the resources, this will ensure even the coordination is done. The higher resource allocation is like the umbrella and so they need to allocate more resources. 
I will give you just an example, yesterday we were in a partners’ meeting for this sub-county, it was organized by the public health department and they were saying some of the key challenges were resources. The amount that they are receiving to run the entire sub-county is very limited. They have an ambulance here but they can’t manage to fuel it. The ambulance is currently in Kitui town. The Kitui hospital is using the ambulance and in case of an emergency here at the sub-county, the doctors here have to call the Kitui hospital to send a vehicle here to come pick a patient and take them to the hospital in Kitui town. Now look at the time that is wasted…if the patient were in critical condition, there is no knowing what would happen. They are saying even these resources, even for them to monitor other facilities to see how they are performing is a challenge. Because if they don’t have a vehicle here in case of an emergency, what will happen? So, if the government can allocate resources, let’s say the funding, you know the government has polices and every department knows what they are supposed to do, the water officers they know what they are supposed to do. In the last meeting, you heard an officer saying they lacked the funding to repair their vehicle, so they were using one motorbike, so if county officers are facilitated, they can move all over to monitor the schemes, identify problems early enough, forward them to the responsible office then they can be addressed in time. They can meet communities frequently and discuss the challenges they face. 
If there is anything let’s say like I talked about the change in mindset, county officers can be able to educate the people because they are mobile. Compare that to the NGO world, like for me if I have a project somewhere and I need to carry out monitoring, I will be facilitated daily. If I want a vehicle now, it will come and I will go and do monitoring. So, if the government can replicate that and allocate more resources, they will ensure that they take lead in everything they see these partners doing like supporting these forums. So, if the government takes charge and they have resources, the other partners will bow to what they say; partners will obey the government’s directives. If for example a partner is taking charge, they will bulldoze the government since they are the ones providing funding and will give conditions to the government. The roles will have been reversed. So, I believe the government can allocate enough resources and take key responsibilities.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Pipeline extensions, water storage and boreholes, that is in Mutomo. We normally have community participation. This office, has its area of coverage so the funds we have is meant for the area this office covers. So, the moment we have partners engage where we go to the community, we explain to them the funds we have and what we want to do. For example, if we want to do a 10KM pipeline, where do you feel we need to inject this money? So, they also give us their views. During that you know we are also participating because if we want to do a pipeline, there has to be a source for example a borehole or a pipeline that you can tap from, so from the community participation they identify the areas that are most vulnerable. It is from that hierarchy that we identify which areas to begin with first and if the money allows, we can go to another level from that hierarchy. Normally it is from the communities that we normally engage them to identify the neediest areas.
Interviewer: Is there a particular data source that you refer to or you are mostly keen on getting and using that feedback from the community for coming up with interventions?
Respondent: One thing about this data, if you get the data from the community, it is normally better than the one that is already documented. After getting that data and information, and you know we get it in partnership with the government, we don’t do these meetings on our own; we do it with the government so that the government can also confirm for us if the data is true. Mostly we rely on that data because if we go to let’s say the documented data it may not even portray the true picture. Unless an intensive survey is done to pick these areas out, but mostly I would say we rely on the raw data. We go meet with the people who are in need and discuss the issues with them and the government. Before we move to the people, we discuss with the government what we have in mind in terms of projects, our areas of coverage and then they tell us which areas they propose for the interventions and then we go to the ground.
Interviewer: Is this data and information you are referring to the one you get from the community participation meetings?
Respondent: Yes.
