
  1. In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation / Water service sustainability in your wereda?  Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation / water service sustainability? 
 With regard to one-wash program, there are certain things that are being strengthened. If you see a WASH committee in the rural area, women are taking leadership role. For instance, in a WASH committee of five members, if two of them are men, three of them will be women. There is also improvement in taking care of water scheme points such as building fence around water points to protect the water. So, this is an improvement in the farmers’ community.  
When you come to the pastoralist community, people and animals are using the same source of water for drinking. There is also lack of a sense of belongingness among the people. Most of them don’t take care of the water infrastructures. Women’s participation in areas where women should participate is poor. For instance, if we invite some ten people to participate on community discussions, only two or three of them will appear. But women are the main users of water in most of their day-to-day activities, so we need to work closely with them if we want to bring change to the society.  
Our zonal water coverage is very law, may be around 33%. Compared to the geographical space and population size of our zone, the water coverage is very low. 
We don’t properly consult the community. If the people are asked to give comments about the place where a water scheme should be during discussions, they will be happy for that since they feel that they are being consulted.  But they are not properly consulted by the responsible governmental offices during designing and construction. So, it seems that discussions between the community and WASH stakeholders are not transparent and structured. It is customary to see that water drilling projects usually fail before completion owing to this problem even if we spend adequate budget.  In areas where most pastoralists are living, there is scarcity of underground water since the area is dry and lowland, Selamago wereda is a good example.  This is the worst place in terms of water access and coverage. One borehole was constructed recently but not functioning because of a problem during design. 
2. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
Designers should clearly discuss with the local people to get information about the potential sites and to check whether the proposed design is the best fit to the community or not.  Stakeholders, including us, need to be involved in awareness creation of the community especially women and their children. I think these problems will be solved by creating a forum for different stakeholders and working to increase the number of women development groups. 

 Interviewer: What is the role of stakeholders and how could they contribute to the solution? 
Once we establish women Development Groups for awareness creation program, stakeholders can participate on various activities working closely with the mainstreaming committee that our office is leading. WASH office (as a major stakeholder) is the one that works on ONE-WASH awareness program while we organize the development groups and make them function accordingly. If we work hard on women development groups, we will be effective in bringing change. 
Since the coverage is much lower than the demand, there is a high need to work on boring more water schemes. Most of the budget that is allocated for the zone is spent for security purpose. Hence, the share of budget that should have been spent on water becomes minimized. The budget that is allocated for a water projects may rise to one million Birr in some other zones found in the same region, like Sidama amd Gamogofa because they are relatively peaceful and are located in the center
 The community can definitely bring about a major change with regard to water and sanitation services if we are really committed to closely work with, encourage and participate in the decision-making process, including designing. In addition to this, we can have a procedure to punish people who don’t properly contribute to the protection of the schemes. 
3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Strengthen belongingness and sense of ownership in the community. This could solve many of the problems. The smaller administration, like the kebele and villages can formulate bylaws to control outliers and manager the schemes properly. When we are committed on this, we can have informed community that can manage and protect existing infrastructures without external intervention.
