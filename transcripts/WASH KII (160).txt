1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Interviewer: The first question is in your opinion, what are the main challenges that affect the sustainability of WASH service or water and sanitation services in the area. How these problems influence the sustainability of water/sanitation service?
Respondent: I think in order to make water and sanitation services sustainable in the first place we have to teach or train the community. That is the community should perceive or consider water and sanitation facilities constructed in their surrounding as their own property. In other words, we should create sense of belongingness within the community. Our previous experience is we develop water or sanitation facilities for the community without creating sense of belongingness within the community. However, we should involve them from the beginning of the construction up to final service provision. This leads them to consider the facilities as their own properties. Thus and effort to develop water or sanitation facilities should involve the community at various stages. But the previous trend is we only discuss with woreda and kebele government bodies about the development of water and sanitation facilities. The community members are missing from the table. However, it would have been great if we would consider the ideas, suggestion and knowledge of the community about how and where to develop water schemes.     
Interviewer: We will come to the solutions later but so far, I took community awareness problem and absence of community involvement in the development of water and sanitation facilities as one of the major problems. Are there any other problems that affect the sustainability of water/sanitation service? 
Respondent: The other major problem is that the care and management provided to the existing water facilities by community is very weak. As we know, water is a scarce resource we have to utilize it properly. Moreover, the electric generators that we use for pumping the water requires regular maintenance services but the community do not pay attention for this. In general, the community do not provide appropriate care and management for the water schemes constructed in their surroundings. 
 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: When we come to the solutions let start from the solution to the first problem you mentioned.  You said that there is community awareness problem on water facilities developed in the area. That is the community lacks sense of belongingness for the existing facilities. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?  
Respondent: In order to alleviate the problem related to the community’s awareness, we have to involve the community in the development of the water schemes from the very beginning to end to the end of the project. That is we have to consult the community and consider their inputs during designing and implementation of water projects. If we involve the community at various levels of development of water facilities, they will develop sense of belongingness for the schemes constructed in their areas. After construction of the schemes, we have to provide them training on proper use and management of the facilities sot that the water facilities provide sustainable service to the community. 
Interviewer: Most of the problems you raised are related to the community. Do you have something to say about problems related to WASH stakeholders functioning in the region? 
Respondent: It would have been good if other higher officials of the bureau respond to this question in addition to me. Any way one of the major problem particularly related to the One-WASH program is that the consultants that are offered consultancy assignment to undertake studies on design and implementation of water projects do not have knowledge and experience of the region. Do not have understanding of the context and situations of the region. They have only been assigned to provide the service as they have financial capacity. Their knowledge and understanding of the area is not taken into consideration during selection of consultants. Because of this their study outputs are of poor quality and do not meet the desired goal. We can even conduct better quality studies using our local capacity. 
The problems that occurred in the study will have obvious negative implication on the implementation of the projects. The problems occurred during construction of the water schemes implementation in turn affect the service we are required to provide to the community. In other words improper water project studies have seriously affect the sustainability of water service.
Interviewer: What ideas or recommendations do you have about the solution to this problem?  
Respondent: If the program (One-WASH) allowed, it is better to conduct studies using our own local capacity. It would be good if we can organize local consultants that have knowledge and experience of the area. As the region is very arid, the consultants do not stay long in the area to undertake rigorous study. However, local consultants that based in the region can tolerate the conditions and conduct the study without difficulties.      
Interviewer: You have mentioned that the community do not provide proper care and management to water facilities and water generators do not get regular follow up and maintenance. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: To alleviate such problems we have to strengthen our capacity. Mostly it is the regional water bureau that is responsible for the maintenance of water schemes providing service in different parts of the region. However, if it is possible we have to capacitate selected community members (i.e. caretakers) on handling minor maintenances based on community resources. Then for the cases that are beyond the capacity of the community, we have to strengthen woreda water offices to handle the problems based on their woreda resources. To this end we have to capacitate the woredas by providing materials support. For those cases that are beyond the capacity of the woredas the regional water bureau should take the responsibility to handle the problems. 
However, the current trend or practice is the regional water bureau is required to handle each of minor problems. To reach each rural area the regional office incurs large costs for transportation, per diem of professionals, etc. but when we reach to the areas the problems found to be very minor and can be handled by local community. Thus, capacitating local communities and woreda water offices is a best solution to ensure sustainable water service in the region.
Interviewer: You have said that there are caretakers in the community. Who are these groups? Who organized them? 
Respondent: These groups (caretakers) are members of the community that are responsible to manage the water schemes (they run the electric generators and open the water schemes for community use). These groups are also responsible to refill the generators with oil and follow up the operation of the water schemes. These groups are available in areas where there are water facilities. Thus, we have to provide solid trainings to caretakers about management the water schemes (i.e. about management of solar, electric grid and diesel based water schemes). If it is possible, it is better to shift the diesel generator based water schemes into electric grid, as the running cost of electric grid based water schemes is relatively lower. 
As we know, our community are mostly pastoralist and do not have cash on their hand. So to cover the cost of diesel (fuel) and other operational costs of the water schemes they are required to sell their livestock. Hence, electric grid based water schemes are much better than diesel generator based water schemes. If there is no electricity in the areas, it is better to relay on solar energies.
Interviewer: Most of our discussion focus on water service. Are there problems related to sanitation (i.e. both personal and community hygiene)? Do you have anything to say related sanitation service in the area?
Respondent: Our community requires awareness enhancement trainings as we widely observe open defecation in different parts of the region. This practice is observed not only in rural areas but also in urban areas. Thus, it is essential to teach the community about the effects of open defecation. We have to create a culture of developing personal and communal latrines through continuous trainings. In addition, we have to aware the community about the benefits of washing hands after use of latrine. There are good experiences in few kebeles of the region in this respect but in majorities of the kebeles, there are such sanitation problems. Our office mainly focus on the water service and the sanitation part is the responsibility of health bureau.
In order to ensure sustainable water service our bureau in collaboration with IRC WASH tried to form Water Unions in a pilot woreda (Mille) that will supply spare parts for water facilities and provide maintenance service in rural areas. To this end, the regional water bureau prepared a draft legislation for the formulation and support of water unions in different woredas of the region. If we get a good result in the pilot woreda (Mille), we will disseminate or replicate the lessons to other woredas of the region.  
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: You have raised different problems with suggested solutions and recommendations to alleviate the problems. As you are well aware, we are discussing on WASH service sustainability. Of the solutions you listed which is the most important to ensure sustainable WASH service? Can you walk me through what next steps or changes would happen if the solutions occurs? 
Respondent: I think the most important solution or the first priority area of intervention is to settle the community in a village. Currently our community is mobile in search of water and feed for its livestock. When we plan to develop water schemes in different areas and start implementation the next day during implantation of the project you will not find the people in the area. They may migrate to other place before the finalization of the project. Hence, we have to first work on the settlement of the society. The regional government has a plan to do this in the short-run. Once the community settle in the form of villages, developing water schemes, constructing schools and health facilities will be valuable. Therefore, if we can settle the community in villages and provide the services (water, health, education, etc.) that the community require from the government, we can avoid the migration of the society to other areas in search of water and food for its livestock. This implies they can get access for water, health and education services in their localities and improve their living conditions.
