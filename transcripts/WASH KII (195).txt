1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda?
[Limited Budget] Many of the schools in this zone lack pure drinking water and better toilets and the reason behind this is that the annual budget allocated to educational institutes is very low. This in turn is because the largest share will be used to cover expenses for school facilities, like books, chairs etc. Lack of finance and hence drinking water is creating difficulty in school feeding. As you know, school feeding is related to basic WASH services like pure water and protected toilet houses. It has been more than 15 years since school feeding has started in south Omo zone and we have been requesting WFB for their assistance, but it is beyond their capacity. Second respondent: Also, when we are thinking of WASH services, availability of electronic devices is vital although these are not problems to water service sustainability, but they can still contribute more to the WASH service because these days, you can do nothing without information and for this computers and other related devices, like IC tools (information communication) are necessary. [Lack of community awareness] there is lack of community awareness about WASH services both in our schools and the community at large. People are not responsible to take care of/maintain the existing water schemes.

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
 To cope up with budget shortage, unreserved coordination of stalk holders at all levels is mandatory starting from schools to the ministry of education in our case. To solve WASH problems at the school level, we need to mobilize and create awareness to the students and their parents. So far, we didn’t practice awareness to the level of satisfaction, and this is our weakness. Second, there are occasional school grants from woreda, zone and region education departments and specifically from high schools, there are funds contributed in the name of students who scored higher grades (e.g. DFID) that could help us mobilize the money to strengthen school and community WASH services. The other issue would be working together with CSOs. We need to invite them to the table for discussion to consult and provide financial and technical aids related to water services without affecting their programs, plans and intervention areas.  
With regard to community awareness, we need to work out from the bottom. You know that many people are not aware about the WASH services, including the enlighten people themselves. Hence, when we start WASH implementation activities, we should work on basic awareness creation campaigns among the rural people and the students. Also, water is vital to the school feeding program, many of our schools are being constrained by shortage of clean water. Because of lack of water, attributed to the limited government budget, we used to transport it from ‘Nery River’ after applying water treatment chemicals just to help school feeding program not interrupted. This is because there is a strong bond between feeding program and water and sanitation. As a temporary solution, the regional government water bureau, together with federal pastoralist affairs, is working to construct water schemes in places/hubs where many schools can be accessed since it is difficult to construct everywhere given the limited budget in the zone. We can’t solve water shortage problem totally, but step by step and through application of more scientific and research based approaches, we can reduce gradually. Also, we want other stalk holders to get involved and work on this, to make water sustained in the schools and avoid its shortage through school roof catchment, digging big water boreholes and maintaining the existing schemes. To avoid shortage of electronic devices, we are at the planning stage and writing proposals to higher government sectors and NGOs that are directly involved in WASH sector.  
3.Of the solution you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
As to South Omo zone water service shortage and context, addressing the budget issues will take the largest share to bring about sustainable water services in the area. We need more budget to buy parts and equipment that are needed to water infrastructure development. We may discuss and solve problems that need discussion, but addressing water service problems are beyond discussion and we should turn them to operation, otherwise they may be left as a paper value. Additional budget is also mandatory to make school feeding more sustainable since we can’t conduct teaching students without water. As you know, water issues need one of the largest budgets from since it needs fight against nature and there are times that we may not get underground water even after investing huge capital

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? 
As to my experience, we are working in close coordination with the zone water, mine and energy office regarding government WASH program. The zone water office collects WASH plans from different zone departments, compiles them and report to the regional water, mine and energy bureau for approval. That is the customary trend so far but not still adequate by itself to answer WASH service demands. What should be improved? We should evaluate WASH implementation procedures together, we need to check what we did in the past, what benefits has the community received and what next steps are needed taking the previous gaps as a lesson. We should give time to sit and discuss these issues together since there are practical gaps in these kinds of discussions. In addition, we should have intervention and close follow up on the short term projects, like toilet and small water scheme development programs, being practiced everywhere. Second respondent: We are saying that we work together with the national one WASH program and that shouldn’t be enough to address the water sustainability problems in the zone, instead we need to have a coordinated action with the NGOs, including but not limited to IRC WASH, Save the Children and Catholic Development. Thus, it needs that formation of a strong WASH cluster coordination office that calls for all the NGOs working in WASH sector and government offices to come to table discussion for coordinated planning and activities is very crucial. 



5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how you believe this coordination would lead to more sustainable water service in the woreda?

Strong coordination is highly reckoned. When we have a strong coordination in WASH sector, we can easily avoid overlapping of programs. This is also important to fair distribution of water schemes and effective utilization of financial and other resources across woredas and kebeles. Second respondent: Let me give you one practical scenario for overlapping of programs, there are more than five toilet houses constructed by different NGOs in a single school, Goldia School, Benatsemay woreda. On the other hand, you can see a number of other schools in the other woredas which don’t have AT LEAST one toilet house and because of this they use open spaces in the field, which will expose them to water born diseases. There are also kebeles where students usually dropout schools because of lack of drinking water particularly schools situated at the peripheral and arid areas while schools found near to woreda towns and humid climatic condition are equipped with roof water harvesting schemes.  Therefore, strong coordination can solve these kinds of unfair WASH services in the zone and woredas
