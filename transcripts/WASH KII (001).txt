
1. In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation / Water service sustainability in your woreda?  Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation / water service sustainability?
 Some of the water pipelines in this town are diverted before reaching the main reservoir. Water should not be distributed to the people unless it is treated at the main tanker. In addition, people are using water that is not well treated with chlorine. This is actually happening because of the low level of community awareness on water and sanitation services. With regard to infrastructures, there are very old and damaged pipelines that have not been repaired since establishment. We have also shortage of larger water reservoirs; water is being supplied through three tankers only. Lack of good governance in the town is another problem affecting its sustainability.   
There are no sufficient personnel in the sector in general and in our office in particular. We have planned to employ six experts in this year. There are only two professional experts at the moment. 

There is one borehole that has already been constructed for the town but it’s working nor since it is not supplied with tanker. We have also a problem of laboratory or any other water treatment plants.  

 2. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
The first thing we need to do is to treat the water from contamination so that people can access pure water and be safe from communicable diseases. Second, very old and broken pipes and available tankers need timely maintenance or total replacement. Maintenance and replacement cost are expensive but it could be covered through regular government budget. Thus, it needs mobilization of resources and finance from the community, private actors and NGOs. We should also teach our people to increase awareness on sanitation and hygiene. There is also a need to engage the community with during design of water projects and recruitment of professionals and provide them with vocational training so that they can be responsible. The pipeline to be procured should have the right thickness and size so that it can be durable and accommodate water pressure. 
Recently, we discussed with the management committee and made decisions on budget allocation to buy and replace existing old pipeline with new ones. We will be keeping this trend in the future as we have many outdated pipelines in the route that need maintenance and replacement. Recently, only three kilometers of pipelines were replaced by new ones through refund of a budget that would have been used to construction of roads. 

Interviewer: You mentioned that awareness problem is another factor affecting suitability, what could be the solution to this? 

We have an office with the name of population council that can work closely with the community representatives in this and other related issues. What we do is we need to create regular meetings and mobilization programs for the public in order to increase awareness. I said this because there are selfish people with in the community that don’t care for the properties being damaged. They do this because they lack a sense of belongingness to the public properties. 

Also, there is a kind of corrupt character with some of the officials working in water and sanitation office. They do this to benefit some people at the cost of the majority. One of the manifestations for this is provision of water services from the main grid that is flowing to the reservoir. Accordingly, these people can access the service easily even in times of shortage.   

Interviewer: What do you think is the solution for the bad traditions of waste disposal? 

Every time, we are explaining the community on the proper ways of waste disposal. We teach the techniques of disposal for both solid and liquid wastes but this is not adequate. Majority of the residents are disposing wastes everywhere and that is why we see rubbish and liquid waste on the main asphalt and river sides  

 3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important?  Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Providing sufficient water supply using the maximum potential possible should be the priority. Once we have adequate water supply throughout the town, we can speak of sanitation and hygiene issues continuously. Most of the people, especially those in rural areas, use normal spring water that is not well treated. Clean water access is also vital for health as it will have little or no access to water-borne diseases. Children and mothers will be saved since they are among the most exposed segments of the population. There is a water scheme that was built long back but it is not giving service.
