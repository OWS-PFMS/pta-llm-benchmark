Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: For rural, I could start with financial issues. Financial aspects are linked towards water management systems or rural water management systems which require a lot of investment in terms of assets. You find that on average one of them is costing about 20 million shillings and so for a community to come up with enough money to sustain that becomes very expensive for them especially in terms of what we call capital expenditure. Capital expenditure deals with things like breakdown of pumps; the big issues. You can imagine a pump that costs 4 million, when it breaks down, to replace the motor could cost up to 2 million shillings. The community can't have money to replace that so for them what they can do is life cycle costing. When you cost for a rural water project, we cost for it in all aspects so for a community managed system even if it's under project manager model you find that they can be able to cover operation and maintenance expenditure and that means they can pay their staff, they can pay for maybe for plumbers, for electricity that is operational cost, they can pay for fuel if it's diesel, they can pay for maybe small spare parts, but when it comes to capital expenditure for example solar system breakdown or a big pipe like HDPE pipe maybe requires replacement for a 2 km distance.  Communities are unable to cover expenditure that is around 500,000 shillings. You find that in their accounts they don't have that money so when that happens the system breaks down and it's not serving people. Once it is not serving people, they are not getting the revenue that they require to run the system, people are not paying for water so it starts there. Once it starts there then everything stops. They start looking for money from either county government or local politicians if it's the county government Even if they want to come and help, you know the way the government works…it has a budget cycle so even if they will want to come to replace their motor or their pump, best thing it can take is even maybe up to one year because it has to be budgeted for, it is approved, once it is approved the money is released so it is a whole process and they will not apply for one project in let's say in Kyuso, or one project in Mutomo. They will want to apply for all of them so it usually takes time. The minimum time it can take is say one year even if they have some money, they also have a lot of projects to take care of. For politicians again that means they use the Constituency Development Fund (CDF) or they use their own personal money which is also not easy for them to do so from there you find that the projects start stalling so when they stall, issues such as vandalism start coming up, because once people see that the facilities are not operational, there are some people in the community who start stealing things like the spare parts or pipes. So, somebody will go and start maybe removing one panel or two panels then after two or three years you find then that project becomes so dilapidated to even return it to what it used to be becomes a challenge so it starts like that so that is one of the major challenges; the financial aspect of water supply. 

Another challenge I can say is also the technology. Organizations can bring in certain technologies but the capacity to manage, maintain and repair is not there. The good thing with solar is that right now there is a lot of awareness on solar but there are some systems even for solar, things like chlorine dozers. If those things break down…you see, it's one or two companies that can come and repair so the community does not have the capacity to repair or maintain them so that is also sometimes a challenge. Getting these companies to come on the ground requires money and resources to be mobilized for them to come to do a report or even to get a replacement. For our Project, there are some things such as pumps for some facilities which have been brought from abroad. If such a pump breaks down you have to go back to the documentation and look at who supplied the pump, call that company, they tell you that they brought the pump maybe from Germany. If it is Grundfos you call them if it is Lorentz you call them. They then tell you that one of their engineers will come to the ground and at times you find that they have either one or two engineers in the country who can do that repair. It requires time, you are losing money as a project so also technology has its own challenges. 

There is an issue which we also face on documentation; it is also another challenge. If you want to develop a water project, there are certain documents that you require. Previously, these documents were under the ministry of water in the district water offices then. Now the responsibility for water has been transferred to the county governments but when these changes happened, these offices did not transfer documentation to the county so you find that for most of the projects that were developed before devolution, the community does not have documentation and the county does not have documentation. For the ones that the county that has done, the county just does maybe one or two documents. It would be good for me to mention these projects. For a water project, first of all before you even do a borehole or even a dam, there is a hydrogeological survey that needs to be done. Most of these things are not done. So, the first document is the hydrogeological survey document, there is the environmental impact assessment, social impact assessments, there are geological surveys that need to be done, there are borehole reports in the case where boreholes are being drilled, there is approval from the National Environmental Management Authority (NEMA), that is under the  environmental impact assessment, there is approval from the Water Resource Authority (WRA) before you even do the borehole. There is the borehole completion report so you find that if it is a private company that comes and drills the borehole, it does not prepare a borehole completion report, and is given the money for that borehole and they go. If it is the county drilling, they go all over drilling boreholes and they don't tell people you know this is your report because sometimes it's an emergency. You just get a call from a member of county assembly (MCA) saying “…go to a certain place, drill a borehole.” You have to go there and drill the borehole, so those reports are not there. There should also be a water quality testing report and a pump testing report, and all these documents are not available. So, if an NGO comes or even a developer comes, and says that they would like to equip an existing borehole, the engineers will first ask for those reports because they cannot design a system without those reports because they help in determining the pumps to install and such. Without the reports, money has to be spent to carry out the necessary assessments and develop the report.
So, it costs so much for the NGO developer which are things that if proper documentation was done, it would be a bit easier for them to come and help. The documentation should be left in the hands of the community and county officers in charge of particular areas.
In our Project, we are still having what we call a transfer to the county government. We are working with them to have a knowledge base, something similar to what you are working on in your Project. We are pushing them to have an information management system where you can access and obtain all the sources in Kitui. That knowledge base, let me call it a library, at the county headquarter should also have all those documents for each of those projects. You can imagine for example, if you reach a level that once we do the mapping of sources like especially the boreholes or the major sources those small ones like hand pumps and the rest, someone can go in a system or a platform and when they click on a dam or a borehole, they are able to see the system specifications, there are also attachments for those documents and this platform is available to anyone who wants to come in from all over. It is like an online platform so if a person gets into the system, and searches for Mutomo, they can see all the sources, boreholes and their capacities, they can access the documents and even download them because these should be free documents. Download them and you have them in your records. When you want to go to Mutomo, you don't need to go anywhere and start asking for those documents again. The county government when they come, they will just ask the developers about the areas they would like to come and support. If they say a certain sub-county, they log into the system find out about the functional and non-functional boreholes, the schemes with issues and once they select where to intervene, they access the facility documents and get to work. When they start working on the facilities, they will most likely not encounter any issues such as missing permits, approvals or licenses from either WRA or NEMA. All the approvals will be available. Most of the people who come to develop don't want to develop something or invest a lot of money and then tomorrow you hear that you put a pump here and this borehole is illegal. That brings a lot of issues because if a borehole does not have a completion report or a certificate from WRA it is an illegal borehole and if does not have a quality water report that means you could be providing water to people which is of poor quality and it can bring issues. 
The other challenge is governance. I have two or three ways of looking at it. Firstly, is the issue of governance at a higher level at the County level. The county itself has its own issues and if you look at county officials like sub county officials or even at the headquarter, they are not well financed to support the work they are mandated to do. There are a lot of politics involved. Recently there was the issue of non-payment of salaries for several months. If the county does not even have money to pay for example salaries for county officials and you want to go with them on the ground, this person maybe does not even have fare to come to the office or does not even have facilitation to go to those projects so you tell them, “okay I’ll come and pick you with my vehicle”, but at the end of the day if someone does not have a salary, they are in a very bad situation even psychologically or even as a professional and they are not able to perform as expected. You can go with them on the ground but their minds might be focused on other things; not on the work itself and so things like allowance become an issue because if you don't give that person an allowance at the end of the day it becomes a challenge. However, if this person is being paid well, they are even receiving allowances to go in the field and so we can work together with them very easily. 
The other issue is also financing to support the projects themselves especially the assets. If the county does not have money then it means that it is the NGO partner to provide those resources and we are lucky as an organization we had enough money to invest in infrastructure because infrastructure can be costly. The other day I was just looking at it and realized that for Kitui we did an investment of close to over 100,000 million shillings in terms of investment and that is just for capacity building. If I look at the work we did, including the work focused on WSPs, it would go up even up to 150,000 million shillings and that is just for Kitui. I know that county budgets can be large and for Kitui the county budget can get to 700 million shillings and that involves salaries, operational costs and other costs. You see, the problem with Kenya is that if you look at our governance issues you can find that for a budget of 700 million shillings,  60% or 70% of it goes to operational cost that means it's paying for things like salaries, things like transport, all those things so if you are left with let's say 30% of 700 million, that is about 200 million and that is for the whole county. If as an organization we did 6 or 7 projects in Kitui with 150 000 million, I know that the county has thousands of projects or facilities and this tells of the the magnitude of water issues in terms of finance from the county side. So, if they are getting 700 million shillings for water and they are using 60% or 70% for operations they are left with about 200 or 300 million shillings. One project takes around 20 million shillings to do fully from start to finish and that means you can only do maybe 10 or 30 projects. Kitui, you see by the time you are able to say that in Kitui we have done 100% coverage or access to clean and safe water; it will take time.  So, those are the things if you quantify them governance comes in there so it requires the MCAs to lobby for more funds, look at how these funds are being used from the county assembly, pass legislation or policy at county level for more allocation to water issues and work on reducing wastage through corruption or put in place policies that support more money to go to the ground, put more focus to rural water because a lot of time a lot of focus is put into monies to support WSPs who are urban and you forget about rural, saying or hoping that the rural will be helped by NGOs or other people. A huge chunk of the budget allocation might go to the urban WSPs yet Kitui is massive and they only cover a small part of the county; the uncovered areas are larger than the ones they cover. This requires policy because it all boils down to how much money is allocated; how much money is coming from national government. It also boils down to what we are pushing for now that is for more money to be allocated to county governments. That is the governance issues at the high level and it goes down up to sub county level. 
There are also some governance aspects at the community level. Governance issues here are in terms of transparency for the management committees, there is also corruption, wastage, revenue is being collected and it is not being invested back into the project, overreliance on the county or politicians, to come and help them. They have their own political issues because a certain person wants to be chairman yet another also wants to be the chairman and this is because they think the projects have money and they can eat the money. These governance issues bring a lot of challenges, take for example in Kithambangii which we did. They changed the committee recently through other means which were not okay because even if a committee is not performing as expected there are procedures to follow for a committee to be removed. It requires an annual general meeting and an election for a new committee to be elected but once it becomes political, MCAs come in, the committee is thrown out and a new committee is brought in and so the new committee brings a challenge for the supporting agents because you have to start again, you have built the capacity of these people, and start all over again with these new people.
There are other challenges like environmental challenges. Currently we are facing very heavy rains and climate change is an issue. There are droughts and floods. When there is drought there is a lot of overreliance on those water systems and they become stressed because if it's in Mutomo and there is drought, everyone will come and get water from that one project which is working, they don't want even their cattle or their goats to die so if a project was designed to serve let's say 5000 people it will end up serving maybe 10000 or 20000 people together with their livestock and so you find that maybe they will have to over pump the water. This means that the generator will breakdown because it is overstressed so when those systems or projects become overstressed due to drought then they can also easily breakdown and if they are not well managed that's the time when even the management committee will be thrown out because if something happens it's blamed on them. When it comes to flood it is an issue of the pipes being washed away or the intakes close to rivers being destroyed. Pipes that are passing through valleys or gullies can be washed away and replacing them is a challenge. It also brings in the challenge in terms of revenue. When it rains there is not much revenue, because people will use the water from the rains so you find that like right now the projects are not collecting any money from users. Sales usually go so low that the schemes collect zero at the kiosks. Most people have tanks in their homes so when it rains, they don't pay; they are not taking your water you go there and you read the meter and find that it is the same as you left it. If any water has been drawn, it is less than a cubic meter and that translates to about 100 shillings. 
If you look at it, because we look at it from the records of the projects, it's two seasons so in the first season which is usually around March, April, May you notice that their revenue will drop towards the end of March up to May. If households collect the water in let’s say March and April, they will still use it up to the end of May. In June that's when you start to see the revenue coming back and it comes back gradually then peak in July. Again, it peaks in September and October. It then starts declining in mid-October. It is a cycle and it really affects them that's why you see they have to plan their revenues very well because if they over expense during the time when they are collecting a lot of money, for example if they hire more people during that time because they think they have money, when there is no money then their expenditures will over exceed their revenues and then that affects them. They either go in to debt and they have to borrow so they really have to look at their planning in a yearly basis, especially as regards how much money they are able to collect in one year so that when it is high they are saving and by the time it reaches September they have about 200,000-300,000  shillings in their account as reserve so that in October November December when there is no much revenue they still have reserve so that when it comes to January again when it starts getting a bit dry again they still have some money in their account. The 300,000 they had saved goes down to around 100,000 and then it starts topping up so they have to plan because of the seasons. 
We have been training the schemes on financial management and there is what we call a business plan. Under the business plan there is something we call forecasting. We have been telling them that because we are doing a five-year business plan, we need to do what is called forecasting. If you do financial forecasting, you look at right now we are in 2019 you look at 2020; how many connections do you have now, how many individual connections do you think you will have then and then that translates to revenue and you also need to look at your expenditure in the long term. You consider the number of staff you have, the salaries you are paying and how much you would like to pay them in the coming year. You also do a forecast of expenditures such as paying for electricity. It is like a model that is run where you can determine ways of reducing expenditures and opportunities for increasing revenues. If the schemes are repaying a loan, that is also detailed in the forecasting.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: For community governance there a couple of solutions but first I usually think that the best way is for an investor or an NGO coming to deal with the community to first start with selection of projects. First of all, it is very important to work with people who are willing to work with you so there is that willingness or sometimes it's called ownership by the community so if these people are willing to work with you then there is ownership. When we began, we used to do some contracts with the committees so they sign the contract and agree to adhere to certain principals like transparency. This way they are cooperative so those principals at that organization, ethics all those things, that's where it starts like any other relationship or contract you are doing with an organization. When it comes now to their own governance issues, because when you go to the community, they think their model is the best. They know their issues and they are the ones who are supposed to manage so you cannot say that all projects have a fixed or a similar solution; it depends with what you find on the ground. Let me give you an example – you can go on the ground at a project like Mumbuni and find that the chairman or the treasurer or the secretary is already operating like a project manager. It is somebody who is trained on water issues and he has some training in maybe finance, even if it is at certificate or diploma level and he is managing the project very well but they still have some issues on governance because as a committee there is politics maybe in terms of the revenue just like any other organization. So, you look at that project and say, instead of bringing another person as a project manager, you already have somebody that you can train further or transform that position even if it is a treasurer or the secretary to also be like the project manager. He can have two roles so you tell these guys okay you guys are the committee but this person, one of your members we want him to become like a project manager that means he will be solely responsible mainly for record keeping, ensuring that all the kiosks for example have proper records, ensuring that all the individual connections are billed on a monthly basis, and ensuring that the revenue collected gets to the bank account and that all expenses also are coming through the same bank account. The manager should have all the records, the business plan and reports to the committee either on a weekly or monthly basis. This is one model where it's like a staff member becomes a project manager. The facility is still managed by the community but this is a community that is at least well up in terms of knowledge on management of rural water schemes and so you work with them to constantly build their capacity via introducing systems or structures that will enable them to manage their water very well. This is an easier approach. There are some projects where we have a one-man show scenario where the chairman is the secretary and the treasurer and is the only one running the project, and running it very poorly. In such a case we diplomatically and cautiously tell the committee that they do not have the capacity to manage the project and we recommend that they hire young people trained in water issues. We recommend someone from the local community to support in managing the system and looking at records, and we recommend that the person selected is paid on a monthly basis. We make it clear that the manager is not replacing them or in charge of them – so that we avoid resistance or rejection. There are other projects where it is best to have the WSPs take over the management of the projects – so, the WSP assigns a staff member to manage the project and all the revenue goes to the WSP. There are all sorts of management models, most of which have been defined by the Water Services Regulatory Board (WASREB). Public-private partnerships are also another model to operate with where you assign a private person, it can be fully managed or managed under a certain contract like operational contract to a private firm like the FundiFix model so all these models also help in terms of governance at community level. The county needs to come in strongly to support these models. There are what I can call, sometimes we normally say “dead on arrival” projects. Meaning that even if a project or idea is very good if it does not have the right support, it will not work out. These models are good and if they come on the ground to be implemented, the county needs to be aware of them and how they operate because sometimes the challenge is lack of awareness. If there is this officer who is maybe getting something from that project and he sees this new thing coming in and he does not know very well how it is going to operate, he is definitely going to fight it. If there is support for these models at a high level, even at a low level, they will have to conform so these models once they are appreciated by the county… good thing Kitui county is very supportive, guys at the county level are very supportive so once at that high level it is understood and they say okay this one goes this way, that way there will be easy adoption and most of these challenges on management governance will be solved.
We also talked about finance. For finance I had talked about county finance solution; more allocation to the county. If there is a pool of funds that can be set up where investors or supporters like NGOs and others can chip in and it is well managed, that pool of finance, can do very well. 
The county can hire somebody who is from a background in finance or somebody who has a background in finance who is managing this pool. If we get like 1 billion shillings from various investors, then projects will be doing proposals just the way you do proposals to NGOs – you do proposals, these proposals are assessed by sub county officers, they see that they are valid, they are approved, there is this whole technical process of approval. Once they are approved, they come now to finance, finance now says okay, we can allocate this money get a contractor, they request bids, people apply, they get a good contractor, then it is financed under the County. Once it is financed they can have many models like the models of recoverable grants and if it is a project that you can recover money from, you tell them okay we will give you this money but you will have to return like 20% or 30% so that you see whether they are serious and I know some of them will be able to return or repay in about 3 or 4 years if you give them good terms.
From this same pool in terms of financing the county can also educate the projects that, if you are doing a good proposal, if you want funding, we cannot fund all of you but there are banks or other institutions that can fund you. To give a good example, if you look at the agricultural sector, through the cooperatives or societies they were being given almost everything by government. There was what we used to call the Kenya Farmers' Association (KFA), they were being given fertilizers, tractors on hire by the government and this was all forms of financing. The county, will not give you money if it's a project even if you do a proposal they will give you the investment of what you have asked for if it's a pump, pipeline, after contracting somebody to do that for you and then those things you will repay based on those assets.

If the county gives people money they will misuse or misappropriate the money. The community will be old that when they do a proposal, we will give you these things but in form of assets that you required or investment that you required through our own sub contracted, approved contractors and they put also very high standards maybe for design of those projects, and implementation. I was giving you an example of the agricultural sector, so before it was fully financed by government or mostly financed by government but it reached a point where also the private sector came in and they saw that even if I give this maize farmer maybe a tractor, he can be able to repay that tractor in maybe 5 years. So, for water projects even right now, the good things are things like solar since their costs are coming down; borehole pump costs are coming down so let's say with an investment of 4 million for example from a bank, we say we can give you 4 million, you invest, you are getting revenues like let's say 50,000 shillings a month, you tell the bank okay, if that it is like for one kiosk or two or three kiosks in an urban area, you tell them okay I’ll give you 10,000-20,000 a month, that maybe translates to how much in a year or how much in 5-6 years so if the financial institution thinks that those are good terms, you get in agreement. Well, we are still far from getting there because of the high investment costs of water and returns and lack of security sometimes but I know of projects that got loans of about 1.5 million and were able to repay quite fast.
They repaid it very fast because they were operating like a private project and it was actually a private enterprise. You know, for a private enterprise, you are very clear on what you want to do with the money so once you get the 1.5 million shillings and it's contracted and you have said that you will pay, private people don't have a lot of issues in terms of repayment but community because they are not very well structured, you give them money, they start telling you, we did not collect enough money…it was the rainy season…and other excuses but for private enterprises, they are more serious. That enterprise I am talking about bought a water bottling system, set it up, got certification from the Kenya Bureau of Standards and are now selling water in one of the towns on Mombasa road. They are running a very good business and they have been able to repay the money I think in less than 6 months. The water sector actually has very many opportunities currently but banks have been hesitant because of things such as collateral or security because if you want to do a borehole and you have a land title deed, they can give you a loan. If you want to do a borehole and it is on your piece of land, they can give you the money and you can invest but if you don't have something tangible, like sometimes, county projects don't have something that is tangible. These are community-based organizations (CBOs) or self-help groups and they cannot say, even their assets are maybe under the county government they cannot say those assets are theirs so if they don't repay, the bank cannot come and take the pump. It is a community asset so that's why you see banks are a bit hesitant provide credit to community groups unless there is a supporter or a guarantor and say okay, we will give them that support and if they don't repay maybe we can support them. So, for finance there is in short commercial financing which are grants and financing from government, county or national level and there is NGOs also. Revenues from them if they manage revenues very well, they can be able to…you remember what I was calling operational expenditure and capital expenditure so there are all these expenses so for operational expenditure if they manage their revenues very well, they can sustain their operations in a long term. 
When it comes to technology it is always best to look at the solutions very well before you adopt. That is the main solution and also implementation of that technology so if there is a technology coming in, okay, where is it coming in from, if it is a local company, so can you offer enough training that this thing can sustain and in terms of your contract, what does your contract say? For our projects we gave what we call a defects liability period of 6 months where 6 months after the project has been implemented or the thing has been put in, 6 months in case of anything you as the contractor and project implementer are still responsible to come and repair or replace but at least for those 6 months if  it serves them very well, you find that most technologies in the first 6 months they will have some things that the community maybe does not know how to do so they will still keep calling and it is still under your contract for those 6 months to keep coming on the ground and show them how it should be done so those are some of the things that can help in terms of technology. The contract itself and this technology is it something that is sustainable by the community, simple things. I can give like an example of the chlorine dozers we introduced in one project. They don't use electricity because imagine if we put a dozer that uses a generator, that generator needs to be fueled, if it uses solar, a solar pump needs to be installed so it is the whole electrical component that needs to be maintained and then now there is a mechanical component of the dozer that needs to be maintained but for us they use the pressure from the pipeline so it's an automated dozer. It just uses the pressure from water in the system. So, you see it's a very simple system that is sustainable because once you mix the chemicals in one container the pump itself uses the pressure of the water to collect the chlorine and it has a gauge and it dozes in the line the right amount so it's a new technology that is being implemented and it's actually good because it is a simple technology and with the Kithambangi project the technician was able to use and maintain it because it is just valves. It is something that can easily be opened and if you need to clean it, you clean it then you return the valves. In case the valve breaks down you just open the valves, take them to the manufacturer and then you wait for a new valve to come and replace. Simple technologies that can be easily adopted and then the training component and the long term contracts at least because the problem has been that somebody comes from Nairobi, the community does not know where this guy is coming from, comes put something and the community has no idea where it's coming from and they are paid and they go so if any problem comes up, you call somebody and tell them okay we are going to come on the ground, somebody does not show up but with those kind of strict contracts which county is also involved, they will make sure that during those defects liability periods or long term even after you have installed that thing is still well maintained.
For the environmental challenges, I will look at them also from an aspect of technology or implementation of projects. At least always take into consideration those aspects when you are doing anything if for example you are siting a borehole, take into consideration floods can come if you are doing a kiosk or anything and just remember droughts and floods are now something we have to live with. In any case when you are doing your projects, just take them into consideration. That's why we do those EIA assessments to look at the whole aspect because you look at both the technical aspect, you look at how maybe it affects them financially, socially, because maybe during floods the community cannot go to a certain place, during droughts the community maybe this is the way the community behaves maybe when there is drought there is famine and there is no food…how does that community survive? There are all those social aspects maybe they cannot even pay for water during drought so do you need to change the tariff during the drought season or do you need to focus on the kiosk like what we did for the Tharaka project? We said that in this area we don't need to do individual connections because we don't see them as a feasible way of doing things so we decided to focus on kiosks only and a few institutions because it’s like a humanitarian project you know there are some areas in rural areas you don't focus on individual connections, there is one household here, there is another household maybe a kilometer that other way so that system to manage with individual connections is a challenge so you just say okay let's do kiosks and schools or health institutions then you leave it like that. So, when it comes to those climate change issues look at also how they affect water quality. For example, during drought and floods, if it is a borehole is it affected during floods. Mitigation measures that can be put in place in designing your systems…maybe raising your intake if it's close to a river if it's a river crossing for pipes putting those things like anchor blocks - proper anchor blocks so that they are not washed away because…I forgot to also mention road construction is a challenge sometimes because when they are building the roads they always remove the pipes so it's also another challenge so when you are putting the pipes make sure you look at where the road ends you add like 6 meters so that if there is any expansion of that road they do not spoil the roads. So, for climate change those are the issues and also look at the data which is there in terms of climate so that you can also do your own forecasting and see how that will affect the systems
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: It’s difficult to say because all these things are interlinked. What I can say for the past few years as professionals we have been focusing on governance you know very well because most of us if we don't put the governance aspect right even if you put financial investments, the governance issues will still remain so that's where the models are also coming into place in terms of governance and sustainability but at the end of the day someone will say even if you do the best governance and somebody does not have money, not much will be achieved. We can attribute our success to having invested a good amount of money in projects.
