
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your town?

I think the first and the most difficult one is the [Topography of the region] This is related to the natural and climatological barriers of the study area in the region. We can’t get the resource itself since the water table is relatively unreachable. The second challenge is [lack of responsibility] to take care of the existing water services. This might be the result of low level of community awareness.  The community lacks the basic knowledge about clean water and sanitation services. There is also [excessive salt and fluoride content] in areas where we have tried burrowing. In many of the woredas in Afar region, the water resource is basically composed of these elements which directly threaten the current capacity of the regional WASH stalk holders because of the need to have excess money and better technology for the process of desalination.  
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to the problems you have mentioned?

Regarding the topography of the areas, I think we should come with detail investigation of the nature of the soil and water table before starting the process of searching and constructing water points. We should also identify availability of potential water sources in the area in order to save both time and money. Concerning awareness issues, we have made reasonable activities so far but that is not adequate. Changing community awareness by itself is a steady process coupled with the agro pastoralist life style. I think, all WASH stalk holders need to include this activity, creating awareness, along with their major annual or strategic plans. This needs working closely with the community leaders at the kebele level and establishing a communication channel through formal and informal institutions which are directly responsible to the WASH coordination office in the region. Along with the formation of this system, we can control and monitor the changes timely. I know that there is an already established system, but that one is very fragmented and need to be reorganized and chained to the coordination office. 
 

One temporary solution to the salinity and fluoride problems could be through digging shallow walls instead of the deep ones. However, this needs involvement of NGOs and other bilateral organization as it is highly linked up to financial and technical capacity. 

3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? What would happen if the solution occurs?
The first and best priority is conducting adequate research to identify water potential areas and water sources which are freely available and with lower or no fluoride content. While doing this, awareness creation and responsibility sharing to the community will also be vital.

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved?
There existing coordination system is relatively weak. This is particularly true among government WASH stalk holders since everyone is busy for department specific tasks.  

What could be improved?
We need to have common plan and regular meeting schedule to discuss issues that are beyond the capacity of individual organizations. Unless we have shared WASH activity plans, it would be difficult to solve the current multifaceted WASH service problems in the region. For example, one of the contributing factors to the weak coordination is the limited mandate given to the regional WASH coordination office. This office is embedded in the regional water bureau and it has no executive power. Thus, my opinion here is that this coordination office should be capacitated in terms of logistics, official and manpower so that it can serve as per the intended objectives.
