Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: I think I will talk about where we are, Kitui East. One of the key challenges we have here it is qualified personnel. When you go to the rural water schemes you find that most of the people are not conversant with services and in particular we talk of distribution. We don't have pipe fitters, we don't have pipe patrollers. The other challenge we have I think is operational costs. Most of the water points here are boreholes and they operate through gensets or diesel energy and you find that the costs are very high compared to what they collect as revenue. The third one is, we have talked of capacity building personnel, we have talked of operational costs, then the other one is we talked of the sources of water you find that we only rely on ground water which is very costly to exploit. And when you talk of clean water like we have very few reliable rivers here. I think we rely on Thua. So what we are doing of late now we have shifted our interest so that we go and develop the water from the rivers. We are relying on sand dams, we talk of intake sump-wells, because they give water they supply a bigger population other than a small borehole these small boreholes they just act as water points. So those are the key three things. We have talked of personnel, we have talked of operational cost then we have talked of scarcity of the sources of water.
Interviewer: Any other challenges?
Respondent: The other challenge is we don't have enough enough water projects such as we still need more dams, boreholes so that we can reduce community travelling more kilometers searching for water. And there is another point which I should mention, we have another challenge we call salinity levels of water especially that one is rampant in Kitui East. You drill a borehole, for example we have a borehole called ndetani borehole it has a discharge of around 30 but you find that the salinity levels are so high so that it can't be recommended for domestic use so you leave it for livestock. There was a time we had a program which was coming through Epicentre we were trying to removing the salt from the water, desalinization but it never reached here. There is a pilot which is being done in Kitui West. So we hope that can sustain, that can be a breakthrough although nobody knows about the cost of that. We hope it will be a milestone towards sustainability.  
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Number one we have talked about trained personnel, because you can't say you are going to employ personnel to the rural schemes because of the issue of sustainability. So we what we wanted is we have staff here at the sub-county office. Then we shall be attending to those things as the needs arise at the rural schemes. That is one way. The other way we had discussed it through the proposed legislation of water policy. We form what we call rural water service providers so that we cluster them then management becomes easier because if you can combine five then you can have all these in place. You can have line patrollers, you can have all these things and pipe attendants. Clustering them for purposes of economics and administration. The next one we have talked about operational cost of boreholes. Some of the boreholes they use high lift pumps that is the consumption of power is so high so one way of mitigating that is what we call solar energy. Renewable energy which the County Government is undertaking step by step. And then the other thing for reducing operational cost is other than diesel we can introduce what we call Kenya Power, national grid. It is relatively cheaper than diesel and it is more efficient. The other thing, because you find that  despite some of the boreholes have very high yield but they act as point source. Expansion of the water supplies so that we have a bigger catchment for revenue.
Interviewer: What do you mean by expansion? Do you mean pipeline or building more resources?
Respondent: We talk about pipeline expansion. As I said some of them they have potential, the water is enough for expansion although some of them the yield is so little they can only act as point sources. Any borehole above 10 m3/hr can be expanded, you can have additional water kiosks. So by so doing you find that you increase the revenue collection base. You increase the revenue potential and then it can sustain itself. But the key things here is renewable energy.
Interviewer: What about the issue of salinity levels, do you have a recommendation to that?
Respondent: There is what we call desalinization but it has never been tested to show that it can be sustained. It has never been tested. We did a pilot project in Kitui West, but the results are still being worked out. That was just recently. We hope it will prove that it can cover the costs. You know that is the only solution and as I have said we have so many boreholes here in Kitui East which are saline, the saline levels are so high that it is not recommended for human consumption. If that one can work then we shall have a breakthrough because we shall convert most of them for domestic use.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The most important one of these ones, I think we have the two key ones. Number one is great skills. We build skills, we train our people or we bring in trained people. That is one of the things because my officers here they are always on the alert and when you go to a project you find that what is a big issue to them is something so minor but you know you find that it is a committee, you are giving somebody on trust to run a project worth millions but now there is nothing you can do. So we normally try to coordinate it from here. So what we say number one if we can have more people trained and number two the other option is if we can be facilitated because now here we operate on the budgets that provision is not with us here. 
Interviewer: So if you bring in trained people to manage the schemes, what will happen?
Respondent: What I had said that if we can cluster them so that now the cluster can manage they are able to offload the financial implications, then it is possible because now if you can bring in line patrollers, you bring in people who can service these boreholes, at the local level. Then it will be cheaper, it will be done without any problem because whenever they have a small problem they normally come here despite the distance, and when they come here sometimes you find that we don't have what they want. We communicate again to the head office. That is what I mean. That chain takes so long, a borehole can stay for three months without operating and when you go find there you find that maybe it is a battery which is not working. So what we are saying is if we can push the services nearer to them. That's what I mean by clustering or we create offices in the wards then we give them qualified personnel. Because here we are only three. Imagine from the Kitui Level 5 hospital up to Tana River. So that is one way. 
The other way we have tried is renewable energy but we a re finding a challenge. Renewable energy is limited to a certain level, it is mostly used where there is a point source, a borehole which is around maximum of 100 but you find that a water supply like this one it needs to pump water 24hrs because the population is so high. So what we are recommending, because some of these things are done without due consideration, like you want to talk of programs like KLIDIP, you find that the component is just put the solar but we recommend that solar comes in line with national grid, it works better that way so that now they are integrated. The surplus energy is steady and it has worked, I saw it working in Musengo during the time of KIWASH. So it has to be taken more seriously so that it can make impact because sometimes when you install the solar system the impact is minimal.
Interviewer: What impact?
Respondent: What I'm saying about impact is because when you put it there it can't operate or decant the water to the farthest end. It can't take water to the farthest storage facility. So it will reduce the operations by certain percentage. So you end up integrating diesel and solar. But if you can use electricity, there is what we call a meter, there is an indicator such that when there is no enough energy from solar, it is supplemented by Kenya Power. That one works so well. So that is the ultimate solution but you know these rural areas you find that we don't have the last mile, they don't have electricity connection in some areas. So we design a way so that we utilize the solar. We pump during day time, then we take up on diesel, we integrate both. By so doing we are reducing the operational cost in a certain way.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Let me talk about Kitui East. We normally go and meet these committees, we sit down and we tour the projects so that when I'm seated here I know what is the problem in Ngomano water project, I know the problem in Koi borehole. So when they come here at least I have an idea and then also you teach them the potential troubleshooting areas.
Interviewer: So you mean one of the interventions you are doing is training the community management committees?
Respondent: We call it on the job training. We do the job together. The other one is we are taking capacity also on governance. Governance is very important because you go to some areas and find that they don't know what is a priority. Somebody will pay yourself allowances and then there is no diesel. We normally take them through a rigorous course. We discuss in open and they are catching up. Nowadays we don't have serious problems. The other one we are doing timely servicing, you don't wait until....we have what we call a job card, when it reaches this time, we remind them they do the service because they always have money only that they don't know the importance of servicing. So that we keep the machines efficient.
Interviewer: Let me take you back, by interventions we don't mean if it's training, you can expound on the components of the training you are doing, but in this question we want to know, other than training on servicing, governance, that is one intervention, training them on these different components. What other interventions, for example like drilling boreholes, do you do such things, those are the kind of interventions we are seeking.
Respondent: I think I had mentioned earlier that out of experience we are finding that boreholes are very costly, there is no guarantee we are going to get enough water, so we are shifting to develop our water services from the available rivers, whether seasonal or permanent. So what we normally do if you find like this town you have an intake sump-well in the nearby river, that one it is guaranteed it will have enough recharge so you can service the population with enough water. We have tried this one, we have tried another one called Kyamatu-Thua water project. We have another one which we have finished recently it is called Thua Ndukuni. We gave it last week to the community. So that is one way of making sure there is enough water and then when we are doing that we invent the solar energy from the beginning. Where there is no electricity we combine it with a standby generator.  That is one way of intervening because the issue here it is as I said we have challenge of ground water potential is not reliable then the other one there is salinity levels so that now we run away from this challenges then we go to the water in the rivers, the water is very clean and reliable.
Interviewer: Any other intervention other than training committees, servicing of the schemes and developing sump-wells. Any other interventions that you are doing in Kitui East?
Respondent: Yeah, we can talk of household roof catchment. Roof water harvesting. It helps because what we campaign for the people is that let them buy as many, when you are building your house, let water harvesting be part of your plans so that now you get...and it takes time if actually now you can have around 30,000L and an average household is around six per household then it can take you to the next season unless you waste the water and it is working, even institutions we take these water tanks to primary and secondary schools and polytechnics. It takes time when half of the dry seasons is served by that water so that we go to the next level where we start running up and down to take water boozers.
Interviewer: So what informed all these interventions?
Respondent: It is experience. Because if I can say clearly, if I am here and it is dry, and it is in October and maybe the County Government did not have a program we call water trucking, you can run away from this office because the principals and head of health centers they come here and you don't have the capacity, what do you do?
Interviewer: So you are also doing water trucking?
Respondent: We do it. So now to move away from that that's when we talked about campaigns for roof catchment.
Interviewer: You are not supplying tanks to the households you are doing campaigns?
Respondent: Households we don't but we normally demonstrate through the institutions.
