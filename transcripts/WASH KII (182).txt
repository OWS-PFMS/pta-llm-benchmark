

1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation / Water service sustainability in your woreda?  Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation / water service sustainability?

 We have serious water problem in our zone. As a means to solve the issue, we made several attempts to meet the intended target through the five years transformation and strategic plan. As a zone, there is shortage of capital goods and we have shortage of budget. Most of the budget that is allocated for water and sanitation sector is   mainly utilized for the staff salary payment. Some of the budget is also used   for procurement. Accordingly, we proceed with our duties on the basis of annual budget which is not sufficient to execute what we have planned. The other issue is awareness problem with our pastoral community. The people don’t have good awareness on the vitality of community’s work, so there is no community participation. Therefore, the waters service coverage is low in the zone due to the aggregate effects of all the aforementioned factors. 

With regard to sanitation sustainability, we use only the government budget that is allocated for maintenance.   There is no sufficient budget to cover all other costs that help to sustain the sustainability. In addition to this, people expect every minor problem of water infrastructure to be fixed by the government office. This is a problem by itself. Community members should be able to fix minor problems by themselves.

Interviewer: What is the reason behind sanitation problem both at individual and community levels? Are there waste dumping/landfill sites? 

In principle, all people should own toilet houses privately at household level, but they don’t actually have. Hand washing basin or pot should also be kept in the toilet, but this is not commonly used here. Schools and health offices don’t have hand washing basins kept outside the toilets. We have done lots of work to change this scenario, but we still have the problem. 



Interviewer: Do you have any other opinion regarding problems related to WASH services?

There is such a problem with regard to outreaching water facilities to all people who live in distant urban and rural areas of the woreda. Even if the rural areas have a good standard of water table, there needs a lot of work ahead to benefit all people and improve the coverage.  

Interviewer: What is the problem that lies behind the budget? 
The budget that is allocated to our zone is not sufficient, so we can’t use the limited fund for all the woredas.  The budget that is allocated to the zone is mainly used for salary, and the remaining budget is used for running different costs. So, we don’t have capital budget to plan and spend on water works. The budget allocation from the region lacks fairness. More than 25% of the budget is already left there. The budget that is allocated is not more than 1 million birr.    So, it is difficult to speak that government’s budget is sufficient for WASH sustainability. There is scarcity in budget allocation or budget flow at Federal or region level.  The existing budget at zone level could not be used beyond salary payment. Out of the total 50 million birr on regional level, the amount of our share should have been like 3.7 million birr; however, we receive only 1.2 million birr. 

With regard to sanitation, there is a serious problem as long as shortage of water supply service is not solved. Latrines are prepared by individuals but they are not properly constructed and managed. Water pans are not used properly to help people wash their hands while going out from the latrines.  Though some enterprises were established to give latrine services to the people, the trend didn’t proceed sustainably.

2. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

 Water work needs continued effort and sufficient Capital.  Establishing water service facility is not an easy task. Since the area is extremely dry, water could not be easily found, so budget should be allocated separately for water facility. Now the government has a new track to allocate at least 65% to 75% of the total budget for capital works and water services. 

So, we need to properly use this capital for water schemes in the future. In fact, the main problem is the shortage of budget which can’t even cover salary of wereda employees. There are about four weredas including North Ari, South Ari, Mali and others that don’t have sufficient budget to cover their salary. So, there is no opportunity to use money from our annual budget and spend on water works. 
For instance, south omo zone water and sanitation office requested some 12-million-birr last year, but it received only 850, 000 birr (less than a million) for water works.

We also need to make the public participate on such activities continuously. With the help of developments groups, it is good if we enable the people develop natural springs and maintain those that have already become non-functional. We also conduct annual forums to encourage NGOs to participate on developments of water, health and education sectors. We need to strengthen this trend and sustain it. For instance, there are some water schemes that have been constructed to serve the pastoralists with the help of NGOs operating in the zone. Previously, we used to give more emphasis to health sector, but we have now shifted our focus from health to water services. The other solution is to improve our information recording and storing system since we have bad information recording trends. 

Interviewer: what about hygiene and sanitation? What do you recommend to bring positive changes?

Since sanitation issue is closer to women, there are certain things that we do with women and children. In the different stages/workshops and meetings/ that we hold, we need to flag water and sanitation issues and discuss to upgrade people’s awareness. If we keep on strengthening what has already been started on personal and community hygiene, there will be a hope to bring change. Health office should take the lions share to give emphasis on the issues and challenges of sanitation. 

Regarding sanitation, there are eighteen agendas that have been incorporated in the existing sanitation tasks. If we collaborate with health extension workers by giving them trainings, we can promote and improve sanitation services. We are now running two projects that are directly related to sanitation service sustainability. There has been a new model of plastic sanitary material that can be used in the toilet. We hope that this can make a difference. 

 With regard to water service, the government is still doing what it has to do. There were some attempts of mobilizing the community to do smaller water schemes some three or two years back. We need to follow this system again. We should mobilize the different water committees to get them engaged in small scale water scheme constructions that can be done manually with human labor. This will have a positive impact on the sustainability of water service. Water sector office has shelved studies that can be used as inputs for project implementers such as the university in collaboration with finance and development office. Since the university has budget for community service, it can even use this shelved data for project implementation. These shelved data are good indicators of the water service gap, responsible sectors and NGOs should see the study results that can be found in water and sanitation office. 

3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

The first thing to do is mobilizing the community members to participate on water projects. Developing sense of ownership for water schemes is crucial. If this is done, the people will adopt good sanitation habits and sanitation problem will be minimized. If people are aware of sanitation, they will have the interest to look for fund by themselves and invest on small water scheme projects. The second solution is to have more budget from the government and spend on water projects. Thirdly, charity organizations should be encouraged to participate on water projects. IRC should plan a new water project and implement in the area. It needs to revise what has already been done, and plan something better for the future. We will also provide information as an input that shows the gap or actual challenges in water works.
