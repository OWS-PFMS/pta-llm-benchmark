1) Interviewer: My first question will be, in this town what are the main challenges water service sustainability? 
Response: Well, in my opinion, the main challenge is supplying. When I say supply, it could be a material supplying or staff or getting access. They are very limited. For example, our access to water resources is non-existing. The city administration has never planned on building a capacity to use groundwater resources. Second, there is no joint plan between Water Authority and City Administration. They are not coordinating well to work together and make goals. 
Interviewer: So those two sectors don't work together?
Response: Yes, we don't have a joint task force. They simply call us when they face a problem. Like most of the time, they call us when it's winter and have either road blockage or machine failure or something like that. I suggest that we should have a joint task force who can oversee what kinds of challenge might come and prevent it from happening.
 
Interviewer: Okay, do you want to add more challenges in regards to water sustainability?

Response: I would like to add about the power supply challenges. We usually have power shortages and activities like pumping water from a ground hole cannot happen without electricity. Also to make the town more modern we should follow better ways of installing pipelines. Currently, we are installing them in a traditional way that some pipelines are not safe at all. They are vulnerable to breaks when we build roads. The pipelines are below standard. In addition to that, they are supposed to be installed below 80 cm or like that, but we are not putting them there. 


2) Okay, you have brought up major challenges. So my next question will be how to solve these challenges. let's see them one by one. You have mentioned about limited supply to capacity and materials. How do you think we can solve this issue?

Response: If we focus on these problems, I think we can solve our problems and supply sustainable water service to the people. 

Interviewer: Yes, but my question is how do we focus on these issues?

Response: [05:00] So to solve the supply issue, we should work together and I have already mentioned that as a second challenge. If we can plan together, if we know where water pumps are going to be installed ahead we can also plan to offer standard materials on time. If we are planning together, the information we have will be as updated as they have and there will be no miscommunications. We can also share pieces of knowledge by preparing training not only with Water Authority but with other sectors too. So if one sector can learn from the other, I think the supply and capacity building challenge will be resolved.

Interviewer: Okay, how about the joint planning issue?

Response: Let's say as a City Administration, our office plans to build few kilometers s of pipelines per year and we do not work as planned. Water Authority also plans and they don't fulfill their plan. But if we work together from identifying the part of the town with water shortage to designing and building the pipeline together real change might come to the town.

Interviewer: Okay, what about the power shortage issue? How can it be solved?

Response: I think the main problem for the power outage is the carrier lines are very old and wind, trees, and rain can disconnect them easily. So changing those lines can help. And also the power distribution center must be well-organized on distributing power. Because there are parts of the town with industrial areas and there are residential estates on the other side. That will have an effect on our pumping machine as it needs boosted power to work effectively.

Interviewer: You have said something about the standards of the pipelines. How do you suggest to solve that issue? Who can take care of it?

Response: Well pipelines must go under quality control starting from the factory where they are built. Also, the pipelines should be compatible with the water force on that line...

Interviewer: Who is responsible for that? Who can take care of that challenge?

Response: Well, the sector purchasing the pipelines and the sector who is installing them should make sure that the pipelines are to the least standard and quality needed. And the state should check up on factories that are manufacturing pipelines to have the minimum standards required by doing a follow-up. [10:00]


3) Response: My third question is of all those challenges you have mentioned plus their solutions, which one should be given priority? You have told me the solutions. Now I am asking which one of these solutions is the most important to that water service sustainability challenge.

Response: Well I think we should prioritize coordination and planning.

Interviewer: So what kind of changes shall we see if coordination and planning are implemented?

Response: When we have coordination, we will be able to get information about public demand and share within each other. Those places without power and water will get those services immediately. Now the plan will be designed with the same coordination and everyone will be aware of it. So everyone will take part and the public demand will be addressed in the proper manner.

Interviewer: So are you saying coordination and planning is the main solution to those challenges?

Response: Yes, I think like that.

Interviewer: And we will see changes?

Response: Yes.


4) Interviewer: My fourth question is on the current situation, do you think coordination between sectors is functional and effective? And what should be improved?

Response: I think I have answered this question by mentioning that they should have coordination and plan together...

Interviewer: Yes, but I want to know if this coordination you are talking about is there currently.

Response: I can't say it's there because I don't see it everywhere. For example, when Road Authority finishes building a road after a while Water Authority or Electric power Authority will come and dig that road without asking any permission. Each sector will have extra cost due to not communicating with each other. But if they plan together and rank projects they can work together and save extra cost. You know when we build a road and after a while, you dig it up to put your materials, you will not put it back in the same way you got it in the beginning. So the quality of the road decreases and that lead to maintenance cost. 


5) Interviewer: Okay I want you to answer it like in details. Which challenges you mentioned earlier can be solved by this coordination issue?

Response: When there is coordination, we can solve most public challenges.

Interviewer: Like?

Response: Like when we find a part of town which needs water service, the Road Authority will also take part and Electricity power guys will supply the power needed to do the project immediately. [15:00] You can see that there will be no misunderstanding or cables or pipelines damages and so on...

Interviewer: So are you saying that on the current situation there is no coordination at all? 

Response: In my experience its close to zero. I have seen Road Authority guys drop by our office asking us to build a road in a part of the town one or two days. Imagine we do not have a plan for such road and I don't even think they do have too. It's better to say there is no coordination at all.

Interviewer: What about NGO's? Do they not coordinate too?

Response: I have never met an NGO based on water service in my life.

Interviewer: Why is that there are no NGO based on water service in Woliso? I see many of them in other towns.

Response: I think in our administration there is no this kind of office that invites NGOs to come to our town. They don't even plan on it.

Interviewer: When you say they, who do you mean? 

Response: I think the City Administration should plan and be a leader in this idea because it's everyone's concern and at the end of it we all work for the City Administration. 

Interviewer: Okay.  

[end time]
