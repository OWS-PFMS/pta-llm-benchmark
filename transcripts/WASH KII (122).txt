
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Water and Sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Potable water shortage in this woreda is the first challenge. Naturally, there is shortage of underground water in our wereda. The second problem is the life style of the society in general. Since the people are pastoralists, they move here and there looking for water and pasture for their cattle. This is creating a burden on our WASH service sustainability as a program.
Interviewer: How does the life style of the people affect the sustainability? 
WASH program affects all the sectors in one way or another. For example, we teach the society about how to construct toilet and use it safely. But if the people move from place to place, you can’t do the work on permanent basis. They may not use the toilet that is constructed and leave to some other places the next time. So, it creates an impact to outreach our program to all the residents. 
Interviewer: You said that there is shortage of water. How does this affect the sustainability of the WASH program? 
It is difficult to teach about and apply the activities of sanitation when there is shortage of water. Water and sanitation are highly interrelated. If there is no sufficient water, you can’t have sustainable service of WASH.



2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
The key sectors- Water, Health, Education, Urban administration, Women and Children affairs offices need to plan and execute their duties together. Since women are highly linked to water services, women and children affairs office is primarily responsible to engage on WASH. 
Interviewer: What is the relevance of planning and working together?
In our regular meetings on WASH programs, different sectors give more attention to their own main task rather than considering the importance of working together on water and sanitation issues. This has to be changed as there is nothing relevant we can do before properly addressing sanitation and hygiene problems. This can be achieved through coordinated plan and action. 
Interviewer: What do you think is the solution to outreach WASH services to those pastoralists?
The best solution is to setup a one-use package where pastoralists can get water, health, education and others services in one place. This will be possible if the scattered villages are gathered in to clustered villages. This solution has been commonly suggested in the different meetings we had so far, but there is a problem to start it from the scratch. 
It is very clear to say that the main challenge in this area for unsustainable WASH service is the scattered way of living. Budget is regularly allocated from federal government assuming that one-health program is being implemented, but it is not properly implemented yet. There is no a system of accountability for its failure. The failure of the package has its own negative impact on education, health and all other inter related services. Had this package been implemented properly, we would have developed a well-organized settlement in this woreda. That is the main problem. 
There is no other solution other than changing the pattern of settlement for the majority of the people. This is the final solution proposed by the federal government and is being supported in budget wise but due to lack of commitment from the local government, we are facing problems related to sanitation and hygiene. It was the responsibility of every sector but you can see that health and water are becoming the main actors. 

Interviewer: What should be done to strengthen the level of coordination you have now? 
As a focal of WASH program, we are the main actors but we can’t do it without coordination. Decision makers, high ranking officials, need to be coordinated and responsible for the program. When they are strong, we will be strong; and when they become weaker, we will also be weaker.
Instead of planning the same thing every year, it will be good to draw out the coverage of the services from the previous year and design to do something new for the next year. The experts do their work with some new initiatives upon the plan that is provided to them. But the administrative personnel should be able to evaluate the implementation process properly. There is a huge gap in this regard, and that is the main problem. On the other hand, budget allocation should be in accordance with the activities that have been accomplished. Those activities that are properly accomplished should be taken as the major source of information for upcoming plan. Otherwise, experts do the same plan every year redundantly without achieving something new.
If what you achieve is zero, you won’t plan again to repeat the same scenario. So, you have to work hard to gain something new when you plan a program. When we plan to do something based on our budget capacity, we need to assess our task continuously. If we don’t meet the target of what we have already planned, there is no doubt for our failure in the next plan. Therefore, we need to have monitoring and evaluation system in general.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which one is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
All the respective sectors need should act in a coordinated way. That is the best solution. 
Interviewer: Assuming that all the sectors work in a coordinated way, what would happen?
We can have all the basic facilities addressed to the society. We will also make sure that one health package is properly implemented.  Again, when pastoralists get the various services all in one, the sustainability of WASH program will become true.


Interviewer: Can you explain the difference between one-health package and one WASH program?
Our wereda is not a beneficiary of one WASH program at all. But I think the notion of one WASH is a sort of using one budget, one input, and one plan altogether. All the NGOs that come to an area will be doing their programs in a coordinative way. I think that is the idea of ONE WASH though it doesn’t exist in our wereda.
Interviewer: Can you tell me the relevance/ framework of one-health package?
One-health package is meant collecting scattered villages into one. If people get pure water, they will be healthy. If they get awareness about sanitation and hygiene, they will take care of their health. When the people live together in one place, it will create an opportunity for the government to implement any of the programs easily. The implementation will also be easier. If the people gain something beneficial from such programs, they won’t leave their village and go somewhere else, too. Hence, the sustainability of one-health package will be met.
