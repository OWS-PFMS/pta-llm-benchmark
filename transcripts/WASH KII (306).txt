Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: To start with I will just say that the major challenge is the issue to do with the community themselves because let’s take an example, when we do projects, we commission them to the community and the county government of Kitui. The overall mandate to run the projects is given to the committee members being supported by the other local members of the project but you find that there is mismanagement- there is actually no transparency and accountability on how the money they get from water sale is used, they misuse the money. The money is collected from water users but is not used for the intended purpose of sustaining the project. To add to this issue of mismanagement, we often find that the committee members do not bank the money collected and at some point, a treasurer or just one of the committee members might get a job elsewhere and they run away with the project money. So, there is no clear procedure for transition of the committee members.
There is also negligence on the part of the committee members in terms of implementing what they are trained on in regards to running the facilities, financial management and keeping records. Their minds seem to be set on mismanaging the money they collect. Also, on the issue of technical knowhow, for some projects, additional or broader expertise is required for the operation and maintenance of the facilities. So, no matter how much we train them, there will sometimes be need for a very qualified technician to handle some of the issues especially for some big schemes.
There is also the challenge of limited human resources or personnel on the side of the county. We work hand in hand with the county government and sometimes they are unable to do monitoring or follow ups of the projects and they come to us and they refer to the projects as being ours yet we have handed over. 
On the side of the communities, they over rely on the organizations who install facilities and they actually do not own the projects or facilities. If they do not own it, they do not feel responsible for taking care of it and with each breakdown, they come to us to repair it and you see, in this way, a project cannot be sustainable.
There is also the limited funding and so we are not in a position to implement large projects that can serve large populations and also to an extent have all components of WASH, that is sanitation and hygiene. So, you find that some facilities cannot sustain communities throughout the year.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: Regarding the training, I would recommend that we don’t get tired of training the communities. We need to keep training them. I am sorry to say that we should not train youth any more. You know at some point the youth will leave and leave all the elderly people behind, in charge of the facilities. I think we should focus more on training women because with women they are not likely to leave the place but with men and the youth, there is a risk of them leaving. This is according to my opinion. Women are very embedded or anchored in their communities and they do not leave. We should focus on women. I do not mean that all people to be trained should be women, but a great number should be women.
We may also be required to ensure that there are legal instruments in place such as policies and laws to deal with misappropriation of funds. You know, the community has been assuming that since most of the projects are donor funded, then no one can question or legally charge them once they misappropriate funds.
I talked about limited funding and how it results to small projects and to not being able to implement complete facilities. Well, we can try and involve the community; the community can contribute in the form of providing local materials, not necessarily providing money. This will also contribute to enhanced ownership and sustainability. 
I think that to address the issue of over reliance, during the first few stages of the interventions, we need to think about drafting a memorandum of understanding with the community and have each party sign the memorandum and strictly adhered to. This will reduce over reliance and they will know that after final handover, they are responsible for maintenance and repair and they will not keep asking for help from us or the county. This will also form a smart element of our exit strategy.
Interviewer: You have mentioned something about people not following or rather applying the training or the content of what they were trained on and so they end up mismanaging the facilities. How can this be dealt with or does it go back to the continuous training that you are talking about, but then if you still train them continuously and you do proper selection of the trainees does it guarantee that they will follow what they have been trained on? Does it guarantee that they will stick to what they have been trained on or how can it be dealt with?
Respondent: I think it can be handled through the design of the exit strategy. The strategy can slot or assign some budget on follow ups on the trainees. We can be able to follow up on the ground whether they are applying the knowledge and skill they were trained on. When we have the money for this, it can be handled.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if this solution were to occur
Respondent: The development of legal instruments to deal with mismanagement of proceeds from the sale of water. When you seriously think about sustainability, why projects fail after being handed over to communities, you realize that it is because of mismanagement of funds. The committees get good amounts of money and in fact they can even establish other projects but the money is not managed properly and the projects fail. You see, if the funds are managed well, schemes can be able to even do pipeline extensions and establish more water points such as through kiosks and other water points. This would increase water coverage and access to the population. They would also have enough money to do repairs and this would mean that water services would be reliable. The community can also be able to do farming with the water and earn income and get good and healthy food from their farms and this will improve their health. If the water is running in the systems throughout, I also believe that this will also improve the hygiene standards and health of the community.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: First of all, in terms of deciding which interventions to implement, we review the County assessment report which details different parts the county and the way in which they are affected in regards to water challenges. Then at our own level through monitoring and evaluation we conduct surveys to ascertain whether the data from the county is giving us the actual picture. The data from the county is collected at the community level. After reviewing this, we also go to the ground and we get information from the community then we identify and decide on the intervention areas.
In terms of infrastructure, we drill boreholes, construct earth dams, subsurface dams, pipeline extensions, hand dug wells and sand dams. We have been doing these due to the frequent droughts experienced in the county and also because of the failed rains.
We also do awareness creation on hygiene and sanitation and this was about by identification of some parts of the county where water borne diseases in schools and in some communities kept recurring and this led us to identify the need. We are also providing water treatment tablets to some communities for household water treatment to reduce the risk of the water borne diseases.
