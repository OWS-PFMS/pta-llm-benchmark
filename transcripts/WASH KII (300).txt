Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent:  Most of the challenges are with the communities. The management committees do not bank the money they collect after selling water and they don’t want to be followed up by the county officers who ask about revenue collected. The committees do not want us to know how much money they have collected. They keep that information to themselves and they also do not update us on their monthly operations for instance – how much they have collected; how much they have spent and the balance. They are mismanaging the funds because when they have even a small breakdown, that time is the time they come to us but before they have a breakdown when they are just collecting the money, when the system is running, they don’t want to see us at all. They also do not want to see the village administrators as well because we are involving them in the running of the schemes where we have them as signatories. They have chased them away. What I have started doing in my sub-county is that I have made it mandatory for the village administrators to be signatories in order for the committees not to mismanage the funds. This mismanagement creates a situation where the schemes cannot do any repairs, whether major or minor. They also begin relying on support from other parties and this is not sustainable.
Another challenge is that they do not do minor repairs to the facilities. They neglect them until it reaches a point where the systems are totally broken down and they can no longer provide service to the community. The other challenge is that some committees pump water to individual connections and they bypass the water kiosks where community members fetch water from. Th community members therefore have to buy water from people with individual connections and it is usually at a higher cost that what it would cost them to buy water from the communal kiosks. You find that the individuals will sell to the community water at 5 shillings per 20 litre jerrican whereas at the kiosk it is usually sold at 3 shillings. So, the community is forced to buy from the individuals since there is nowhere else to buy water from. 
The other challenge is just political interference. You find that the Members of the County Assembly (MCAs) are interfering with the management committees. Sometimes they ask for money too be withdrawn from the schemes’ account and often it is not meant to support the project in any way. Again, the MCAs incite the management committees and tell them not to interact with us – the county officers. They tell the committees not to meet with us unless an MCA is present. This is affecting our work in the schemes and is also misleading the committees, because the MCAs are not providing good leadership to them. They are just misusing them. If the committees agree to withdraw money and give to the MCAs, they will not have any money to do repairs or support their facilities and when the facilities break down, the MCAs do not support in doing repairs. 
There is another challenge here of meter readings in the schemes. The management takes too long to take the meter readings from the individual connections and to bill them Sometimes you find that they can even skip two months without billing the individual connections. This creates a scenario where the schemes do not collect all of their revenue and without the revenues, they cannot run or sustain their facilities adequately.
There is also a challenge in terms of the committee not knowing their roles. In some schemes you find that the chairman is the treasurer, he is the one collecting money from the kiosks, he is the pump attendant and he is also the line patroller. The other committee members are not given any duty at all. The chairman dominates everything and, in some cases, you find that the whole committee is not involved in meetings. It is only the three executive members who meet. The other committee members may not be called for meetings for even up to a year. If the executive dominates like this, misappropriation of funds and poor governance of the scheme becomes a real challenge.

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: The one on MCAs can be handled by coming to an agreement with them, such that they are told to concentrate on their roles and work and to leave the handling of water issues to us as sub-county water officers. We can agree that we will be briefing them on what is going on regarding the water sector – this would be very fair.
The other solution is to restrict people with individual connections to only use their water for domestic purposes only. They should not be allowed to sell water to anyone. By doing this, the committees will have no option but to pump water to the kiosks. This is something we have begun pushing for. 
In the case of communities not being able to do minor repairs, sometimes you find that the issue is finance related or skill related – they may not have enough money to do the minor repairs, or maybe they lack the skill to do the repairs. For this challenge, maybe we can link them up with service providers, that is maintenance service providers though we have not started in our area, but there are some projects – water supplies that have started, because sometimes, even when a pump is found to be faulty, they call some people who do diagnosis and tell them what the problem is. In some of the well managed schemes, at times they are able to purchase equipment needed and have repairs done.
We have noticed that at times the management committees delay to pay their kiosk attendants. So, the kiosk attendants pay themselves first before they give the money collected to the committees, then the committees do not bank the money. We should have rules that require all management committees to have active bank accounts and we should be updated on how the accounts are being managed.
In order to be up to date with the scheme operations, what we have started to do is that we have told them that whenever they are having any meetings they should call the village administrators so that they can attend the meeting. This is because we do not have adequate staff on the ground, so the village administrators can support us. We have also given them a monitoring form which they are expected to be filling every month and submitting to our office. Through these two ways, we will be able to constantly know what is happening in the schemes and we can also identify problems and intervene before things get worse.
I also talked about taking meter readings and billing being a challenge. Maybe in this case the schemes should employ a scheme manager so that he can be taking all the meter readings and billing in good time. The manager can also be following up on unpaid bills to ensure that the schemes have money to use for their operations. 
To deal with the lack of awareness on roles, capacity building is important, but I think the best solution would be to have a project manager who would manage the entire project and report to the committee. The committee can just provide oversight for the management of the scheme. 

Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The one for billing - early meter reading and billing. In this way we can know maybe how much has been banked and how much revenue is collected. Therefore, they will have some money to do the repairs, if they are minor or big ones and this will enable continuous service provision.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: We are doing there are some pipeline extensions, construction of earth dams, repair of boreholes, replacement of pumps and other spares. We are also training committees on schemes management in partnership with an NGO working in the sub-county. You see, our interventions are influenced by county plans and feedback from the public participation process which is conducted during the annual budgeting process. The public tells us what their needs are and we factor that in the annual budgets and our workplans.
