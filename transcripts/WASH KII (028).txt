Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: In my view the major challenge is that the personnel that are manning most of the water supply schemes may be empowered, they may be knowledgeable, they may be even very enthusiastic to perform their functions but sometimes other competing interests come in and you find that for example their focus is not bearing fruit. Let me give an example, you find there is a rural scheme somewhere or even an urban scheme where there is water available which is supposed to be treated, which is supposed to be pumped and then distributed to the communities, but anytime there is a breakdown the repair of the breakdowns are not done on time, the revenue is misappropriated, even the personnel who are engaged some of them are not professional so the keenness is lacking. So Kenya Water Institute being a training institution we have really empathized with some of these schemes and we feel the beginning should be hiring people who are very very keen on running these systems and then these people should be held to account so that if they are not delivering they are disengaged from the system. 
So sustainability is a problem because the people manning these systems some of them feel that they are there because of some godfathers or political patronage so they are not very keen to deliver. We have had some schemes which are privately run which are operating very well. But unfortunately there are few of those. They are able to sustain themselves, they are collecting revenue, and they are using the revenue well. But very few of those are within the county. Very few. And I think way forward is to have those ones which are doing well, behaving as the marking schemes to be bench marked with and for that to be done, there has to be goodwill right from the top. So that everybody now behaves the way those ones are behaving but they are very few. There are very few such.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Part of the solutions of course is to hire the right people, train them, although I want to emphasize, most of the problems are not due to lack of training. It's maybe issue of attitude or having that complacency. Hire the right people, train them well, and then hold them to account. This aspect of somebody feeling once I've got a job I've got it for life, till I retire, permanent and pensionable, should not be the case. People should be signing contracts, they should be signing performance contracts and if they don't deliver at whichever level they are released. The area we are in some people believe that we have a challenge of scarcity of water. But even the little which is there, the way it's managed leaves a lot to be desired. So in my opinion, it's having people having the right attitude towards these schemes, how they run them.
Interviewer: What else?
Respondent: What else, apart from that?
Interviewer: Yes.
Respondent: Of course there is also the support. We need more support from the County Government, from the National Government, from donors, and this support should also be followed through to ensure that for example if it is a system which has been installed in place, it is working the way it was designed to do. So more support can be handy, we need to develop, exploit more sources of water. Every year in this place it rains a lot but this water is let to run to the Indian Ocean. If we had more reservoirs catching this water and treat it can be of more benefit to the communities. So that's another solution that should be explored.
Interviewer: You've mentioned about support from the County and National Government, what kind of support is that?
Respondent: The support can come in terms of for example drilling boreholes, it can come in terms of some the schemes which are not self-sustaining, injecting some funds so as for example to pay wages or to enable them undertake repairs, it can also come in terms of the training itself and also because water is a devolved function, any external actor coming into the county should be of course facilitated through the county government so even that seeking these external partners the county should really be on the forefront of identifying them.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: If money was not the issue, the most important for me is how to harvest more water from the region. That means we construct more dams, more reservoirs, then these dams we do treatment, and distribute water to more communities. This will enable us to access more water because now this water is easier to treat and avail to the communities downstream without pumping. Because most of the schemes that are now not self-sustaining, they are unable to sustain themselves because for example the energy needed for pumping the costs are very high. But if we were to do more dams and then distribute water downstream, there will be more irrigation, there will be more water supply to the communities, there will be less waterborne diseases, the benefit will be much much better and the impact will be higher than for example drilling more boreholes, or distributing more water from Masinga Dam. So to me that is the most important. It is on top of the list. Of course still I'd appreciate that to do that the investment is quite high. But I believe that is the most critical one.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent:  KEWI (Kenya Water Institute) Kitui campus are based in the county specifically because we want to identify the problems in the county as far as water supply is concerned and we appreciate that the area is semi-arid that even the water for example that is coming to Kitui town has to be pumped from another county so many kilometers away. So KEWI is involved in the training of students who will be able to undertake all the water processes, that's irrigation, treatment of water distribution, drilling, all those interventions. And apart from that, we also now tailor make our courses. Those short courses that now address the challenges in industry. Those who are already working in the sector and have some gaps. Both technical gaps or management issues or issues of customer care; we are able to tailor make courses that are very specific to the needs of the workers in the industry. 

Then on top of that we do now consultancy, we do research which is very targeted in the problems that the communities and the players in this sector are facing. And because we are in Kitui we are very targeted towards the challenges in Kitui, the challenges in Makueni and Machakos counties. Those are our target areas and we feel we are playing our role and we will continue to play an even bigger role as we move forward.
Interviewer: Any other?
Respondent: We also interact a lot with County Government because we are part of the central government but water being a devolved function as i said earlier it means that we have to localize our interventions. So our working is very closely with the local government. We are involved even when for example they are working on their policy documents, issues to do with the Water Act for example, so we feel that we need each other. We are intertwined in the way we co-exist and both of us we appreciate each other. So that's another intervention that we interact with them and they also interact with us for the common good of the community.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: That's very good. First of all it gives an avenue to get to know who are the other players in the sector. It's a wonderful opportunity for us because if we didn't attend those forums, this is very very critical because we get to know what the other actors are, what they do, and how we can co-exist, how we can chip in and how they can also chip in to KEWI operations. So that one has been a big help, and then it has also given us an opportunity even to attach our students to these organizations. Because when we release students to industry, it is critical that these students get to learn some skills from industry from people who are already having some activities in the area. So it makes our attachment avenues easier and when we are also following up to see how much they have learnt, that rapport, that inter-connectivity that we have with them is of a big help to us.
We also like to imagine that when those who need our support in terms of training, it's easier because we have already met, we know what they do, they know what we do and it's easier that way. So those are the most of the positives that we have got from those WASH Forums.
Interviewer: Any other?
Respondent: They're many, in terms of even when we get to present and we get the encouragement or criticism, which areas we need to improve, that is something we can't take for granted. Also on the employment opportunities both for our staff and our students. We find that a lot of our staff, a lot of our students, there is a lot of back and forth movement. For example, the part-time lecturers that we have had in KEWI Kitui campus in the last four three years, we have had around more than 10 moving over to the County Governments. We lose as KEWI, but we think that for them to take them from KEWI it means that we are training them well. They appreciate the kind of lecturers we are able to attract and they know that these are guys that when they go to County Governments they deliver. Apart from County Governments also some of the players in the WASH Forums are also taking our lecturers, some on part-time basis others on full time engagements and we are also tapping in from their pull of trainers like when we have short courses on pump selection, we get some of our people coming from Davis & Shirtliff for example. Davis & Shirtliff we've met in those forums. And then when we are having lunch and a cup of tea, we ask them, "By the way you are an expert in this area, why don't you come and share with our students?" And they are very willing to come at no extra cost most of the time.
Interviewer: So you mean there is knowledge sharing in the WASH Forum?
Respondent: There is a lot of knowledge sharing and some of it ware not paying for. It's because we have had that rapport when we met in those forums which is very good for us and for them i believe also. 
Interviewer: Anything else that you participating in the WASH Forum, how else is it influencing your KEWI interventions?
Respondent: It does influence for example periodically, after every so three four years we do a curriculum review and when we do that we go back to industry and ask them, "What else do want us to add to our curriculum, how do you want us to deliver on the curriculum?" So a lot of very positive feedback comes up from these engagements and because they know us and they are not afraid to speak up, it has made us as KEWI better because we know these are trusted partners in the sector and what they tell us is critical because if we don't consider, take positively what they tell us, very soon they'll be taking their employees from elsewhere so we have to continuously align our training programs to what these industry players are saying.  And because we always continuously do that, they continuously give us the positive feedback and because of that I believe that we are continuously able to provide the kind of graduates they need. So that's one area that we have become better because of those engagements and we hope they'll continue like that.
Interviewer: Anything you'd like to add before we close this part one?
Respondent: I must say because I've worked in a number of campuses in KEWI, I find Kitui County unique. 
Interviewer: You're saying Kitui is unique in what sense?
Respondent: In that there is very active collaboration between the players in the water sector. Nairobi campus there is no such thing. We are just the campus and the Ministry and the County Government but the players, there is rarely where all the players in the sector meet and discuss the way we discuss here in Kitui. So in that way I wish all counties, I'm not aware about maybe the other counties, Machakos etc. but where KEWI is I wish those counties will also benchmark with Kitui because the others are not doing this. They are not doing it the way we are doing it here and I believe this is something we should be encouraged in all the other area. because when we are in the water sector,  whether you are the central government, County Government, training institutions, NGO's  I don't believe we are competing because all of us are serving the same purpose. We are complementing each other. So if you find that there is a role you are playing and somebody else is playing a role which is similar to yours then because everybody is already served, you are not competing. So this model, the WASH Forums, the engagements, should be replicated in more places in the counties. So I like the way it's done in Kitui County.
