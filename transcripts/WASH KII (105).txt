
1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability in your town? 
Respondent: First and for most, there is lack of institutional coordination. Stakeholders that are involved in the WASH activities have different levels of commitment and efficiency. So, it is critical to have standardized way of integration among these stakeholders. But, practically lack of inter-sectorial collaboration is one of the problems we have been facing for long. Because there are many duty actors in the sanitation sector and it is not something that you leave it to the health or other sector. This is the responsibility of all, starting from households to the biggest institution. Therefore, there should be a strong collaborative network that facilitates the activities and plans of these many stakeholders. However, in my opinion, coordination and collaboration is really poor.
Second is lack of awareness among the community members. Because of lack of awareness there has been continuous problem related to communal health, including widespread of communicable disease among children and mothers, hygiene and sanitation. 
There is also lack of capacity. When we say by capacity, it is to mean that we have lack of resource, technical skill, man power. I believe it requires capacity building at government and institutional levels. I think there are skill gaps in these levels. Also, resource gaps in terms of finance and man power are the main problems. There are many more problems but I think those issues I mentioned earlier are the main ones. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: For the institutional coordination, it requires a facilitator or overseeing body. There should be responsible institute/s who is dedicated to facilitate the coordination of different stakeholders, mainly including from health office or water utility office or may be in coalition. Ideally, there is such a coordination structure in the paper but they are not functioning well. Therefore, it requires establishment of a firm stand and responsible group/s to oversee plans and implementations of WASH stakeholders. 

Interviewer: The water utility office has been coordinating such efforts, before but as you said there is no a substantial change, what should be done? 
Respondent: That is right, when I say responsibility it should be linked with accountability, not just responsibility only. Only then we will implement what we planned and we will be more action oriented. In the absence of such a binding system and accountability in the mere presence of the structure it will be difficult to think about sustainability of the services. There should be alignment of responsibility, accountability, and obligation to act. If we implement these three together in the system, the plan will be achieved easily. 
As you have said, we can have the system but we fail to turn it to action. This is because there is no ground to oblige the implementers. Therefore, we should be able to improve on these if we want to make a difference. 
Second is awareness. It is similar to the above. There might be a minimum awareness level currently needed from the community members; but it seems that we can’t see practical changes coming out of the awareness program. On the other hand, the community resists any of the changes coming from the local administrators, even if there is enough knowledge. For this fact, we should work out hard not only to create awareness but also to see practical changes and to follow on these achievements. The community members should be assigned with responsibilities and they should also be able to teach other members of the community. This process will gradually bring communal and town level changes. 
Third is about resource and capacity. Government, NGOs, and partners should first identify the existing resource gap. Assessments should be conducted periodically to spot capacity gaps. Once the gaps are identified, recommendations and action points should be made to concerned bodies about the solutions. Then, there should be a follow up to cross check whether the recommendations are implemented or not. If we act accordingly, we can sustainably solve sanitation problems at the town level. Ultimately, the ownership of the issues is shared between the government and the community. In order to develop this sense of ownership, strong integrated and coordinated efforts are required.  

Interviewer: Before I jump to the final question, regarding the capacity building issue I heard repeatedly that the assessment for the landfill has been completed. And it required more than ETB 200 million for its construction, but there is still financial gap. What do you think should be the solution for this issue? 
Respondent: This requires fundraising. Some have already supported us. What we are lacking now is the share from the government side. The government should contribute its share. The community should also donate starting from individuals to large companies and hotels in the town. There are large companies in the town which need a locally organized fundraising system by organizing a campaign and explaining the extent of the problem. Since the problem is real and when reasons are justified properly by creating continuous communication with them, people will not resist to contribute money to solve this issue. I have seen this from my personal experience. If the local fund is organized and aligned with the foreign aid we obtain, we can make a difference. This requires frequent discussion with the institutions and community members through different channels in a regular manner. It might take time to collect the amount of money needed, yet it is possible to collect certain percentage of it annually. 
3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I do not have much information on the current situation so, I cannot prioritize the solutions. However, I believe the town has massive problems related to waste collection. Despite the efforts, there is extensive sanitation problem in the town. So, one problem is solid and liquid waste management. Second, the town has not only been urbanized but also industrialized releasing wastes from different sources. If we do not start to handle these issues now, it will get worse in the future. Therefore, waste disposal sites should be built shortly. Besides, youth and other community members working on WASH should be empowered. Strong linkage should be created among these actors. 
Interviewer: If this solution is set in place, what would happen?
Respondent: A clean and beautiful environment will be created in the town. The town will be a suitable place to live. It will also be convenient for recreational and touring purposes. Besides, job opportunities will be created. Many things come alongside such developments. Several businesses such as cafeteria, library can be opened. 
Moreover, the communicable diseases that are affecting residents of the city can also be eradicated. These goals can easily be achieved. The revenue that would be collected from the town’s aesthetic value, attractiveness, beauty, and tourism will contribute to the development of the town to a certain extent.
