1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda?
Interviewer: What do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your Woreda?
Response: In General in our city, we have a shortage of water coverage for our society, because the population of the city has become very large. Currently, our plan is to cover all demands. Currently, we have a big shortage of water coverage addressed to the needs of the community. In our plan we aimed to distribute water for the entire community, because everyone must have water access. Currently, we have a huge gap of distribution. For example, in my village we only get water 3-4 days per week.
Interviewer: As a city, why does this shortage happen? What is the main reason for this? What is the challenge? Is it a problem of shortage or of service? Is the problem growth the problem?
Response: Now I can only see a problem of distribution shortage. It is not a problem of quality.
Interviewer: Why does this shortage happen? Why not covered 100%?
Response: In my opinion, it might be a balancing problem. For example, we have ground water. Our problem is we have a shortage of budget, and the other problem is that we can’t find donors. These are our problems.
Interviewer: What is the main problem? Is it finding donors or the budget?

Response: In our city, there aren’t any NGOs that work on water-based issues.

Interviewer: Why don’t NGOs come here? Why don’t they work here, as in others?
Response: In my opinion, the problem is not that we can’t find donors. Currently, for example there are a lot of NGOs who work in the WASH sector. The problem is we have not invited them to cover the shortage.

Interviewer: Yes, I was asking about the WASH sector. Actually, WASH is working on (concerns) sanitation and hygiene and so on. What are you doing about these aspects of WASH?

Response: What I was saying is that we don’t give priority to these issues.

Interview: Explain what you mean by “not giving priority.”

Response: What I mean is we are not treating these issues as a big problem. We are not aware of the problems inherent in not focusing on hygiene and sanitation. If we give attention to these issues, and if we talk to NGOs, we can solve the problem easily.

Interviewer: Is it a problem of human resources, development and community?

Response: We don’t have any problem regarding human resources, because water and energy offices have enough resources. All the others like the Health bureau have enough as well,  regarding resources. What I want to say is that we should consider the community as a resource.

[TIME STAMP: 5 minutes]
Interviewer: Is the community aware of this, and do they have a contribution?

Response: For community awareness, we have done a lot of trainings with city health extension workers. Even if we can’t say they are 100% aware, still we have some gaps on those sanitation and hygiene areas. This gap was created, because educational backgrounds and culture has its own impacts. So we can’t say all communities are aware. But whatever it is, we can’t conclude that these problems stand in the way of addressing our water shortage problem.

2.What ideas or recommendation do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewer: So when we look at solutions, first is there a contradiction between demand and supply? The number one issue is water sustainability. Or, as everyone says, there is a lot of demand but a shortage of water. Is it limited? So who is responsible for this? What will you do?

Response: What I want to say is that those who are responsible for this must join together to invite NGOs to the Kebele. If we do this, we will work easily on hygiene and sanitation.

Interviewer: Who are those responsible?

Response: Those are the health bureau, city administration and water & energy office. If they collaborate and invite NGOs, we will solve this hygiene and sanitation problem.

Interviewer: Do you think we will solve these demand and supply problems?

Response: Yes
Interviewer: How?

Response: Because whenever we invite NGOs, they will help us to work on the ground water construction or on infrastructure issues.

Interviewer: The second question, as you said, we have a lack of donor/NGOs. What is that? What are you supposed to do to attract NGOs?

Response: Right, now we should invite them, first. 

Interviewer: How? Who will do that?

Response: It is not only one organization that is responsible for this. All must work together. They must devise a plan. If they work as a team, the problem will be solved easily.

Interviewer: thank you. If the city is not giving attention to water shortages and distribution, who is giving attention to this? Who is responsible?

Response: When I said the city government is not giving attention, for example, currently only the water & energy office is working on the issue. But if all those sector bureaus work together, it will be solved. But for one organization, things will be difficult. 

Interviewer: So you mean the city administration should working on brining unity between the various government offices?

Response: yes, the city administration must be the one to bring unity and focus.

Interviewer: The other thing is on the awareness of community health, including WASH, to solve all problems, what must be done?

Response: Already as I said earlier, we are working on awareness of hygiene and sanitation with extension workers in the community.

Interviewer: As you have said, you have done work on the community awareness, but where is the gap in addressing the community?

Response: Yes, the owner of the water resource is the community itself. They must become aware of this. They must work to break the gaps in education on WASH issues. This will solve all problems.

Interviewer: You are saying that if you were more serious in providing awareness, is that what you mean?

Response: Yes, if we work toward this goal, why not.
