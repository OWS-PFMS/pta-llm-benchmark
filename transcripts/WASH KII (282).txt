I1: In your opinion, what do you think we can do ensure we always have clean water running in Kamuli?
R: That depends on how you the professionals plan for us. It could work or not work. 
I1: Meaning?
R: For example, when TRUST came by, they told us they were going to change our water distribution to a clean water system but it didn’t work because of many reasons. 
I1: What is the biggest hindrance to Kamuli acquiring regular clean water? 
R: It is community members and the district’s leadership’s failure to work hand in hand with water focused organizations in Kamuli. 
I1: What recommendations can you give to help solve this problem?
R: Continued sensitization because knowledgeable community members on this matter are few and the illiterate are many. Also, community members’ dedication to maintain and deliberately want clean water for themselves. 
I2: How?
R: Doing what has been taught to maintain clean water, attending community meeting on clean water and paying maintenance fees. 
I1: From all you have mentioned, what do you think is the main solution?
R: For us community members to properly practice teachings on clean water maintenance. 
I2: Do you think boreholes are currently being maintained well? 
R: There are some things still left to be desired. For example, TRUST promised to move us from unhealthy water to clean water, however, they failed to deliver on their promise. Although WAVE is doing a good job, we hope they can deliver on their promises. That is, borehole should remain operational and regularly maintained. 
I2: Any more recommendations you can share with us on how we can acquire clean water and maintain our boreholes in Kamuli?
R: Just the one. It is important that organizations keep the promises made to communities on what is said to be delivered.
