
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: Problem during bidding and procurement of contractors and spare parts. Due to delayed procedures during procurement, less attention is given during bidding of contractors to be engaged in the water infrastructure development in the area. Establishment of water schemes with poor quality materials and spare parts is causing extra costs for the community since they are easily damaged and need to be changed spontaneously. 
Lack of awareness, sense of ownership and commitment at the different levels of institutions engaged in water services delivery starting from WASHCOs to regional water bureaus is another challenge. 
Poor stakeholders’ coordination: This is particularly a challenge among the three main sectors: water, education and health. The coordination platform is also poor among all the WASH stakeholders found both at the zonal and woreda levels. 
Interviewer: Can you tell me the names of institutes involved as WASH Stakeholder? 
Respondent: water, mine and energy, zone administration, education, health, women and children, trade and market development, NGOs, Agriculture and natural resources, Environmental, forest and climate change department. 
Lack of capital budget in some of the woredas, out of 11 woredas found in the zone, 4 of them don’t plan for capital budget to be used for development of water schemes. This is because they couldn’t have adequate budget to cover basic salaries for their regular workers. There is also similar problem in the 11 town administrations found with in south Omo zone 
Due to limited capital budget, we are not even be able to maintain the existing schemes since maintenance needs a smaller finance than developing new infrastructures. 
Shortage of vehicles and inadequate spare parts: even when spare parts are available in some of the woredas, at the same time other areas couldn’t access them easily. 
Limited attention and priority given to the water sector from other sectors is also another problem in this zone. Since we are not supporting, monitoring and motivating WASHCOs continuously, I know that there are model WASHCOs which retreated back to their normal stages in rural areas. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Great, I think you listed out the major points challenging sustainability, let me remind them one by one, what should be done to improve procurement and bidding system?
Respondent: One of the aggravating factors for this problem is inflation of construction materials. So, when we are designing schemes and planning to construct, we should consider the timely cost of materials and other expenses and start the work immediately. This includes, as much as possible, trying to finish a project in its allotted time. In addition to this, we need to develop a system of information storing, data analysis and decision making instead of talking without valid reasons. 
I think institutionalizing and providing regular technical and financial support to our major branch, ‘water institutes administration and equipment maintenance directorate’ is very important as it is mandated to oversee and maintain all water schemes in the zone. We do have shortage of technical man power in this branch. So, capacitating this directorate with adequate technicians and other relevant resources would solve many of the challenges we are facing with regard to water services 
For the lack of awareness and sense of ownership, the main thing is to strengthen the role of WASHCOs in rural areas. Once we have strong WASHCOs supported by awareness creation, small level damages and other financial problems would be solved gradually. 
Interviewer: what about the challenges related to capital budget?
Respondent: to solve the financial problem of those woredas, we have been discussing last time with the zone administration and are able to allocate capital budget for two woredas from the existing four even if it is still inadequate.  
With regard to skilled manpower, we are trying to provide skills training at the WASHCOs level but this has to be developed well to have more trained technicians, including plumbers and electricians everywhere.  This can be done through continuous discussions with the zone administration 

3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: 
The most important thing solution is working on financial problems. Once we solve budget problems, we can minimize many of water sustainability challenges in the zone. This still needs collaborative actions between the zonal administration and the region. This may include borrowing heavy machine, like the crane and others from the region temporally 
However, we should also work out to continue working with WASH stakeholders since this doesn’t need additional financing.
