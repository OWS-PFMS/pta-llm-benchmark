1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: low service coverage due to lack of commitment at least to properly handle the existing water schemes
Low level institutional coordination, for instance, our department is one of the contributing members of WASH committee in the zone but we are not working together with others to organize and support women development army at the grass root levels. To manage water services at the community levels, our department is particularly working with these development groups each of which consists about 30 women members. However, there is a gap in creating awareness and endorsing a sense of ownership among the members of these development groups. In addition to that, since majority of these women groups are not well organized, they couldn’t manage low level and local problems, including minor maintenance for their water schemes 
 Interviewer: I am receiving similar information from other stakeholders, including water office with regard to availability of gaps in the institutional coordination, my question here is that are you, as women department, contributing something to fill in these gaps ?
Respondent: No we don’t. This is because we are challenged with inadequate finance to support our groups properly and to provide continuous awareness programs.  
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: increase and strengthening popular participation and support. This includes consulting the community during development and maintenance of water schemes. This is due to the fact that the community has the local knowledge and practical experiences about the sources of water. On the other hand, we should integrate the local knowledge with the scientific ones through capacitating experts that can conduct a research before establishment of schemes   

Proper management of the already existing water schemes and maintenance of damaged once can also increase the low level water coverage we have now. 
Interviewer: who should take the initiative to manage and maintain the existing once?
Respondent: Actually this is the main mandate of the water offices at different levels as far as they have adequate budget. This can be done through supporting the kebele stakeholders at the lower level with a close follow up from woreda stakeholders. 
Interviewer: you have also mentioned budget problem as a challenge which is being flagged by some other institutes. My question is that how and by whom do you think that this problem could be solved ?
Respondent: I think the local government should closely work with development partners by conducting proposals to request their assistance officially. Second, we should convince the existing situation to the region and request more capital budget. Third, we can also mobilize the community to use the untapped labor force as a complement to fill the financial gaps. 
To alleviate the problem of lack of sense of ownership from the community, we should work hard to change the negative perception that our people have already developed for long. This is dependency syndrome. This may need building strong WASHCOs at the community level which can cover small expenses like hiring security, maintenance costs and others though their regular in-kind and in cash contributions.  When they start paying for such types of expenses, I hope that they will build a sense of ownership gradually. 
To fill in the gaps for the lack of coordination, as you know our department is cross sectoral by its nature where plans are developed by our departments but they are usually implemented by other stakeholders/sectors. This doesn’t bring the desired change. In order to be successful then, all sectors should plan together and implement the plans together. We are spending excess budget and resources because we are trying to achieve missions independently. 
Interviewer: You raised a good point. I think you have a WASH steering committee here, so what are the functions of this plat form then? 
Respondent: Yes, we have the committee and common plan. Both the technical and steering committee have also evaluations quarterly though we not observing practical changes. 


Interviewer: You have also mentioned that there are some water schemes left out of service, what should be done to maintain these and increase the service coverage? 
Respondent: the first thing to do is to mobilize the community because at the end of the day they are the main beneficiaries. For example, if a woman has the opportunity to fetch water in her residential area than traveling a long distance, she is saving her time to take care of her children and family on one hand and her life will be secured on the other hand. If we can bring collaborative working experience involving the government, as a lead, partner development agents and the community, we can minimize the cost of developing new water schemes by properly managing and maintaining the existing schemes. 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I think the first thing is to closely work with NGOs, the stakeholders and the community. The NGOs should do and bring practical changes as they have signed and promised to do when they come to this zone at the beginning. The government should also take the lion’s share in leading projects and organizing stakeholders. 
Interviewer: What next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Coordinated effort would bring lots of changes to the community as far as we are dreaming water service sustainability. As a citizen, everyone would get a change to get access to pure water, health conditions will improve.
