1.Interviewer: In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: Do you mean problems related to water and sanitation?
Interviewer: Yes, I am asking about the main challenges that affect the sustainability of water/sanitation service in your woreda. Please feel free and tell me what you have observed in the area?
Respondent: As it is known, in rural areas of the woreda there is a problem of access to clean water. This is mainly due to absence of rural road infrastructure. That is there is no road access to reach the rural sites and develop water schemes. Secondly, as our society is nomadic in search of feed (grazing land) for their livestock, they even leave water schemes developed in their area and migrate to other places. However, in most parts of the rural area there is very limited access for clean water.
Interviewer: Just to make your point clear you have said the nomadic (pastoralist) nature of the society affects the sustainability of water service in the area. Can you explain this a bit more?
Respondent: For example, if we settle a given community in one village under rural settlement programs and develop for the water schemes, they do not stay long in the area unless there is grazing land or feed for their animals. Even if there is water scheme for their consumption, they migrate to other places in search of pasture for their animals. 
Interviewer: The problems you raised so far are mainly related to water service. Are there any problems related to sanitation service in your woreda? When I say sanitation, I am referring to both personal and community sanitation.
Respondent: Regarding sanitation, for instance, there are roads constructed in the woreda town. There are also ditches in both sides of the roads. However, wastewater do not flow through the ditches rather it stagnates in the ditches. This results in serious sanitation problem in the town. Particularly during rainy season, the stagnant water creates a favorable ground for reproduction of mosquitos that transmit malaria.      
Interviewer: Pertinent to personal hygiene, are there problems related to use of latrines?
Respondent: Use of latrines is not known in rural parts of the woreda. Mostly it is only known and practiced in the woreda town and in some kebeles that are proximate to the woreda town. In addition, the community do not have a culture and experience of washing their hands with soap and water after using latrines. In urban areas to some extent there noticeable improvements, however, in rural areas there is no such handwashing culture after use of latrines. This leads to various public health problems.
Interviewer: You said there is better handwashing culture in urban areas than the rural areas. What is the reason for this?
Respondent: This is mainly due to awareness problem of the society. In the first place, our society do not have latrines. Most of them practice open defecation. Even in some areas, use of latrines is considered as a bad culture. Al in all lack of awareness is the major reason for this. 
Interviewer: Before we go to the solutions, are there any other problems you want to mention?
Respondent: No.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: The first problem you mentioned is that limited access for clean water particularly in rural areas. What ideas or recommendations do you have to improve access for clean water and ensure sustainable water and sanitation service?  
Respondent: To improve access to water IRC is exerting some efforts. Moreover, the woreda water office in coordination with IRC should develop water schemes in areas where there is no access for clean water. On the other hand, in areas where there are water facilities we have to aware the community on proper use of the schemes. If all responsible bodies do these two things, we can improve the access for clean water and ensure sustainable water service.  Water service is the responsibility of almost all governmental sector offices. For instance, Finance, Health, Water, Education, etc. Offices are responsible for water service development. Thus, these sector offices should work in close coordination to improve water service in the area. 
Interviewer: So you are saying strong coordination between sector offices can alleviate the problem related to access to clean water. But is water resource available in the area in the first place? If the sector offices have strong coordination, is there a ground water resource potential in the area?
Respondent: In my opinion and thinking, there is no water resource problem in our region. However, there are problems related to budget and qualified workers to develop water schemes. In some areas, the water might be salty and not suitable for consumption, but I do not think that there is water resource shortage in the region.
Interviewer: The second basic problem you mentioned is that absence of rural road infrastructure to develop water schemes and ensure sustainable water service in these areas. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: The government is responsible to alleviate the problem related to infrastructure. Until we make a solution to the road infrastructure problem, we should rely on solar energy based water schemes because it does not require transportation of large materials like diesel generators.   
Interviewer: When you say the government which of the government body for instance at this woreda level responsible for this? Do you think the government alone can alleviate this problem? 
Respondent: The government alone cannot solve the problem. Thus, the community should also be part of the solution. Without community participation, the government alone cannot bring a solution to the problem. Similarly, without the government support the community alone cannot alleviate the problem. Hence, there should be strong collaboration between the government and the society.
The government is established to alleviate communities’ problems using its own budget as well as with the support of other donors or aid organization. When I say the government, I am referring to the government sector offices like Health, Water, Finance and Education Offices. Thus, it is a coordinated effort of these government offices that could bring sustainable solution to the problem.
Interviewer: You have also mentioned a problem related to sanitation. We have also noticed the problem when we move from place to place in the town. That is the ditches are not well kept or they are not getting proper care and management. Due to this, they are not functioning well. What should be done to alleviate this problem?
Respondent: The problems of the ditches happened from the very beginning of the construction. That is the community has not been involved and did not be aware of the proper use and management of the ditches. For example, the households that are residing on roadsides dispose their wastes inside the ditches. This obviously create health problem for themselves. Thus, we should enhance the community awareness in this respect. 
Interviewer: Who is responsible for providing awareness enhancement training to the community? 
Respondent: The town administration and health office including health centers are responsible for enhancing the community awareness. 
Interviewer: Are there any community awareness creation activities so far?
Respondent: I have no information or evidence on this but I think we should work on community awareness to alleviate the problem.
Interviewer: Lastly, you raised the nomadic nature of the society as one of the major challenge to ensure sustainable water and sanitation services. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem? 
Respondent: The government has community settlement program and in this program in addition to facilitating access to water the government should facilitate supply of feed for animals in the area either by producing pasture in the area or by accessing feed from other places. The society has no migration hobby rather they move from place to place due to either lack of clean water or absence of pasture for their animals. If they can access their basic needs (water, feed for their animals, education and health services) in one place, they can settle in their locality. 
 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: You have raised problems with their suggested solutions. We are talking about sustainability of water and sanitation service. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? 
Respondent: In my opinion, water is very essential for human life and we cannot exist without water. Thus, working on improving access to water is the most important solution and apriority area of the community.
Interviewer: Can you explain more about improving access to water?
Respondent: When I say access to water, I am referring to clean, quality and sufficient water to the community.
