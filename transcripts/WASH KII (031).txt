Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: The first one is political interference that is with the MCAs (members of county assembly) sometimes they interfere with the projects, they want to be part of the project. When the committees have accumulated some funds, they want themselves to be controlling the accounts so that they tell them what they are supposed to do and what they are not supposed to do. That is one. The other thing is on maybe maintenance of the facilities; maybe if something like the pump gets spoiled they are unable to buy. So in this case we have to chip in because they don't have enough money to buy the spare parts. On the pipelines they do themselves. The other thing, in some projects we have given the WSPs (Water Service Provider) like KITWASCO (Kitui Water and Sanitation Company) now, in this area. There are some projects like Kavisuni, Mbitini, Mamole, Katwala, we have given to the KITWASCO WSP. Those ones they are going on well. But the ones which are not yet given out, we have a lot of problems.
Interviewer: Which other challenge?
Respondent: Maybe the other challenge we have we don't have maybe transport to go and monitor all those projects.
Interviewer: You as now?
Respondent: The sub-county water officer. We have a challenge of transport, and we have also given them some forms which they are supposed to report on monthly basis. That is reporting format for the water management committees. But some of them they fill and they return every month but some they don't want those forms at all because they don't want you to know what they are collecting, what they are getting, things like those.
Interviewer: And then you are saying you don't have means to follow up?
Respondent: To follow up, yes.
Interviewer: What other challenge?
Respondent: The other challenge also, in case of a breakdown we have to call somebody from the headquarter. The RRU, we have two RRU teams, rapid response unit. They are two teams. One in Kitui and the other one in Mwingi. So you have to call the one in Kitui. If they are not busy, they come, if they are busy you have to wait. 
Interviewer: For how long?
Respondent: Until when they'll get time to come to those places because they are booked. In the five sub-counties in Kitui they use RRU1 and the three sub-counties in Mwingi they use the other one.
Interviewer: So the challenge in that is?
Respondent: We don't have staff at the sub-county to do those repairs like for pumps, whatever to check what is wrong with those things. The other challenge is most of the projects, they are using genset it's only a few that have been hybridized.
Interviewer: So what about that?
Respondent: Fuel cost is too high.
Interviewer: Any other challenge towards water services sustainability?
Respondent: Sometimes maybe in the dry spell some of the boreholes dry up and also the sump wells, also sometimes they dry up also, we have three sources of water that is from river bed, boreholes, earth dams and those sump wells now which we have constructed along the streams, the rivers.
Interviewer: So you mean the climate issues?
Respondent: Yes, climate change issues.
Interviewer: Which now cause the water resources to dry up?
Respondent: To dry up, the recharge is too low at that time.
Interviewer: What other challenge? 
Respondent: The other challenge we have is on maybe earth dams, most of the earth dams are silted up. They have stayed for long before they are desilted.
Interviewer: So you mean now the water volumes go down or why?
Respondent: Even if it rains, they retain some water but it cannot stay for long. Just stay for a few days and then off.
Interviewer: What other main challenge do you think is contributing to these issues of sustainability?
Respondent: The Water Bill is not yet out, because even in some projects, we are having some land issues, someone says the land is theirs so they need to be given free water, to be given a free connection.
Interviewer: So what about the Water Act?
Respondent: The Water Act or the Water Bill?
Interviewer: You have said the Water Bill is not out?
Respondent: It is not yet approved by the County Assembly. Maybe if it was there, maybe we could cluster these committees now. If it Kitui Rural, in one ward we cluster and we give them one WSP under the main WSP. Maybe clustering so that they collect funds and then they have one kitty for maintenance. If maybe one borehole breaks down, they can repair.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Maybe just training the management committees on sustainability.
Interviewer: But these trainings have been there.
Respondent: You know you have to push them and have to continue training them still. Maybe we find somebody locally who can identify when there is maybe, a borehole breaks down, to go and identify what has caused the problem or what is the main problem after it has broken down. Because for diagnosis we have to come to the headquarters also, maybe we have somebody at every sub-county. 
Interviewer: I thought there are sub-county water officers at every sub-county?
Respondent: Okay, we have sub-county water officers at the level but the sub-county water officers they are not able to identify if a borehole maybe if it's the borehole or the motor which is faulty, something of the sort. We need somebody like electromechanical somebody in each sub-county.
Interviewer: What other recommendation?
Respondent: I don't know if that one can fit because at the sub-county we have only three staff, the sub-county water officer, the deputy and one more staff, an artisan. So we are very few. Because of the pipelines, we have that artisan. That one helps them to repair maybe the big lines. On the pumps, there is nobody to do that.
Interviewer: So what about the three staff at the sub-county?
Respondent: They are not adequate.
Interviewer: So what are your recommendations for that?
Respondent: We had requested for additional staff to the sub-counties.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Clustering the PMCs, the project management committees into one. Every ward to have one committee monitoring all the water projects on management issues.
Interviewer: Because, if we cluster them what else, what will happen?
Respondent: At least there we will have maybe, the revenue will be high, and if we have a breakdown we can take the money from there instead of all the time coming to the office. Because there we have some projects which are using solar, electricity and others gensets. So those that are using solar you know they not incurring much expenditure. Expenditure is only on the payment of the maybe operator and the repairs only.

Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Interventions that we implement in Kitui rural are pipeline extensions, earth dams and sump wells complete with a pipeline. That one they got from the public participation forum. Before implementation there is a public participation that is done, you prioritize your projects through the community; they prioritize if it's water, roads, agriculture, etc. They prioritize then we pick from there, from the headquarter now after they have been prioritized from the ground.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: During the WASH Forum, maybe some partners tell us what they are doing on the ground. Like Tanathi, sometimes they tell us what they are doing on the ground. Though Tanathi all the time we've been telling them that they are not informing us on what they are doing because they are doing some boreholes there we are not aware, they equip, they give them to committees without involving us. But other partners, they involve us when they are doing some projects. It's only some partners who are hiding I don't know why.
Interviewer: So how has your participation in the WASH Forum influenced your organization's interventions? You've talked about there is some information sharing with some partners, so how else?
Respondent: Information sharing and also when we have that WASH Forum we also know how other sub-counties are doing and maybe when they have breakdowns what they do like Mwingi North, they are using the Oxford. They are the ones who do their repairs. Their repairs are being done by Oxford but there is a fee that the management committees pay so that they can be...if a borehole has broken down, so that maybe their need can be taken by Oxford.
Interviewer: So there is the information sharing, there is also borrowing from what others do in case of breakdowns, any other?
Respondent: Maybe new technologies. Like the last time we had the WASH Forum, we saw in Kitui East there is a technology they were doing there on implementation of a sump-well differently from the way we do .I can't remember which organization was doing that one and also the Davis&Shirtliff they gave us also a new technology on how to maybe the new pumps we are supposed to be using.
Interviewer: So all that you got out of the WASH Forum?
Respondent: Yes.
Interviewer: Anything that you would want to add, either on the challenges, either on the recommendations?
Respondent: Maybe on the challenges I can add maybe if our office maybe if breakdowns are there we do them immediately, we respond to them immediately.
Interviewer: So that is one of the recommendations?
Respondent: Recommendation yes because some communities are staying for long. Some of them have stayed for more than one year without their motor or pumps being replaced, they are not using the water and also we are changing committees all the time because of maybe that is the interest of the MCAs now. We are changing all the time, you elect a committee today, tomorrow you hear the Governor say you elect a different committee. There is a lot of interference in these management committees especially where the water committees are collecting a lot of money. In those cases you have to elect someone that supports the governor.
Interviewer: So what ideas do you have that you've said you recommend repairs to be done immediately or as soon as possible, how can this be done?
Respondent: Maybe we buy spare parts and pool so that maybe if it's something which is not there we go we diagnose that a borehole doesn't have a motor, we just come and pick. There was a time we used to keep them in here, in case of a break down it's just picked from here. Maybe things like control panels, motors, pumps etc.
