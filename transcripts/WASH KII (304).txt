Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: The main challenges in water service sustainability in Kitui County include poor management by the community management committees. The community sometimes may not have the expertise on how to run these schemes and you will find that upon encountering a breakdown however small it is they may end up not having the right people to go address that, again they may also not be able to source the needed materials to respond to a breakdown, and also, as they are trying to address the breakdown they may end up causing some other unforeseen faults at the facilities because of the lack of expertise. This causes poor service reliability and lengthy downtimes. The second challenge is that there are some major breakdowns which are beyond the capability of the community committee to handle, like the major components in a scheme for example the submersible pump which is highly costly and maybe the community even upon doing the water sales cannot raise funds which can purchase such water pumps. The others are like the generators, upon becoming obsolete or its efficiency is lost. now restoring a new genset might be a challenge to them and so they have to seek external funding like from the county or partners and this cannot be depended on in the long term.
The other challenge is somehow technical-that is on the issue of high salinity levels, because you may find that some boreholes are highly saline and the community end up avoiding or disowning them hence, they are grounded and so they do not provide service at all. 
Interviewer: Any other challenges? 
Respondent: That is it. Mostly it is the lack of expertise and poor management and also the cost of the equipment needed when a breakdown occurs. 
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: I would recommend that we do some community trainings on simple operation and maintenance based on the components in each scheme per the community, we train them on the equipment they are having and this will deal with the issue of lack of expertise. The issue of poor management we can also do some guidance and also training on leadership and also financial management, bookkeeping for them as a community even to keep good records so that they can easily save some amount of money. When it comes to ensuring that schemes can be able to cate to major breakdowns, an idea is that we can classify these schemes into some clusters whereby we will establish some funds which will be set aside to support these schemes and even upon them selling the water they will be injecting some amount to this fund. When a breakdown occurs, money can be drawn from the fund to buy equipment for repairs.
On the issue of salinity, maybe we can think about advancing in technology to an extent that in the hydrogeological surveys before drilling boreholes we can predict the areas where we can get fresh water. We can also adopt other technologies like developing alternative water sources which have low salinity levels, like sump wells in seasonal rivers. Sump wells in seasonal rivers do not go large depths as compared to borehole depths, so the surface water may not be that saline and that would serve as an alternative to saline water.


Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if this solution occurs?
Respondent: The one I consider the most important is the one on developing alternative sources. We can embrace the latest technologies of having sump wells in seasonal rivers because these wells are not deep and so we will not require huge pumps or pumps which are expensive. Again, the operational costs based on the mode of abstraction of the water will not be that expensive. 
If these alternative sources, now the sump wells, are adopted we will get safe and clean water to the communities and we will also encounter fewer breakdowns or even no breakdowns. Again, the mode of abstraction will not be expensive so the community will be able to run the facilities with ease. Training the community on the operation and maintenance of the scheme will be easy for them and they will be able to learn to run it in the long run without encountering problems and major breakdowns. Few or no breakdowns will lead to reliable water services for the communities and they will be happy.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: First, I will touch on the management bit. We are going around the schemes ensuring that they all have valid management committees, that they are keeping records and they also have bank accounts. This is to track their water sales because they must save money which will be used for the operation and maintenance of the scheme and to an extent avail some allowances for their meetings. 
We are also keeping an inventory of all the equipment in all the schemes so that we also establish the prices of components so that when we are not encountering breakdowns or maybe we get a donor who can support us, we can get some funds and purchase the equipment and keep them in our stores, so that whenever a breakdown occurs, we will have the equipment ready. It is sort of like keeping spare part reserves.
We are in partnership with FundiFix for the repair and maintenance of water facilities for sustainability of the schemes, whereby the schemes are signing up with FundiFix and so they enter into agreement and FundiFix responds to their breakdowns and this is ensuring continuous water flow. The number of breakdowns has been reduced and also reduced the number of rehabilitations in the schemes because those are addressed earlier before the schemes sign up with FundiFix. Since we are not doing many rehabilitations, those funds are now being used to purchase the equipment I was talking about-the reserves. 
Interviewer: What informed the selection of these interventions? 
Respondent: For the management issue, we are putting the management committees in place so that they keep records of the schemes including records on daily water sales and records of how they are spending and controlling the funds the committee is collecting, because of late they have been misusing the money, collecting the money, misusing it and they end up suffering yet they are collecting the money. 
For the inventory of the schemes, we have been encountering scenarios whereby we have a breakdown, and maybe in the county we are not having such funds to purchase such equipment. So, having an inventory of all the components we want will now enable us to stock up and keep the equipment in the stores so that when we encounter a breakdown we just pick the required equipment and go and fix. This makes it easy for us because we will not have to go through the lengthy procurement process and we will greatly reduce downtime.
The partnership with FundiFix was necessitated by difficulties in accessing spare parts. We realized that FundiFix is stocking the spare parts and is also located within our vicinity so upon requirement of any spare part we can easily get it from FundiFix. They also have expertise in the form of their officers and they have got plumbers and technicians who can diagnose problems in the scheme facilities and address them within the shortest time possible; reducing the down time and hence ensuring continuous flow of water. 
We really need to ensure that this sustainability model that FundiFix is based on is backed up and that all the schemes are registered under it. Now, we have to do more sensitization to the communities and have it as policy that each new rural infrastructure set up is signed up to the sustainability model.
