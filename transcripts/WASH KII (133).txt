1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your woreda? 
Interviewer: What are the main problems that affect the water service sustainability of the woreda?
Respondent: The main problem of water service provision is in the town.
Interviewer: What kind of problem is that?
Respondent: There is a management problem and due to this huge amount of water is being wasted. Besides, the existing water pipes are too old. The water supply management system is also traditional in terms of computerizing the customer and utilization data.
Interviewer: When thinking as a woreda, what are the problems that you have observed that hinder the water supply sustainability?
Respondent: Where there is solar panel there is not that big of a problem. However, the problem is lack of timely maintenance. Besides, as the population is growing developing additional water schemes is lacking. Moreover, there are some places that still use hand-dug well. 
There is also poor performance among WaSHCos at kebele level. They have lack of training and material. Experts are traveling long distances to maintain water schemes. 
The community members also lack to contribute what s expected of them. They sometimes hesitate to pay the fees timely which are vital for purchasing spare part and maintenance of the schemes. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned many fundamental problems. What would be the solutions for these problems? For instance you have said that there is a management problem. what is the solution for this?  


Respondent: The town’s water supply operation is run by a board.
Interviewer: Who oversees the board?
Respondent: The woreda administration supervises the board. The board includes woreda finance and economic development, water and energy office, and woreda administration. The first thing should be the board should monitor the water supply problems and propose and implement solutions continuously. 
Besides training should be given to the experts in the water utility office and data should be kept electronically. Moreover, the old pipelines should be replaced with new ones. This will be helpful to reduce water wastage and prevent diseases that come from stagnant waters created due to the wasted water. 
Other stakeholders that are not members of the board should assist the water service provision through material and training supports. 
Interviewer: You have said that there is lack of water schemes and maintenance problems in the rural areas. 
Respondent: The first solution is to develop additional water schemes. Second is shifting the hand-dug wells to solar or motorized systems. 
Interviewer: These solutions have cost implication.
Respondent: Resource should be mobilized from governmental and non-governmental stakeholders. Besides, the water utility office requires transport facilities such as vehicles to conduct timely maintenance. 
Interviewer: Who should do this?
Respondent: The regional government should take the responsibility. Besides, road conditions should be improved in the rural areas.
Interviewer: You also said that the WaSHCos are not functioning well. What is the solution for this?
Respondent: The woreda administration has financial dependent attitude. Rather, the administration should take the initiative and allocate some budget to train the WaSHCo members. If necessary they should ask for the assistance of other stakeholders. The water utility office should encourage the committees to open bank account and collect fees to cover their expenses.
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: You have mentions many solutions; and which one do you think should be given priority?
Respondent: I don’t think there is water availability problem in the town. Priority should be given to the problems in the town; because the residents of the town are suffering from lack of clean water due to minor problems. Therefore, these management problems should be addressed first.
Interviewer: What would happen if these management issues are addressed?
Respondent: The residents of the town will have access to clean water properly.
