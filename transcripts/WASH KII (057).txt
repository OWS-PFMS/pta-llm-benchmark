
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:    Looking at the various partners, what is very key in the water department is sub-county water officers, WASH coordinator’s office at the county level.  The two are very critical in sustaining water projects in the county level.  Once we coordinate, issues on design, we are assisted with these sub-county water officers.  So the two are very critical.  

.  

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:  The problem in kitui is basically access.  When we started this project we were looking at the immediate need of getting this person water – regardless of quality, no matter how turbid.  Water access was a real issue.   On issues of management, there’s a training we conducted on O&M of water structures– we realized the earth dams, earth ponds we’re doing, the community’s don’t look at the critical areas, auxiliary structures – fox example, the dams get silted very often.  Sanitation component, handwashing, such things.  I think we can say they need more training, more capacity building for sustaining water of the systems.  You can have a dam but its’ not serving its purpose because of 1,2,3 things.  That’s why we conducted those trainings and we are still going to do more on the sustainability of water.  We have sand ponds, earth dams, some do rainwater harvesting through roof catchment.  We encourage them to be able to harvest, because rainwater is more pure.  So issues of information.  At times they don’t know.  So we need to do more on water issues.  We don’t do our priorities right because there are projects which some donors can support which don’t really address the real problem. The main problem in kitui county is water.  If the government does something around water, she’s going to win the souls of many.  So I think privatization is what is missing in many areas.   

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee:  I think for us to solve the problem of water in this county, as stakeholders, I think it’s very important right now the WASH forum, which is very handy in this.  Once we share best practices, we see who is doing what where, and if we do that we’re able to solve the problem in one point, we will avoid duplication of duties.  If all of us come together as one, as partners at the county level, and maybe have a joint project: I have a budget of this much and would like to do a mega dam in Mwingi North, 50,000 cubic.  I think the targeted beneficiaries will have the problem sorted.  Another partner comes in with money for lining of farm ponds, and I tell them: I have done farm ponds and I’d like you to support us with liners.  So I think that kind of partnership is very critical.  That joint kind of support and partnership is very critical.  

The county priorities should come out clearly on issues of water and also provide funds on this being the main problem in this county.  

Being able to avoid duplication.  So this forum is very useful.  If we do that we’ll be able to get these problems solved to some extent.  We’re not going to solve all of it, this is an area that receives little rainfall, but we can do something to assist our communities.  

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee:    I think aligning CIDPs (County Integrated Development Plans) is very important because the county government has the budget for supporting its people.  Projects, water projects and any other projects.  I think that’s very important because we’re all looking to the county government.  So if the resources that are there, if they are well allocated to the various projects and we have these activity steps that partners are proposing and they’re aligned with the CIDPs, then these problems will be minimzed.   

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee:  I think we’ll have access, our people will be able to access water at reduced distances.  We’ll have improved health because you have water, you’re not going to spend a lot of time going 10 km to fetch water.  You have it at 100m or 1km.  You can save a lot of time that can be engaged in economic activities at the household and community levels.
