
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Water and Sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
In rural areas of this wereda, we have a critical problem to access water. This by itself is related to the limited road access to and from kebels and woredas. Second, since our society is basically a pastoral society, they don’t have a trend of permanent settlement. The society’s life is strongly connected with their cattle and pasture. Establishing basic sanitation and health facilities has become difficult owing to this nature. 
Interviewer: What are the problems related to hygiene?
Lack of coordination among the different offices: for example, the pipelines we are using now were filled with solid wastes following the ruins houses during road construction. The water has already been contaminated in the reconstruction process and this is affecting the health of people. The people in rural areas do not have the culture of using toilets like that of the people living in urban areas. They are exposed to OD instead. The other problem regarding personal hygiene is lack of awareness on washing hands after toilet.  Since many of the households in rural areas don’t have toilets around their residential houses, they are frequently using OD.  
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
The first solution is improving the access to water. In the region, there is a plan targeted to multiply the number of boreholes in the near future. That could solve the increasing demand to pure water we have now due to increasing population number. However, water access by itself couldn’t solve the sanitation problems, it should be supplied with continuous awareness programs. This also calls the need to work closely with all stakeholders

In our woreda, we have plenty of ground water. The problem is lack of adequate budget for boring this groundwater and we are requesting that from the government. Lack of skilled and technical manpower in the sector is another factor. The water, of course, is full of fluoride and other chemicals which may create additional burden even with the availability of adequate budget.  
In some of the kebeles where there is no road access, the government should supply with solar panels and generators to use them to pump the water from its source.  
Water, health, education and other sectors of the wereda should work in coordination with the public to solve the problems. Coordinated work will definitely bring change. 
Interviewer: What should be done to avoid hygiene problems along   the ditches?
While working the design of the ditches, the responsible office should consult the people. Urban dwellers, especially those living closer to the roads dispose their wastes to the ditches. They are doing this because they don’t have open space to dispose and they are not well informed. It is the responsibility of the officials to provide the space and let them know the negative consequences
Interviewer: What about the way out to the pastoral life style of the people?
Before working on bringing them to villages, we should plan for the possibilities of having grazing land for their animals. We can work this with the experts from agriculture office. Once we have that arrangement, it is also mandatory to establish basic and durable services like schooling, health facilities and water schemes. 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which one is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Priority should be given for improving access to pure water. This wouldn’t be an overnight works and a responsibility given to one office. It is through process and working together with all stakeholders that we can make sure that all people in the woreda have access to pure water. Sanitation and hygiene problems will be solved once we have easy access.
