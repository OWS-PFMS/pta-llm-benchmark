1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda?

In my opinion, the main problems of water sustainability are the following. [Many of the projects are found at the planning stage (paper value)] the speed and process of turning available water projects in to operation is very slow. This may be because of different reasons including lack of commitment from the water office, weak coordination among the stalk holders and budget delay. [Lack of coordination among the stalk holders] There is no strong coordination office in the woreda and this is because every office runs to complete its plans, there is no common plan and coordinated activities. On the other hand, lack of coordination is leading to improper usage of budget and wrong decisions of the places where water points should be located. 

[Lack of pure water resources in the woreda and poor infrastructure development] this is because of the nature of the topography, ups and downs and desert climate; we can’t find pure water easily. Though there are small water projects being implemented; many of them don’t last long given the limited roads connecting rural kebeles, the poor quality of materials used for water infrastructures and lack of skilled man power around/near the water sources. Due to this reason, the largest section of the population are found very far from the woreda town and hence subjected to drink river water. The quality of the existing water sources is also questionable. There are water points which have already been finished but not giving services yet. Usually, assigned project leaders/bidders have don’t concern to the sustainability of the water sources at the first place when they are digging the ground/looking for water sources. It seems that every one runs for emergency, there is also water projects found at the planning stage and not turned to operation stage due to budget problems. 
5:35 
Lack of fairness in allocation of water points (pumps) it is obvious to see that few officials are corrupt in selecting water points. Instead of choosing places that have strategic importance for both the current and future sustainability from transportation and access to parts and equipment perspectives, they choose places near to the places where their relatives are located. They may select points which are difficult for transportation. Due to this fact, certain Kebeles (smallest administrative units) will be benefited and others excluded. Unfair distribution may also be because of lack of proper monitoring and evaluation systems in the water office and other stalk holders. In addition, we are not sure whether this budget [improper use of the budget] is directly used for the water services or not and in fact this one of the major bottlenecks for water service sustainability. Hitambok and Adanu (the second and third participants) added at this point. There is no clear auditing and recording system of the money collected from the tap water at the community level. Had it been for the clear financial procedures, the WASH cos and the money collected from that service would have been used for the maintenance and they will be self-sufficient.  Budget and expense disbursement is not clear and there are rent seeking officials in the sector. This needs the coordination of all lines offices from woreda-zone-region in monitoring and evaluation of the water finance system in general. This is a good solution, please note this one.

2.What ideas or recommendation do you have about solutions to these problems?
For the planning and operation problem: the water office should play the leading role in monitoring and evaluation of the projects found at the beginning, implementation and operation stages, by prioritizing them from the perspectives of urgency. When the projects are late for operation, the office should identify the problem behind, be it economical or material and act accordingly and should be presented to other stalk holders in a formal report form. 
Regarding the coordination problem, it would be ideal to have sustainable water service without strong coordination of partner organizations and other line offices, including our office, education and health. From the NGOs perspective, as partners, they should come together to discuss plans and implement their projects. For this to succeed, the woreda water office should play a major role through allowing a formal meeting and panel discussions for both partner organizations and government line offices. I see that there is a big gap on the side of water office in this regard. I (Baye) have taken a training related to sanitation and hygiene of women and children from the zone, but didn’t start practicing in my woreda because the leading and coordination role has been given to the water office and I am waiting for their invitation 
Regarding the lack of pure water and quality of the infrastructure, during construction of water points, we need to take care of the sanitation cases both from the quality and beauty perspectives, we should make sure that they are well protected from wild and domestic animals and they should be equipped with strong fences. On the other hand, supervision and follow up of the daily water services, both by the community and the local government bodies should be given a priority, including adding water treatment chemicals. This assignment should be given for line offices, particularly the water office. Also, we should think about the quality of cement, concrete and water pipes when we start construction of water infrastructures. 
 Regarding improper use of water budget, we know that a budget allocated for the water and health services is relatively huge as compared to other sectors’ budget but at least the available budget should be used wisely. There should be established chain of structures starting from the region, zone and woredas, to control over how the budget is used. Partner organizations should be invited to follow on how the budget is being implemented since they are one of the sources for water fund. We know that IRC WASH has contributed a lot in some of rural schools with very good initiative and has established a WASHcos in this area.

[Bidding problems] (Arose while discussing solutions for budget) During bidding, the water office should give emphasis to the experience and capacity of the bidders instead of going through traditional and unlawful acts. We know that sometimes bidders are selected without considering formal procedures; this affects the sustainability of established water schemes, as it raises the quality/durability issues. Therefore, care should be taken in recruiting bidders and the procedures should be clear and legal. Hitambok (another participant,); since the entire bidding system is a rush out and the construction time as well, soon it requires huge amount of money to buy equipment and pay for the maintenance. He added, we obviously see that there is no a clear system during recruitment of bidders, it is not based on their competencies (long time experiences and skills) 


To at least reduce the problem of fairness in allocation of water points, government officials should work in close coordination with the local communities. We should work on public awareness to have same understanding of managing and using water services from the community. In few areas, people are drinking river water and in other areas people are using pure water because of unfairness in allocation of resources to water services.

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water services? What could be improved?

At the first place there is week coordination among WASH stalk holders that is why ongoing water projects are not completed yet. Lack of quality of the existing water works is also resulted from poor coordination. What could be improved? WASH plans and activities should be discussed together, one office cannot be effective. There should be common plan, including capacity building of WASH cos. Partner organizations should also involve in the follow up process, this might include sending their quarter year report to water office and participating in annual and other meetings. Other government sectors are not fully involved in WASH sector, the health office, education; women and children and other sectors should produce in input to WASH services.
