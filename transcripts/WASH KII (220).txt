1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Interviewer: The first question is what are the challenges related to water service sustainability?
Respondent: It is better to see the challenges in two categories; those gravity schemes and other with pressurized schemes. Gravity schemes are expected to be sustained by local community and only need minor maintenance. However, pressurized schemes exhibit more challenges due primarily to lack of technicians and fuel expenditure at community level. This is related to mismanagement of cash collected from the community. It varies from community to community, but roughly community members contribute ETB 5 or 10, which will be collected through the WASHCOs. However, it is not being handled and allocated properly. 
When we see schemes that are powered with solar, there is no supply of spare parts in the market in this area. Besides, shortage of trained technicians is vital. For instance, there is s water scheme constructed by IRC, Luka water scheme, needed more than ETB 100,000 to purchase a single accessory. Such amount of money is difficult to find at community level. 
Regarding supply in the local market, there is no supply of 2, 2.5, 3 inch pipes, even at zonal level. You cannot find the pipe or the fittings. It is a must to go to either to Hawasa or Arbanch/over 200kms/ here ½ inch pipes and fittings are available.  
Regarding government bodies, there is no follow-up after water site is handed over. They only ask additional projects but they do not coordinate local community and government’s limited budget to undergo sustained maintenance. Even though we have discussed these issues in different forums, we have not seen anything beyond that meeting talk.  
There is also lack of community initiation to protect and manage water schemes. For example, communal water schemes leaks water all day long due to a broken faucet that costs less than ETB 60. Similarly, the community lacks putting a fence around water schemes and repairing it timely.
There are water points that are not functioning well and that have design problems. For instance, some water schemes do not reach most of the water distribution points.
More importantly, our current water coverage is 35% because we have funding problems both from government and NGOs to build new schemes and maintain the existing ones. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: It is important to set up associations and establish institution that can provide spare parts for the water schemes. There are some private suppliers but they are rather pricey. Recently, there are a few establishments around Key Afer that provides solar power supported by NGOs such as Christian Aid Ethiopia and Women Empowerment Action, but I don’t think that  they are functional. 
Water pipe of different sizes should be provided by the government. For instance, cement and other materials are being provided by government wholesale shops in town, but we cannot find pipes. Because a whole water system can stop because of lack of a single fitting, a union or a pipe. 
Interviewer: Just due to simple reasons?
Respondent: Yes! Therefore, government should either supply or establish enterprises that provide these products because most retailers in town do not provide them because of lack of demand.
Related to community, strong capacity building and awareness should be carried out. Communities expect the NGOs to build the water schemes and keep maintaining them regularly. This might be due to lack of awareness.
Government’s system seems to be reckless and there is lack of follow-up. Government workers may seem to work with NGOs just for the sake of the allowance they get from the NGOs. Public servants lack the sense of serving their own fellow and make continuous follow up and maintenance.  
Likewise, NGOs should raise additional funds to rehabilitate and carry out sustainable follow-up because local government lacks the commitment to maintain the water schemes continuously. NGOs conduct rehabilitation only if there is an emergency, so they should be able to rehabilitate the existing water schemes.  
Strengthening WASHCOs is also vital. There are caretakers at community level enhancing them in terms of equipments and training is imperative. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: Which is the most important solution?
Respondent: The most important solution is strong government involvement as it has leading role in connecting the community and NGOs. Local government should work on its recklessness, provide spare parts, and issues that cannot be solved at community or kebele level should be identified and addressed by woreda, zonal, and regional levels.  
NGOs also should find continuous funds to sustain existing water schemes. The problem is not only just lack of funding but also lack of proper use of the existing ones.
