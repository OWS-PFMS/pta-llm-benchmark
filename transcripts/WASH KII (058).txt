
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: I think the most important aspect that I believe is working well is basically the sharing of information, because for example: for the sub-county water officer, once we get to the ground after our proposals are approved, what we do is we start with the sub-county water officer and the communities for them to share with us the information that we need, so that if it’s a sand dam we do it in the places that are most affected.   

Interviewer:  My second question is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining rural water services in Kitui?

Interviewee: Ok, basically I would point out that most of these projects are failing because of the sustainability factor of training communities on how to handle the money collected from those water points, and also the spare parts.  Take an example of the AfriDev and the India Mach 2 most of the spare parts are not available in Kitui – you have to go like get them in Nairobi and you see most of these communities don’t have access to towns like Nairobi.  

Interviewer:  Is there any other problem?

Other than operation and maintenance, I don’t think there are other problems with regards to the water sector in Kitui.  

Interviewer:  And my third question is “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?”

Interviewee: Ok, basically like I had indicated back in the WASH Forum, there is this system of selling water.  Once you come up with a water kiosk or a water point, you can install a Water ATM.  And that is information I said I’ll share during our next WASH Forum.  Where instead of having a community have a small committee of people collecting cash from their waterpoints, you come up with a solution where community members are issued with Water ATMs, and that Water ATM uses a mobile payment system  where instead of depositing cash, you use water points that are already in that card.  I think I’ll explain that more during our WASH Forum.   

Interviewer:  How about the challenge you mentioned about spare parts?  What would you consider a solution to that?

Interviewee: Once you install the water ATM, I think one will be able to install maybe a solar pump or maybe an electric pump which will be using solar energy.  Once we eliminate that handpumps for solar pumps I think the spare parts are quite available in the local markets, say Davis and Shirtliff here in Kitui or other outlets.      

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: The Water ATMs.

Interviewer:  Can you walk me through the next steps that would happen if that solution were to occur?

Interviewee: If we install the Water ATMs, they are quite expensive. But you see, if the initial cost is quite high but the operation and maintenance for the community is a bit low, then that is the best and we would not have those collapsing schemes anymore.
