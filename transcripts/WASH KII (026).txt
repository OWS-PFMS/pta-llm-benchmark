Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: The major challenges affecting sustainability of water services in Kitui can be categorized into technical, governance and financial. Those are the main three challenges. And in analysis now we can expound to each of them. Maybe there is necessity. Because when we talk of technical, is the aspect which affects the water supplies system itself, which range from project identification, selection and implementation. We have found that some of the projects either the source, one of the factors is the source limitation. The source might not be adequate, Kitui being an arid region, you find that the source is a challenge to most of the water supplies and in this case there could be a challenge in terms of quality and quantity. So this becomes a technical factor. Then when it comes to the topography of the county itself you find that it is hilly and most of the water supplies and the resources are within the valleys. So you find that there is an element of pumping. So this also becomes a challenge. And due to high cost of fuel and electricity because most of our systems use either genset system or the electricity. It has been a high cost and it's only of late that we have been campaigning about the use of renewable energy after reasonable prices within the market of solar systems. So we are now working on either hybridization of the systems to address that one of those challenges of high power consumption. 
Other technical factors are...Most of the projects especially those which within the rural areas, there is an element of the cost sharing where we have the community contribution. Either IPs or implementing partners’ contribution and the County Government contribution. And most communities contribute in kind and we normally prefer to local materials as part of their contribution and sometimes these local materials might not be meeting the quality, the standard required. There are also factors of designs, there are no standardized designs. Although we have what we call the ministerial design standards, but some of these small schemes you find that the standardized designs are not followed. So this also brings a challenge in terms of sustainability and then also the market, the materials used in the market in terms of the quality of pipes, the quality of materials used is also a challenge. So you find that technically there are all those factors. Then when we come to governance or management you find that, especially within the rural set up, most of these schemes are managed by the committees who have no knowledge on the issues of water supply management, O&M (Operation and Maintenance). Because they are like voluntary. They volunteer themselves. The schemes are not run professionally and where we have few schemes with project managers, some might not be qualified to understand the management of a water supply. 
Then it also comes to political interference in terms of the management because most of these committees are either elected aligned to the political interest then this brings conflict among the communities. You find that now most of the committees align themselves by political interest and this affects the management of the scheme. In terms of management you find that most of the schemes are because of the voluntary element of the management. It is either they are left just in the hands of either the executive or one person and now it becomes an issue of conflict within the community. So I think in terms of governance, management, these are some of the major challenges. In terms of financial, you find that the schemes are not run professionally and most of them either fail to have the accounts, bank accounts, they don't account for the collections, their schemes are not metered, they don't have records and it is also in terms of monitoring it is also another weak area, a challenge. And these ones basically come to the county whereby we have weak monitoring framework systems of these schemes. So these are some of the challenges which affect sustainable WASH facilities.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
 Respondent: As a county we have been working on how to improve sustainability of these WASH schemes because one factor we have done a lot of rural water supply schemes and finally each year we do our development, we focus on doing new projects rather than focusing on sustainability of the existing ones. So we are now shifting the focus and now thinking of rather than now allocating most of the funds to new projects. Whichever area we have selected we now first establish whether, what are the now existing water supply schemes, what are their potentials and can they be able to cover the area now we’re focusing and because of that we have started rehabilitation program, where now those schemes with potential sources we are now working on them, to rehabilitate, hybridize and extent the networks. And now we have as a county, started developing what we are calling the County Water Policy which now gives direction, we are also developing the County Water Bill, which also outlines some of the major challenges and whereby we are now saying in terms of designs, let's adhere to the standard designs which is now our policy direction and also our Bill. In terms of management, we are now working on the best models and we are also incorporating the public-private partnerships elements so that we can say the schemes are run professionally. And these guidelines are clearly outlined within the two documents. 
So we are also coming with a very strong monitoring framework system and whereby the committees are also held up accountable and they should register as a water user association with the registrar of societies so that they can have constitution which can now be followed clearly as we do the monitoring. And in terms of financial we are also encouraging audit of their books and therefore what we are doing is we have come up with also very strong capacity building program where now we'll have to take them through the steps so that at one level we can now say, we can now subject this kind of a scheme to this kind of management.
Interviewer: And on the issue of political interference?
Respondent: Solutions to challenges on technical, especially where we are talking of quality and quantity or the inadequacy of the resource. As a county we have come up with programs where we want to do conservation especially to enhance replenishment of the ground water systems. Because here people rely mainly on the ground water sources. We have found out we can say although Kitui is arid and semi-arid region, but the amount of rain we receive annually if well conserved it can result to adequate or enough source to meet the demand within the county and therefore we are doing programs in water harvesting that is; construction of sand dams within our river catchments, we are also doing construction of earth dams and we are also emphasizing on environmental conservation. We are actually coming up with a plan in terms of water security. 
So we are focusing on and working in collaboration with water resource users association so that we can do a lot of conservation of the rain water we receive. So that one is under our major programs although the challenge of financing has been one of the major issues but with devolution at least and some responsibility by the national government and collaboration we are doing with different partners, we can say, since devolution, what has been achieved in various programs is more faster than when the devolution was not there. When it comes to issues of politics, political issues are part of development and it is not easy to address political challenges but as i have said, with the policy and the water bill and regulations which are focusing to start developing, we are now coming up with basis of argument whereby we can defend the political interference by reference to these documents. 
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The most important is governance.
Interviewer: Do you want to add anything on that?
Respondent: As I have said, when you look into the water sector we have done a lot of development but when you look through sustainability, some of the key challenges, some of the main effects arise from the governance system because we have a project which has been sound approved and handed over to the management but within a very short period, some of the major key components either they break down and when they breakdown, the element of repairs and maintenance has been a major challenge. So in this case, repairs and maintenance, or O and M is a key component of management and therefore that's why I’m saying, management issues, governance, is one of the main contributors and issue number one which if well addressed sustainability can easily be improved.

We are now saying, if rural schemes can be subjected to small WSPs, which can be categorized into various models and whereby we are now saying, privatization can also be a part of it and these are some of the interventions we are now trying because like in Kitui, we have seen challenges in the rural areas as I have indicated are mainly repairs and maintenance.

Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: Actually what I can say is WASH forum is one of the most important meetings we can say has influenced the way we have been doing things. WASH forum is one of the most important forums we have within our department because we have them on quarterly basis and this is the forum whereby we share most of our WASH activities and it brings together partners, national agencies undertaking water development, the County Government, It also brings in the service providers, community management committees. 
So it's a forum where our development has been majorly influenced and one of the key areas it addresses is about the equity in terms of resource allocation. So we will now be able to say, in accordance to our audit we are likely to say this area is lagging behind in terms of provision to safe and clean water so we now focus and see what kind of a development intervention we are supposed to do in those areas. So we prioritize. We also analyze projects which maybe require been upgraded, where population has also increased, where some projects have outlived their design period because development of water facilities is a recycle process because there is always either demand becomes higher than the supply we have so we are kept on doing analysis to see where we can gap in on the challenges.

As it is this time of the COVID-19 pandemic there are now other issues which are been incorporated within the time of other cross-cutting issues. Other issues are being incorporated. So as I was saying, WASH, the forum, has been of more influence in terms of the county interventions we are doing and that's why I’m saying it has so many other ways especially even in terms of enhancement of collaboration and monitoring. These are also other aspects which have come out strongly as a result of the WASH Forum.
