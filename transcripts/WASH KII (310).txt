Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? 
Respondent: First, we can talk of maybe the mode of management, because you find that most of the projects mostly the community projects their management system, I think is not the right one. Then there is the element of not meeting the operation cost.  
Interviewer: Is this still under management or it is an independent challenge?
Respondent: I think it should be under the management, the operation and maintenance is supposed to be under the management. Then, maybe the technology which they use most of them use electricity and as we go to the field, you find most of the community projects which were using electricity most of them are not operational but some are now changing to solar. 
Interviewer: Does the use of solar energy improve the running of the systems? Is it better compared to the electricity you are talking about that was being used before? 
Respondent: In our case, we had worked with them when we were going to do the final inspections, so, I think it is improving, because if you see they are changing from electricity and diesel to the solar, then I think there is some element of change. 
Interviewer: Any other challenges?
Respondent: Another challenge is reduction of borehole yields. Some boreholes used to provide high yields but you find that some of them now are dry. This one maybe can be attributed to the climate change

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: One, there is the need for capacity building of the water supply management committees on operation and maintenance and financial management. You find that most of them when something breaks down, they are not able to replace it because they do not have money though they are selling the water. The other one is using appropriate technology. 
Interviewer: Which kind of technology would you recommend as appropriate? 
Respondent: In Kitui, I think that solar energy is appropriate. For wind, I do not know because I have only seen one place where they were using wind energy for pumping water from a borehole but did not follow up to know its performance. 
When we talk about climate change and reducing yields, we should have enhanced catchment conservation.

Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Capacity building. I think that one should take the lead. Then after that we adopt the appropriate technology. 
Interviewer: If the capacity building of the water management committees were to be done, could you walk us through the next steps that would happen, in terms of water services sustainability?
Respondent: I think the sustainability would be improved because the committees would be able to manage their utilities properly. 
Interviewer: Could you expound more on that? 
Respondent: They would be able to manage their finances properly; they also will be able to manage the system itself properly. 
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: In our case what we do, when groups are applying for the permits to use the water, we ensure that first of all the group is registered and they have a constitution. If they are registered and have a constitution, we also request to see the list of members; the membership of the group ours now stops at that. We do not go further to follow up on how they employ their constitution, since it mostly touches on service provision which is regulated by the county government through provisions and guidelines developed by the Water Services Regulatory Board. 
Interviewer: So, you do not follow up after user groups register? 
Respondent: No. We just need the registration to see that they are registered, there is a constitution and now we have the membership. In our case, we give permits for the service providers or those who are offering the services to be able to use the resource, to go and give the water to their customers. Once we have given them the permits and ensured they are registered and they have a constitution, the rest is the County’s work. Ours is now to go and see whether they are complying; a group has said they are going to abstract 20 m3, to see whether they are abstracting the amount on the permit or whether they are over abstracting because over abstraction is illegal. We also monitor whether they have meters to control the abstraction and whether they are keeping the record of the water abstracted per day.
Interviewer: Is it being done in Kitui County? Are they complying? 
Respondent: Yes, they are complying because for us to give a permit there has to be a meter; even if somebody has completed the works and during our inspection, we find that there is no meter we give them a period to install the meter because it is a requirement. If they do not install the meter even if they have a permit and we give them that period and they fail to install the meter we can use that a basis of cancelling that permit. This is our new approach for enforcement.
