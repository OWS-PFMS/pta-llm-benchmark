1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: The first challenge that affect sustainability of water service is absence of strong water management committees (WASHCos) in rural areas. In principle when the government or NGOs develop water schemes in rural areas, WASHCos will be formed to manage the schemes. The water management committees composed of seven members of which five of them are responsible for the management of the scheme and two of them are responsible for minor maintenance of the schemes. However, these committees do not dispose their responsibility properly. Because of this, the water schemes do not serve the community for long period due to minor problems.
On the other hand, the woreda water offices do not provide the required support to rural water management committees (WASHCos). The water office do not regularly monitor the water schemes and provide technical support for WASHCos. In general, the monitoring system of the woreda water office is very weak.
Interviewer: Are there any other problems related to sustainability of water and sanitation service in the areas? 
Respondent: In pastoral areas, the community lack awareness on management of water schemes. For instance, they do not contribute money that will be used for maintenance of the schemes when they become broken. They simply use the schemes when they are functional but they are not willing to cover the regular maintenance costs.
Interviewer: Do you have anything to say related to sanitation service?
Respondent: Relative to the water service the sanitation situation of the zone is in a better condition. This is mainly because the government has a health extension structure up to village level. The health extension workers regularly teach the community about basic health and sanitation problems. Moreover, our organization in collaboration with the health office conducted a campaign to avoid open defecation and to create areas that are free from open defecation. In general, the sanitation service is in a better situation and there is a gradual improvement in the public awareness.
However, in some areas the latrines constructed by the community do not serve for long period due to use of improper materials or poor quality materials. The sanitation marketing system is not developed in the area.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned that the water management committees (WASHCos) are not strong and do not dispose their responsibility properly and this affects the sustainability of water schemes in rural areas. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: There should be regular capacity building training for WASHCos. To this end, the woreda water offices should regularly visit rural kebeles and identify gaps related to water schemes management and take appropriate action to address the gaps immediately. 
The woreda water offices argued that they have logistic problems and high staff turnover for regular monitoring of rural water schemes. Thus, the regional government and zonal administration should capacitate the water offices by providing logistics, recruiting qualified labor and introducing incentive system for the staffs.
Interviewer: You have indicated that in some areas, the community lack awareness on management of water schemes and even they are not willing to contribute money for maintenance of water schemes. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: One of the problem for unwillingness of the community to contribute money for maintenance of water schemes is that the water scheme management committees (WASHCos) are not transparent and some of the committees abuse the public money. Because of this, the community lack interest to contribute money for water schemes. Therefore, we should regularly monitor WASHCos and push them to open bank account for the water scheme management and properly deposit the collected money and use transparently. In addition, we should provide awareness enhancement training to the community on the management of water schemes to create sense of belongingness.
Interviewer: Related to sanitation, you have raised that the latrines constructed by local community are not durable or do not serve for long period due to poor quality of the materials used for construction. What do you suggest to alleviate this problem? 
Respondent: We have to work to introduce sanitation marketing in rural area. We should also facilitate access for sanitation materials with reasonable price in rural areas. It would also be good to construct model public latrines in the areas so that the local community can gain experience about construction of proper latrines. There should also be awareness enhancement training on construction of durable latrines with the help local materials.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I would like suggest that strengthening WASHCos is the most important solution and the first priority area to make water schemes sustainable. Most of the problems of the water schemes are simple and can be managed by WASHCos. Thus, if we capacitate the WASHCos by providing continuous capacity building trainings and equip them with required basic maintenance tools, we can ensure the sustainability of the water schemes. Moreover, if we can create strong WASHCos, we can reduce the burden for woreda water offices for the management of water schemes.
