
1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Respondent: The biggest challenge we are facing now is that there is severe budget problem in the woreda as water service requires huge capital budget. We are currently experiencing massive budget deficit. For instance, even though this year’s capital budget is around 500,000 birr yet we can’t pay salary of our staff for more than nine months. 
Interviewer: Why is the woreda allocating enough budgets to other sectors but not for the water sector?
Respondent: the woreda is doing its best. Nowadays, water sector is one of the prioritized sectors during budget distribution; in fact, other sectors are envious of the budget we get, because they think NGOs also support the sector, but in reality that is not true. 
Respondent: Another problem is shortage of manpower. 
Interviewer: What do you mean?
Respondent: There should be skilled workers in conducting assessment and design, preparing documents and fund rising. During construction the projects require at least three people. After the project is completed, there should be at least eight to nine staff members who should manage it. Nonetheless, if you look at the number of current staff, it is not more than 10% of the required number. In our woreda, there is no expert who manages assessment and design activities. We only have one female construction expert, which should have been at least three or four in reality. We only have one maintenance expert. This is directly linked to the shortage of budget.
The third problem is a long standing attitude problem and lack of awareness among stakeholders. The problem of water service is not merely the problem of the sector. For instance, in the agriculture and natural resource sector, all woreda administration cabinet participates in mobilizing the local community; we have not seen this in the water sector yet. Besides, there is dependency on NGOs or the government within the community itself, many community members think it is the role of the NGOs or the government to build, manage and maintain water schemes. Local community doesn’t have the practice of properly handling, utilizing and maintaining completed water schemes. 
The fourth problem is that currently clean water coverage of the woreda is only 26%. Most of the water schemes are built by NGOs, but these days, due to the COVID-19 pandemic, most of them are not active. We still have some rural kebeles with 0% clean water coverage.
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have told me the problems clearly; as you are part of the solution, what would you say about the solution for the four problems you mentioned? What should be done?
Respondent: there are some problems beyond our capacity. In our woreda the opportunity we have but couldn’t potentially utilize it is the existence of many springs. Kebeles that necessitate shallow well or boreholes well are not more than four. Therefore, we can build water schemes with less budget than what is normally is needed. 
For instance, we are discussing with some NGOs such as SLM on how to build three water schemes with the cost of one water scheme by some technical and financial adjustments and strong community participation.
Interviewer: What about the long lasting coordination or synergic gap?
Respondent: Demanding water service does not solve the problem. The local community should be able to contribute their own share. So, the attitude that ‘somebody will build water schemes for me’ should be changed from the community. We should work on that. Stakeholders have also the same problem. They should take part in the efforts that the water sector put in to address water problems. Discussions are crucial to identify problems and roles that we should take. This could be considered as a solution. 
Interviewer: well, have you addressed the problem related to human resource?
Respondent: the issue of human resource is difficult to solve because of lack of budget. The solution I propose is, however, assigning additional tasks to currently existing staff members. In addition, we can try to hire one or two additional staff per year if possible. 
Interviewer: This is temporary solution, what do you think is the permanent solution?
Respondent: permanent solution is discussion with finance and woreda administration to at least allow for us to hire one or two additional staff members every year. We should have the budget at hand to hire new experts.
Interviewer: With regard with the coverage, you have mentioned it is still 26%, this is horrifying figure, what should be done to tackle this? Is there anything that has been done yet?
Interviewer: the solution for this is the solution we proposed to address the budget problem. We should coordinate our resources to reduce non-functionality rate of water schemes. If we can change some of the non-functional ones functional, the coverage will automatically increase. 
The other thing is identifying issues that can be addressed with little expense and work on these issues rather than those that require huge expense. As I have mentioned earlier, we have springs that do not require huge budget. So, we can use this opportunity by discussing with stakeholders. Local community should also be able to construct some of these with its own budget, before expecting external body. For instance, if the community provides sand, and government could provide cement, we can build water schemes with little workmanship expense easily. We should do the simple things first.
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: you have already mentioned the most important solution; I think it is solving the budget issue, right?
Respondent: Yes, you are right.
Interviewer: I would like to know what challenges the budget issue would solve. 
Respondent: if the issue of budget is solved, everything would be solved. Our staff members are having hard time to get allowances to go to the rural kebeles and conduct maintenance of water schemes because we have budget and/or cash shortage in the woreda. If the budget issue is resolved, we can also address issues related to spare part, awareness creation, and maintenance.
