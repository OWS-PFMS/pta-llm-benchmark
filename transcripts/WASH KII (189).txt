1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: There is sanitation problems in our area. That is open defection is very common practice. The community is not willing to clean their surroundings. Understanding this problem the health extension department of the woreda health office is trying to provide trainings to the community on sanitation and hygiene. However, the community do not practically implement what they have learned from the health extension workers.
Interviewer: What other problems are there related to sanitation and water service in the area?
Respondent: The water schemes developed by the government and NGOs are not sufficient for the community. Because of this, in some areas people use river water and this predispose them for various water borne diseases.
On the other hand, there is lack of spare parts to repair the existing water facilities. There are no maintenance tools that can be used by local communities. Even though we have tried to collect money from some willing community members to purchase maintenance tools and spare parts, they are very expensive and beyond our financial capability. Due to this problem, most of the water schemes in the kebele are not providing service to the community.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned that the community lack awareness on sanitation and not willing to practice what they have learned from the health extension program. What do you suggest to alleviate this problem? 
Respondent: Sanitation issue should not be left for woreda health office alone. Other WASH stakeholders should also engage in awareness enhancement campaign on sanitation. On the other hand, we have to award those households that practically implement what they have learned on sanitation. I think this would motivate other members of the community.
Interviewer: You have also raised that there are no sufficient water schemes in the kebele and it is difficult to develop water schemes by local labor. What do you recommend to alleviate this problem?  
Respondent: The existing water schemes are shallow wells and provide service to small number of households. Thus, I would like to suggest that the government and NGOs should invest in the development of deep boreholes in different parts of the kebele. Boreholes can serve large number of household relative to shallow wells. 
Interviewer: You have also mentioned that there is serious shortage of spare parts and maintenance tools or equipment. What do you recommend to alleviate this problem? 
Respondent: The water users associations should increase their members’ monthly contribution. Unless we mobilize funds from water users, we cannot ensure the sustainability of the water facilities. We should not expect support from the government and NGOs to sustain the service of the water schemes. Once we get the infrastructure, the community should be responsible for regular maintenance of the schemes.
However, the government and NGOs should facilitate access for spare parts and tools with reasonable price in local areas. Moreover, they should form micro enterprises that supply spare parts in local areas with reasonable price. 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The most important solution to ensure sustainable water service in our areas is that we should solve the problems related to access to spare parts and maintenance tools. If we have access for spare parts and tools with reasonable price in our locality, the water schemes can serve the community for long time and we can protect our society from using river waters that predispose them for water borne diseases.
