
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: Through networking with stakeholders, for example water ministry, sub-county water office, you are able to understand the technical issues like now training water management committees, also getting licenses from WARMA.  That gives good reputation that you’re working the right way.  

Interviewer:  My second question is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining rural water services in the county?

Interviewee: One of the major problems is the management of the water sources.  Committees have a major problem of embezzling the funds and making their stations not work.  Also, lack of knowledge – the technical skill of how to manage the equipment – I think that is another problem.  Secondly, few water sources, and most of them are not-functional – we have few, and as there are few, not all of them are functional.  So those are challenges.

Interviewer:  Any other?

Interviewee: The other one is of course funds - funding.  Funding is a challenge.  Funding from donors to support and put up the facilities.

Interviewer:  And my third question is “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?”

Interviewee: I think first of all is to ensure the stakeholders, the government agencies, support to make sure the few water resources we have are well managed.  Secondly, is about the training.  Training of the people who man the water sources.  The other one is the technical knowledge, the technical skills.  The government and the county should have experts who support the local people to manage the water sources.  I think the other one is about research.  Maybe our donors are willing to support us to have the water sources like the boreholes, but we need resourceful people like maybe the geologists.  We have had instances were we have clay boreholes and we need research to identify the places where we have rechargeable boreholes, rechargeable places.  

Interviewer:  My fourth question is, “of the solutions you have listed, which is the most important?”

Interviewee:  I think the most important is the technical – to maintain the water sources in a good condition.  Even if we have few, let them be in good condition.  You can say, functionality.    

Interviewer:  Can you walk me through the next steps that would happen if this solution were to occur?

Interviewee:  There would be consistency in water supply.  You’ll see we have few and then some failed, because people do not have enough knowledge to keep them running everyday.  The other one is about now the training, training for the people who are running.   Technical and finance for sustainability.    

Interviewer:  You said that you consider technical knowledge is the most important.  If this technical knowledge was to be available, what else would happen?

Interviewee:  No breakdown.  When we have the consistency – at least you see when you have one thing and you maintain it very well.  Also there will be good reputation, even the donors will be willing to support more.  But if they put up one, and then they come and find that that one is already failing, then they’re not willing to give more.
