1. In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation / Water service sustainability in your woreda?  Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation / water service sustainability?
 Nowadays, there are water scheme constructions being constructed widely, but there is still very low water coverage.  We're talking about how this coverage needs to be improved at different times. In terms of making the service sustainable, the community is primarily looking for sense of ownership.  Water schemes maintenance committees have already been set up for them, but they are still suffering from challenges related to operation.  It would be better to start by saying that lack of ownership is the main problem.  I have to say that the water sector often suffers from corruption because the infrastructure of the water sector is often damaged. Therefore, lack of awareness is by far the main problem for the problem of water service sustainability. In addition to this, even the existing water schemes are not well functioning owing to lack of readily available technical experts.  
 Interviewer: Why are they damaged frequently?
 Although the problems are simple, many of the institutions are frequently damaged due to the lack of maintenance timely .There are many water schemes that are nonfunctional now. And that is almost our second problem.
 The third problem I assume is that spare parts are not available when needed or one has to travel to regional cities to buy these spare parts.  Those accessories can be very cheap, but what matters is that we can't find them easily. In the capital of this zone, Jinka, spare parts are not available unless we go to Arbaminch or Addis Ababa (some 200 or 700 kms away from the town, respectively).  Going there for small items is not logical in terms of the time we are spending and financially not feasible. 
Limited and inaccessible water sources are also challenges in this zone.  Recently, there are people who fetch and drink water from rivers/springs in the countryside. People who use water out of sand by being together with livestock are also common in the outskirts the town.  Water sources are not widely available especially when we go far away from towns to the lowland areas. 
There are problems related to personal hygiene and community sanitation.  One of the reasons for this is the nature of the rural life itself, majority of the people being farmer/pastoralist. They are spending most of their time with their animals and farms and there is no way to care for their sanitation

What do you think is the cause of   this problem? 
The main cause is lack of awareness in personal hygiene. The second problem is lack of water itself.  Lack of sufficient water may lead to have poor hygiene. 
 2. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
 Awareness creation is vital in order to increase the sense of ownership for the services.  Earlier, I raised the issue of the lack of water facilities, but even in areas where the facilities are available there are problems with apathy, and misuse.  Therefore, in areas where these facilities are available, there should be increased awareness creation programs to enable the community to utilize water resources efficiently. 
 There are good beginnings in some of the kebeles. Some people are taking initiatives by themselves to train people to use the service properly. We should encourage these people, train them and upgrade their skills so that they can be models for others. We can organize people by village so that it is easier to manage. 
By organizing funds from different sources, we should increase the number of boreholes we have now especially in rural areas. Water facilities must now be expanded.  Not only is it the government's way, but also the humanitarian organizations do a great deal.  It is still to strengthen this work.  The government has to take this responsibility and one of our problems is that there is no fixed budget. It is clear that the government budget alone can’t bring changes because construction of water schemes by itself costs large sum of money. It is only through joint actions, involving the community (may be with labor), the government and NGOs that we can achieve this mission. There is of course some water schemes made available by the NGOs with little involvement from the government. This is what we need. 
 3. Of the solutions you listed, which one is the most important?  Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Community awareness; Once we have informative community members, it means that sanitation and hygiene services will be automatically improved. People also start taking care of their facilities. This is sustainability for me.
