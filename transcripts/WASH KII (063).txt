
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:  One thing that I know, in this county we have a water shortage that is for sure.  Water is a problem in the county, especially in the offseason when the water reservoirs have dried .  People walking long distances to access water.  Many people have taken advantage of that – saying that maybe they can do water projects in Kitui, but you find that most of the water interventions which are done are not sustainable, simply because one, you do a water infrastructure and you don’t involve the community, so the community is like “this is your project”.  The community are not trained on how to run that water facility, so immediately after the donor withdraws the project dies.  You find that this water structure has some components which wear out.  This community is not aware that at some point these components will need to be replaced.  Even if they’re selling this water to the community, the management of the money which is collected are not managed well.  It ends up in people’s pockets.  When a small thing breaks down in this structure, that can make the structure not work anymore, because they do not have anything.  That’s the time they’ll start visiting the offices in the county, because “we have this problem and we want this structure to be repaired”.  But maybe that was not in the budget of the county – maybe it was a very good structure left by the donor.  But due to the component of management, which was lacking, this project ends up becoming a […]. 

What is working well, and what I think will assist the county, is that maybe this water could be managed by companies, not community.  Communities have been trained over and over but most of the projects managed by the communities are not working.  But the few which are run by the companies are better.  I cannot say they’re good or best, but they are better off. What they need is a little support.  Water is controlled in terms of pricing to the companies.  You’ll find one jerry can of 20 L sells at 2 KES, and once you calculation the amount you get from the sale of water and the power required to push that water to where it’s being consumed, sometimes they can’t break even.  We need to put it somewhere it can sustain itself in terms of financing.  I think privatizing of the water sector is the only way to sustain water not only in Kitui but most of the counties.

One thing I’ve known is that there is a lot of water thefts.  Water is not like electricity – people fear electricity, they’ll be electrolyzed or whatever.  But water, people can do connections at night – they dig and do their own connections.  The most wrong thing is that these connections are done by people who are involved in that water, who work in that community scheme, who call themselves the plumbers engaged by the community are the people who do illegal connections.

The other thing I think can work, there are those auto-water selling machines.  Yes, those ATMs kind of.  I think those ones can sustain water.  Because no one will handle money.  When people handle money, it ends up people’s pockets.  But if the county can install those in water structures, you just load your whatever, your sim card or whatever, and then you come and you draw water and you go home and you come tomorrow.  

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:  The water quality is a challenge because most of the boreholes, Most times the chemical analysis, most of it is contaminated.  It’s not fit for human consumption.  Although communities go ahead and use it.  Last year we have done two boreholes in Mwingi, after taking it to government chemists for analysis, they think that water is not fit for human consumption.  In Kitui the groundwater has high minerals, so that is also.. you’ll find that you can do what they call reverse osmosis, but it is very expensive.  So that is another challenge.  Even the quantities of that water is not enough.  Most of the communities depend on open water reservoir, and there’s a lot of evaporation in these hot months, July August September October so it ends up drying very fast. That forces them to trek long distances to get water. 

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee:  One, I think, you know most times the donors or the partners because of the little resources, they may want to do an earth dam somewhere, but the money budgeted for that activity are not much, so you just do a water pan – but you know a water pan won’t last.  My suggestion would be, if different partners can bring their resources together and do one mega reservoir, so you’re sure when it rains that reservoir can last two months without other rains coming.  Then you can follow-up in another sub-county and do another one.  Then over time you’ll find you have bigger reservoirs in each of the 8 sub-county.  But that can only be possible if we bring our resources together.  Caritas, we have our small funds, we go and do our small earth dam, like 10-15,000 cubic meters, but that will not last 3 months after it rains.  [If] We do something like 100,000 cubic meters, that one will not go anywhere.  That’s for water quantity.

For the quality, I would suggest we go for run-off water harvesting, rather than that water which you don’t know the mineral it contains.  You can do a borehole, and it could be dry and you have pull at the money.  Me, I would advise we go for the run-off harvesting water.  And each household can be envouraged to have that roof harvesting, at least 5000 L to 10,000 L, that one can take a family of around 6 members 2-3 months.  We are blessed because we normally get 2 rains per year.  December rains can be used for Jan, and Feb.  In March it rains again, that will push you for the next 3 months.  Then you only have a small period where you are strained.

Capacity build the community.  Train the community.  Train the water users associations so that they know the operations, maintenance of their water structures.  They may be also trained on the issues of financial management.  Let them open accounts for the projects.  Let them not make one of them to be the bank, because that’s the mistake they do.  They just give money to one of them so that later they can go to his home and borrow money.  They know you have money from the community, so they’ll come and borrow that money from you – you’ll not deny them because they know it’s there.  

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee:  The most important is capacity building.  Knowledge is more important than anything else.  

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee:  You’ll have water in the community.  Even if not throughout the year, you’ll have an improvement.
