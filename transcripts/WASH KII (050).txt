
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: FundiFix adopts community projects and it is easier, because they have the technical expertise, so they sustain them, whenever a failure comes in, they are ready to respond and I think that is a good thing. The relationship between the office of the assistant director and the community schemes also works well because whenever the item required is beyond what the community have in their savings, the assistant director’s office always looks for a way to sort them out. Actually, all projects originate from the assistant director’s office and then they are handed over to community schemes then the community I supposed to raise money to sustain the project. The community is also directly under the sub-county water offices, so it is the office of the assistant director through the sub-county water office sorting out the community schemes, so I would say that that hierarchy or chain works well. 

Tanathi also funds projects and afterwards they hand the projects over to the County through the community and they also rehabilitate projects that are broken down.

Other places like in Kitui South, Sweden Mutomo does new projects and they look at making them less costly in terms of running so that when they hand them over, whatever revenues generated can sustain them and the savings can expand a scheme. They also hand the projects over to the community, though they work independently from the county. They form committees and…I also think they do a better job because before they completely hand the project over, they take about 6 months. They do not just commission and handover. They take about 6 months, training and observing before they release it fully to the community. 

Catholic Diocese also finances projects and they also hand them over to communities and I think…My understanding of thing is that the client is one. It is Kitui County. It is anyone in Kitui who benefit from water. So if it was financed by Caritas or by the County Directorate, or Tanathi, or Sweden Mutomo, I think the end user is…it is managed by a committee at the community level.

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The main problems would be…though nowadays it is getting sorted out slowly, it would be the cost of running a project, especially if it is a diesel-run project, where you are using a generator, you would need to sell the water at a slightly higher rate than someone using solar so that you can meet the cost of buying the diesel.

I also think that there is a lot of politicking at the community level when funds are…when a project starts generating funds. Communities will change the committees every so often, with the mentality that whoever is on the committee is eating up money.

The other thing would be…I would say, donor funding especially for people like…ok, it is not really a problem, but it is a challenge…especially for projects that are perceived to end up being challenging to keep running. An example is when you have a borehole, and the water from the borehole ends up being faulty and you need to so some form of treatment. So, you are already trying to run the borehole and also provide cleaner water. So, especially for donor funded projects, they look at it and they wonder if it is worth pursuing that venture. 

I think it also changes depending on whoever is in power. There is a budgetary allocation for water, and whoever comes in like in the last year when we changed the governor, and she has a budget proposal, so you can have this amount allocated for water and she is more geared towards health or she is more biased towards water, so it can work either way. …but I wouldn’t say it is really a problem, it is just a vision that someone has and the way they break it down, if you are looking to finance water projects or rehabilitate. 

Something else that I noted and should have told you about the relationships, I think the County also gets partnerships with World Vision and Kenya RedCross and I think they work well, coz these are donor-funded projects, especially when the budget from the national government is not enough to sort out what the county needs to be done in terms of water, that works well.

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: Problem number one, is the cost of running, nowadays it is easier because we have more sustainable solutions where you can use solar power and that makes the running cost really low. You can also have wind…though wind does not really work well in Kitui, it is still under exploration.

Number two, which is politicking, I would say that all these community projects are handed over to the county…they should all be made accountable to the sub-county water office and they should have audited accounts (for lack of a better word) so that there is a lot of transparency in terms of whatever happens with the money generated from the project.

I also think that before they are handed over completely especially from the county, they should take a lot more time when they are running as core partners; the county and the community until the community is properly trained on how to manage the project.

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: The second one about the committee…the governance issues. I am picking that one because, way before solar energy came, we still had these schemes running under generators and running under whatever and way before devolution came, these schemes were still there and they were being sustained. So what has changed? These schemes were there, were being sustained by money from the committee or from the collections generated. Now, when the devolution…when the counties came, it is like people have gone back to “serikali saidia serikali saidia” (government help us, government help us) yet when you are making money you are not remitting it to the same county government. That is why I am thinking, if it was better organized, you can say that you have raised a certain amount of money and you just need a top up from the county instead of asking the county for a whole rehabilitation project or a whole broken down generator yet you have nothing in your coffers. 

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: there would be more accountability and a lot of trust within the community. The community would own the project 100%. Yes. That is my perception. So that you know that whatever you are rehabilitating directly benefits you and no one is swindling money from whatever you…and you would also have lesser cases of sabotage and lesser cases of “he said she said”. You would minimize the petty things that are usually coming up every so often.
