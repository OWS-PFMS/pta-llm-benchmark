I1: In your opinion, what do you think has ensured Kamuli has regular running clean water?
R: Regular maintenance of the boreholes. Supervision of the boreholes on a monthly basis and repairing them on time. 
I1: Are there hindrances in trying to maintain constant clean water flow in Kamuli despite the system in place? 
R: Community members don’t want to clean their jerrycans (poor borehole and household hygiene). They don’t want to attend meetings either yet it’s where problems are aired and solved. Collection of money from the community for maintenance is difficult as they can’t afford it or don’t want to pay at all. 
I1: What recommendations can you share with us to do away with these problem?
R: Community sensitizations and blocking off the water source until it is properly cleaned. 
I1: Which of these recommendations do you believe is more important?
R: Blocking off the water source. People are triggered that way either to pay or clean up. 
I1: Do you believe the current maintenance system is effective?
R: Yes. It is effective. By the mechanic constantly coming by to check the water pump, it is not allowed room for failure and that has reduced our water hardships. 
I1: What do you think is lacking in this maintenance system though? 
R: Please provide solar pumps as promised to reduce the pumping delay and lining up. This will reduce lining up and fighting at the water pump. 
I2: How many people use the pump?
R: About 500. 
I2: How many jerrycans are taken per household a day?
R: 5 a day for a household.
I2: How often do people line up?
R: When people come back from the garden and it triples when children come back from school.
I2: How much do people pay per jerrycan?
R: About 50 shillings.
