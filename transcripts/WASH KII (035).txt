Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: I think that one of the challenges is with the tariffs because they price the water in such as way that it is supposed to be affordable to everyone. You see now with such tariffs they can’t raise adequate money to sustain that project; they are not able to pay a watchman to guard the facility or to pay a sales attendant and to also save some money in case of a break down. They always need to get funds for from other entities like the county and other organizations. I will give an example. There is a project we did in Athi ward in Kitui South sub-county and after sometime they were calling me thinking that I am the one to provide money to pay for a watchman. This is because they are selling water but they are unable to pay both a watchman and an attendant, they can only pay one; they can’t pay both because of how much they are selling their water at. They were charging 5 shillings for a 20 litre jerrican but this was not adequate because of the nature of the project; it had chemicals dozers. It had chlorine and flocculants so buying all that plus getting money to pay salaries was quite challenging to them. 
I also think that another challenge is the availability of alternative water sources. You can set up a project near a major water source which may make it not very marketable to the population because if you do a project in a town like Athi and the main river is flowing through the area and before this project the people were used to digging or scooping shallow wells along  the river or a nearby channel, they may not actually draw water from the project. They may draw water from the facility but the facility may not reach the full potential because you had targeted a certain number of people then you get just about a small percentage of that buying water because they have alternative sources. The facility will not raise enough revenue to sustain it.
I think the mindset of the people in the sense that projects or facilities should be maintained by the county or by the organizations that set them up is a challenge as well. If they are not educated and told that the project after handing over is theirs, then nobody takes real ownership of the project. If it breaks down, nobody is bothered with the repairs and so it fails.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: You get a strong committee so that after the project is commissioned, the committee can take over the project and you keep monitoring them for some time. There is an organization in Mutomo with which we have done projects and most of the projects succeed because once we commission and hand over, we take up to three months training them and then the NGO keeps monitoring and checking their accounts, their figures and those are the projects that I have seen which run for even three…four years before there is a breakdown at least because there is a strong committee, if you don’t do that then you will have all these politics, he said she said, this one stole money, this one took this.
Interviewer: When you say a strong committee, what would you say are the characteristics of a strong committee?
Respondent: The constitution of the people maybe you get teachers, or people who have been involved in planning, you don’t just get anybody because he is from there or because he is a neighbor to the borehole, yes something like that and somebody who is responsible and accountable, someone who can give a breakdown of how the money was banked, spent or saved.

The issue of tariffs or pricing may not be very easy to deal with especially in a case where the elect leaders talk about affordable water and they say that it should be priced at 2 shillings yet it is a project that even involves buying of chemicals to treat the water. It may not be very practical to sell the water at a higher price because it’s something that the local leader has influenced, I can’t quite say that you have a lot of options or ways to solve that, but you can look for better ways like powering systems through solar which doesn’t have running costs as compared to powering a system through generators I think here it is important to simplify the system so that it has few consumables. Basically, lowering the operation costs.
On the issue of changing mindsets, that has a lot to do with training and it’s more like what the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) tells us, “Pay taxes; be independent”. Organizations just need to tell the community that the projects actually belong to the community. If the projects fail, it is the community that will suffer through disease outbreaks, women going for long distances to fetch water, you will need your donkeys to go all over the place to get water and such. That is something that requires training and sensitization and more like what I was telling you about the organization in Mutomo. You just take a lot of time training and sensitizing them until they feel it and understand the purpose. You don’t just handover and take photos and everybody is happy and then after three…four months they call you to say that the project is not working.  Same thing, when a project is implemented; people need to be told that it’s something that is manmade. It requires to be taken care of because it is not something that you can give a lifetime guarantee. It’s not something made by God, if it is manmade it can fail. That’s my thinking, whether it is a genuine machine from Europe or from America or fake machines from China, every system every machine just requires to be taken care of.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent:  I think the one on training the population. I would pick that as the most important. Once you’ve trained people, then you instill responsibility unto them and when they learn that they are responsible, then they can come up with proper pricing for their water. It won’t be influenced externally and they can also look for cheaper ways of running their projects by cutting down on cost and they will also be able to create a kitty; some savings in case the project fails then they will have some money even if they need to be boosted by the County or an NGO or by a donor they have already a certain significant percentage of what they need. Also, if they are trained, we can change their mindset, then it doesn’t matter whatever you install whether it is generator powered or solar powered or it has chemicals, whether it is a complex system or a simple system they will run it successfully.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: We are largely talking about community projects but I also do individual projects which are pretty easy for me because I get to recommend the best because when somebody comes to me I talk to them and I find out their needs then I come up with a solution for them then it’s up to them to choose the options that I recommend. In the case of the county, we advise the county on emerging technologies and their appropriateness for the projects within the county. We also design systems for the county and train them on the technologies that are emerging in the sector. Once I design and train them, they implement the projects and I can say that it has worked because it has lowered the running costs for most of these projects but there is still a lot that needs to be done in terms of enlightening them, that is the sub-county water officers and the communities too because most people think that now once you install a solar system, then everything is sorted. At the schemes you will at times find them letting the pump run throughout the day, it goes off at night, then starts pumping the next day. There is no system that can run like that. Sooner or later it will break down whether it is from Europe or from the USA; it does not matter. Like I said, it is manmade so it can only be pushed to a certain limit. 
We also train the attendants at the scheme level but then more often than not, you find that after three months the attendants changed because there was someone suspecting that they are misappropriating money or someone is benefiting from the scheme wrongly or someone wants their son to be employed as the attendant. Just some issues here and there. I think that where there is money there requires a lot of transparency. We do training them although I won’t say it is enough because most of what happens with contractors I don’t think there is a provision for training in their costing because training is not just a one day session it should be something that is continuously being done so if we have finished with the project, we go there with the sub county water officer, the contractor is there if as an organization we are sub contracted we also attend and then we do serious training. The sub county water officer is the one who institutes a committee and checks in to make sure that the committee is functional.
