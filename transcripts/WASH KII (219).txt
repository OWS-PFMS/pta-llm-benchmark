1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Interviewer: Let’s discuss about the main problems of water service sustainability, what are they?
Respondent: One of the main problems is very low coverage of clean water in the town. Besides, the water that is currently being provided to the community has also impurity problems. 
Most public water schemes are not functioning well, and we are using private tap water at household level which is hardly available. Water in the city is being distributed in shift, but there is huge complain about it, because most of the times they release the water at night while everyone is gone, at bed. 
As the water system utilizes electric power, when there is a problem in the electricity, we might not get water for two to three months. Of course, lately 2019/2020, we have seen some improvements. 
Most of our town’s water problems get worse in the dry seasons typically after December. These are the main problems I have in mind.
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Well, let’s proceed to the solutions. Now you have mentioned clean water coverage, water quality, shifting problems etc. What would be the solutions you propose to sustainably tackle problems you mentioned? I’ve seen the color of the water for myself and it looks yellow.
Respondent: When for instance we consider Jinka town, the water GI pipes are too old, and rusty. So replacing these pipes is vital as far as we are planning for sustainability. 
Interviewer: So, the first solution is replacing the old pipes. 
Respondent: Yes, the first solution is replacing the existing old pipes. We sometimes see external objects/additives and the color of the water at that time become yellow, as you saw it. 
Secondly, Improving the water treatment system and continuous monitoring water quality is important. Because of poor water quality water-borne diseases are common in our town 
Interviewer: You have said communal water schemes that previously serving are not functional now, so to resume these water schemes, what do you think should be done?
Respondent: One solution could be maintaining broken water schemes. 
It could be helpful if we use community organizations such as kebele and villages to create sense of ownership. The community members can collect cash from the services provided by these water points and conduct simple maintenance without waiting for the government. There are some exemplary communities who have been doing so, like in my neighborhood. We might have to wait long lines but we still get water from these communal water points. If there had been more of such water schemes in the town, we would not have been stood in a long line.
Interviewer: Who can this get done? How can we make this happen?
Respondent: One thing is that if the government and local communities work together, we can do it. First, there should be continuous community mobilization program. Communities may start by contributing some money and request government to conduct assessment and construct water schemes in their neighborhood. 
Coordination among stakeholders is vital, if there is strong coordination, it is possible to resolve the issues. Because there is also a distribution problem even if the water table is not too far.
Interviewer: the other thing you mentioned was if there is electric blackout, water could wait for two to three months what would be the sustainable solution of this?
Respondent: We should think of other options other than the existing electric system such as solar power. For instance, in South Omo zone in general, majority of them are independent and use solar power. 
Interviewer: Have they started such a system?
Respondent: They have started in some places. This could be one solution. However, there should be trained technicians 

Technicians in the water office are a little bit indifferent and irresponsible. They lack to continuously monitor the distribution system. Similarly, electricity office workers do not respond quickly. For instance, when an electric transformer is damaged; replacing it takes very long time. So, they should solve this dilemma 
Interviewer: What about the solution related to replacing old GI water pipes with plastic: who should be responsible to address this issue? 
This could be addressed if local government and communities engage together, plan together and collaborate. As it requires massive investment, they should work together; it is not a problem that can be solved by the government or the community alone.
Interviewer: Hasn’t this happened yet?
Respondent: There have been some attempts to some extent. New woredas are currently using plastic pipes, but in old towns such as Jinka it is still metal or GI pipes. As water is released after long times, you have to wait for a while until the water cleans the rusty pipes.
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

Respondent: Community ownership is the most crucial solution because it will create sustainable follow up, and management of the water schemes by themselves. 
Interviewer: What would happen if community ownership is created?
Interviewer: The community will take care of the water schemes, by putting some money together and save, collecting cash from the service. Government may focus on new projects, so community will be able to maintain the existing water schemes. There should be permanent technical committee such as WASHCOs.
