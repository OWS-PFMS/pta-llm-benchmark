
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:  Without having donors injecting funds to the Miambani accounts we wouldn’t be able to survive.  Having donor providing funds is the most critical aspect of the whole thing.  Then, in terms of equipment we get from our suppliers.  We wouldn’t be able to do repairs without reliable suppliers, so that’s also critical for us.  The other one is on the county administration – the assistant director’s office and the WASH coordinator.  Initially when we went to the ground as Miambani we were only able to get 3 signups, but after the WASH forum we were able to make a break though and have over 30 sign ups.  We can’t do without suppliers, donors or administrators.  Maybe in the long run when the income flows from the community improve, we can do without the donors, in fact that’s what we’re aiming at.  

.  

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:   The main problem is to some degree the political influence.  Because people have a “free water syndrome”.  They don’t understand why they should even pay a single coin to get water.  If we can do away with that mentality from the people at the ground, then it would be very easy for us to make a breakthrough as fundifix. People will know we have duty to pay this fee, and now we get reliable services, our projects won’t break down and we won’t have to suffer.  then o. But as long as the mentality of we should get his free of charge, that’s when the problem comes in.  It’s compounded by politics – politicians say you should be getting this money free of charge.  If he is in office today and tomorrow he won’t be and he won’t be able to give the people free water.   That’s the major ones – the failure of the communities to pay, because they don’t understand the concept of sustainability.  We hope in the long run they’ll be able to understand.

Our contract has 3 parties: we have communities, we have fundifix and the county government of kitui.  The community is supposed to provide the basic manual labour to fundifix mechanics, it’s the responsibility of fundifix or Miambani to have the spare parts and skilled mechanics to fix the problem, now depending on the degree of failure, there are some challenges that are meant to be addressed the county, like the issues with generators and submersible pumps.  But the challenge with the county being our partners is the bureaucracy of the procement of these things: our contract says 5 days to fix these things, but the procurement process for the county won’t allow that.  It could take weeks, months or years. So that’s a challenge, but as of late things have gotten better.  If it goes beyond weeks, it won’t go beyond months before we get a pump or generator.  

One of the problems is that there is standard work done by contractors for the county.   Let’s say a contractor has been funded to a tune of a million (KES) to do 1,2,3 things, but he does 1,2 and leaves the 3rd one.  As fundifix, once the project is rehabilitated we are supposed to come in immediately to conduct maintenance services. If the contractor has done shotty work, then Fundifix inherits liability  that it’s not able to address.  The money given to the contractor and person who is supposed to address the issue hasn’t addressed it the way it was meant to be.

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee:  Serious supervision of the contractors.  If someone has been given money, whatever they’re supposed to do, it should be done.  But completion certifiates issued to the contractors even before the work is satisfactory.  Those are very serious challenges to fundifix.

Win political good will, without it I don’t think you can make a lot of progress.  We need political good will to make the Fundifix dream a reality.  

I recommend the money for contractors be given to Fundifix.  Fundifix is not like the contractors who are running away, because if we are the ones who do the rehabilitation, after the rehabilitation we take over the maintenance.  So that means our rehabilitation be very key because we don’t want to create liabilities.   If fundifix could be financed, and those funds channeled to our accounts, then we can take care of rehabilitation and then takeover maintenance.  It’s something to consider, because I believe we have the capacity of the contractors.  

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee:    The last one.  That will give fundifix some autonomy.  We are not running after contractors If we are allowed to do the thing ourselves, and it will be easy for us to deliver and give reliable results.    

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee:  We could increase our human resource capacity so we’re able to deal with issues of rehabilitation.  If its test pumping, cleaning boreholes – we can take care of that.  Then start to increase human resource capacity and further training of staff – that’s where we’d start.  Get the equipment and get more mechanics on board.
