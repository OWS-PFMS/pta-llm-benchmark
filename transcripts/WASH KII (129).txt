1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your woreda? 
Interviewer: what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your woreda?
Respondent: The woreda experts are not consulted when projects are constructed. As a result, contractors often use low quality materials which leads to frequent bursting of the pipes and breakdown the water distribution system as a whole. 
Another problem is the water reservoirs that are being purchased do not consider the local climate. Most of the reservoirs do not last more than six months. 
Interviewer: Why is that? Why you don’t buy the one that fits with the local climate?
Respondent: Most of the reservoirs are purchased by the regional water bureau and NGOs. So we don’t have the power to choose. We have repeatedly informed them.
Interviewer: What about the problems of clean water supply in the town?
Respondent: There is no water meter in many households and residents are paying their utility fees arbitrarily. This is because the water doesn’t have enough pressure to get through the faucet to the users. The reason for this is that the current reservoir is 140,000 liter. This is built when the residents of the town were very few. However, the population has increased but the water supply has not. The time needed to fill the tanker is increasing from time to time.
Interviewer: What about the complaints that some houses get water while others hardly see it. 
Respondent: People who get water from the main pipeline always have water. However, some locations don’t get any water unless the reservoir is full. In order to fill the reservoir the pipelines that take water to households before the reservoir is full should be cut off. And doing so has been a difficult task for the water utility office, because it might have other implications. Therefore, the reservoir could not be full which make the rest of the residents to have water in their home for not more than 30 minutes or so. That is the problem.
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have said that there is lack of water reservoir that fits the local climate in the rural areas. What would be the solution for this?
Respondent: We are trying to fix the cracked reservoirs which could just be a temporary solution. But, the permanent solution should be given by regional officials and NGOs.
Interviewer: You have also mentioned that there is lack of manpower in the woreda. How could this be addressed?
Respondent: Many sites have rather difficult road and woreda sector offices are not willing to lend vehicles because of the poor road condition. Therefore, it would be vital to have their own transportation means for the maintenance teams. Due attention should be given in terms of budget and other resources. 
Interviewer: Don’t the water utility office have a vehicle?
Respondent: No, it doesn’t.
Interviewer: But I asked the woreda administration earlier and they told me the office has a vehicle. They said it is undergoing maintenance. 
Respondent: We, the experts, have no idea about that. We do not know. We do not have a vehicle that is suitable to travel to the rural areas.
Interviewer: So, if the transportation problem is solved the problem of manpower will be addressed. 
Respondent: Yes. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: Out of the solutions you have mentioned, what would be the most important one that could solve the other problems?

Respondent: The most critical thing is adequate means of transportation. If we have vehicles we would be able to conduct monitoring and maintenance services timely and properly. The second thing is improving the amount budget for maintenance and allowances. This can solve many of the challenges coming out of lack of financing.
