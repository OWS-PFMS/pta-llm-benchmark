Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: One, we have water management committees which lack skills on how to manage the schemes. You find, when we have a committee running the project, and we don't know how the system should be operated, we have the challenge that we have the committee but they don't know what to do, at what time, sometimes water may not flow at the kiosk and they call at our office. "We have this problem, our water is not flowing,” you'll find they  are lacking skills like they don't have someone who can identify whether the pipeline has an airlock, they lack the technical team which can assist them on the right way to handle the challenges of water supplies.
Interviewer: What else? What other challenge?
Respondent: Another challenge, you find in different water projects, due to politics some of our politicians have told people they have brought water for free so they don't want to pay for this water. So you find that the community wants to use water for free which will hinder the sustainability of such projects. Because you cannot sustain a project whereby a lot of resources have been invested there and there is nothing coming for sustaining the same system. Politics have played a big role. Because when you ask the people they tell you, "We were told this water is for free and it is ours." So to change their mind so that they can come up to start paying water it has become another big challenge for us.
Interviewer: So you've mentioned about the water management as an issue, you've mentioned about political interference, you've also mentioned about lack of skills or maintenance and technical skills, so what other challenge do you think is contributing to or is affecting water service sustainability?
Respondent: Another one, as per the projects I've visited, we have another issue of land issue but it's not coming out that much whereby we find the land owner, from the start they had an agreement with the committee which is still in office but as time goes by they want to change. Which is interfering with the committee now. Maybe from the original agreement they were given a certain amount, as part of the condition amount of water, later they realize that they want more water. And now they come to the committee.
Interviewer: You mean the land owners?
Respondent: The land owners yeah. We have an issue with land ownership in our projects. You find that they come to the projects, they start arguing with the committee, and since the committee they don't have some of the mandate, they feel like it is becoming a hard issue to handle this project to an extent of saying that they want to take these projects back to the owner, who is maybe at this point, a sub-county water officer on the ground. So those are some of the challenges we face when managing these water schemes. 
Interviewer: Any other?
Respondent: There are issues of breakdowns, the major breakdowns and you find that the schemes cannot support. Like the purchase of motor, it takes like two years since it broke down. Because maybe in our system in the county maybe we don't have the program of repairing, so we may not factor such kind of repairs. And the spares need a lot of money for re allocation and which may hinder the schemes to operate as it should.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: One like the one for committees, we can train them or rather coach them on maintenance, because you find a project is complete and you just instill the committees and let them continue with the operation and yet they don't know anything. So it is good immediately after a project is complete, when we are handing it over to the committee we ensure that we train them on the basics so that later we can come and now coach them entirely on how the project should be managed.
Interviewer: Any other recommendation?
Respondent: Also, for our side to be in office we can have a program on how we can have some kit set aside for emergency because a borehole can breakdown when there is no allocation for funds. Such that we can have funds which can ensure the boreholes can run continuously, when it breaks down we can get funds immediately. Because you find the funds which we collect for water supplies they cannot sustain the major breakdowns. They can’t.
Interviewer: So you mean the county to set aside a kitty where money can be withdrawn anytime there is a breakdown?
Respondent: Yeah. In this case a major breakdown. Because the minor we can call the committees, we see what they have, and then we can repair.
Interviewer: Any other that you think is a solution to the challenges?
Respondent: On the political interference with the systems, I think we should have at least a law to guide on this issue so that the politicians don't put their hands on water systems. Because they are interfering with what is already laid there and they put in minds of the people that we are going to do this and it is not right according to the office and yet there is now…they can say we want to change this committee because of their own interest, and yet the committee was good and it was operating. That interference is causing some of those. 
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The most important one, since us as ministry we cannot be able to run the water schemes and they are so many, and I know maybe the water service providers, not all of them may be able to get all those water schemes because some of them low yield, others high, so the water service providers choose because of maybe what they are going to get. But the committees we have in the community, if they can be well supported, we can at least ensure that they are well supported in terms of capacity building. We can have systems which can be run as they should. Because that is the key area. We cannot manage those schemes as water department. We have to engage other stakeholders like the committees from the community to help us because they are so far from the office. And if we can also cluster depending on the....because we have some of the big projects in smaller other projects. So we can cluster these projects such that and we look for maybe a manager managing from one point, so that they can manage like five water projects under one umbrella so that we can have focal point of in case we have a problem, so and so can tackle a certain area instead of we are at the office and we are managing like all those schemes, it is not possible. 
Interviewer: You have mentioned about capacity building, do you have an idea on how that can be done because it has been happening?
Respondent: Yeah, we tried one in Mwingi Central and I think if we can do it at the local level, whereby we don't take these committees all the way from the village to town to come and train them at certain point.
Interviewer: A central place?
Respondent: A central place. If we can train them at their borehole area, if it is at let's say Nguni, a borehole called Kyelamoko we go to that point. Now they'll get the sense more than when we take them to town, central point yes, with many committees but some of them maybe they have stayed there for a long time coming to Mwingi, maybe the noise from the area may hinder them from getting the right skills at that time. 
Interviewer: So you mean instead of bringing the committees at a central place you mean the trainer, the facilitator to go down to their projects and train them? Onsite trainings?
Respondent:  Onsite trainings. If not so you cluster them. Don't make them come to town. Ensure that they are still at the village level.
Interviewer: In their environment?  
Respondent: Yeah.
Interviewer: So can you walk me through what next steps will happen if these committees are trained at their project sites? If they are trained so what?
Respondent: If the committees are trained we need to follow up one, on whether they have bank accounts because those will be part of the training. Whether they have certificates, whether they have registered, whether they have opened bank accounts and ensure that they have the members for the project. If they have opened bank accounts, the money they collect since we've trained them on how this money can be kept, and how they can save it so that it can be used for maintenance, we ensure that they have opened a bank account which will help save these funds and funds will be used to sustain the system once maybe it breaks down.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Some of the interventions, we are doing more of earth dam construction and if we have once it has silted, we do desilting. Simply because with the boreholes, we found that we did borehole, the water quality is not that fit, you find that the sustainability of that project is becoming an issue because some may choose to go to another source because this water quality is not fit for them. And you also find that the operation of a borehole compared to an earth dam, is a little bit cheap.
Interviewer: So which other interventions are you doing apart from the earth dams?
Respondent: We are also doing sump well construction with the installation of solar system, so that we can pipe this water to maybe a raised place, they have a water system, pipe network and kiosks from, if maybe the river we are doing the sump well has a lot of water we don't need to do a sub-surface dam. But in case the river is not that big, we do a sub-surface dam so that we can have enough water from the sump-well.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: From the last meetings we had in the WASH, it has helped us as the organization. Because there are partners like ASDF (Africa Sand Dam Foundation) who are doing only sand dams and when the sand dam has yielded enough, they do the sump well. So from that learning, we realize that from our rivers, because we have big rivers, we can do a sand dam and at the same time do a sump well which has improved our water sources. In this case we also go ahead to use solar system since there is a green energy which is not having any cost. We've learnt from the WASH and it has really improved our way of doing our systems.
Interviewer: How else?
Respondent:  It has also helped us because we've developed list and manual for coaching and training our committees, which is at least assisting us on going to the point for these committees. So that we don't train things and they are not helping the committees.
Interviewer: That came out of the WASH Forum?
Respondent: Yeah we developed something which now we can go straight. At least they revised the manual which we have been using.  And they gave us the points.
Interviewer: During the WASH Forums?
Respondent: Yeah.
Interviewer: How else has it influenced your organization's interventions? 
Respondent: It has also challenged us. Because we found that some of our projects we are investing a lot of cash but other organizations are using very little and they are achieving the same. Like ASDF are using community based kind of approach whereby community can contribute. Like construction of tanks, they contribute the local material and the organization only supports on the labor and maybe the other materials which they can't acquire locally.
