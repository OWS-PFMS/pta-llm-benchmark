
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the region?
The problems to water service sustainability are related to the climatic condition of the area. The region is very arid and it is very difficult to get ground water. In some areas we may not get water by digging deep down. There are only limited sources of water. That is there are only few rivers that cross the region.
In high land areas you can get water by digging only 4 meters, but here in Afar you may not get water by digging about 600 meter. Moreover, the water cycle is very poor here in Afar because the area get very few rainfall throughout the year. 
In addition to these natural problems, there are no coordinated bodies that work to ensure sustainable water service in the area. For example, there is water bureau in the region but this bureau has a capacity problem to address all the problems related to water service. And the existing non-governmental organizations including Pastoralist Community Development Project (PCDP) that work in the water sector are not coordinated. For example, PCDP may have some budget to construct water scheme in a given kebele but if that budget is not sufficient to develop the intended water scheme, it shift to other projects that can be accomplished by the available budget. However, if there is coordination PCDP can realize the water project by combining its resource with other stakeholders. 
There are different fragmented small water projects implemented by different organizations, however, these projects cannot bring sustainable solution to the community. But if we create coordination and combine resources we can bring sustainable solution.
There is a map that indicate areas that has good ground water potential but stakeholders do not follow this map to implement water projects in appropriate areas. For example, in areas like Afdera (one of the most arid areas of the region) instead of trying to dig boreholes it is better to supply water from areas that has good ground water potential by installing pipe lines. I think this approach is cost effective. Because in some case organizations may dig up to 600 meter and fail to get water. But if they follow the map they would not incur unnecessary costs.

Most NGOs focus on emergency activities instead of sustainable water service. Mostly they engage temporary water supply when there is drought in the area. They do not engage on the development of sustainable water services.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
First, we should identify potential water sources in the area. Second, we should know the available resources at the hand of the regional water bureau and different NGOs. And then create coordination between stakeholders to achieve sustainable solution to problems related to water service in the area.
Most NGOs functioning in the region do not involve the community in finding solution to problems related to water service. However, community involvement is very essential to ensure sustainable change. For example, the community has indigenous knowledge that can be used to solve the water service problems in the area but most stakeholders do not consult the community in finding solutions to water problems.  If we involve the community in planning and activities of water projects and create coordination between stakeholders we can bring sustainable change in the water sector.
Regarding the budget problem, for example if PCDP allocate USD 50,000, Save the Children do have USD 100,000 and other NGOs have USD 200,000, instead of engaging on different activities separately it is better to combine these resources and engage in sustainable water project. For instance, one organization dig the borehole, another install the water pump and the other work in the installation of water lines etc. Thus, collective effort can bring sustainable change in the area.
However, currently different organizations try to dig boreholes in different kebeles but because of budget constraint they do not install the water line and the water pumps. Because of these there are many idle (non-functional) boreholes in different kebeles. But if there is strong coordination between stakeholders we can alleviate the problem related to budget.
Moreover, there is no information or data about the number of functional and non-functional water schemes in the region. However, this information is very important for those organizations that have interest to engage on rehabilitation activities.

3.Of the solution you listed, which is the most important? 
The most important solution to bring sustainable change in the region is creating strong coordination between stakeholders. Because the coordination can solve the budget problem and the information gaps in the sector. Coordination can also avoid duplication of effort. Thus, there should be strong coordination from planning to implementation of projects. Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs? If there is strong coordination between stakeholders, there will be sustainable water service in the area. This is because if there is coordination we can aggregate our resources and engage in sustainable water projects. For instance, previously PCDP has failed to successfully implement its water project due to lack of budget. But if we had found an organization that coordinate with us, we could have been implemented the project successfully. Plus to this coordination can avoid duplication of effort. Thus, if there is coordination, we can ensure sustainable water service in the area.

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your region for sustainable water service?  
Honestly speaking we have coordination only with the regional water office. However, to ensure sustainable WASH service there should also be coordination with the regional health and education bureaus. For instance, PCDP construct schools in Pastoralist areas but related to development of the school WASH facilities both the regional health and education bureaus do not cooperate with us. Even our coordination with the regional water bureau is very weak. For instance, to construct water schemes PCDP do not have its own water experts that conduct feasibility study. Because of this we request technical support from the regional water office but we do not get response on time. I can say they are not that much cooperative in this regard. The regional water office do not provide information about non-functional water schemes in the region to engage on rehabilitation activities. So we have weak relation even with the main stakeholder (regional water bureau) of the water sector. Totally we have no coordination with the other stakeholders functioning in the water sector. 
What could be improved? In the first place there should be stakeholder forum on which we can evaluate our plans and activities as well as create coordination between us. We should also share tasks that can be implemented in the sector. We should also share information about implementation status of our projects.

5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the region? For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?
I don’t believe that there is no activity in the water sector. Both governmental and non-governmental organization are implementing some activities in the sector. There is also resource at hands of different stakeholders. Thus, if we coordinate these activities and resources, we can avoid duplication of efforts and achieve sustainable water service in the area.
