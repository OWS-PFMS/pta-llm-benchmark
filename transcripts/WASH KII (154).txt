
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the Afar Region?
The main problem to water service sustainability is [lack of resources]. Resource could be manpower. It may also be lack of [skilled manpower] who can give proper services. We have problem related to capacitating manpower needed to maintain and supervise water infrastructures timely. We always observe that new borehole are being constructed everywhere but there is no established strong system to inspect and supervise the water points which are already functioning and at the same time the community is not formed to be responsible. Do you mean follow up? That’s right. So, working on capacity building followed by strict supervision timely support for the existing water schemes would be the solution.
We will come to the solution, let’s finish raising the problems. What was the second problem you have mentioned relating to resource? Many of the water schemes constructed are not functional. There is attributed to the [financial problem] we have to maintain even minor disorders. So can we categorize resources in to finance and manpower? Yes. As I told you, even lack of long lasting water infrastructures and minor problems are the results of the limited capacity of the existing technicians. The existing technicians may not have the proper skill and knowledge required to maintain minor and major failures. These issues are of course related to drinking water service. Hygiene and sanitation is related with behavioral change. It’s observed that there is lack of awareness in the community. In order to make WASH systems consistent and effective, three things should come together: environment sanitation, washing hands and accessing clean water in their houses. One of the basic problems hindering the community to achieve the key behaviors of hygiene and sanitation here is the nature of the life, people are mobile. They are affected by drought and heavy wind. There are common catastrophic disasters that can damage established structures. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems you have mentioned? 
05:31
Actually, we can’t totally eradicate financial problems but we can minimize it. When we construct water services, it can be easily damaged by manmade and natural phenomena which could force people to leave from the area without getting service from the constructed water schemes and installed solar panels. 
Look, this scheme has required investing huge amount of money and other resources. On the other hand, we usually transfer established institutions to the government and the community, but no one would follow on the established structures.  Therefore, we should motivate the community to handle the supervision in a way that they are paying to the water service they use. We know that the money collected can’t afford major maintenances, but still it can be used for minor maintenances where the community could feel a sense of ownership at the same time. . 

What could be done to solve the problem on skilled manpower? In many cases, organizations build boreholes and leave them to the community after giving training to water committee. Training is needed not only for this but also for minor maintenances which could be done on the generator itself, pumps or other infrastructures. As to CARE, we are working on this. We have electro mechanical professionals separately. We are giving skill training on motorized scheme grouping, labeling individuals as ‘care takers’. It helps at least how to open and close the equipment, how to operate minor damages on the generator, checking and changing water.  These may seem very minor but they will have overall effect on the scheme’s life span. It’s important to work on such areas.  If the trainees have toolkit, manageable in their level, at least some changes would come regarding the operation processes. The other solution which has to be considered nationally is the emergence of solar schemes, which would be the best fit to regions like Afar since it is a tropical region. However, the problem is the lack of skilled manpower at the regional level which would even get worse when you go down to woredas

 How could solar scheme help in sustaining water services? It’s only the initial cost that would be very high. Once the solar scheme is installed, water can be sustainably used. There is no additional cost for fuel. In addition, if it’s handled carefully it doesn’t require tough maintenance. Ii is also environmental friendly. It requires installing panels in proper manner. There will be a reservoir that can be used as a generator. As I told you earlier, since we are starting this technology, we couldn’t have adequate skilled manpower. 

The good opportunity in afar region is that there is no underground water shortage, instead the challenge the salinity problem which can be even solved through the adoption of new technologies in the area. For example, a pilot desalination project has been started here in Afdera by CARE Ethiopia. In general, proper adoption of technologies and innovations are very helpful in the construction of water projects and their sustainability as well. 

What could be done in bringing behavioral change for sustainable water services since the community in Afar region is mobile? We are using Community Lead Total Sanitation Approach which is internationally recognized participatory approach. Even though the community’s mobility is challenging, the approach is more effective in places where there is more concentration of people.  Actually, when we say that the community of Afar is mobile, it doesn’t mean all the family members would move. 
Only young men and women move with their cattle and elders and children stay at home. Therefore, we are using the CLTS approach and creating awareness within the community following their destinations. In some areas, we are trying to hybrid CLTS with other approaches which is called the Social Analysis and Action to the Community approach. It’s based on the community conversation. We are creating awareness for the community and we use opportunities to advocate the approach on different events and campaigns. Teaching about washing hands can’t be applicable without water service because sanitation and hygiene can’t go further without water services. Therefore, our entry point to woredas is water. Continuous dialogue is very useful not only for the community but also for the health extensions and woreda experts including provision of trainings and capacity building programs. If these groups are updated, they can benefit the community in return. This can contribute a lot to the sustainability of water service.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? What would happen if the solution occurs?

15:40
I have mentioned earlier that constructing water schemes alone can’t be the solution for the sustainability issues. We have started better opportunities on water schemes. Instead of leaving the community only by providing trainings, we are opening bank accounts for the trainees. The community who shares the water service usually appoints committee which will have their own regulations. The community contributes to the account opened for the committee. This small share of contributions has great role to the sustainability of the water scheme. We also give book keeping trainings to the committee members so that they would be careful and refrain from corruption. The community pays and contributes for the water they use and this money is used for minor maintenances. Therefore, institutionalizing the WASH committee and training the committees to legalize their financial management would help improve the water service sustainability. However, in practice this kinds of activities are less in the region and there are small number of WASH committee across the region compared to the work done in other regions. The committees are established in the rural areas in other regions and the woreda will give them supportive letter to the finance office. Then they purchase receipt. So there would be water billing system in in each of the kebeles. There is no this kinds of systems here in Afar region. 

Water schemes might be constructed but the community doesn’t know that this is their responsibility and they have to pay for the water they use.  Usually, seven people having 4 men and 3 women would be trained on how to handle the money but they will be left without follow up. If there is no established monitoring system they wouldn’t work properly. There should be receipt for the money contributions because it helps them for reporting financial statements. Monitoring system has value for the institutions. 
This has to be related to bureau of finance and economic development (BoFED). The receipts for water service should be kept in the woredas (BoFED) but these kinds of trends are not common in the region. In other regions there is a water billing system which has serial number in woreda level. It is needs only providing trainings and establishing WASH committees. Unless there is well organized system of financial management and book keeping skills at the committee level, there wouldn’t be permanent solution and this requires strong commitment. 

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved? 
21:51
There is regional water task force here in Afar region which is led by the water bureau. We are working with this program and report on monthly meetings. We also present our quarterly report to the government. Accordingly, we may reproach others or we might be reproached by them so that project overlaps would be solved. In fact, the commitment of stakeholders may vary but we do have frequent communication with UNICEF. 

What could be improved? 

There is overlap of water scheme projects because we see that sometimes similar water scheme projects are given to different organizations. This may happen for different reasons; one of this is that institutions might be waiting for the fund from the donors. The other one may be lack of accountability and responsibility both from the government and non-government organizations. This has to be improved as it creates activity overlap and resource duplication.
