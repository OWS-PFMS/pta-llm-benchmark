Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: Some challenges are natural, such as climate change and it is having a toll on the operation of the schemes because I found out that there are boreholes which were indicated as being high yielding when they were constructed but now the water levels have gone down. They have to pump for some time and wait so that the levels may rise and then now restart the equipment, so that’s a challenge and the community does not get adequate water from these sources. 
The other thing is the source of power. Most of our schemes are operating on gensets and we have found out that where the gensets are being used the operation and maintenance costs are quite high.  One of the strategies of the county is to hybridize those schemes. We incorporate solar to reduce those costs and even where we have electricity, we also combine with solar so that we bring down those costs.
Facilitation of sub-county water officers is a challenge per sub county because resources are not decentralized. We require some form of facilitation so that we may carry out day to day activities including monitoring, supporting with operation and maintenance and finances to cater for recurrent expenses – that is transport. The financial resource will create a scenario where we engage the directorate for those big things like major asset replacement, which we cannot handle at the sub county level. This will reduce the time it takes to do repairs in the schemes.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: For climate change I think it is a joint venture; we may not do it on our own, we may require other agencies like the NEMA (National Environment Management Authority), water resources authority and the ministry of environment. We require to conserve the water catchment areas that one for sure we have to do it. We have to do some works to arrest or to alleviate the effects of the climate change. We are doing it, for instance we are constructing sand dams which will help raise the water table because when we construct a series of them along our water course, eventually the water table will improve so those are some of the measures. 
To reduce the operating costs earlier on I mentioned that hybridization is a solution and it is a strategy that the county is already implementing. 
Regarding facilitation generally, I think that the directorate is handling that. There are plans to purchase vehicles which can be shared by the sub-county officers and there are also plans to repair old vehicles and motorbikes. In terms of finances for the sub-county offices, there are plans to have some allocation go to the sub-county water offices from the annual allocation. The money will go to a certain account which they can use and then account for it.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: It is facilitation in terms of transport and financial resources. Once that one is done, we will be able to address these small issues – the breakdowns at the schemes and even do frequent monitoring. There are some small issues which make you look like just a figurehead at the sub-county and because of that people will just bypass you and they report directly to the headquarter because they have done it in the past and all the time when they report to you tell them to just wait and that you are waiting for something from the headquarter to address the issue. So, because of this they feel that it is as though you do not have the means to assist them. Once we have that, once we are facilitated in terms of transport and finances, I think our presence will be felt at the sub county. We will be able to run diagnosis and troubleshooting and support repairs. We will not have to wait for technicians who might be held up somewhere in an emergency that might have occurred somewhere in another sub county and they have been called from that other sub-county, then again here your scheme breaks down, instead of attending to it promptly you will have to wait, you buy time, you tell the community to wait and that your people are engaged elsewhere but if I had means of engaging specialized technicians, I can easily engage them to fix the issues and get the systems up and running within a very short time.
 Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: All the interventions that we do in terms of infrastructure projects have been put forward by the communities and we also have the County Integrated Development Plans (CIDPs). Every financial year we do planning for the projects which are going to be done that financial year but then before we select the projects which are going to be done, we have to consult and to make sure they are in line with those in the CIDP. The community have to be consulted so that they prioritize; we don’t just sit and propose projects. Before we meet at the headquarter to do the final annual planning, we are given time to engage with the communities and their leaders through public participation then now we agree. We are told beforehand how many projects according to the different categories we are going to do per ward. If it is earth dams how many are going to be done then now you divide and you know in your sub county you get how many and then again you sub divide and you know the number of projects to allocate. When you are going to meet the community and their leaders, you tell them we have this kind of projects to do in your ward so now you agree on which one will be prioritized. 
There are areas where ground water potential is poor so in that case, even if you find that they want a borehole, our technical input comes in we tell them that in as much as they may want a borehole, it will not serve them because it has been tried before and it is not working. We tell them that the ground water potential is very poor and that they should consider either an earth dam or a rock catchment. This information on the potential we get from past experiences in some of the areas and also from the hydrogeological surveys that have been carried out before. We would like to do ground water mapping so that we can accurately advice and this is something that is being discussed.
We are also training the communities. We mostly train the management committees but we are facilitated by the assistant director within his docket so once he is allocated funds we do. Where we have NGOs they also assist with the training of the management committees. On our part as the county, training is subject to availability of funds because there is no fund which has been set for that work – the budget has been thin, if it could be improved, I think this would work better.
