
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the Afar Region?
The first challenge for water service sustainability is that the community has [no/little awareness] on water and sanitary services. The second one is [less attention given to the WASH stream committee by the regional state’s administration] to the projects and offices related to water and sanitation. The third problem is that [the operation system is not integrated]. Schools and health centers are built without toilets in the previous system. There was no coordination among the government WASH stakeholders. These are the main challenges.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems you have mentioned?
03:40
One Wash program, UNICEF or any other stakeholder usually construct toilets in schools. But after one year, you will get them filled with stones or other solid wastes and it will soon be out of use. This is because nothing is done on the software of the students and teachers/awareness is not created. With improved awareness campaigns, the community would feel like it owns the water institutions. They have to know that there would be costs when it is out of use. Peeing outside the toilet and open toilets should be forbidden. I think working on the software and on awareness creating programs would bring change. The second one is that there should be strong commitment from the administrators and the WASH stream committee. 

Who are included in the WASH stream committee? In the ONE WASH program, the stream committee is lead by the regional president and the secretary is the regional water bureau head. Mainly, the committee consist the water bureau, health bureau, finance bureau and educational bureau. There is also a technical committee embedded under the steering committee that works on technical issues. This committee consist deputy head of water bureau and the deputy from health bureau. We have planned and structured the task force this way. However, it’s observed that the main officials in the committee are too busy on political commitments and for this reason other professionals are working representing them. If you need the name of the committee members; Abdruzak Mohammed from water bureau who is supply core processor; Mohammed Ahmeye from health bureau who is plan and program specialist and health bureau representative and Ibrahim from finance bureau are appointed and are managing the technical committee. Currently, ONE WASH is working in 11 Woredas and two towns. 
The people in these 11woredas consider this program as one of the NGO but it is not nongovernmental organization, it is government program. Making the people responsible to the water schemes is the stream committee’s work. Political awareness is well forewarned to the people but there is a problem in approaching the community with regard to development projects like the WASH intervention. It’s the community who should control and take care of water projects, but community awareness campaigns are not addressed particularly at the woreda level. There is also commitment gap in the stream committee itself; the members give attention to other administrative and political agendas. 

What is the solution to improve attention by the committee?
First, the WASH coordination office should have regular monitoring and evaluation system to control the efficiency of the committee and the projects. This WASH Program should be reconsidered as one of the major national programs and the stream committee should be influential. For instance, there are lots of works that are left without completion by WASH program. For sustainability to happen the projects should be supervised, controlled and evaluated and political solution should be given from by the stream committee. Is the other problem lack of integration? In the near past the WASH activities were not integrated and there were no toilets at school level. But now, in village program settling, there are toilets for every school.. The health centers have sanitation and other infrastructures needed. We have decided not to allow settlement for the community in places there is no water service. What about other sectors other than health and education? There is still gap on the other sectors. We select and work in similar woredas with the health sector and evaluate and monitor the challenges together. We have information about their plan. The health sectors have information about what the education bureau does. But there is gap between these two sectors and the water bureau. As you know, naturally water work is difficult and it doesn’t go as you planned. Thus, it is structural; involving a number of stakeholders, once it fails it would be difficult. One WASH regional coordination bureau is established and there is Ibrahim there. Education and the health bureau are more coordinated and we work jointly. Though, there is a gap on to construct water services for schools and health centers timely, I think this will get improved in the future.
3. Among the solutions you have listed, which is the most important?
11:37
I think the best solution would be creating awareness to the community on sanitation and hygiene. The community should be aware that peeing everywhere is not right and taboo. Even the community can mobilize and make their villages clean with their own effort. If awareness is created back to community’s roots and if the community accepts that I think things would get improved. 
4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your region for sustainable water service? What could be improved?
13:20
There is poor coordination. This is because organizations don’t have the information what others are doing.  For instance, I know what One WASH program is working on but I don’t understand what Save the Children is planning to do regarding WASH. There should be coordination since we are working in the same sector. One organization should cover what another couldn’t afford. If all organizations have the same plan and implementation systems, situations wouldn’t be like this. Actually, if we continue working like this change would take long time. But if we do in collaboration and help each other, we can bring change with in short time. The gap we have now is that One WASH has its own work; UNICEF has its own work; Afar water bureau has its own work. The coordination rate and helping each other is almost “0” Every organization hassle with its plan.  Therefore, change may require much time. There should be inter - connection. We should have hydrogen bond. If there is check and balance and accountability for every activity we do, there would be improvement.  
5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?
15:36 
First, coordination would help identify the exact source of problems related to sustainable water service. Coordinated action helps to do eligible work; helps the community understand what is being practiced; creates awareness and makes the community participate in politics. Action could be taken together whenever it’s needed. For example, if we know plans and activities of each of the stakeholders, there would be a check and balance system. In general, if there is improved coordination, there would be change regarding the problems of water service sustainability.

Does coordination contribute to the stream and technical committee?
Of course it has a lot of importance. There is negligence when there is no coordination. For example let’s say the stream and technical committee have reported their annual performance, and Save does the same to the WASH coordinator, this would create duplication of efforts and unnecessary time and resource wastage. Therefore coordination is very vital for sustainability of water service.
