
1) Interviewer: Okay, please note that I am asking for your personal opinion on this interview. So what are the challenges of maintaining sustainable water service in this town? I am asking about the problems that we should tackle for the sustainable water service in this town.
Interviewee: Well, though am not working in The Water Authority office, as a residence in town I can see that the office is making an effort to provide clean water to the residence. But there is always a shortage of water and also sometimes the water that comes is not clean. These are the challenges I see.
Interviewer: Okay, but what do you think are the challenges for this shortage and the quality of water?
Interviewee: Well I think it's the finance issue. Since we are not a developed state we don't have enough money to finance our water services projects. I think that's the only problem because as you can see Woliso is surrounded by many water resources.
Interviewer: How is that financial problem affecting the water services?
Interviewee: You need money to distribute water to the people. Like the other time the water becomes dirty and when we asked the reason, they told us that the waterhole was dug 10 years ago and now it's becoming out of use. But they don't have money to dig a new water hole. So they still continue to use that water. Even they don't have money to repair it.
Interviewer: You have said the water service is not sufficient to the people. Is it because the water holes we have and the numbers of residents are not proportional or...?
Interviewee: Well the population is growing up and our water service is stuck on previous numbers. So it is difficult to distribute the water to everyone. For example, we have this river called Revu. Farmers are using the river for irrigation. So the residents are getting water at night time. If we had enough financing, we could have left Revu for the farmers and we could have used another water source for the residents. This implicates that the finance shortage is playing a vital role as a challenge...
Interviewer: Oh, you are telling me that the farmers use the water for irrigation?
Interviewee: Yes, On daytime farmers use Revu river for irrigation...
Interviewer: They use the purified water?
Interviewee: No, they intercept the water before it reaches the point where it becomes clean water. So the town residents are using what is left from the irrigation and that brings a shortage of clean water.

2) Interviewer: Okay, you have mentioned three problems. First, you have mentioned about finance shortage issue. Then you have mentioned about the irrigation issue. And you have also stated about the water becoming dirty and the holes are not repaired. [05:00] My second question is what should be the solution to tackle those challenges? Let's start with the finance situation challenge. How do we solve it?
Interviewee: Well I think the government or the Water authority should look for a means to increase the budget on the water service...
Interviewer: Is the budget mainly for the expansion of the service or repair the previous ones?
Interviewee: It's for both. 
Interviewer: Okay you need it for both. So you need the government...
Interviewee: Not only the government but even the NGOs can help us on this matter. 
Interviewer: You have also mentioned about the imbalance proportion of the town residents and the water resources that we are currently using. How do we solve that?
Interviewee: We need to add more water holes. We also need to repair those we currently are running.
Interviewer: Okay, how do we solve the challenge of having dirty water on the taps?
Interviewee: If we build new water holes and we also repair those which are damaged, I don't think that will be a problem. You know this problem is a serious matter. I am living on the side of town where Addis Ababa is located and we have not used our tap water for drinking in the last three months. If there is no water today, then it comes tomorrow, the water becomes dirty. It will be dirty for some hours and then it becomes clean. I asked concerned parties about the issue and what they have told me is, the pipes are very old and have loose ends in many sides. So sometimes the water is mixed with rainwater or flood water. Termites are also breaking the pipes. So we can't use the water for drinking. 
Interviewer: So you are saying the pipes should be repaired?
Interviewee: yes the pipes and also the holes should be repaired. We are using expired water holes. When a water hole is dug, the experts put an estimate on how many years this water hole should give service... 
Interviewer: So the water holes have passed that limit...
Interviewee: Those water holes have passed that expiry date. But still, we are using them. And since our landscape is a bit sloppy, when the rain comes flood run into the hole.


3) Interviewer: Okay, you have mentioned the solutions to the challenges we have. Now, which challenge do you give a priority? Which solution should be done as soon as possible?
Interviewer: Well, since building new water holes takes time, I think we should start repairing those we currently have ASAP. You know if the rain comes without us repairing the holes, our problems will rise and that's the challenge we should give priority. 

4) Interviewer: Okay, so let's say we implement the solutions you have mentioned. What kind of change do you expect to see in Woliso water service?
Interviewee: We will definitely see the water shortage go away. There will be also an improvement on health issues as currently, most residents have to endure water born diseases. Also, it will have an impact on our economy as currently, we have to buy bottled water from markets for drink use.

Interviewer: Okay, my fourth question is is there coordination in between agencies that provide water services? If it is there, how do you see it? Is it good or weak?
Interviewee: This is a good question. You know starting from our agency; all agencies should coordinate and plan to tackle these water service-related challenges. [10:00] For example we this big river called Ejersa. We have federal prisoners who come from different part of Ethiopia. We can use them to work on our rivers. You know the water pollution is not like the way we are talking about it right now. It's worst. For example, the other time we started a research project for 3 months. We were visiting farmers and one day we got this farmer who hands have a serious infection. We asked what the problem was and he replied by saying "You are sending us poison through the river." Even some battles are seriously infected because of the water. You know I grew up in this area. Twenty years ago, we were using water directly from the river and we were not getting sick or something. But now we are receiving water through tap and still that water makes us sick. The other time we were visiting a river by the name Gentile, you know Amora right? I saw an eagle took a bone out of the river and died while eating it. There is a hospital nearby and their drainage system is linked to the river. There is no green area around the water because the water is poisonous. If all concerned parties come together and work together, I think we should tackle such problems.
Interviewer: Okay, but how do you see the current relationship between concerned parties?
Interviewee: I don't see any coordination right now. Every party is running on its own lane and no coordination is happening between them.
Interviewer: So what needs to be improved in regards to coordination among those concerned parties?
Interviewee: I think factories, hotels, and hospitals should not release their waste materials directly on the river. They should stop that. They should not pollute the river. 
Interviewer: I was asking about the coordination between agencies that provide water services.
Interviewee: I have already told you that everyone is running on its own lane and there is no coordination happening between them. I only see Water Authority trying to bring them together yet that is not quite successful. They are not planning and working together.

5) Interviewer: So my last question is if we implement this coordination, how is it going to have an impact on the sustainable water service challenges you mentioned earlier?
Interviewee: If the coordination is there, we can keep our rivers clean because everyone will be aware of the problem and will avoid polluting the rivers. Then if we have clean rivers most of our problems will be gone.
Interviewer: Okay, Thank you very much. I am done.
