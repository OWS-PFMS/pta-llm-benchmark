Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: From my perspective I see the major challenges of course being lack of sufficient amounts of water. The county faces serious challenges in terms of availing this resource, then number two where the resource is available, the way it is utilized is also a challenge because there is quite a lot of mismanagement in some of the schemes especially the rural ones. Even in the urban utilities non-revenue water levels are very high. That is quite a problem because this is a scarce resource even when it is available and again, the utilization is not that efficient and effective. 
There are also issues to do with recycling – that is another concern that recycling is not done well if at all. There is a lot of wastage because any water can be recycled even the water used in sewerage systems can be recycled. This is being done effectively elsewhere, yet in the county recycling levels are low. As a training institution of course, we want to believe there is a component of educating the population that is needed. We have quite a number of interventions as a learning institution in terms of outreach in the form of long term and short term trainings to create awareness to educate institutions, individuals and schools on efficient water use and how it can bring maximum benefit because we also must appreciate that when water is available and it is wholesome water even issues of diseases will go down so it is a big benefit if all these challenges are addressed appropriately. 
We can also not downplay the issues of harvesting rainwater especially. Rainwater whether it’s from roofs, catchment, rock catchment or even surface runoff, I believe there is more that can be done in that regard to harvest this water other than to let it run off. When it rains in Kitui, although we say the rain is not that much, a lot of this water simply runs off and it’s not well utilized and this is another area where as  an institutions we are creating awareness on roof catchment systems on how to utilize this water whether it is for domestic use or for irrigation and agriculture. This is something we continuously want to do. So, I believe that those are the main challenges which are affecting the sector and they are being addressed they are being mitigated but I believe the county can do better and by the county I mean all of us who are within the sector in this county, whether it’s training institutions, NGOs, county government, national government there is a lot more we can do.
Interviewer: How do these challenges affect service sustainability?
Respondent: Without a sufficient resource base, the amount of water to be managed is definitely very low and with poor management, service sustainability and reliability is not attainable. When I talk about recycling and rainwater harvesting, it also goes to the issue of having sufficient amounts to supply and manage. This will in turn lead to the need for establishment of better and effective management structures to sustain services.

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: Some of the recommendations that I can come up with that are already being implemented but can be enhanced. One is more exploitation of the ground water; this water sometimes is quite wholesome for use but sometimes need further treatment whether it is through reverse osmosis or through other means. A good example is Kitui town itself; there is a nice reservoir of water available in the ground. 
Another solution is to empower people to harvest rain water. Another solution is to address particularly the rural water schemes, the intervention here would be to educate the rural water schemes on how to manage the resource better and to encourage people to pay for the resource when they utilize it and to carry out systems maintenance to ensure that the systems are in good working order all the time. The urban WSPs should aggressively address the issue of non-revenue water.
Recycling is also quite critical and I believe this is an area where we should have more training institutions like ourselves and the NGOs address. We know that there are quite a number of interventions in that area and unfortunately, we don’t see any of them being applied within the county. So, even if it means for example that we have a model here which is something we are intending to do very soon, we have a model recycling plant so that people can come here and see how it operates. We can start with the water from the sinks and hand wash basins and show people that it can be recycled and used elsewhere. This is something we want to have in the near future as a quick win then people can come and learn here and apply in their homes then we can scale it later on to recycling of even the water from the sewage treatment plants even for raw water so that we can see that it can be reused.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I have to say that it is the one of addressing the challenges in rural schemes where we have a lot of interventions from the National government, from the County Government, from NGOs and even enterprises. These entities have developed schemes that should be able to supply water to communities but those schemes fail a very short time after commissioning. They fail due to lack of maintenance, mismanagement, and sometimes maybe they were installed in somebody’s land and the land was not properly acquired. Making sure that those rural schemes work, as planned is also very critical. The rural schemes operate slightly differently from the WSPs because of the ownership structure and the management structure. As an institution last year, we trained water resource persons on management, operation and maintenance of schemes. Addressing the challenges in the rural schemes requires prompt repair of the system after a breakdown and this would be greatly be supported via linkages with maintenance service providers and proper reporting structures for breakdowns so that the county or whoever is responsible can be aware of breakdowns promptly. Proper reporting and prompt repair will ensure continuity and reliability of water supply.

Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: For us we are a learning institution and our interventions revolve around training. We do long-term training of students and then source for them industrial attachments locally in the county so that they can apply their knowledge and skills locally. There is a high absorption rate of our students in the water sector locally and they have supported through developing innovative ways of billing and water conservation. We also offer short-term training to organizations working in the sector here in Kitui.
We also do consultancy and outreach. We do consultancy for a number of organizations within the county and we undertake research and consultancy also within the County. As regards outreach, we target schools and communities. We also buy airtime on a local radio station to discuss matters of conservation, water efficient technologies and other matters, aimed at educating the people of Kitui on these matters. Currently we have a two-hour show on County FM. We talk about the benefits of using solar pumping, which has a lot of potential in this area, we talk of rainwater harvesting and other issues relevant for the county.
I think you also asked about what informs these interventions. I would say that it is based on our mandate as a learning institution to train or educate, to undertake research and outreach. Additionally, the establishment of this institution in Kitui county was as a result of recognition that this a semi-arid, if not arid county. We train and do outreach in relation to the challenges experienced locally because we would like the knowledge to be applied locally to improve the situation. So, the interventions for the county are targeted because of the specific needs of the County.
