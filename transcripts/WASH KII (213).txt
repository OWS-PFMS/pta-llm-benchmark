1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Interviewer: The first question I would like to ask you is about the challenges of water service sustainability in South Ari woreda? What are the bottlenecks of water accessibility? If you take the woreda’s clean water coverage, for instance, it is only 24-25 percent. Why do you think this is low? Let’s talk about the problems, and then we will proceed to the solutions later.
Respondent: One of the challenges is that there are only few water schemes constructed by the government due to inadequate budget. There is also lack of awareness among the community to take initiative and build water sources by itself. Community members usually drink river water.  
Interviewer: What is the reason that there are only few developed water springs? This is one point. The other is that are those which are already developed being properly managed?
Respondent: First, even though springs have been developed by the government, NGOs, or different stakeholders, local community lacks the trend of proper handling to extend the lifespan of the water schemes. They don’t have the understanding that it belongs to the community, and they should properly take care of the water schemes. The community lacks the awareness that they should use the water points carefully so that service can be used for long. 
Secondly, once after it is constructed there are incidences that water doesn’t reach the distribution point due to technical problems. Although I am not a professional in the area, I hear from people talking about that newly constructed water schemes dry out after two weeks or a month. When we ask about the reason why, it is usually found that there are problems related to technical defects. They tend not to identify the correct source of the water spring, and the community also has gaps in properly using the water points. 
Interviewer: What else? If there are issues you haven’t mentioned. 
Respondent: Despite budget is allocated for water scheme construction annually, new water schemes are not being constructed due to shortage of cash. Even completed projects need finance to monitor its quality and continuous follow-up. There is mismanagement of funds that sometimes payments are made for works that have not completed in building water schemes. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Well, you have said there is shortage of potable drinking water because of insufficient number of water points. What do you think should be done to address these issues? 
Respondent: In my opinion, huge water tanks should be built in selected areas. For instance, in Zomba town, a place where the water for Gazer town comes from, a large water tank can be built and water can be distributed through pipes to the residents of the town. This can reduce the problem to some extent. I believe that in order to sustainably address these issues all stakeholders, including the government, NGOs, and local community, should play their role. 
Interviewer: It might be a temporary solution, Right? Building a water tank is not a permanent solution. To solve the problem permanently, what do you think should be done? There is also an issue related to community awareness. The local community lacks a sense of ownership. So, what should be done?
Respondent: It is necessary to educate the local people and create sustainable community awareness about water service utilization. We need to inform members of the community how to take responsibility, maintain when damaged and protect all water schemes found in their locality. Community members should protect, put fence around, and hire guards for the water schemes. 
Interviewer: You have mentioned another problem related to technicians that selection of the right water source has not been done properly. And you have said because of that water schemes run out of use after a while.  What do you recommend should be done to solve this issue?
Respondent:  First of all, when a water scheme is to be constructed in a certain area, all stakeholders should be consulted. Once all concerned bodies are involved in the project idea, the water technicians should work together if there is a skill gap and produce quality work with great commitment and attention.  They should prioritize the long term community benefits over short term temporary gains. In order to motivate them, the professional discussions should be conducted and continuous monitoring and supervision should be in place. 
Some of the highly skilled professionals who should have been in the field are in leadership positions/spending most of their time in offices and they send less skilled ones to field works. 
If we facilitate conditions where senior and skillful ones can share their experience for the other expertise, there will be a long-lasting solution for the problem we are discussing now. 
Interviewer: You are right; there is shortage of experienced technicians, right? How do you think this should be solved sustainably?
Respondent: I don’t think that this will be resolved in a short time period. Many technicians have been trained for years, but we have not seen more skilled people sharing experiences to the new comers and properly spending their time to solve problems at the grass root levels. 
In order to fill this gap, providing a more effective and short term trainings to the local community could be one solution. 
Interviewer: You have pointed out that finance is one of the problems. What should be done to solve that? 
Respondent: experts working in the finance office only think about their personal benefits and other routine activities. They don’t care whether the water schemes are working or not though we have discussed in vain many times about this issue. Payments made to contractors should be monitored by a committee and projects should be evaluated before payments are made. It is difficult to correct the substandard water schemes after payments have been made. Had it been with responsible committee to control quality of the work and payment terms, we could test the water schemes and enable the contractors to fix the technical problems if any.
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: Out of the resolutions you have mentioned which one would be the best alternative?
Respondent:  What I recommend is that increasing the number of trained technicians is vital. Also, those who lack experience should be trained, as this would solve problems of maintenance. Because currently technicians are coming from a very long distances to maintain water schemes, it is resulting in massive financial cost. If we have enough trained technicians we would be able to use the existing water schemes properly and add something to the current water coverage level.
