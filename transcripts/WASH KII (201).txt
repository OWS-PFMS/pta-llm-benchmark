1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda? 
In my opinion, the main challenges raised related to water service sustainability are: [Budget] first, we have limited source of budget. To diversify our budget source we are currently working with NGOs. To this end, we have prepared documents (proposals) and submitted to NGOs to diversify our budget source. Secondly, there are large number of kebeles within the woreda and the budget allocated to the woreda could not satisfy the needs of all the kebeles. Even though we plan to work on water supply, sanitation and hygiene, we face a budget shortage to fully implement our plan. Thus, the first challenge related to water service sustainability is lack of Budget. The second challenge related to water supply is [community awareness] when we say community awareness, there are a number of water projects implemented using government regular budget, WASH program budget, NGOs funds. However, the community do not properly use and maintain these water schemes due to lack of awareness. In this regard, even though the woreda water office is responsible to enhance the community’s awareness, so far they are not working on this front. [The water office did not also identify and document the areas where there is water service problems] but now we are working together to identify these areas and to find out the solutions. For example, there are about 48 kebeles in the woreda some of them do not have any water schemes but these areas are not properly identified by the woreda water office. However, this information is very essential both for the government and NGOs that are interested to work on water supply in the woreda.

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
In the first place we have to allocate sufficient budget for water office, however, it is difficult to fulfill the required budget at one time. To this end, it is also important to find out NGOs that work in coordination with the water office. Regarding the community awareness, we have to teach the community on proper use of water schemes. We should also form groups that protect and monitor the water schemes. All the woreda stakeholders should work to create awareness on proper use of the existing rural water schemes. 
Concerning problems related to identification of non-functional water schemes, the water office should assign water experts and assess each and every rural water schemes and document their status. This information will serve as an input for future interventions. For instance, the problems of some of the water schemes may be very simple and can be repaired with little cost. Thus, if we identify the problems of each water schemes properly, it would be easy to find out sustainable solutions.

3.Of the solution you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Alleviating the problem related to budget is the most important solution in my opinion. We should find various budget sources. Without sufficient budget we cannot ensure sustainable water, sanitation and hygiene services. If the problem related to budget is solved, the community’s questions about clean water supply will stops. In our woreda when we go to kebeles, the community raise three basic questions. These are electricity, road and water. However, the problem related to water is the most important. For example, when there is kebele level meeting, the community shows to woreda administrators contaminated water they are using. They even say to the administrators “we are drinking this contaminated water so you should also drink this”. This implies the problem related to water supply is very serious and much emphasis should be given to this problem. If we find budget sources, we directly go to implementation and the problem related to water service will be solved. The community is much disciplined. Having all these problems they are not nagging the government. If the budget problem is solved, we can alleviate the problem related to clean water supply. We can also reduce the incidence of water-born diseases. For instance, giardia and other parasites are affecting the community due to the use of contaminated water. So if the budget problem is solved, these health problems would be alleviated.

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? 
Not only now, we have good previous experience on this issue. For example, when NGOs come to our woreda, we receive their proposal and discuss on the detail. Then we aware the community about the project to facilitate the implementation. When we aware the community about the possible challenges that would affect the sustainability of the water schemes in advance, there will not be implementation problems. We have also advice these external organization (NGOs) about the areas (kebeles) where they should intervene. Based on their budget capacity we advise them the areas they should give priority. What about the coordination with woreda stakeholders? At woreda level we have no coordination problem. Particularly the WASH program supports water, health and education sectors. There is no coordination problems in these sectors. These sectors use their budget according to the plan without violating the finance regulations. If their pans are not feasible, our office evaluates and reject their budget proposal. Thus, our coordination with these sector offices is good. What could be improved? Related to budget use the sector offices should have action plans that clearly indicate their budget use procedures. This action plan also help us to monitor their budget use.

5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the woreda. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?
If there is improved coordination between sector offices, there will not be misuse of budget. If the budget is properly used, the community will stop its question related to water supply. Supporting organizations (NGOs) will also be encouraged to diversify their interventions. This will reduce the problems related to water supply sustainability. Improved coordination can also solve the problems I mentioned before. Because if one organization works separately, it might face several challenges. For example, it might develop water schemes in areas where there is no community interest. However, when there is coordination we might exchange information about priority areas where water schemes should be developed. Thus, coordination can lead to sustainable water service.
