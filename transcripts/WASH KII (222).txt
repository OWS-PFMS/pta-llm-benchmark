1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Respondent: A number of NGOs operating in this area such as IRC, World Vision, and the like have built many water schemes and trained us on how to take care of them. However, we have some challenges related with running these water schemes. One of the main problems is a shortage and inaccessibility to spare parts. 
Interviewer: Can you elaborate this one more? Whose problem is that?
Respondent: This is because woreda and zones don’t have these spare parts and even if they are purchased through the revolving fund by the unions, there are no good coordination systems between woredas and zone to send technical teams to maintain defects. Moreover, when some spare parts are out of stock, there is no trend of a swift procurement process. 
Another challenge is lack of coordination between WASH stakeholders at woreda and zone level to respond to broken water points promptly and develop new schemes in a synergic manner. Instead, they create trivial excuses creating unnecessary bureaucracy (such as the absence of power) not to issue spare parts on time. In other words, the process is becoming increasingly frustrating and cumbersome, as a result, we are waiting for more than a week to repair damaged water points.
Interviewer: Before we proceed to the solutions, are there other problems you didn’t mention challenging sustainability?
Respondent: Even though residents are paying water bills every month, there is still substantial problem related to equity and fairness in terms of accessing the service. For example, some households/villages could not access water for more than a week or even for more time.  Almost everywhere in the zone, including the capital, Jinka town, water is still distributed in shifts. Even though we reported the problems we are facing, responsible bodies  are not ready to respond to our complaints and many of  the problems associated with service delivery are not yet addressed.
The common problem that we observe is that no one would care and monitor when water bulbs get burst and the water may continue flowing in an open space for days. There is a huge problem in promptly repairing hand-dug wells. For example, in Geza kebele, I know that the existing largest water point is burst and there are 11 hand-dug wells that are not functional at this time.
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Let’s proceed to the solutions. You have mentioned that there is severe problem regarding access to spare parts. What would be the solution?
Respondent: The stock of spare parts that are available at woreda and zonal levels should be used in a coordinated way. If spare parts are not available at the woreda level, it may be found at the zonal level, thus, inventory management should be improved.
Interviewer: but what would be the long-lasting solution for the shortage of spare parts?
Respondent: As a long term solution, coordination of revolving funds at all levels with the enterprise is vital. If land is provided to build a store and the enterprise is offered monetary support to be able to purchase spare parts as needed, the issue can be addressed effectively. The enterprise office would also have the ability to identify problems timely and offer maintenance services according to local needs. I, as one of the enterprise owner, am planning to send a request to the functional NGOs for some financial support. 
Interviewer: What do you think should be done to solve bureaucratic processes in the offices?
Respondent: If the enterprise provides spare parts and services, people can get the service more conveniently and efficiently. In addition to this, we need to discuss in meetings on how to ease the process for issuing spare parts from zone and woreda.
Interviewer: You’ve said there is water accessibility problem in general and people wait weeks and months without water. What would be the solution for this?
Respondent: If we (spare part enterprises) are provided with training in coordination with woreda, the community can easily access quality and fast maintenance service.  Also, ocal people should be trained on technical issues to reliably access maintenance services. 


3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

Interviewer: Which of the solutions you listed out is the most important one to solve these issues?
Respondent: In areas where there is a very acute problem to access clean water,  digging a shallow well reaching up to 72 meters, and building larger water points with their distribution tankers will be the most important  solution. 
Interviewer: Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: By doing so, the burden of water shortage will be alleviated significantly. Surprisingly, people in some kebeles in our area are still drinking running water from unclean rivers, while some of them use chlorine; others boil the water before they use it for drinking purposes. Thus, the construction of big water sources and distribution points could avoid these issues sustainably
