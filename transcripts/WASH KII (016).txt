Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: The challenges that influence water services sustainability probably number one would be mismanagement because you find out the trend mostly in rural water sector is that the contractors and probably the government, the first step that they come they identify a borehole then they construct. There is no post construction support that is accorded to the community so the management of water resource is left in the hands of the community. Most of these communities then elect a user management committee which is responsible for the management of that water resource. So most of these user management committees are basically people who do not have any experience or any professional background in terms of water and in terms of management of those resources. So you find that after when a breakdown occurs or when there are no funds that are actually allocated to maintenance and repair and further sustainability of the project so that would be number so the fact that there is no post construction support according to communities, after a project has been constructed. 
The other one would be lack of professionals in the rural water sector. The rural water sector is managed very different from urban water sector so there is very little to know players in the rural water sector. Private water companies who are willing to engage in the maintenance of this rural water resources because number one it looks like it is uneconomical to do it. It is not a viable model to maintain rural water services. The other one would be probably lack of finance on the part probably of the government because the responsibility of the water resources solely falls on county governments but most of them are limited in terms of finances. The other one would be there is no institutional coordination between probably the county government players in the rural water sector.
Interviewer: Which other challenge? What do you mean first by no institutional coordination?
Respondent:  You find that for example in many areas like Mwingi North we've tried but in most areas you find that the county government is not probably in line with the NGOs and other people who are working there to construct the water resources. So you'll find people come and install equipments at site then they leave them they go, the county government probably has very little knowledge about even what has been done there. So after they leave it becomes very hard even for the county government to come in and say how do we manage this. For example there is a water treatment plant that was installed some place in Tseikuru by World Vision. The water treatment plant is from Finland, it requires membranes, it's a good system. It's a solar powered system. It requires membranes, it requires filters, it requires all that but one day it will stop functioning. It will probably be impossible for the county government to revive that station again for it to start working because the people who came and installed it, they installed it and they left. 
The community who were left probably to manage that thing have little to no knowledge about even how to operate the system itself. So you find some years the thing will have stalled and once that happens the county government even them they will say we initially had no say in what was being installed here so that coordination probably should be there. In my opinion if something probably more local would have been installed at a place like that and the county government probably they had a say, when it get spoiled they would have probably been unable to conduct repairs. Another challenge it looks sort of obvious but it is basically the mismanagement by the user elected committees and the lack of transparency in terms of finances, accountability there is little to none. So  you find a project has been operating a pipe scheme for example, then for some time when it breaks down there is no money for repair. So that affects the long-term sustainability. These committees are elected probably for a limited period of time, let's say three years. There was a committee that was managing this water resource for three years and once their time is over they leave office, their accounts are zero. The next committee comes in the thing breaks down. When they break down where are the funds? There is nothing.

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Number one I think the first recommendation would be the rural water sector is a market that is probably untapped and there are no really major players in it and one major factor that actually contributes to that is the fact that there is no data to support and show that the rural water sector can be a viable financial option that probably a private company can enter into. So there is no information about the rural water sector, there are no players about it. So one of the things would be probably to provide that information and then the next step would be coordination between probably a private company and probably the local county government. Because you find a private company like FundiFix, FundiFix is here in Mwingi North. FundiFix coordinates with the county government and then we coordinate also with the communities. So there is that sort of operational model that can be instituted and also hand in hand with the community users and the community project. So this is something that would work and it would make rural water services to be sustainable.
In terms of sustainability I would say management of the water sources probably to be sort of a smart way of managing the rural water resource. I have given an example probably on Kathima where we installed a smart ATM. So the ATM now there is an audit trail. There is volume sold, what the community send through Mpesa then there is what is billed by probably FundiFix for their repairs, so that amount is clearly seen. So when you come to a table there is the community member, there is FundiFix there is the county government. Where there is such a smart way of monitoring and managing of that resource it becomes very easy because there are no questions. So that is a project that will be sustainable even it will be there 10-15 years from now. Because there is that sort of smart monitoring.
Interviewer: On that issue of using the Paybill, are there people who pay by cash and how do you account for that, do they have a way of recording the amount that is not paid via the paybill?
Respondent:  They have a way of recording that. What FundiFix does for every community project that we sign management contract with we give them sort of meters log to fill for the meter readings so we've installed meters in all those project. So probably the kiosk attendant the first they come in the evening they record the meter readings so Mpesa also fails so there are cases where they'll sell water through cash basis so with the meter reading and with calculations you can clearly see the amount collected. So I think that one now comes down to how you manage that probably pipe scheme or that project. So there are those logs so we are able to see.
Interviewer: What more?
Respondent: In terms of what can be done? I think the other issue I would probably say is, you see like in Mwingi North FundiFix is supposed to operate on an insurance logic basis where we pool these resources from so many water projects to one service provider who provides the maintenance service so we collect little from all these people then we pool all these resources together then we do repair and maintenance for all of them based on the ones that have broken down. So we would operate efficiently that way. The other option would be we have the performance contracts so we are supposed to provide services to communities based on we are not supposed to exceed three days for a repair so if there was probably a fund created by maybe the county government or the national government where thereby they know this service provider is the one who is responsible for maintaining our water sources in Mwingi North, then this service provider trades performance for finance so you give services in exchange for finance. So I think that will also be a model. 
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I've listed three, the one that would probably be best is number one there needs to be that coordination at the county level between the sub-county office and the service provider. There needs to be a service provider, probably a private service provider on the ground. So they can do coordination with the county government.
Interviewer: What exactly do you mean by coordination?
Respondent: I'll give you an example, FundiFix has signed contract probably with five communities so these communities we manage their projects. If there is a breakdown, we do that. These communities probably have generators. So the responsibility of servicing the generator falls on FundiFix so FundiFix decides it is time now to service, so we go and service then you find the county government receives probably resources from National Drought Relief so they want to service all the generators in Mwingi North then they go ahead and buy equipments even for these five generators which we have already serviced. So that coordination so that there is no duplication of efforts. Us we are doing this and you are doing this. That coordination also comes in in terms of, us we can probably do pipeline repair. The county government has the resources to do replacement of equipments. For example a pump breaks down, a pump is worth probably half a million so if there is that coordination between FundiFix and the county government, us we do this, and you do this, that would work. It is our working relationships. So number one that coordination, number two that pooling of resources to one service provider, it is a model that would work. 
Interviewer; Now tell us, if that coordination is well done, if there is proper coordination between the county government and service provider in the ground, now what will happen?
Respondent: There will be efficient management of the rural water sources so you find for example you find us we are service providers, so as a service provider we have noted that where we are able to manage a water resource more transparently it is something that is not sustainable so with the coordination in mind and then now us we come in for the management and we are submitting performance probably to the county government or somebody else there is sustainability. 
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Probably I can talk about now the management of the water resources, the ATMs, we also have smart handpumps, so smart handpumps is devices that we install on the handpumps, they are able to monitor the abstraction volumes and they are able to give detailed data on the operation of the handpump. So that is on the part of smart monitoring so for handpumps we bill them a flat rate of Ksh.1000 per month. We don't have a rate that we calculate a rate with the handpump. So there is that and then probably the other thing would be right now we are doing COVID response. So for COVID response we are identifying health centres, health facilities then we want to equip them with handwashing facilities and rain water harvesting facilities. This will ensure that they have water even post the COVID period. In addition to that we want also to be able to supply them with water incases where there is no water. So that is something that we are working on currently.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: Participating in the WASH Forum has number one made our impact known even to the other sub-counties. So participating in the WASH Forum you find there is a lot of information from all these different sectors from the different regions I mean. So it has made our impact known to these people. It has made our interventions or our work in Mwingi North known to these other sub-counties and it becomes now a point of interest because they are able to see that water resources can be managed sustainably. The other thing would be participating at the WASH Forum has enabled us to learn about the challenges in different regions and different localities so we are able to know that some challenges are unique in some areas, and also enable us to sort of think of better ways of how we can improve our scope and our interventions.
