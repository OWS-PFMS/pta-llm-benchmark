

In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in South Omo Zone?

AMREF works particularly to alleviate the WASH problem related to trachoma (the common eye disease in the area). In this regard we are developing rural water schemes in different kebeles to solve sanitation problems that predispose eye diseases. We also work on awareness creation of water, sanitation and hygiene. Our main intervention areas are South Ari and North Ari woredas. Trachoma is highly prevalent in these two woreda. Thus, we develop water schemes for the community and for schools. Moreover we construct public latrine (toilet).  

When I come to the main challenges to WASH service sustainability, most of the time the problem lays on utilization of water schemes. Even though there are WASH committees at kebele level they do not properly manage water schemes constructed in their areas. Do you face any other problem to sustain WASH service in the area? Our organization works closely with kebele administrators at grass root level, and we also coordinate with woreda water office and zonal water department in planning activities. We also coordinate with these stakeholders in supervision and monitoring of water schemes. Thus, as such we did not face any challenge related to our WASH service. However, sometimes some stakeholders are unwilling to coordinate with us in some WASH activities. 

WASH service has soft components such as trainings to the community, we also work with health office in this front.

What other problems are there related to water service sustainability? Particularly in some kebeles of North Ari there is infrastructure problem to transport raw materials for the construction of water schemes. As a result it is difficult to accomplish projects in the intended time period.

What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

We work to develop water schemes in our project areas. Once we developed water schemes we provide training to the community on proper utilization of the schemes. Further training should be given to the community to create sense of belongingness to water schemes developed in their area. If the community do not consider the schemes as its own property, the schemes will not serve for long period of time. Thus, to ensure sustainable water service we should enhance the community’s awareness on proper utilization of the water schemes. 

When there are new projects, we usually provide training for WASHcos and care takers. When we also construct latrines in schools, we provide training for school health clubs about washing hands after using latrines. When we construct latrines, we also develop hand washing schemes near to the latrine. We do this to encourage school children to wash their hands after using the latrine. The school health clubs also involve in the awareness enhancement programs.

Related to infrastructure problem, when we go to remote areas to develop springs, the springs are mostly found in mountainous areas that are covered by forest. As a result it is difficult to transport raw materials. To alleviate this problem we discuss with kebele administrators to mobilize the community to participate in the transportation of the raw materials. That is we transport the materials up to areas where there is road access, then the community transport the materials to the final destination using manpower. Because of this our projects in north Ari were delayed for long period of time. Thus, to implement water projects in remote areas there should be cooperation between the project implementing organization, the kebele administration and the community.

To ensure sustainable WASH service in the area, we should enhance the community awareness on operation and management of water schemes. Moreover, we should train selected individuals that will monitor and supervise the water schemes. We should also strengthen WASHcos through refreshment trainings. Thus, if we do this, we can ensure sustainable WASH service in the area.

In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved? 

In South Omo Zone most of the NGOs work in WASH sector but we do not have any communication or coordination at all. Every organization run on its way separately to achieve its own target. What about your coordination with governmental organizations? We have coordination with government bodies, and they also give recognition for our activities. The Finance and Economic Development Office is responsible to monitor NGOs finance and activities. Thus, the office regularly monitor our activities and give recognition for our achievements. We have also work with water, health and education offices regularly. We have a formal communication with these offices both at woreda and zonal level.  

In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?

Coordination obviously solve the problems mentioned earlier. But the coordination should begin from the Federal and Regional level. Because stakeholders at zonal and woreda level mostly focus on implementing the plans given to them from the central office. They have no mandate to create coordination with other organizations at field office level. Therefore, if there is coordination at central level, there would be strong coordination at lower level (zonal or woreda level).
