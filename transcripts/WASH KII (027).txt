Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: So in Kitui County, sustainability is a challenge.  Are we focused on rural or even urban?
Interviewer: You can mention both.
Respondent: Generally?
Interviewer: Yeah generally.   
Respondent: So to begin with rural water supplies, the schemes are usually developed by either the county or partners and donors and then the projects are handed to the communities to manage. So that means we have a management committee that manages the schemes. So the challenge that we have majorly is on the aspects pertaining management. For example, finances.
Interviewer: What about finances?
Respondent: So about finances, they sell the water at a certain cost maybe three bob or five bob, some don't have accounts, so they keep the money in their pockets. That means they can easily use the money to cater for their families' problems or issues. So there is no accountability. When there are breakdowns, they don't have money for repairs, then the water schemes take a long time before they are repaired because they rely on the County Government or other donors which may take time before they repair especially if a repair is huge. The other one, governance. Do I connect governance and politics? We have politics within the communities you find that a certain leader will want to manage the scheme so that means some of the officials may take side. Let's say the chairman and the treasurer they may take side of a certain leader. That means they won't be in terms with the other officials. So they get the money, they squander it with the certain leader. 
The other thing we have the issues of salinity. Some boreholes have high saline levels, so that's a challenge in Kitui. You find that you may have so many boreholes but due to the challenge of salinity, all the boreholes don't really serve the people due to high saline levels. Some might be abandoned, or others it will be used for only livestock, it won't be used for domestic use. 
Interviewer: You've mentioned something about governance. What about governance? You had started to elaborate on the issue of politics within the community management committees.
Respondent: It's just whereby you find that we have two teams some say we go this way the other side says we go this way. So you find that there is conflict and this might bring down a project.
Interviewer: So in summary you've talked about financial management, governance issues, political issues and the water quality that is the salinity. Any other challenge to water services sustainability? 
Respondent: I can't remember right now.
Interviewer: If you remember just let us know as we continue.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: So for one, capacity building to the management committees so that they'd be equipped with knowledge on how to, there is an aspect of record keeping. How to record keep, to read meters, how to repair small repairs, whom to conduct when there are challenges. 
Interviewer: What about capacity building, because I know so many actors have been doing this, day in day out. How else can the capacity building be done in your opinion?
Respondent: Maybe more follow ups, it should be a continuous thing. You don't capacity build then you just go away. You capacity build, then you do follow up. We trained you on this, what is the progress? Are you now keeping records, do you have a bank account?
Interviewer: Not just class work?
Respondent: Yes.
Interviewer: Then there is need for follow up?
Respondent: Follow up to see if there is implementation of what was trained.
Interviewer: Coaching to internalize what they learned in class? That is what you mean?
Respondent: Yes. And also because elections are usually done after like every three years, so you may train this regime then another regime comes. So it should be something that is continuous so that the communities are equipped with the knowledge.
Interviewer: What else?
Respondent: There is the FundiFix model. It has done good work in Mwingi North. You see that when there are breakdowns, they repair within a shorter period. So if it's rolled out to other sub-counties, I feel in my opinion then the breakdowns and the repairs could be handled and then the water supplies could be more sustainable.
Interviewer: What other ideas?
Respondent:  For the challenges?
Interviewer: What other recommendations do you have about solutions to those challenges?
Respondent: Maybe having a kitty that is ready. Such that when there are big breakdowns which are not budgeted by the county, then from this kitty money can be drawn and do the big repairs. Because you know majorly the county is focusing on new projects. So you find that the ones that are existing, there might be no much funding. For example this year we've budgeted something like KSHs 35,000-50,000. You find that it is not sufficient to cater for repairs. So if we had a certain kitty that can be drawn from to take care of these breakdowns it could cater for the challenges. 
Interviewer: So you mean a kitty set aside by?
Respondent: Maybe county and donors they agree and then they pool a certain kitty that can be used specifically for repairs and sustainability of our rural water supplies. 
Interviewer: Good ideas. Another idea or recommendation?
Respondent: Involvement of the communities in every step of the project development. At times you find that they lack the ownership of the projects which is also a challenge. So if you don't own a project, you don't care what happens with the project. So if they're involved from the initial phase until handing over and then follow ups then I’m sure the projects could be more sustainable. Then there is another challenge on operational cost. You find that most of our schemes they were done from the olden days and even now we are still using gensets or grid power that is KPLC (Kenya Power and Lighting Company). You find that it's costly and yet in Kitui we have so much solar. If we can tap into the green energy, then we could also save in the operational cost and make the projects more sustainable. Interviewer: And the issue of salinity? Do you have any recommendation or ideas on how to deal with it?
Respondent: Yeah, technology. It's just a matter of technology that can clean the water and make it to the levels that can be consumed by the people.

Respondent: They are all important.

Respondent:  So I give most three, one, two?
Interviewer: Just the first one. You can talk about the most important among those.
Respondent: In my opinion or the county's opinion?
Interviewer: In your own perspective.
Respondent: Can I give two?
Interviewer: You're speaking on behalf of the county so give the county's perspective. Yeah, you can give two or one. And then you walk us through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs. Like the theory of change. If this happens, this will happen and that.
Respondent: So for me I'd think tapping into the green energy would be the most important for now, coupled with capacity building.
Interviewer: So if there is green energy?
Respondent: If there is green energy we save on the operational cost, that means we'll have more kitty in the schemes and the communities are capacity built. So this money can be used in maybe pipeline extensions, from the main scheme to underserved areas. 
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: We usually have public participation that is done across the county moderated by the treasury department, economic planning. As a department our key priorities are; we are developing sub-wells, we are doing earth dams and earth pans and rock catchments, we are also drilling boreholes and equipping them and pipeline extensions. At times we factor for capacity building but most of the times it's not funded. Then we give subsidy to WSPs that is the KITWASCO and KIMWASCO. Those are the major that I can say. For hybridization mostly-- there is a time we factored it but it wasn't funded from our end, it was done by the Ministry of Education. But then there are talks with our chief officer, with the World Bank and the governor. So we are hoping they'll support us in hybridizing boreholes across the county.
Interviewer: What influenced those decisions, those interventions?
Respondent: They've been there because it’s majorly, you know county is mandated to provide water.
Interviewer: Let me just be clear.
Respondent: Yes.
Interviewer: What informed the selections of those interventions? Like you've mentioned about the sand dams, drilling of boreholes, pipeline extensions, what influenced those decisions?
Respondent: Provision for water to the communities because it's our mandate. 
Interviewer: You mentioned the project of hybridization being funded by Ministry of Education. Was that implemented in schools, or generally to the communities?
Respondent: To the boreholes.
Interviewer: All the boreholes?
Respondent: Community boreholes, yeah. Community water supplies.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: Having the WASH Forums brings together the WASH partners so this has enabled us as a department to know what each of the partner is doing, and there is more coordination and cooperation in what is happening on the ground. It has improved on the information passing and priority. Most of the partners pass through the department. They ask what we want to be assisted in then we give them, then they implement. So that we have a common goal.
Interviewer: Anything else that you would want to add?
Respondent: We appreciate the support from USAID/SWS. It's been a good learning partnership together and we hope you extend your period and be with us more or bring more interventions and ideas that can benefit the Kitui County residents.
