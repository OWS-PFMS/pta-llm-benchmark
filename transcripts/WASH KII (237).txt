1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: the first thing is lack of vacuum truck. The city water and sanitation office bought one vehicle and it was giving service during winter season. But that service can’t sustain during rainy season, because the street or the road is not functional on rainy seasons. Then we have lack of damping site places in our city. The city administration together with woreda were tangibly assigning places for that purpose out of the city and even the farmers took compensation fee around 80,000 birr for that land. But the communities living around were complaining that money was not enough for them and they were not volunteer for that facility to be built there. These problems make the project to stack in its beginning. Now, when we see my kebele, we have 27 communal latrine where each can serve 20-30 persons. Every latrine has its own committee assigned from the community and they have monthly fee to pay to that committee and they clean it regularly every week and we have monthly sanitation program in general. Here the problem is, all the latrine are full because of the land type which is watery and we can’t get place to avoid it away. Our city administration is planning and allocating budget to buy a vehicle for sanitary service because it was needed since we have populated area.
People are aware of sanitation issues and there are changes on their understandings because we did a lot on awareness. 
But there is still a problem from top to bottom budget allocation and we have shortage of finance and man power to make our city clean.    
2 What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems
Respondent: in my opinion, zonal, woreda and city administration should involve and work aggregately to persuade the community and start working on that land or allocate any preferable place for damping site in order to have a sustainable solution.
The solution for the full toilets is going to be, motivate people and use their own finance for the vacuum truck fee if the damping site is available.
Regarding the budget problem, since the people are aware of sanitary issues they will not wait for the municipality or customs income of the city. We should assign enough man power, check. If we collect income which is more than the expected one, Oromia region administration will see the report and focus on our city to allocate enough budget for the next time. If we stand together and submit all the collected earnings to the region, then we can get enough budgets. We also have assigned committee from each kebele which works on collecting earnings, security issues and market stability. We all are working well since we are here to serve the community which taught us without learning for themselves. 
3 Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: the basic thing I can raise is our city should be clean and develop different green areas in order to initiate projects and welcome investors like Hawassa, Bahirdar and other cities in Ethiopia. 
The second thing is assuring security in our city. 
The other thing is to allocate a place for damping site.
Interviewer: if the question of the place for damping site is successfully solved and appropriate, what changes do you think will happen? 
Respondent: the main thing to be happened is our people will get relief from their lifetime concern and we will get satisfied too. Because this is the number 1 question for our people and this will make them to actively participate on solving security issues and other infrastructure concerns like water and electricity power issues.
