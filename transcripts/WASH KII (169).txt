
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent 1: Lack of sense of ownership from the community once the schemes are built. Usually, partners engaged in water scheme development areas will not stay for long for different reasons. They will do everything, form WASHCO, train few people for maintenance purpose and finally leave those areas. The problems come when damages occur or the WASHCO is not as strong as it is expected. Some groups from the beneficiaries also think that it is the duty of the government or partner organizations to take care of everything. So, when we talk about water sustainability, the main issue to be flagged is the existence of already established schemes but not working due to minor and major damages. 
Interviewer: Great, can you also tell me other challenges?
Respondent: the other problem is lack of well-coordinated actions among the different stakeholders. Had the institutional coordination been strong, as the principle, we would have solved many of the challenges we are facing now and increased the water coverage percentage 
Lack of proper awareness and positive attitude from sides: beneficiaries and respective offices mandated to manage the water schemes is another main challenge. At the first place, the community is not invited during pre-implementation and implementation phases of a project while they remain the main beneficiaries later.  
Absence of incentives, such as daily perdiems for the existing experts when they are out to rural and remote areas is another problem 


. 

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: Solution for awareness: representatives of the community should attend meeting concerning the projects being conducted in their locality, they have to be consulted about the location of the water points, the total financial amount planned to be invested. For example, when we inform the community that the amount invested in one of the schemes is say 2 million birr, they will start caring about it since this is a huge money. 
It is also important to integrate the role of water services with other sectors, for example, with education and health in the manner that without which it is impossible to get those services so that people can have more attention and value to the service 
Interviewer: I think you responded to the second problem, let me remind you the other challenge: lack of sense of ownership, do you have some ideas as a solution? 
Respondent: the main responsible person here is the government. It should work hard and regularly monitor WASHCOs so that they are well informed about their mandates and increase their performance financial capacity. Let me give you one example: A kebele has some 1000 residents in it, if we assume that there is a strong WASHCO with in this kebele which can collect monthly contribution of at least 10 birr per month/individual dweller, the total will be 10,000 birr and this would be some 12,000 birr annually. If it is properly managed, this money would cover different costs, including maintenance. This is the reason why I said, we should work on empowering WASHCOs and the rural community if we are caring about sustainability. 
Let me also give you another example, in Luka Kebele, Benatsemay woreda, there is a strong WASHCO which is succeeded to save over 50,000 birr from their monthly contributions.  Later on when the water schemes get older and damaged, they started to use some of this money for maintenance services. They have still some 20,000 money left after their expenses. This kebele should be taken as a model for others. 
For the incentives, it is also the mandate of the government to find more funds to get the services functioning. It can invite local and international partners and initiate fund raising activities, including continuous request to get support of vehicles or motor bikes 

Interviewer: you have also mentioned that lack of coordination among respective stakeholders is another challenge affecting sustainability, any solution? 
Respondent: this needs a common plan, to be evaluated at least quarterly and report the outcomes. 
Interviewer: I know that there is such kind of platform but it is not functioning well, what do you think is the reason behind? 
Respondent: Yes, that is true. There is WASH committee but the problem is that the board chairs are usually heads of departments and zone administrators who are too busy for office level and other duties. Instead of that it is important to strengthening the technical committee which consists of experts as the majority of its members. 
Particularly, closely working with the respective partners, WASHCOs and regularly monitoring the functions of these organs would be very important. 

3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: 
The most important thing solution is empowering the WASHCOs and the community in general. Working on WASHCOs regularly would solve many problems. For example, we can have secured and protected water schemes in the kebele, they can also cover maintenance and replacement issues with their own resources and technicians shortly
