1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: As woreda stakeholder, we have received trainings on how to make water service sustainable in the area. Different stakeholders have been involved in the training and get a clear understanding about their role in sustaining water service in the woreda.
One of the problem for sustainability of water service in the woreda is lack of qualified work force that regularly monitor water schemes. There are more than 50 kebeles in the woreda but we have only two water technicians at woreda level.
The second problem is that there is no capacity building training for woreda water office staffs and rural water management committees (WASHCos) on management and maintenance of water schemes. Most of the water schemes become out of service due to minor problems. If we capacitate WASHCos by providing technical trainings and supporting maintenance tools, the schemes can serve the community for long period.
Interviewer: What other problems are there related to sustainability of water and sanitation service?
Respondent: The other major problem is the woreda water office has serious shortage of budget for the management of water schemes in different parts of the woreda. Finance is a big challenge for woreda water office to dispose its responsibility properly. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned that there is lack of skilled work force in woreda water office. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: There are graduates of technical and vocational training colleges that are unemployed in different parts of South Omo Zone. Thus, organizing this unemployed youth in the form of micro and small enterprise and provide credit access from the woreda revolving fund can be one solution to reach large number of kebeles of the woreda. This arrangement enable to create employment opportunity as well as to provide maintenance service in remote kebeles of the woreda.
Interviewer: You have raised that rural water management committees (WASHCos) are not supported by trainings and other technical skills. Because of this, most water schemes become non-functional due to minor problems. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to this problem? 
Respondent: All stakeholders including NGOs operating in the areas should have plan to strengthen WASHCos through providing technical skill training. It is also good to create opportunities for WASHCos to visit successful WASHCos in other areas. Moreover, we have to provide light maintenance tools for each WASHCos functioning in rural areas.
Interviewer: You have indicated that the woreda water office has serious shortage of budget for management of water schemes and this affects sustainability of water service in the woreda. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: The woreda administration and woreda finance offices should give priority for water sector, as water is life for human beings. Thus, the woreda should reduce the budget from other sectors and allocate sufficient budget for the water sector. NGOs should also support woreda water office both technically and financially.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Of the solution, I mentioned earlier strengthening WASHCos and organizing micro and small enterprises that provide maintenance service in rural areas is the most important solution to ensure sustainable water service in the area. If we can do this, we can protect the community from water borne diseases and we can minimize political instability due a claim for access for clean water. In general, we can improve the efficiency of the existing water schemes.
