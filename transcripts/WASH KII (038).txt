Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: One, it is about management. When I say management, it is about the management committees. Some are active and others are inactive. Two, there is lack of information, three there is lack of capacity building, sensitization and creation of awareness. 
Interviewer: When you say there is lack of information, could you explain further what you mean by that?
Respondent: I mean, the wrong choice of technology. Depending on the budget, if they are not advised accordingly, instead of picking earth dam, depending on topography, they can go for pipelines. They need to be advised and provided with the right information on how they can choose the reliable technologies and facilities, for instance boreholes where they are required. Most of them say that they need boreholes which could turn out to be saline. In other words what I’m saying is that communities should be guided when choosing the type of projects they wish to be implemented in their areas. 
Interviewer: What about capacity Building? Could you expound on it?
Respondent: Training of committees especially on operation and maintenance specifically and we must have sub committees to undertake that. Also, training on procurement, finances and record keeping. For example, if a water scheme has 9 committee members, each one has to be allocated duties. If one member is a treasurer, the responsibilities of a treasurer are well known. For example, 3 of them can be in charge of operation and maintenance, 3 to be in charge of record keeping and 3 to be in charge of procurement and the like. 
Another problem with these rural schemes is procurement. Once you visit some of these rural schemes, you find that procurement is a problem. Procurement has become a cancer. There is a project in this sub-county by the name Kithiini. They don’t have a bank account, they share the money amongst themselves, as though it is relief food and so I had to intervene. So they have to be trained on all the necessary leadership skills.
Interviewer: Any addition to the challenges?
Respondent: Another one is ownership of the project. Under this we can also capture land acquisition. Basically, those are the key issues. Here I am talking about having proper land agreements before setting up a project and also ownership in the sense that the community having the feeling that the project belongs to them. It is through ownership that you can talk of sustainability and most of our water management committees are inactive and dormant and they need to be brought to a point where every member knows what they are supposed to do. As a water officer, I am also very important in those systems. Ownership, land acquisition and what the parties contribute matters a lot. For example, if we have a project A which is just starting, I have a part to play. The community also has a part to play. So we must bring together all the key stakeholders involved so that we can be in a position to achieve continuous sustainability. It’s not about one individual. 
Another key challenge is transport for mobility to access the active management committees. You don’t expect someone like me to move around in a motorbike but my assistant is young so he can use a motorbike. If this office can be provided with transport, I think we can deliver and we can be in position to make sure the policy of the governor, the one about 65% of the total population to access safe, clean, reliable and affordable water. But with what we are undergoing, I am sorry to say but we are not in a position to deliver that and I’m appealing to donors to support us. I went somewhere, in Italamu earth dam, it is 100% silted up and they dig small shallow wells inside and the soil is lose which is a danger. The water is also contaminated and turbid and it is the one they are relying on. Kitui south sub-county has real water problems.
Another challenge, because of power, that is electricity and diesel in most of the water schemes, they just decide their own tariffs. Tariff setting is guided by the Water Services Regulatory Board (WASREB). When we look at the way the schemes operate, since most of them are motorized, they are always asking for meetings so as to review their water tariffs. We are not against tariff review because we know that schemes have operating costs which should be considered when setting the tariffs. However, the committees and the locals must sit, discuss and agree on any tariff changes.

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: The inactive water management committees need basic training, that is why I spoke about capacity building. They also need to be guided when choosing the technology and type of project that NGOs, national or county government wants to implement for them and they must know how to rank them.
Interviewer: Who in your opinion would have the responsibility of guiding the community?
Respondent: The sub-County water office which represents the county at the sub county level. Due to inadequate personnel, we are not represented fully at ward levels. When you look at agriculture, they have enough personnel and if I may say, excess. They are represented even at village level. I am in charge of 6 wards with 41 villages and my colleague is in charge of coordinating 2 out of the 6 wards.  So, for us to deliver, we have to be in touch with the sub-county and ward administrators for reliable data. 
The way our organizational structure is, there is the governor, minister, the chief officer, director, deputy director, assistant director, the heads of planning then the Sub County officers. If you refer to the organizational structure chart, where we are there is a bit of a gap because we are not represented at the ward and village levels. My colleague who is in charge of 2 wards does not have a car and all the motorbikes are broken down for us to survive, we have to be beggars in terms of transport.
In general, communities should be guided by sub-county water officers assisted by the relevant partners such as NGOs, public health officers and any other relevant entities.
For sustainability of the schemes, I have decided to approach it in a different way. At the village level if there is a new project, we involve the village administrators. This will solve the issues of ownership. 
Interviewer: There was the challenge of ownership, solution would you recommend for that?
Respondent: From the beginning, the community must be taken through the process of having the project. If it is a borehole, particle sounding, installation of casings step by step, pump and motor, civil works and the like and they must also be taught about ownership. Before devolution, we were having the water services board but now things have changed because water is one of the functions which have been devolved to the county government. At the sub-County level, we have the Kitui Water and Sanitation Company (KITWASCO) which is mandated by the Water Act 2016 to provide water on behalf of the county government because the county government cannot do all that and the company has to make profits. Sometimes, when a project is complete and the contractor wants to hand over, there is that confusion because the community wants to run the project but when you check the components of the project, they cannot be able to manage it. That is where you bring in the issue of KITWASCO to take over. If KITWASCO takes over as a water services provider, I will recommend that all the viable water schemes to be given either to the water service provider or a caretaker. By caretaker, I mean that a project may be having a committee, but there should be one person who does all the work and reports to the committee. This is what will save us. The schemes should have a caretaker or the water services providers should take over.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? 
Respondent:  Capacity building, that is thorough training of the management committees on the basic things especially on operation and maintenance, procurement, finance and record keeping. If you go to the rural schemes, you find a treasurer, whose work is keeping of money from water sales performing the duties of a chairman or the duties of the technicians. I think developing clear job descriptions for the members will be helpful for the schemes. If the chairman is unable to carry out the duties, they can delegate. Let the executive committees have small sub-committees and have each member do their own work according to their job descriptions. I have introduced this approach and it is working. On the issue of artisans, we trained water resource persons, when they went back to their wards and villages after the training, they claimed to have powers instead of assisting the committees and learning about the key fundamental issues like procurement and record keeping.
Interviewer: Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: These schemes have money and if well managed, they can be sustainable. Rural schemes have a lot of money. If we could have youth, church representatives and trustees to manage them. This would be very good. Even the projects could have enough money to expand and have other projects.
Interviewer: You have said capacity building is a solution and you consider it as most important, so if these water committees are trained, what do you foresee? 
Respondent: I can see projects being run well and being able to operate very well for all the years within their design period. We normally design projects for 10 years. If capacity building is done, the schemes will run well without any problems. In other words smooth running of the water schemes and this will have an impact.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui?
 Respondent: Normally, as per our work plan, we normally budget for a borehole, 2 earth dams and one pipeline per ward. That is from our end, not mentioning the NGOs. Like now, our financial year has ended and the financial year for 2019/2020 started in July so we may get the funds very late but we must move very fast. We have a problem and I would prefer the way the things used to be. Devolution is not so bad but if properly handled, citizens can get the benefits. The issue of money coming from the national government to the county government is taking long. We may get the funds in October which means I’ll have nothing to report on. This month is almost ending, so we will not be to achieve as per our work plan and we normally prepare the work plans earlier before the year ends. I am yet to get the printed final copy of the work plan and I am happy at least I have seen it has captured the existing projects and if for example, a place like Mutha is leading in the number of boreholes, no need to do other and we have decided to embark on the existing ones. It does not make any sense to drill another borehole when the existing ones require only a replacement or repair of the motor and pump. This time we want to see if there is proper distribution of these water structures. 
Interviewer: What influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Through barazas (public meetings). For these projects to have an impact we must go to the local beneficiaries. There are those who may say that they don’t want water projects and would instead need health projects. If they say that they need water projects, they give suggestions on whether they want a dam or another water facility. After this we go to the field to survey. Like now, I am from checking a proposed dam site but we have had to change because as a water officer I saw that it is not a viable site and we agreed to change the siting. So letting the community decide on the projects really helps in terms of ownership.
Interviewer: So, it is the feedback you get from the community that helps you to plan? 
Respondent: Yes. Especially with the new constitution, citizens have been empowered and have a say in the development within their areas.  
Interviewer: I am curious to know about the maintenance of the rural schemes. Who does it here within Kitui South?
Respondent: In Kitui South, we have some fairly active water management committees which have some bank accounts with money, as you are aware we have not received any funds this financial year, so for example Kalamabani we are communicating with them and giving technical advice on purchase, they withdraw some funds then we give them an artisan for installation works. For the committees that are not financially stable, we have to use our kitty. Normally there is an officer who is in charge of borehole services at the headquarters and the kitty is for buying items for the minor spares. For the gensets, we buy for them but for the new water schemes, we contract either the water service provider, private persons or a caretaker.
Interviewer: So that is the direction you want to take?
Respondent: Yes. With the knowledge I have about rural water, I think that can assist.
Interviewer: Yes and there have been discussions about it.
Respondent: Yes if properly monitored, advised and proper guidelines. There is another scheme they are doing the merry go round with the money from the scheme which they use for maintenance. So I think for sustainability to be achieved, there should be training and proper handling of the money. The projects have been giving us their monthly reports on the revenue collected, proposed revenue earned and expenditure. For example the Kanyangi borehole is yielding well. They have 17 kiosks and they are selling the 20 ltr Jerri can at Kenya Shillings 2 and they have 13 casual workers. Also, Kisasi borehole I was there and I pushed them to build a standard water kiosk.
Interviewer: Are there local maintenance service providers in this sub county?
Respondent:  It’s only KITWASCO. Now, I want to give them the Kanyangi-Kangukangu water line. The water flows up to here but because of the heads, there is another tank, 500 m3 a steel tank. We assume the water will be coming to that tank then we have a booster there to raise it then the water will be injected to the exceeding pipes.
