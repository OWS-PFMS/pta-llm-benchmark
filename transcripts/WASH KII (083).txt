1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability in your town? 
Interviewer: The first what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability in Debrebirhan town? For instance if you say limited capacity what do you mean by limited capacity? You will tell me the details. So, you will tell me the major problems especially regarding public latrine, communal latrine, and disposal site. 
Respondent: You see, sanitation is a broad concept. It is not just about public and communal latrine. On the one side there is solid waste and on the other side there is liquid waste, and again there is the environment. 
In Debrebirhan town the major problem sanitation sustainability regarding public and communal latrine is from my observation lack of awareness. It is difficult and takes time to create the awareness needed towards sanitation and hygiene. For instance, we have planned we will create 23 Open Defecation Free towns by 2037, including Addis Ababa. To do so, there should be a long term effort to develop the awareness. We should work on school children.
Interviewer: We will come to the solutions. Tell me about the actual problems. You have said that one is awareness gap.
Respondent: Yes. It is but, awareness is not created in a short period of time. It need long journey. We should work on school children. The town should support this.  
Interviewer: Ato Tamirat, in your broader definition of sanitation what are the main problems.
Respondent: In that broad sense, one of the main problems in the town is that sanitation is not institutionalized. 
Interviewer: What do you mean? Explain it.
Respondent: When I say it is not institutionalized, there is no body with accountability assigned to the issues of sanitation. For instance, solid waste management is under the municipality. But it is not backed in terms of finance, logistics, and legal framework. At federal level there is a directive. However, when you come to regional level there is no any legal document ratified by regional council. 
Liquid waste management is completely run by water utility office. The office’s ultimate goal is to collect it distributes. The waste might be collected through sewer network, satellite collection station, mobile toilets, and vacuum truck and recycle the waste water. The office is, however, poorly equipped in terms of human resource, logistics, and finance. There are many core processes in the office. A process comprises many positions; but practically, you might find just a process head or one person. They are not paying due attention like distributing potable water. There is no waste water treatment plant except in Addis Ababa. The wastewater is just being pumped and dumped somewhere without treating it. This is due to lack of understanding what consequences will it have. It should be institutionalized like the water distribution. 
Debrebirhan has becoming an industrial city. Due to the growing number of industries in the town, there will be huge sanitation problem after a few years. I have seen many treatment plants of the factories in Debrebirhan. They do not have complete treatment process. There are primary, secondary, and tertiary treatment levels. You can treat wastewater from latrine to a level that it can be potable water. There is a technology to do so, even though it is costly. But, town level facilities can be shared. For instance, in our town we mix up domestic and industrial wastes. This is due to lack of commitment. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned many major problems. What would be the solution?
Respondent: The solution for this is paying equal attention for both the water and wastewater management in the water utility office.  The sanitation sector should be strengthened in terms of facilities, manpower, and training. 
Higher education institutions should also devise a curriculum and teach sanitation as a department. 
Interviewer: What about to improve the community’s awareness? Open defecation is still a problem in the urban and rural areas. 
Respondent: Awareness creation should be implemented through religious organizations. People tend to listen to spiritual leaders, so these leaders should teach the community.
The other point is that there should be legal support for the penalties. For instance, when you want to prohibit urinating in a specific location, there must be a legal framework that punishes offenders. Otherwise, if you go to the court, there is no directive that penalizes lawbreakers. 
Moreover, media outlets should work on this. This should be done.
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: you have mentioned many solutions. Among the solutions you listed, which one do you think is the best? Is it institutionalizing, training manpower, or creating awareness? Which one would bring bigger concomitant positive effects?
Respondent: You see, each of the solutions has their own role. It is difficult to make a difference with working on one solution and ignoring others. I don’t think it is a wise approach. What I believe is we should work on all of them. We might assign different weights to each of the solutions.   
Interviewer: Just give me your priority
Respondent: That would be simpler for me. I would say that working on school children is crucial. We should work on elementary school kids.
Interviewer: What difference would that make?
Respondent: Well, if they are taught that open defecation is shabby, they themselves will not do it and they will pass the awareness to the next generation. Secondly, spiritual leaders should participate in awareness creation. Third, awareness should be created by stakeholders and partners such as TETRATECH and IRC WASH.
