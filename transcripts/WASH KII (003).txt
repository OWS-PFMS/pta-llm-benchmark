
1. In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation / water service sustainability in your woreda?  Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation / water service sustainability?
 The major challenges in terms of sustainability of the city's sanitation and water services is absence of land-fill site where solid or liquid waste can be dumped. In addition to that lack of coordinated actions to sewage and solid waste disposal system can also be another problem contributing a lot to the problem in the town.  Lack of awareness from the community is another challenge affecting sustainability. 
There is shortage of potable water in the wereda as a whole. There is also shortage of finance to implement water scheme that are already designed and shelfed. Water infrastructures design and pace of construction is not congruent to the increasing population size in the area. In fact, water project is costly. Due to power outage, water pumps don’t give service regularly as a result of which we are forced to provide water by shift. Lack of well-maintained and functioning roads connecting the main road with water pipes has also been a problem affecting proper inflow of the service. 

Interviewer: To what extent do the problems create impact on the sanitation sustainability?

There are three things that need to be fulfilled to have a proper and well-functioning disposal system: Temporary waste collection site, vehicles to transport the waste and land-fill site where all sewage and solid waste is dumped. Lack of one/all of these services will create a burden to the community in such a way that wastes will be leaked everywhere and everyone will be exposed to communicable diseases, the consequences will be even worse than we might expect. 

Interviewer:  How do you relate the lack of awareness with the sanitation problem that you mentioned?

Majority of the community don’t have the know how about the consequences of the waste and they are not caring as such. What they do is just collecting waste from their home and throwing it everywhere, be it open space or private land.  Municipality service doesn’t have the capacity to collect the waste thrown here and there and that will gradually backfire against the community through disease, leaking to the water pumps and roads. 
2.  What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
First of all, persistent coordination is required among Land administration office, Water and Sewerage office and Municipality.  There is Green and Beautification work process unit in the Municipality which is directly responsible to work collaboratively with Urban Land Administration and Water and Sewerage Office.  Additional budget is needed for the project to be effective, but the offices need to have common understanding about the problems we are facing now in order to bring a coordinated action together.





Interviewer: Can you give me a bit more explanation on the relevance of coordinated actions? 

There is sufficient land that can be potentially allocated to a land-fill site. We can also look for additional budget to be allocated for this project, however, unless we have joint actions from the different actors working in WASH, we can’t go far from what is stated in the paper.  What we should do is to look for sufficient money that would be used to pay for land compensation if we need it to quickly take land from individual farmers for the purpose of land-fill. But this has not been properly handled in the last decade. That is why I said there is lack of coordination. For instance, the master plan of this town shows that the land-fill site is located in the border area of this wereda. Since it is a border area, it may not be as such easy to take land, fence it and damp everything. It requires frequent consultation and payment to the neighboring farmers. However, it won’t be impossible if you negotiate and discuss with the people around there. Paying compensation to the people who may be displaced for this purpose is one that can solve the problem that may arise from the people surrounding the border area. 

Interviewer: What about the solution to the problem of potable water in your area?

The most probable solution could be providing cost effective hand-pump water wells. Following this, power outage problem should be solved either by requesting additional MW or buying an independent generator that would be used in the absence of power from the main grid. In the absence of power, however, hand-pump can also be used but this is not a sustainable service rather an emergency aid.  

3. Of the solutions you listed, which one is the most important?  Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
 The immediate solution I suggest is developing cost-effective water pump machines so as to outreach the people with water and sanitation service. Though it is not a long range action, it could at least respond to the persistent requests from the community. What matters most is to mobilize all resources to use maximum potential in developing WASH infrastructures and to use them sustainably. Continuous awareness creation schemes are also mandatory to get people and institutions alarmed of the consequences of sanitation and hygiene. 
Interviewer: Do you think that community has initiatives to bring about changes with regard to sanitation? 

I think so, as far as the officials from the local government side are working diligently, we can change the attitude of our community. I know that some people are throwing wastes everywhere and expecting the municipality to come and collect, however, if we can set a system that can monitor and mobilize people in this regard, we can bring changes.
