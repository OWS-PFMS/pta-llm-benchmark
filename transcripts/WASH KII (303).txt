Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: The main one could be management whereby the committee involved at times misappropriates the funds collected from selling water. If there is no money then when a breakdown occurs communities do not have water at the water points and it takes a very long time to get the systems repaired. The second one would be in terms of capital replacement of maybe gensets, pumps and maybe something which requires an amount which the committees are not able to afford. If they cannot afford the repairs, again the system remains broken down for very long unless someone comes in and supports them.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: In terms of management, we need as an administration to monitor what these management committees are doing so that they are able to report on a monthly basis and make sure that the money that they are collecting is also banked and there is a system to account it such that they do not have a window of misappropriating the same. In terms of cost of repairs, for that we have been supporting them by doing asset replacement but we hope that they can overcome the challenge with time. We are trying to invest in renewable energy, whereby in terms of cost, operation and maintenance costs would go down and they will be able to manage and even save up money such that they are able to buy some of these items on their own. For the asset replacement, at times the county government has to chip in because maybe it is not affordable for the schemes. We are not even able to save enough in case of a major  breakdown, we may require to replace  a pump, a genset, maybe even in future when we talk about renewable energy…to replace a whole set of solar panels it may be not possible for the community but maybe if the county government is not able to maybe manage at times to replace those items, then we require support from non-governmental organizations or maybe we suggest that in future we may have a fund which could come in handy and replace the same such that there is proper planning in terms of that fund whereby it is envisaged that we will be able to buy for them some of those major items. 
The other option would be if the money collected from these water projects is put into a kitty and then it is managed as a common kitty to address issues of sustainability that would also be a solution. The other option would be having a water service provider which covers a given area and it has operations like the existing formal water service providers such that it is autonomous in terms of its management. We initially had one, when the water reforms came in, that was in 2005 where by we were calling it a water board company which was covering the whole region of Kitui but because I think it was being managed outside Kitui; the board was being managed in Nyeri, maybe people saw it as an external entity but if it is internalized by the community and they are sensitized and it is not politicized it can still work. I believe it can work if we have a major water service provider which takes care of all the projects across the county and we have maybe managers at specific regions who report  to the main water service provider then we will be able to address some of these issues. 




Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I mentioned that now the major issue would be management to deal with management by putting in place monitoring and accountability systems because in terms of the money collected if it is not properly accounted for then it is used for other purposes other than for the project; maybe even individual purposes and there is no system on how the money collected and the water produced is recorded then we are not able to say this project A was able to collect a hundred thousand this month and the expenses are maybe fifty thousand, the saving is so much, so the first initial stage would be make sure that we know what is produced by installing a master meter in all our systems, then once we know the water produced and the sales are also recorded then we will have a system where we are able to hold somebody to account in case of misuse. That would be the first step then the other would be now banking this money and having a management which is able to account for the funds collected and  be prudent to request for use of the same if a breakdown occurs or some repairs are required other than just using it without any procedure. 
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: At the moment we are replacing water management committees who are not able to manage their schemes prudently. We normally replace them and take the new elected committee, take them through training whereby they are able to address some of the issues we had mentioned about the management, finance, water produced, and how they come up with the cost of water per jerry can maybe because we consider those issues of operations and maintenance and other factors which are considered in the production of water. 
We are also doing hybrid systems for water pumping systems such that we are able to address the cost of operation and maintenance. The other one we have also involved the county administration that is the village administrators whereby they are able to get in touch with those management committees and they are able to report to us as quick as possible where management issues have arisen before the situation comes to a level whereby we are not able to intervene.
Interviewer: What informed/influence the selection of these interventions? 
Respondent: In terms of management, because when we get to the bottom of the matter, where we have management issues we find that they are not accounting for the money they collect, they do not have a system where they are recording whatever sales they are making, and at times they do not have working meters. When we replace those committees, we make sure those systems are in place such that there is a reporting system and we are able to get records from them such that we are able to see whether they will be able to meet some of those minimal expenses. In terms of renewable energy, we are focusing on this because of the realization that the operating costs for diesel and electricity powered systems are very high and at times these costs drain their finances even if they are prudent in terms of the management. 
We are engaging the village administrators because we are thin in terms of the number of staff on the ground and as a sub-county water officer, you may have just one or two staff and you are based at the headquarters. We do not have even staff at the ward level. So, it is easier for the management committees of the schemes at the village level to report immediately to the village administrator who will at times even walk to the facilities and see the initial problem and maybe now report to us before we now move in and intervene.
