1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: The main problems related to our water service provision are: 
-Inadequate budget: This is related to the limited capital budget we receive from the region for the region as water service infrastructures require adequate financing. 
The second main challenge is the problem to mobilize the community in stream development and in other small level projects. There are some model woredas and kebeles which are developing streams and providing potable water services by mobilizing their community both interms of financing and labor works. Others couldn’t take lessons from those successful areas. This is also attributed to lack of commitment from the frontline experts and decision makers, including our office. As you know, the main contributing factor in rural water service sustainability is mobilization of the community to contribute their labor and finance and to cover the gap. For instance, some kebeles in South Ari woreda are now engaged in their own initiatives where members of the community are contributing cement, concrete, stones and pipes to build small water schemes in their kebeles. So, the problem is the limited commitment to escalate these types of good initiatives to other kebeles and woredas where there are still acute water shortages. Had this been implemented by the government, it takes a very long time due to bid bureaucracy and other factors. 
The other challenge is technical gaps and lack of commitment from the government to write proposals and look for a specific intervention from the development assistance/NGOs. 
Interviewer:  Could you explain this a little bit more? Is the challenge from the NGOs side or from the government? 
Respondent: actually, the main challenge is limited capacity and commitment from our side. Majority of the NGOs are interested to assist the community and to fill the gaps as far as they are directed and prioritized to a specific issue. 

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have raised some important challenges earlier, now let us discuss about the solutions to these problems. Let’s start from the budget problem
Respondent: when we see the budget issues, this is a common problem everywhere including the region and the country itself. Due to this, some woredas were even unable to cover the cost of the salary for their workers for over 4 and 5 months. The capital budget to be allocated annually is still limited and that is why the region and the zone are working with NGOs. 
Interviewer: Yes you have niggled out what is meant by budget problem but my question here is that what should be done to alleviate this problem sustainably?
Respondent: Yes, the first thing is to continuously mobilize the community itself and facilitate their participation in the local financing and in kind contributions. Second, the government should work hard to attract more and more NGOs to the sector since it can’t bring expected changes and solve the problems of water sustainability by itself. 
Interviewer: What about the solution to the current gaps the government has to mobilize and create pubic awareness in the water service issues?
Respondent: In this case, it needs to have continuous and closer discussions with the community. 
We should transparently discuss about the locations, the share of the capital needed from the community and types of water schemes to be developed since they are the final beneficiaries at the end of the day. It needs commitment from the zonal and woreda level administrators to travel to the local areas, discuss with the community at the grass root levels 
Interviewer: you have also pointed out that there is technical gap in producing proposals indicating gap analysis so as to attract development partners, what should be done then?
Respondent: In line with this, we need to have frontline experts and administrators that should lead the institutes and water stakeholders with knowledge and technical skills. The good opportunity in our zone is we have adequate number of development partners; we have also Jinka University to fill the gaps of knowledge and advisory. So, what we need is to organize and coordinate these main stakeholders to take common actions against water sustainability problems. We should also create technical teams who will take the responsibility of studying gap analysis related to the service and who can present their findings in a platform where all the stakeholders are attending. It also needs to coordinate technicians at the ground level with those located at the zonal and regional levels so that they can find out issues like ‘what is need where’ if we can follow these procedures, I can say that we can solve water service problems sustainability. 

3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: You have forwarded important ideas or recommendations to the problems related to the water supply shortage, now let me ask you that of the solutions you mentioned, which is the most important and should be given top priority?
Respondent: I think the most important thing is closely working with the community and engage them in the planning everybody knows about water management at household level. By mobilizing the community, we can allow them contribute both in terms of resources they have and the labor to solve small and manageable problems. 
Interviewer: Okay, what next steps would happen if you closely work with the community?
Respondent: Good point. Assume aggregate contributions of individual efforts to fill the resource and budget gap, since the community members in different levels are many and diverse; bringing them to a common plat form would be the most important action. By doing so, the community will develop a sense of belongingness, in terms of overseeing the security and sustainability of the water schemes developed around their localities.
