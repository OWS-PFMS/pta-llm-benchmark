1) Interviewer: Okay, my first question, what are the challenges of water service sustainability in this town. Try to list them one by one and we will also look for a solution to each one of them. 
Interviewee: The first challenge is that the farmers are using a traditional irrigation system...
Interviewer: What does it mean?

Interviewee: I mean they put a dam on upper stream without doing any research. They have put 13 dams on the river and when the water reaches to our project it will be very few and we can't provide the town with enough clean water. That way of irrigation is not scientific. They are using traditional way and it is affecting the water capacity we are getting to filter and make it usable for drinking water. The second issue, I think the government has started working on it, it's about urbanization. We can not restrict people from water access. If someone comes to the town, build a temporary place and asks us for water, we must provide...
Interviewer: But your limited services cannot accommodate all new arrivals...
Interviewee: Yes our capacity is limited and the population growth is affecting our service.
Interviewer: I think that is a big challenge. Okay, do u have another challenge to add? 
Interviewee: Rain is also one factor on the capacity of the river and since we are not having much of it recently, the river and streams are getting dry. So, even the weather affects our ability to provide sustainable service.  
Interviewer: Okay, so these are the challenges you are facing in providing sustainable water service to the town.
Interviewee: Yes.


2) Interviewer: Okay, so as an expert can you suggest what should be done on solving these challenges. You have mentioned about the traditional irrigation system. How can we solve that challenge so that the town gets a sustainable water service?
Interviewee: I think the irrigation system should change from traditional way to scientific way. We can do a research and practice Irrigation canals which will not affect the water capacity of the river so that both the farmers and the town can get the water they need to use. I can't say the farmers should not use irrigation. What I am saying is that the irrigation system should be improved so that the water is used in an efficient way for both of us. Most parts of the town get water only twice a week and even for that, we have to knock every door in the government office starting from the zonal office to the mayor office. 
Interviewer: Do you mean that you'll need a queue to get water?
Interviewee: Yes in most parts of town you need a queue to get water service. And mind you this town is the capital city of our Zone. 
Interviewer: Okay let's discuss the second challenge. What should be done about this urbanization issue? Who do you think is responsible for fixing that issue?
Interviewee: Well, we need to update our projects with the growth of population in mind. You know our projects are based on the population back a few years ago and they are not complying with the current population and this need to be improved.
Interviewer: Can you explain this a bit more?
Interviewee: Let's say we have a project started two years ago. This project is not updating with the current population rather it is focused on the population back two years ago. But if we update each of the projects frequently so that they can comply with the population growth they can be effective.
Interviewer: So in your suggestion, who is responsible for updating these projects?
Interviewee: This can be done in all offices related to water services...
Interviewer: Offices like?
Interviewee: For example the city administration, the city finance office and NGOs working on water-related services.
Interviewer: Okay, what about the rain issue? How do you think it can be solved? I think this is a natural or environmental issue and how can it be fixed?
Interviewee: Yes, it's natural and environmental issue. The solution, it needs a budget of course. We can store water when the rain comes so that it can be used when the rain is not there. If we can allocate some budget on this issue, we can build a huge storage area like the Gefersa in Addis Ababa. You know we hear through media that most African countries do that and we are not using our water resources effectively. If we have the storage, we can use it during the summer time and everyone will have enough water running. 
Interviewer: So what do we need to implement this storage issue?
Interviewee: I think it needs strategy, planning and of course a budget to make it happen. We are losing water due to this challenge.




3) Interviewer: You have mentioned three challenges and their solutions. Now which one do you think is more essential? Which one should be prioritized in order to see visible improvement in sustainable water service?
Interviewee: I think we should focus changing the irrigation system. Then we can also focus on the water storage issue so that during summer time we will not have a shortage of water.
Interviewer: Okay, but which one do you prioritize from those two challenges? Is it the irrigation system or the water storage system?
Interviewee: The more important thing is the water storage as we are facing summertime soon and we need to start storing water soon. 
Interviewer: So this storing water requires the installation of distillation and...
Interviewee: It requires many installations like distillations, treatment and another kind of projects.




4) Interviewer: Let's talk about coordination. You have mentioned some offices earlier and as a water supply office staff, you coordinate with many offices. So how effective is this coordination on maintaining sustainability water service in this town?
Interviewee: I think we have good coordination currently, yet we can improve in the future.
Interviewer: Can you specify which offices you are related are having good coordination?
Interviewee: We have a good relationship with the city administration and the finance office. The finance office holds our budget given by the city administration. We are also talking with Land Managment Ofice about a waste disposal place. Their feedback is good. They already have set up a committee working on our proposal and I hope we will get it soon. 
Interviewer: What about NGOs? Are you having a good relationship with them?
Interviewee: We were working with Projanist [?] [09:36] 
Interviewer: Projanist [?] [09:39]?
Interviewee: Yes, it was an NGO working under Water Aid Ethiopia. I think they have phased out recently, but we had a close relationship with them.
Interviewer: What about now? Is there an NGO whom your office has close relationship?
Interviewee: Currently there is no NGO working with us.
Interviewer: Okay, you have said there is a good relationship with administration and finance offices. And also there is no NGO currently working alongside you. What needs to be done to improve coordination between offices who are giving water-related services? 
Interviewee: You know water service is everyone's concern. Almost all government offices and some other NGOs who are not listed here are related to water service in one way or another. We cannot attract investment in our town if we are not providing clean water to the residents so even the investment office is concerned on this matter. My advice is we should have more planned and scheduled meetings and get help from every concerned sector from the government and the NGO.


5) Interviewer: Do you think the improvement of coordination can help solving those challenges you mentioned earlier?
Interviewee: Yes, of course.
Interviewee: Let's see them one by one. How does coordination help solving the traditional irrigation issue?
Interviewee: You know water is everyone's concern. Even the farmers need clean water, right? They can't get clean water from the irrigation system they are conducting right now. So if we have coordination on the change of the irrigation system, we can accommodate both the farmers need and the residents need.
Interviewer: What about the urbanization issue? The town is growing up along with its population and your office is not keeping up with providing sustainable water service to all residents. How does coordination help to solve this issue?
Interviewee: Everything is about exchanging information between us. If we have a frequent meeting we can update each other and know where we are in regards to the population growth so that everyone would start planning to upkeep. But that can be done when we meet frequently and update each other. Urbanization is a natural process and you can't stop it. But if we work together we will have all necessary information to focus on our responsibilities and success will be guaranteed that way.
Interviewer: Thank you for giving me a feedback. We are going to communicate all these offices. And I hope you will help me. Is Girma coming tomorrow?
Interviewee: Maybe...
Interviewer: I will also call you. We are here the whole week. 
Interviewee: Do you want something out of this paper?
Interviewer: Yes, can you make a copy of that paper?
Interviewee: We don't have a copier.
