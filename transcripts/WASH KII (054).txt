
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:    One is consulting.  We’ve talked about KEWI – capacity building for communities to run their schemes in a more sustainable manner.  Fundfix, the influence they have, we have learned about where they have started in Mwingi North sub-county.  We think it can work, even if there is resistance from the communities, I think with time it can work and that way we can reduce the downtime of our broken down rural systems.

.  

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:    I think the major problem which may not be unique to Kitui County, is that we do not have a policy to run rural schemes, like the case with urban schemes.  I think we need to do some policies at the county level to be able to run the schemes which will have to be adhered to when running the schemes.  Bigger schemes have a platform where the performance of the scheme can be monitored – we have the water regulatory authority, we used to call it water regulatory board.  Those schemes, there is a platform on monitoring them and measuring their standards.  For the rural schemes we don’t have that, so I think that is where we have to start.  

The other problem that is also being addressed, you know, we need resources at the sub-county level.  If something goes wrong, we’ll have to come for resources from the county treasury.  If we have some resources we control at the sub-county level, we can respond faster to some of the minor issues.  And maybe the big ones can be handled at the county headquarters.  

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee:  I have started already delving into recommendations.  We need to do a policy, the county needs to do a policy on how the rural schemes are going to be run.  Platform of regulatory reporting to be sharing successes, maybe failures and successes in the running of the rural schemes.

And then decentralization of resources, we need some at the sub-county level for responding fast enough for some small issues.  

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee:    I think both are very important, we need the policy for the rural schemes.  And also decentralization of resources is equally important.  You see we’re reaching a situation where the communities see we are just there but we can do very little apart from giving information.  They may think we’re not able to assist them, so they’ll start making requests directly to the headquarters, to the directorate, so I think we need to have something at the sub-county level to respond to small breakdowns.  I think we need resources first to the sub-county water officers to resolve some small issues.  

The other thing is, at the WASH forums we’ve been saying that, when it comes to our mobility, we don’t have transport.  We need also transport.  I think the CO (Chief WASH Officer of Kitui) has said he’s going to look into that.  Because that is a challenge.  We need that transport to be able to move across the sub-counties.  

Interviewer:  If you were to get these resources at the sub-county level, what would occur next?

Interviewee:  I think our performance would improve.  For small matters it would not take time.  The way we work now, if there’s a problem it has to be reported to the directorate, then in most cases it will take time and customers will be suffering.  But if we had some little resources at the sub-county level, small matters can be solved faster.  The bigger ones can’t be handled at the county level.
