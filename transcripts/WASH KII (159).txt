
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Water and Sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?

The first problem is lack of awareness. The community lacks knowhow about the long-term benefits of the water scheme projects for them and their upcoming generations. One of the causes for this gap could be because water schemes are handed over to the community only at their final stages; there has not been a trend of inviting the community during site selection and designing stages. There has not been a consultation experience at all. Due to this reason, the community has developed a kind of negative perception on water projects, they perceive that these are the projects aimed to benefit the government and not the people of the localities. That is to mean that they don’t have developed sense of ownership from the very beginning.  

The other challenge is that it seems that there is lack of transparency between the community and officials in our office. This has resulted in lack of responsibility sharing and trust from the people. A simple example is that no one is responsible to report to the bureau when water is left untapped and when the community is being affected due to problems related to maintenance.  

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

The first thing that we should do is to create systems to closely work with the community. This may include formation of community representatives and inviting them to participate in meetings and workshops regularly. By doing so, decision makers can easily capture when they actually need and gradually indorse a sense of ownership throughout the community.  

We have also a challenge from our side. This is related to lack of knowhow and insights from consultants who are expected to be winners of the official bidding process. Usually, in government bidding, consultants with the lowest financial proposal would be selected and when it comes to operation stage, they will either start and disappear or never try it at the beginning. We have lots of such experiences in our region with some projects that are left unfinished. Therefore, we are changing our system so that procurement should come through regional water bureau or local team of consultants. Previously, the whole process has to pass through the federal water, irrigation and electricity bureau and that costed a lot in terms of finance and time. 

By working together with the community, we can share responsibilities. For example, by providing technical skills to the local community, we can have minor problems fixed on time without intervention of our regional level experts. This can save time and money. We can also provide capacity building trainings to woreda level expert continuously so that they can themselves oversee problems happening in Kebeles and help care givers at the local levels.   

Interviewer: Who are those care takers?

Care takers are those people who are assigned by water utilities to manage water structures regularly. They receive salary from not from the government budget but usually from the water utilities. Improving the skills of care givers is improving the skills of the community since they are communicating people in their day to day life.  

Interviewer: What are the basic problems regarding sanitation and hygiene?

Lack of awareness is the first one. It is rare to see public and private toilets and latrine houses, majority of the people use open field defecations, particularly those people living in rural areas. There is little or no trend of hand washing after toilet. It is because they are not well introduced about the consequences of OD for their health. It was our responsibility to teach them to avoid OD but we didn’t go so far in this case.  We are planning to form unions who can manage the water schemes with full responsibility with the help of African Development Bank. This work has already been started in Mile woreda, and if this is successful, we have planned to share the experiences of this to other woredas too. 

3.Of the solutions you listed, which one is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Changing the life style of the people should be the first thing to be strategically planned by the government. As you know, people in Afar region is pastoralist, they move from place to place with their camels and goats due to changes in climate. This has exposed them to water born diseases and sanitation problems. Therefore, bringing the people to live together in a particular area for long time can help us achieve our goals. This can be done through establishing education, health, water services and other infrastructures so that they can develop a sedentary life in the long run.
