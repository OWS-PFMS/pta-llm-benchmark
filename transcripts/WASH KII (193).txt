1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your woreda?
[Lack of basic supply of WASH services], this is particularly true in pastoralist areas. When we come to zonal level, WASH problem mainly lies to environmental sanitation and other than access/supply of water services. For example, the water may be polluted by household solid and liquid wastes, human faces or animals. Lack of individual and communal awareness and low level of monitoring system is the main cause for this the sanitation problems. 
Interviewer: What is the cause of low supply of water? One of the reasons for this could be [financial and capacity problems]. We may need to dig deep and shallow boreholes or very long and quality pipe lines which need high tech machines and huge amount of money.  On the other hand, due to the fluctuating nature of the environment and climate particularly in pastoralist areas, we may not get adequate drinking water even if we could have access to finance. [Lack of adequate skilled manpower] this is one of the basic problems to water service sustainability. Adequate and skilled man power is vital to manage the existing water services in a sustainable manner and repair the damaged ones on time. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
4:16 
Regarding the lack of WASH services, our focus will be on environmental sanitation and supervision since this is one of the basic challenges to the supply of pure drinking water. So, we are working hard on this issue across woredas, particularly we are working on community awareness campaigns, promoting that every house hold should have separate toilet rooms and through community leaders and local community groups. 
One of the major agendas related to health transformation is woredas transformation. [ODF 5:10 hard to understand] Number one agenda from woreda transformation programs is creating kebels free from open space toilet users. In order to achieve this, we have set statistical requirements to avoid open space toilet use in each of the kebels and small community structures in every month and every year. 
Regarding the financial and capacity related problems, this needs strong cooperation and and creating a good working environment with different projects and partner organizations. Given the limited government budget, looking for other partner organizations which are working in WASH related projects, through preparing proposals and meetings, will be a vital solution for this. 
Regarding adequate skilled man power, the high turnover due to the difficult climate conditions and unsafe services of the rural areas is the main problem. To solve this widespread problem, we need to train large number of expertise at the national level through universities. At the woreda level, woredas should adopt techniques of retaining skilled power in their woreda. 
Interviewer: How could woredas trap this turn over? They can have a system of providing different incentives. Also, they can create conducive working environment and attractive management systems for their employees
3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs? 
I believe that working on community awareness is the basic thing to WASH service sustainability I have previously mentioned that there is low level of community awareness with regard to community sanitation when I was talking about lack of basic supply of WASH services. This includes training and forming the community to be responsible to take care of own water resources. This is because we cannot achieve sustainable WASH development without working on community awareness and capacity building of the rural population at large even if we get sufficient financial capacity. 

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What should be improved?
There are gaps on coordination, for example water, health and education departments are among the major stalk holders that are connected to WASH service sustainability, however, it seems that the current coordination scheme among these and other stalk holders is based on meetings only that are not exceeding to the scheme of effective coordinated plans and activities. Therefore, the major stalk holders should have a common WASH agenda, including effective meetings, monitoring and evaluation agendas and other common implementation procedures in every month or quarter year, if not possible. What should be improved? In theory, there is an established system/structure for this, the problem is low commitment to meet and discuss issues regularly and monitor the progresses. That should be improved. 9:57 
5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how you believe this coordination would lead to more sustainable water service in the woreda? For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?
Strong coordination can solve a number of problems, as I said earlier.  For example water office may use the maximum budget to the provision of water services, health office will work on sanitation and hygiene to be accompanied with the provision and education office can created awareness especially at schools, as students need pure drinking water for themselves and expected to teach their family and community in turn.   
NGOs operating in our zone are playing key roles in water service provision that needs the need to create a strong coordination with these partners. There is also national one WASH program that needs to be integrated with the plans of the NGOs. There are also NGOs working on structure development and promotion of hygiene (example, Save the Children and IRC WASH). They are also working in crating awareness to the public at large. They send reports of WASH activities quarterly and discuss with the line offices regarding implementation procedures and problems they have confronted 
Can coordination solve skilled man power? Large number of manpower by itself cannot solve water problems unless equipped with adequate knowledge about the ongoing WASH activates in the areas. So, it needs that timely update and building the capacity of the existing employees and new comers through effective and continuous training systems is very important. 
This couldn’t be achieved by the efforts of line offices alone but through unreserved coordination of partners. In fact, they are working on capacity development and community awareness schemes right now, but has to be in a more coordinated and programmed manner. For example, NGOs are involved in the implementation process of SLTS program (14:54 hard to understand but the Amharic meaning is “community led hygiene and sanitation’) programs through building the capacity of extension workers and local WASH cos. Partners are also vital to assist implementation of big policy agendas and national programs related to WASH   
How about its role in solving financial problems? Yes, indeed it can solve this problem because when we work together, we can prioritize problems, including finance. For example, regarding financial problems we could set strategies that could identify who should be responsible to fill the gaps, government alone/NGOs/other private firms? These issues will be discussed and digested in common. So far, I didn’t see the existence of a strong GO-NGO coordination task force that is established particularly to WASH, instead there is informal meetings and discussions between government line offices and NGOs regarding WASH and other emergency intervention areas.
