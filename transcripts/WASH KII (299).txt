Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: I think the biggest challenge that as an organization we are observing here in Kitui is basically on the maintenance of those systems once we leave them to the community. You know, as much as we try to put a very sustainable plan, at times the community will always fail because if you put up let’s say a water point for drinking water, you leave a committee in place, train the committee, do all that is required but once you leave, those committee members will break up in less than two months’ time. I think the maintenance part now becomes a challenge because if you are not collecting any revenue from the water point then it becomes a challenge to maintain the system.
The other challenge I can talk about is availability of spare parts. You see for a borehole, if you were to move around Mwingi it’s very hard for you to see a shop that is selling borehole equipment so if something breaks down the community has to go to Nairobi. By the time they get the parts, the community that depends on the water point will surely have suffered.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: I remember that there is a very good program that someone presented about back at the forum called FundiFix, I think that is the way to go to solve the issue of availability of spare parts. For FundiFix, it comes in handy for both challenges because it solves the issue of spare parts as well as the issue of maintenance. 
I would also recommend that the county government comes into action more strongly because you see now they have left the communities with the NGOs and for me I feel that they are not doing so much in regards to what they are supposed to be doing because you see, mostly I feel they are doing more of supplies than maintaining the systems that have been put up at the moment so I would request the County to come in and assist also in the maintenance of those water points.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I would go for the FundiFix approach because I believe they have a very good model because you know for the government because of the processes and the bureaucracy repairs tend to take a very long time. Adoption of the model is very important because it will make it such that the county will come in when it is necessary, in case of maybe very major breakdowns. I think for us the NGOs our projects are usually based on donor support and you know they last for three to four years and once you leave, if you leave the community with such a model, I think there will be success. Once an NGO comes up with the system through developing infrastructure, and from there they incorporate FundiFix maybe in the 2nd and the 3rd year before you exit and then you bring in the community on board, I think this will be a very good exit strategy when it comes to the sustainability of those water points because this thing about leaving the water points to the community has proven to be a little bit of a challenge.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: In Kitui County we have several interventions. We have done some water points in some places at Kamuwongo and Kyusyani. What led us to those two water points, for the Kamuwongo one, there is a school within that town and the school houses around more than a thousand kids. They were really suffering and then sometimes afterwards they also came up with a secondary school which also had a boarding section so we really felt we needed to put up a water system within that town so that the people there and the school would not have to go for 10 km to get water, because some were going for 10 km and others for 6 km depending on the side of the village they live. So, we felt that we needed such an intervention within that town. For the Kyusyani one we were just upgrading on a water point that we had. Basically, what we did is to install a water ATM, you see that water ATM where you put a machine where you just swipe and get water so that is what we did. The water ATM decision came about as a result of mismanagement of funds at the scheme level. You see, we had to separate the money from the community as a way of sustaining the water point because once the money is collected through an MPESA pay-bill number, if something breaks down they are able to withdraw money from a Safaricom MPESA agent and then they get the spare parts that they require as compared to when customers used to pay them and the money ends up in their pockets and then once you need something no money is available.

For the water ATM, initially what we did when we set up the system because we have an office just next to the water point, we decided to have the paybill as our organization’s paybill where they just pay directly. They have a water committee and in case they need something they just come to the organization, fill some form about why they need the money and how much they need then we just disburse the money. Now because we are closing up that office in the next one month, we are looking into now transferring the paybill to the community or maybe them having their own paybill to operate the water system. Another option we are considering is to have a rule that requires them to have authorization from the water officer within the region to withdraw money from their paybill account. If they need to buy a water pump, the officer has to sign that this money is going to buy a water pump and he has to stamp. This can definitely deal with the misappropriation which is very common.
In Mwingi we are doing sand dams and we are incorporating a food security element and water an element on water service provision too. We decided on this based on a vulnerability assessment that we did for the area. They were getting water from very distant sources and again all the water that comes through during the rainy season was going down to Athi River and then to the Indian Ocean so you see every other time it rains, water comes but then it overflows within the next month no water so the vulnerability part of it led us to Mwingi.
