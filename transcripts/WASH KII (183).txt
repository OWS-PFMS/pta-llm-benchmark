1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: Our zone has pastoral and agrarian woredas and the community in these areas have limited access for clean water. The available few water schemes in these areas are not operational due to absence of regular follow up and maintenance. In general, there is serious shortage of spare parts and other maintenance materials in remote woredas of the zone.
Due to shortage of clean water supply, farmers and pastoralist forced to use river and spring water and this predispose them for various water borne diseases.
Interviewer: Are there any other problems related to water service in Jinka town and other woredas of the zone? 
Respondent: Jinka town is expanding and the size of the population is growing rapidly but the water supply remains the same. This implies, there is water supply and demand imbalance in the town. Thus, there should be water supply expansion plan and activity to mitigate the supply shortage.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: You have mentioned that pastoral and agrarian society of the zone have very limited access for clean water and the existing few rural water schemes do not serve the community sustainably due to lack of spare parts and absence of regular maintenance service. What do you recommend to alleviate these problems? 
Respondent: I would like to categorize the solution as a short run and long run. The short run solution to improve the water service in rural areas is providing regular maintenance service and facilitate access for spare parts in rural areas. In addition, we have to provide maintenance skill training to local communities to enable them manage minor maintenance on their own. 
On the other hand, we should provide water treatment chemicals or materials to rural community to protect them from water borne diseases.
The long run solution to ensure sustainable water service in rural areas is developing deep wells that can generate water during dry seasons. Since most of the rural areas have arid climatic condition, shallow wells could not serve the community during dry season. Thus, the government should allocate sufficient budget for the development of deep wells. As this task requires huge capital budget, NGOs should collaborate with the government to fill the gaps.
Interviewer: You indicated that there is also water supply shortage in Jinka town due to the expansion of the town and the growing population. What do you suggest to alleviate this problem?
Respondent: The town is growing rapidly and new institutions like Universities and Technical and Vocational training colleges are established in the town. This implies there is high water demand in the town. Thus, the town administration should work on water supply expansion by undertaking situational analysis study on the water demand as well as potentials source of water. The administration should put this task in its short run plan and mobilize fund for the implementation of the project.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The water supply shortage is more severe in arid pastoral areas. Thus, we should give priority for the development of water schemes in these areas.
If we find solution to water supply shortage in rural areas, it will have multi-dimensional benefit for the society. For instance, we can protect the community from water borne diseases. The community can keep its personal hygiene. We will have productive and health community. In other words, we can improve agricultural productivity.
