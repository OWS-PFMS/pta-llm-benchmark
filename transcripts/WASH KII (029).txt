Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: The first big challenge that we have on water sustainability is the maintenance part of the infrastructure, if it's a borehole or if it's an earth dam or if it's a rock catchment. So the maintenance part is really a challenge because in Kitui South if we take an example of the boreholes, most of the boreholes are salty so at some point the motor will have an issue, at some point maybe the pipes will burst or rot because of the salt and all that and at some point you realize that there can be periods when people are not fetching water from a certain borehole because it has broken down and it's the same with electricity if the borehole is powered by electricity or even solar because some systems of the solar they also fail and they need maintenance. Like as an organization, Sweden Mutomo projects International has drilled four community boreholes but three are equipped. The one that is not equipped had high chlorides and fluorides and so it did not meet the required standards for domestic use and it was not equipped. So for maintenance, we have seen two of the three boreholes that are functioning have had breakdowns here and there and as an organization we've been able to fix and that's why until today the people are fetching water. So that is one, maintenance. 
Then, we also have the management issues by the management committee that is managing water. Some of the people, if it is the chairman and maybe the treasurer or the secretary they don't get along here and there. Because you see they handle money because water is sold, water is not given out for free. So the moment they have issues sometimes it affects the incomes because some of the people can just use the money they don't want to be followed up and things like that. So the management aspect is an issue especially like for us. We are always having meetings with the committees solving these problems, and at some point you are like, can the borehole still run without this management committee because it takes time to just solve conflicts and you still have other jobs that you are supposed to do. So the management issue is also a fact that affects the sustainability of the projects. 
The other challenge might be the finances, you know finances will affect the maintenance because the maintenance you need funds to fix the problems. So when we check in our boreholes, the amount of money they have collected from the sale of water sometimes it's not enough to take care of the maintenance aspect that is required and so like the donors have to continuously send money to fix up the problems. That's also another one. With the management issue being an issue maybe, I really doubt whether it's now an issue of training because we have done trainings several times to the committees but they still have challenges so you are like is it an issue of lack of knowledge or like lack of knowing exactly what you should do. I think it is more than that and that is like the personal interests that hinder good management of the water because people are self-focused not focusing on how this water can help the community but on how much money am I able to make out of this project. For me now I think that is a problem. Initially I thought it is the lack of knowledge maybe they don't know how they should keep the books, they don't know how they should run but us as an organization we have invested in training the groups but you know you talk to them, you come again after a month they have not improved so now it's not an issue of lack of knowledge it's more than that and I think it's just the personal interests in trying to see how can I get an income out of this project.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: They are complex problems bearing in mind that water is never enough in this community. The sources of water are few than the population. People still have to manage with the few water points that are there especially in areas that don't have rivers and all that. The first big thing that I have been thinking about now for like two months I was like us as an organization we can run the boreholes straight without putting in the management committee in place without having to rely on the management committees like I skip that. Although I know policy wise it is different but it's something that I have been thinking about but you see that is for us as an organization it can work because we will employ someone who will sell water  and we fix any problem that comes up and we take care of the boreholes that we have drilled but in the bigger scale I'm thinking if it's a borehole for example that has been done by the county government you know you cannot skip maybe the management committee still has to run it. So I'm not really sure of how maybe that one will be. On maintenance I think if funds are availed by the County Government, to fix the problems that come up so that if it's a borehole that has broken down then it can be fixed maybe in two weeks time at most and then people can have water. Because that goes a long way into ensuring that the community gets water throughout. 
Interviewer: Which other ideas or solutions?
Respondent: I think the other solution will just be the continuous training of the management groups. You know in as much as they are trained and they have self interest, in as much as like personally I would wish to skip them so that work is easier for me.
Interviewer: You've said  you have trained every month, so what else could be done in the trainings, maybe the methodology? Do you think because you have said you have trained but you still realize there are gaps.
Respondent: That is true. 
Interviewer: So what ideas do you have on that?
Respondent: We have tried changing the management teams, like you take this group away, and then you bring in another group but then you also realize that maybe the new chairman is still has issues. I think maybe moving forward it would be like sunctioning if it is the chairman or the treasurer that have misused the funds then you go ahead and take them to the court. Of which I know it's another tedious process because it takes longer and then that maybe that can send signals to the rest of the people that it is serious, we need these funds well managed, we need this water point well managed and things like that. Because us, we have not taken that route. We just go and maybe is the secretary who has an issue and then we remove the secretary and get another secretary, things like that. Us we've not gone through like going to sue that you guys have eaten this money and we need to go to court so that this and this can happen. 
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: For me I still think the most important is on the maintenance part of the water sources. If it's a dam for example, we have a dam around that is silted but we've not been able to desilt it for example. And like if a borehole is not functioning to avail the funds for maintenance. Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: In the sustainability of the water sources or what?
Interviewer: Generally, in WASH, what are you doing?
Respondent: In WASH we are doing boreholes, we are drilling boreholes to provide water, we have one borehole that is very salty and we desaline the water but then the cost after desaline goes high because the desaline unit has strict maintenance schedules and all that so the cost goes high a bit and then still it does not give out lots of water. You know now you have reduced because you have to take it through the desaline. And the other one is on pipelines, we have done two pipelines from a borehole we have piped to like two kilometres and then the last one is on provision of water tanks to schools. After we renovate the schools we put water tanks so that they can harvest rain water. I think in WASH basically that is what we are doing now.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: In the WASH forums we've been able to meet other partners together, like now we are wiser like if we are doing a water project I have to check maybe the CDF are doing it or the County Government so that we do not duplicate the projects so that is one and then now we've known issues of policies, factors that influence the sustainability of the water also like the things that we are talking about. So it has been an eye opener to us as an organization. Because in as much as we are in water, we do not have a specialist in water or like water engineer for example in the organization. So we depend on the staff rom the county government to do the technicalities that should be done when we are implementing the projects.
Interviewer: On the interventions, what informed the selection of these different interventions that you are doing?
Respondent: It's because of the nature of the Kitui South region, the fact that we don't have many rivers around, maybe permanent rivers around, and the fact that in the villages people still walk far many kilometres to fetch water so we felt that if we are doing boreholes, we can reduce these distances where they go to fetch water and they can use that time to do something else that can generate income for them.
Interviewer: How else has participating in the WASH Forum influenced your organization's interventions?
Respondent: I don't think whether there is any other way.
