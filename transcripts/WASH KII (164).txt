1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: This is a difficult question to answer. As a regional WASH stakeholder, we are trying to identify the major challenges related to water/sanitation services in the region. However, as to my understanding there are there main problems related to sanitation service in the region. First, the regional government authorities do not give attention for sanitation service and its consequence on public health. They do not allocate appropriate budget for sanitation service as well as for advocacy of sanitation. It is non-governmental organizations that show some initiative to work on sanitation issues in the region. The role of the regional government in ensuring sustainable sanitation service is negligible. 
The regional government prepare annual plan for various activities like water service, health service, irrigation development, etc. however, there is no plan to improve the sanitation service of the region. The government do not incorporate sanitation service in its short term and long term plans.
Second, there are various NGOs (like Save the Children, CARE, GIZ, One WASH etc.) that work in WASH sector in the region. However, there is no coordination between these organizations. Each organization try to address WASH related problems of the region on its own. This independent action cannot bring sustainable solution to the problems. They should have coordinated planning and activity to gain maximum benefit from their efforts and resources.
The third problem related to sanitation is that the community lack awareness on sanitation at household and community level. For instance, they do not clean the surrounding and their houses regularly. They do not have a culture of using latrines rather they practice open defecation. This poor community awareness result in various public health problems. The community do not have a clear understanding of the cost and benefit of constructing latrines and cleaning their environment. However, due to poor sanitation they spend a lot of money for health service.
Interviewer: Would you please tell me the problem related to water service in the region?
Respondent: The first and most important problem related to water service in the region is scarcity of water resource due to aridity of the region. For instance, in some areas we cannot get water even by digging 700 meter down. This makes development of water schemes in the region very costly.
The second problem related to water service is that there is mismanagement of the available scarce water resource. The community do not properly use the water schemes and responsible bodies do not regularly monitor the function of the schemes.  
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
Interviewer: You mentioned that sanitation service did not get emphasis by regional government. What do you suggest to alleviate this problem?
Respondent: Like any other activities, sanitation service should get proper attention and should have appropriate plan that is supported by sufficient budget. The federal government in coordination with non-governmental organization should enhance the awareness of regional, woreda and kebele level government bodies on sanitation service and its consequence on public health. 
Interviewer: You also indicated that there is no coordination between NGOs that work in WASH sector of the region. What do you recommend to improve this problem? 
Respondent: I think we have to create strong coordination between these actors by involving them in the Learning Alliance which is initiated by IRC WASH. Making them part of the Learning Alliance is the ultimate solution to create coordinated planning and activities among the organizations.
Interviewer: The other problem you mentioned related to sanitation is poor awareness of the community. What do you recommend to enhance the awareness of the community?
Respondent: The government in coordination with NGOs that work in WASH sector should undertake awareness enhancement campaign through various forms of Medias like TV, radio, etc. We should also strengthen the capacity of woreda and kebele level stakeholders that has direct link with the rural community. That is assigning health extension workers in each kebele and engage them in sanitation awareness enhancement education. 
Interviewer: You have also indicated two problems related to water service. The first one is scarcity of water resource in the region. What do you recommend to mitigate this challenge?
Respondent: Most of the rural areas of the region have poor ground water resource. Thus, to provide water service in these areas we should rely on surface water. For instance, one of the largest river of the country (Awash River) crosses the region. Hence, we can construct dam and treat the water and supply to water scares areas. Instead of developing water schemes in different kebeles by investing large amount of money, it is better to develop a surface water treatment plant and supply to water resource scares areas. This is mainly because in these water resource scares areas we are required to dig more than 700 meter and sometimes we obtain fluoride and hot water after wasting large amount of capital. Therefore, surface water is a best solution to mitigate the problem. 
Interviewer: the other problem you raised related to water service is mismanagement of the existing water schemes. What do you recommend about the solution to this problem?
Respondent: First, we have to train the community on proper use of water schemes and their minor maintenance. Second, the government should assign qualified water technicians in each woreda to undertake regular monitoring of the functionality of the water schemes. Currently, considerable number of water schemes do not provide service due to lack of qualified technicians at woreda level. The technicians that based in regional water bureau cannot reach each kebele to provide the required maintenance service. Thus, the government should capacitate woreda water offices by supplying skilled technicians.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: In my opinion, the first and most important solution to ensure sustainable sanitation/water service in the region is that we have to enhance the awareness of the regional governing bodies on WASH issues. This may enable them to give appropriate emphasis for sanitation service and allocate reasonable budget for the service. Consequently, they will guide and provide instruction to lower level administrators to allocate part of their time and resource for sanitation service in their respective administrative area.
Interviewer: What would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: If we can enhance the awareness of the regional higher officials, we will have appropriate sanitation service annual plan with the required budget. The regional higher officials will also instruct woreda and kebele level government bodies to pay attention on sanitation and water service. There will be regular monitoring and supervision of the existing water schemes. Last but not least, NGOs will be encouraged to work in coordination with the regional government to alleviate WASH related problems of the region.
