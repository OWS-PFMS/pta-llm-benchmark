
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the Afar Region?
I started working in WASH sector three years ago. In our region there are practical challenges related to water shortage and demand. The first thing is that Afar region is very remote and desert area, majority of the people who live in this region are pastoralist and agro pastoralist. It has been almost four years since WASH accounting program started operation; where budget fund raise is increasing from year to year. There are also World Bank missions/representatives who are under commitment and regular visit of the financial coordination program. However, [Delay in budget release] is one of the main challenges affecting the sustainability of water services in the area. 
Budget release is not only the problem from multilateral organizations, but also the region itself. Low level of execution and mandate coupled with lack of commitment from WASH coordination office and the finance bureau are creating additional burden to the delay in the budget release.  The WASH coordination office was mandated to coordinate the different WASH stakeholders, including the WASH stream committee and the technical committee, in principle but failed to do that in practice. Prior attention given to cult the political instability in the region and the extended time taken in the transition of power could be also taken as potential obstacles diverting attention from the WASH sector to politics. Imagine the delay and inconsistency of budgets and water projects when government officials at different levels are moving from the previous offices and positions to others.
Improve the flow of funds through the system and address the problems associated with the unnecessary delay in the transfer of funds from upper to lower tiers of government should be applied. The system should include some form of enforcement mechanisms for the relevant bodies involved in the transfer of the funds. 
Procurement stage; Huge amount of budget is allocated to water construction projects from the ministry of water. To this effect, there are procurement stages that are processed directly by World Bank. By approving funds, World Bank is responsible to process procurement in advance in the interest of time, i.e. to begin construction work on time. Outsourcing construction works to private consultants is more time consuming than expected (long-lasting procurement processes by the district). 
Again if the selected consultant is not available at work and not performing as planned, we also start procurement from the scratch. So, these types of problem are very challenging in almost the 13 woreda’s. Due to the long and complex process of funding and procurement, in very remote areas (such as Dallol, Gunaba), there is no/little initiation to start the water projects. Thus, it needs to develop a strong internal system that improves the flexibility of procurement processes and when funds are approved, local governments should be able to process procurement in advance. 
We should also be assisted by the ministry of water because we can’t avoid the existing water service problems alone given the limited budget and the lack of human resource.  But of course as a developing region, we are disadvantaged compared to the other regions. Look, the procurement process here is directly managed by the minister of water office. But when we see other regions, procurement process is carried out by them; they usually compute and select the required consultant with full mandate to execute the process.  They even ask for additional budget when they know that the money they were given is finished. Taking this as a lesson, the ministry is very reluctant to monitor and evaluate the ongoing water projects in Afar region. 
There is a gap between the regional WASH coordination Office and the zonal WASH offices on policy documents, implementation procedures and in terms of assimilating the targets further down to the districts. The regional WASH office should assist and develop the capacity of those found in the zones and woredas. This will have positive implications on the timeliness of expenditure reporting systems from the bottom to the upper tier of government which will improvement of financial absorption. Also, the Program Operation Manual that guides financial management for the WASH sector should be fully cascaded down to local governments. The central government should make sure that local governments fully understand the operating procedures agreed, and help ensure that the funds provided by the upper tier of government are used efficiently. 
The National WASH Coordination Office should organize various strategic dialogue forums at national and/or regional level aimed at establishing vibrant communication mechanisms between the Ministries and regional bureaus. This will also have positive implications on the timely collection of expenditure reports from regions, in turn facilitating the timely release of the subsequent tranche of funds.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to the problems you have mentioned?

 From the resource point of view, we have full resources; even we have good relations with the NGOs operating in WASH sector. In the process of accepting and internalizing the National WASH program, the developing regions (Somali, afar and Gambella) were relatively late, the bad thing is that no one has come to ask why and to solve multiplier problems.  
 It is now four years, since the lunching of the program and visiting at time would be valueless for these regions. Even the national WASH program by itself is not efficient, they do not work properly. Before the introduction of the current national WASH program, the same project was divided by sub-groups such as World Bank, African Development Bank, etc. At the grass root level, however, the program is not coinciding to the plans and procedures of the national WASH program.

Budget delay; in 2016/2017, problems of budget delay has showed a good improvement, one of the reasons for this could be the discussion with World Bank discusses on the feedback of the previous implementation procedures and we will expect a drastic change for this in the new Ethiopian year. From our side, we usually send the required financial data to the water coordination office and the ministry on time. 
The challenge we had with the WASH coordination office is now being solved. The number of people working in the coordination office has been increased.  What about problems related to procurement process? It would have been better if we are allowed a full mandate of processing, otherwise, the board should solve the existing problem through assisting the experts that are involved in procurement process. This means, to avoid delay in the procurement process they should share detail information instead of going to higher level experts and taking longer time in the meantime. Generally speaking, water office is not using the WASH budget on time rather they use the required money from their own budget and reimburse it after long time, i.e. when the WASH budget is released. This is because release of budget from the WASH program usually takes long process. Woreda’s are responsible for design, planning, implementation, monitoring and the evaluation of WASH programs. They  are  also  responsible  to earmark  budget  and  properly  implement  mitigation  measures  proposed  by  the  national WASH program.

3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? What would happen if the solution occurs?
Awareness: awareness should be given at the kebele level because they are the final beneficiaries from the project.  The one WASH program is integrated with sectors; it includes health, education and water (sanitation and hygiene). Therefore, when we transfer information to the community level, we have to consider this integration. 
As a recommendation, I want to say that awareness a very important tool to the water sustainability problem and should be continuously given to the community and local governments. Next if this is done, woreda experts will have full information and clear information flow to the beneficiaries

4. In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved?
The current status of coordination between WASH stakeholders is not good. We don’t see strong coordination between the higher government officials and those found at the lower levels, including woredas and kebeles in terms of executing WASH activities. There is no joint review discussion and supervision between woreda and higher officials, to check and balance the responsibilities given to each level. More than 70% of budget is spent by the water minister for the water construction works which is affecting the relations with other sectors such as health, education as the projects are done without the knowledge of these organizations. As I said before, there is no/little supervision of the water office and the stream committee from the higher level on how the budget is being spent and how the progress of the projects looks like.  No one is responsible from sectoral offices to oversee and discuss failures and other eye-catching problems happening in the water sector. However, it seems that things are being changed after the appointment of the new regional president recently and we hope that accountability and transparency issues would be given more emphasis from now on. 
The coordination chain and openness in afar region is a bit different from the realities of the other regional states. From my experience, what I learned from other regions is that they have better institutional integration and work spirit. For example, if the water sector is facing budget deficit it can request from health or education departments to meet its annual goals successfully.  Conflict of interest during procurement stage among WASH stakeholders is also another challenging issue that needs to be addressed. 
5. In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?
The regional water office has all the executive mandates regarding finance and project implementation activities.  On the other hand, the office is no capable to execute its mandate given the limited human resources and technical capacity. For these reason, the status of coordination among the WASH stakeholders is not promising at the current level.  To have improved WASH coordination system we need a strong institute capable of leading all the technical and financial. 
At the first place, the water coordination office which is assumed to be the center of the WASH stakeholders is dependent for its budget, manpower, formal signature and stamp and even office to the regional water bureau. Thus, this has to be considered as a challenge and needs to be restructured in a way that it can work independently. This has even created another problem for the NGOs intervention towards the sector. Generally, if national WASH coordination office is isolated from ministry of water having its own budget and decision power, it will have great role in leading the WASH stakeholders as planned        
Efforts have been made by both government and NGOs working in the region to mitigate the problems but due to lack of coordination and adequate planning among concerned parties, solutions have been limited. NGOs involved in the WASH sector development in Afar region are also inadequate in number.
