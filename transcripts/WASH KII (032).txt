Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: I think I’m going to mention a few and my colleague adds more. We have the issue of operation and maintenance, whereby when we have the WASH infrastructures in place after we have the project under community leadership, or even under the water service providers you find that the issue of maintenance is not taken that very key and with time you find that a water infrastructure which has been operating for quite time has stalled and is not operating and this time you will find a breakdown, a clogging of a pipe, or some vandalism and as a result you find that the whole project stalls because of a minor repair maintenance issue and people don't get water, probably they return back to the old polluted water sources. So the issue of operationalization and maintenance of the water infrastructure to me is a challenge especially in our county in Kitui.
Interviewer:  So you've talked about operation and maintenance, what other challenges are there to water service sustainability? 
Respondent 2: I think one key challenge which is really coming up strongly in the area we are operating in Kitui, is about management. You discover these facilities are being managed by the community or Kitui service water providers. But the people in terms of the skills and also management skills here, for the people who are managing these water schemes, i think there is a gap there. And also at the community level, whereby the water is being managed by the water user committee we need also to ensure that they have the adequate skills in pertaining to them so that they are able to run without depending on other people. If it stalled today, we are waiting for somebody to come and repair but rather if they have the good management schemes from the revenue that they are going to obtain, they'll be able to sustain this project. 
Another thing which is also a challenge there is about monitoring. Because you look at as much as we talk about these schemes are managed by the communities but also we have the government bring the monitoring. So if this monitoring can be enhanced then we will ensure that we are bringing accountability to the water user committees to ensure that the resources that they are using, they are using it well but if the monitoring aspect there is some lapse then there is also lapse in terms of the water user committees who are managing these water schemes. And as a result of that we are looking at the project stalling.
Interviewer: What else?
Respondent: Probably to add on the issues of management, you know for a project to run we need finances and when we have water infrastructure there is the billing, there is the payment but the internal controls of the financial management aspect is wanting such that you'll find committees are collecting revenue, then from collection to banking, to expending you find that a time comes, a breakdown happens and the committee says there is no money so you wonder, when you talk of there is no money,  and the system has been operating, people have been paying for water, so where has the revenue gone which should be used to be ploughed back to make sure the systems are operating. So i put it as internal controls on how to manage the finance aspect of the water infrastructure especially the revenues accrued after the sale of safe clean water. 
Interviewer: Any other challenge that we think affects water services sustainability?  We have talked about operation and maintenance, we have talked about inadequate skills, we have talked about accountability, any other?
Respondent 2: I think we can also talk about the design. From the design we should also be looking at the element of sustainability of these water schemes because we are looking at here, we are coming up with some water schemes whereby the running cost is not sustainable if it's handed over to the community or even to the service water providers. So i think here from the design stage you should also be looking at the element of sustainability in a way that we know the power cost here to be specific, the power cost that we have to be more focused on hybrid system where because we are blessed here with a lot of sunny so we can ensure we move away from genset system to solarization system so that we reduce on the running cost as a way of sustaining these projects.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
 Respondent: I think one has been given by my colleague which is resorting or adopting the renewable energy that's solar pumping instead of gensets or electricity because this is not expensive, sun is free, and this will mean the running costs are lower hence sustainability can be achieved through adopting the renewable energy way.
Interviewer: Any other recommendation?
Respondent 2: I think we need also to strengthen monitoring of water projects in Kitui so that we can enhance accountability.
Interviewer: What do you mean by enhancing monitoring?
 Respondent 2: Enhancing monitoring I think should be a norm or we increase in terms of the frequencies that we are doing monitoring to these people who are managing these water schemes so that we remove the  opaqueness of these water committees and we ensure there is transparency and accountability within the water user committees, on the water service providers, so that at the end of the day we know that the water schemes that we have in Kitui they are generating revenues and those revenues are well utilized within that water user scheme.
Interviewer:  Let me understand, you are saying we need to enhance, who?
Respondent 2:  If we talk about we need to enhance is about all the WASH actors.
Interviewer: So the WASH actors need to enhance monitoring?
Respondent 2: Yeah.
Interviewer: You talked about operation and maintenance in terms of community lacking skills.
Respondent: That one comes in when we hand over projects. Sometimes you find we as the WASH actors, the community are less prepared in terms of the skills especially on the technical skills like plumbing works, maintenance works of the structures, so the community or the committees managing these community projects should be capacity build in a good manner such that when we phase out or we hand over a project to a community, they can well maintain and they know the repercussions of not maintaining the system because water system needs servicing like a vehicle or any other man-made structure for it to serve for a longer period.
Respondent 2:  I think also we need to strengthen the policy frameworks for governing service provision for water in Kitui County. So that we look at the huge water schemes, we are able to invest much to ensure that the service providers are the custodians of these water schemes because if there is a good WASH service provider that is best in the county and is managing this, in terms of looking at the operation and maintenance, I think they will be well managed compared to such a huge investment handed back to the community.
Interviewer: Any other idea on the challenges that we had mentioned? 
Respondent: I think I have a thought here which can also bail us from this snug of WASH infrastructure not operating. Suppose there is a policy in our government which will allow for competition, when I speak of competition I speak of private actors are allowed to install their systems and they sell the resource because like now we are in a town now in Kanyangi, there is the water system, suppose a private investor is given that permission through the Kenya laws or through whatever arrangement also to have a borehole and to pipe to people. You know people will choose where to get their services and although the private investor might be a bit expensive in terms of service provision, he might be better and serve people in a better way and me who might decide to have the services which are a bit expensive  but they are reliable, all the time, water is there at night, during the day, it is safe and that one will create a competition such that whatever is community based, private based, government based, they will be now pulling up their socks so that they be in business. That's my one way of thinking to make sure that water is available to everybody.

Respondent: That's a tricky question because I think they are all important, but to me the skill capacity building of the communities on issues of how to run their schemes, and the management aspect is also very key because people when they are responsible and they know technically what to do, and where to get which spare they will not be consulting they will be sorting out their own breakdowns and continuing to offer the services to the people.
Respondent 2: I think to me I look at the element of private partnerships because we look at if we can ensure we link the water management committees to private partnerships so that it can enhance maybe the metering system, it will enhance revenue collection and also they should be focusing on universal coverage that is individual connections. So that at the end of the day, we are not only depending on the kiosk to generate revenue for the management of the water schemes but rather here we have individual connections where we are billing on monthly basis and we are enhancing our revenues. And in terms of the private partnerships, if it can come strongly in a way that there is like an insurance mechanism that these water schemes are being insured, and there is like a small levy they are paying for this private service providers that in case something breaks down, they are going to repair. And I want to site a scenario where we tried that in Mwingi in Kasaini borehole where we are trying to ensure that the water user committee work handy with FundiFix so that we ensure that at the end of the day, that project that we handed over to that community, still serves the community for a longer duration. 
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: As we speak from our side, we are doing pipeline systems with water banked from a reliable river, Athi River, to a treatment works then we have a stretch of pipeline covering the whole of the Yatta and Mutomo so it's an intervention which has taken us sometime but it's a design we've done partnering with the county government of Kitui to make sure that clean and safe water reaches people within Lower Yatta, people within Mutomo, by that pipeline which is treated water which is safe for consumption. So alongside the pipelines we do also boreholes. We drill boreholes and after pump testing and doing the water quality when the borehole passes the test we design and distribute the water near to the communities, to the market centers, the institutions, schools, health centers for the purpose of making safe water be near to the people so we do boreholes and we also considering water harvesting, that's for institutions we do masonry tanks, 50m3 sometimes when  it's an emergency we do plastic water tanks to some institutions to make sure that the rain water is harvested and stored for use by the patients, by the students whatever the case. 
Interviewer: What informed the selection of those interventions? 
Respondent 2: I think one thing that informed those interventions is not rather a decision of the organization, but rather community based decision that we are looking at what are the community priorities at the community participation stage and one of the key priority of the area that we are working in is water access. So what influenced that is that water accessibility in the community is a dire need so that's one of the factors. But a second one is about the county strategic plan which talks about taking water closer to the people so if we dig a borehole and leave the water there, remember we are not taking this water closer to the people so as much as we do the pipeline extension we are looking at the element of reducing distances that communities and more so children and women specifically girls are walking looking for this water. So to reduce that distance we are looking at how best can we ensure that this water is closer to the people. 
Another influence is about the SDG (Sustainable Development Goals) indicators. As an organization we are also focused to ensure that our WASH interventions are also assisting the county government and more so the National Government to ensure we achieve the SDG indicators in terms of water access. Then a part from that i want to site another intervention that we are doing. Those are menstrual hygiene management facilities and toilets or latrines. So we have supported various schools with latrines, with MHM(Menstrual Hygiene Management) because we are looking at it, one factor which influence us to think that way that we should ensure that at the design stage our latrines have also MHM. These girls are being exposed. They don't have changing rooms to change their pads. So as a way of enhancing dignity among school going children in the community that we are serving, we ensure that we have incorporated the element of MHM in the facility that we are doing.
