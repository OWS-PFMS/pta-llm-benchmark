
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: Davis and Shirtliff, KEWI, KIWASH – those are doing the best, in terms of the way they are doing their work, and the way they are presenting.  Presentation to other stakeholders [and] to us.  

Interviewer:  My second question is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining rural water services in Kitui?

Interviewee: The main problem of sustaining community water projects is management of funds, and they are lacking skills.  For example, they take things for granted.  When they misuse funds, there is no law that can be used to… punish them.  So, there are bylaws and there are laws.  

Interviewer:  And, what ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: There are rules to be revised, so if someone has misused community funds, action can be taken upon him or her.

Interviewer:  How about-- you mentioned lack of skills.

Interviewee: More training.  Others don’t know how to keep the records.  If you go to them there are no records.  So, they need more skills and more trainings.  

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: The most important is to give them knowledge – that is training.  

Interviewer:  Can you walk me through the next steps that would happen if that solution were to occur?

Interviewee: If somebody has knowledge, he can do things the better way than before, and he is sure “when I mismanage the funds, action will be taken” because he has the skills.
