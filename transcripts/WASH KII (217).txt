1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of water service sustainability in your town/woreda? 
Respondent: Most water schemes are not functional due primarily to poor feasibility/assessment procedures that did not include local community. 
Even though schemes are built and trainings are given by many NGOs such as IRC to local community members, a number of water schemes are not providing services for the time period they are expected to serve, because of lack of community’s sense of ownership.  
There is huge gap between the demand and available service: Increasing demand of water by the community and the budget allocated for the water sector is incomparable. These days government is not allocating adequate capital budget, even the available small budget would be re-allocated to other emergency issues. 
More than 54,000 inhabitants reside in Jinka but there are only five water schemes of which three of them served more than 20 years. Available pipes are too old which made them prone to frequent break down, and for the community to lose its trust on government.  
There have been gaps regarding properly managing the currently existing schemes, particularly; issues that should be addressed in coordination with different stakeholders are not addressed. I would like to thank IRC WASH for their efforts. The office has been inviting different stakeholders to participate in the learning alliance meetings, and to identify unresolved issues together. However, the coordination and political commitment among local leadership, starting from the zone, has been found poor rather. 
Water sector is not considered as a priority sector by the government, especially during the dry seasons the problem of lack of water is worse. As a result of this, the current coverage of clean water in Jinka town is around 27%.
There are also problems related to updating our billing system, the current system to collect service payment from the community is too old, manual and tiresome.
Water sector should have been led different from other sectors, as it is being run by a board of stakeholders. So town administration and zone should understand what the sector requires.   

2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: Water schemes should be preceded by proper feasibility/assessment procedures that properly involve and represent local community. This will create community’s sense of ownership. Government should allocate adequate budget to bridge the huge gap between the increasing demand for the service and limited finance. The gaps regarding proper management of the existing scheme should be addressed in coordination with different stakeholders. 
Coordination and political commitment among local leadership, starting from the zone should be improved. As I said it before, water sector should be considered as a priority sector, especially during the dry seasons the problem of lack of water is worse. 
ICT led water information and billing system is also vital to properly store, manage and plan information needed for the equitable distribution of the service. 
As a long-term solution, a treatment plant is under international bidding process to be constructed within roughly five years. This is a promising step towards solving water supply problem in the town. If we try to run this plant the old way, the way we are currently handling the sector, we will face the same problem. We are expected to pay cost-sharing of the project, which is around ETB 30 million. Out of this more than ETB 10 million has been paid.  
In short-term, maintenance of the existing water schemes and replacing GI pipe lines are not recommended, instead they should be replaced by DCI or HDPE.   
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: You have proposed many solutions, which is the most important one?
Respondent: Working in collaboration with the stakeholders: The reason why we lack working in close coordination seems to necessitate a special research. Even if new schemes are underway, it would be rather hard for us to sustainably run the schemes unless we work in collaboration. 
Interviewer: What would happen if there is good coordination? 
If we work in coordination, we will be able to identify our common problems together, share amongst us and find common solutions together. Thus, stakeholders will share the difficulties and way out.
