1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent: our zone is typically divided in to pastoralist and famers’ community and the challenges we are facing also differ accordingly. The problem of water is more adverse in the pastoralist community as it is mostly characterized by a desert climate. In this community, the main challenge is unable to get access to the water table itself due to arid climate. In some of the kebeles, there are also projects started but left unfinished because of the financial constraint. 
So, we can take the problem of budget as a common challenge in both the pastoralist and farming community but it is more severe in the pastoralist community. 
In the farmer’s community, access to the water table is relatively better compared with that of the pastoralist community though low water coverage is still a common problem in both communities.  Particularly in N and S Ari woreds, Oba Ari areas, the challenge is not water shortage but unable to develop streams due to lack of adequate finance, coupled with absence of rural roads and vehicles. People are spontaneously affected by water borne diseases in these areas. 
Interviewer: Okay, before you go to recommendations, let me ask you if you can add more challenges to the water service sustainability?
Respondent: Yes you are right; there are a number of water schemes that are not functional currently due to limited number of skilled man power to maintain and make these schemes functional. Sometimes, we couldn’t fix problems at the woreda when the damages are difficult and out of the capacity of the local experts. In this case, we usually appeal to the regional level experts for support and this may take a very long time to be effective. 


2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Okay now, we can move to your recommendations and let’s start from the financial constraints. What should be done, by whom?
Respondent: Okay, I think the first action should be involving the community itself to fill in the government budget gaps. The second and most important thing is that both the regional and zonal governments should be fair in allocating budget to the different sectors. As you know, South Omo is the most challenged zone with in the region with regard to potable water accessibility but it is also one of the marginalized zones as far as the capital budget is concerned. This needs to be revisited by the governments. If you visit some of the pastoralist areas, you will automatically get bored and saddened as they are still drinking water from the Omo River.  As a representative of the people, we always flag this issue in the regional council meetings but we couldn’t get this solved yet. 
Interviewer: you have also mentioned that people are being exposed to different water borne diseases due to lack of adequate and quality water schemes, what should be done in line with this problem?
Respondent: I recommend that mobilizing the community to contribute both in terms of labor and finance is would be a good way. As far as the government is working with esteemed commitment to lead, motivate and engage the local community, we can solve problems related to shortage of water schemes and other incidental hazards, including diseases. 
It is also the mandate of the regional government to construct adequate road infrastructures in this zone. For example, if you can go and observe some communities in North Ari, water shortage is very severe because of lack of rural roads. This is the basic problem to be prioritized by the government before thinking about development of water schemes in these areas since you can do nothing without access to roads. 
Interviewer: what about solutions to the shortage of technicians?
Respondent: This is simple, the government should hire more experts, provide training to the existing experts. We have a very limited number of experts even at the zonal level; I think they are not more than 3 or 4. These have to respond questions to the 11 woredas which is difficult and not possible to reach out to all those woredas on time. 


3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I think the first most important thing is to get rid of financial constraints.  When we have adequate financing for the sector, we can have adequate boreholes, spare parts; we can hire more experts and increase water services coverage gradually. Side by side, developing road infrastructures is also another basic requirement.
