1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your town?
The first problem is the [difference in agro ecology]. In the zone, there are humid or temperate woredas having a better water access and coverage though it may not be pure. In arid and desert woredas the nature of the weather makes them prone to climatic shocks unless and otherwise these woredas exist around the basins of rivers. In this case, I think the main cause is nature of the areas itself. In addition, water is salty in low land areas, including woredas with rich underground water but with desert climate/low lands, good example is Desanech woreda”. Other areas like North and South Ari and some areas of Mali and Bena Tsemay have better water resources although there is no adequate clean drinking water and its infrastructure.
The second challenge is [lack of capacity and finance]. There is lack of capacity and finance to access springs or wells from long distances. For example, in Mali woreda, Erob kebelle, the Catholics development dug a well that has 150 meters depth which is recorded as the deepest water wall from the zone and this required a very huge capital, beyond the capacity of the zone and woreda finance. The other challenge is [lack of skilled manpower] in Woredas to maintain shallow wells and other smaller maintenance schemes. We need to bring a skilled person from zone or region (when we need machines) because we don’t have these people at Woreda level.

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Regarding the natural/agro ecological challenges, we should build the financial capacity to have better and nature resistant capacity at zone, regional and woreda level. We should also develop and invest in digging wells, at the woreda and zonal levels. I think, the zonal water coverage is around 39% which is less than 50% and more investment is needed. If this is difficult, roof catchment should be practiced by putting more water containers around schools, health centers and health posts as we practiced and learned from the previous drought. Other partners should be asked to invest more in these projects. We are getting water containers from partners such as IRC WASH and other partners. More and more water containers are being installed. As much as possible digging wells is also important in pastoralist areas. 


 In order to cope up with the skilled man power it needs woredas commitment, we don’t have people in the woredas that can at least maintain minor problems related to water infrastructure.  What we usually encounter is the inability to get even small maintenances for simple and technical problems. There is also lack of accessories so there is a need of investment on skilled people (who will be standby to fix technical difficulties). We need skilled man power for other departments too.  We also lack skilled people in these areas not only for fixing problems related to water infrastructure but also in offices as well. This requires investing on the development of skilled man power from now on.

With regard to finance and capacity building, when the government sectors draft plans, they should always account the need to incorporate other partners. Our interest is to discuss budget and capacity gaps with the partners so that the partners can fill out on what the government cannot achieve. In this way we may solve the problem of water sustainability in our area. In woredas where there is adequate water access but lack clean drinking water, such us South and North Ari, we can work on developing springs that need reasonable capital. 
3.Of the solution you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
I give priority to working on increasing water coverage through developing of our financial capacity at all levels. What would happen? As I told you before, currently the coverage is below 50% which is at low level and if we have to choose between water quantity and quality, we should first choose quantity then work on the quality. Once the communities have accessed water, purity will come gradually and even they will use it treating by chemicals.  Second priority should be given to working on skilled manpower which should be followed after we increase the coverage of water.

Parallel to this, we invite partners in water supply, animal feed supply areas and others.
