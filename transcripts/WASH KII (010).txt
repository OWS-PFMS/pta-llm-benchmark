Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: First of all I would want to say my name is Philip Nzula, the assistant director of water Kitui County and I have been dealing with the sustainability of rural water schemes, that is water supplies in general and I than you for inviting me to this interview. There are several challenges that affect water sustainability in Kitui County and one that we have witnessed is about political influence. There is a lot of push and pull in terms of water service delivery and what I mean by political influence you find that sometimes communities, they are the ones that are supposed to deal with the sustainability of course through the capacity building that we have done all along. But you find that they have not absorbed this and whenever there is a breakdown the political clash now comes in and try to defend the communities simply because they are not getting water. They are not keen on their part what they are supposed to do because ideally we are supposed to facilitate and then they are supposed to through the monies they are collecting then they are supposed to inject that money back to the water supply system which is not been done and that is a serious problem that we are facing. The other one is the communities themselves. 
There is that dependency whereby they have not come to realize, despite the capacities that we have done to them, that this is our water system and it is us who has the responsibility of maintaining, taking care of that water system because when it breaks down is the same communities not the government, not the people in the offices that are facing the challenges of water. And that also impacts negatively into part of the social dynamics. Ideally you find that like kids they are also being used to fetch water instead of attending schools. So there is that ripple effect based on that water supply being not maintained. Overtime we have seen communities even coming for subsidies in terms of fuel yet they are collecting monies. So this brings another issue of accountability which is a problem to the rural water schemes and the communities in general. The other challenge that we are facing is as a county there are some items that we are supposed to support communities repair especially when it comes to capital items which are very expensive and which cannot be catered through the monies they are collecting. So you find at times there are challenges in procurement processes, they are taking too long and you find that the water supplies remain unattended for quite a long time yet it is our part as a county that we are supposed to do that.
Interviewer: Why does the procurement process take long?
Respondent: It is the way there are so many stages and again even as you do the procurement, you must be within the law. So you cannot float a quotation today and then tomorrow you expect something to have come. It is a procedure, certain bureaucracy in procurement and that we cannot subvert that because again it brings an audit query. There are challenges in terms of the way we organize the entire WASH systems. For instance few elements, you find that people are coming, they are doing WASH programs yet they have not been institutionalized very well. So we find challenges of some facilities that were done, they were not handed over to the County Government, they are out there because there are several partners, even Tanathi, which is now the water works, they still do boreholes which they are not handed over to us and you find that that coordination and we have raised it for quite sometime with them even in WASH forums but somehow things have not streamlined. This is not just to pinpoint a certain organization but again there are also other partners out there for instance, even technology wise you find that some technologies that are presented to the communities they have not been internalized within them. Like now we are changing some boreholes to solar which is more efficient but again in terms of handling the equipment that capacity may not be there. Part of big challenge is that when it comes to capacity building which again streamlines some of the technological gaps. You find that some mainstream government we Ideally don't allocate funds for capacity building. Although it is emended within the entire whatever the activities that are being done. 
Capacity building is very key to be done in an official way with a budget. That way you put some things into consideration. Like now when you train informally, there are somethings that you might not follow to details. Not everybody knows how to handle a genset, how to handle maybe a controller when it has broken down so you find that that capacity continuous capacity building. Again we have seen over time a lot of changes in terms of committees and this is also affecting because their constitution there is a time lapse. When that time comes you need to have new fresh elections and it can go to an extend that none of those that were there before because ideally there is no clause that says that this quarter must remain. Either a certain percentage of the members that were there must remain within the committees for continuity so everybody comes afresh with different ideas, so we begin another set of training and again within that period you find that there is also time lapse and people may not get everything, you may not reach everyone at the same time. 
So that also affects. Once you do an election you don't do it and train at the same time, it takes time because you can have several committees that their time has elapsed. We are talking of over 600 water committees so you find that in subcounty to reach every committee in good time once they have done elections it becomes a challenge. Because you have one or two officers in county so spreading may become a challenge also that you don't reach everyone in good time and within that time you find that maybe a facility, maybe may tamper with the machines. We have seen that whereby like a scheme in Kamwiu it is a scheme that somebody just took a basin, there was a leakage within the machine then he took a basin and began to tap this water that is coming out. So when the machine was tattered then time it was damaged, a machine worth one million. Now it is damaged, that machine is damaged because there is nothing to cool it, the radiator has also been torn within that process, so you find that there is a lot of damage, just because of a simple mistake. Not that this someone does not know but that happens quite a lot. 
There is also another aspect about the fuel for those that are using gensets, there is a challenge in the type of fuel that is being used. Sometimes you find that fuels are being adulterated so they interfere with the injector pumps. So there are myriad of challenges within that, some of them can be addressed, others there is challenges because other people are involved in them. Mostly it's though I have said it is handling finances and that's a big challenge because everybody is fighting for the finances, there are people I have seen with the water supplies with their milking cows. There is a lot of resistance when you want to eject that committee out. There has been a lot of problems in Kanyangi even as we speak now. There is problem in Kawongo and it's all about financial management they way it's being handled. Maybe the other one is budgeting cycle, our budgeting cycle, sometimes it brings challenges because when we expect the ultimate pumping and when we expect more breakdowns, is when also we experience a close of the financial system so at times you find that you may want to. a capital equipment has broken down and if the committee doesn't have the capacity to repair, then that means it's going to stay without water until a next financial year starts. That is a big challenge.

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: First of all I know there is a bill that we started but we have not been able to conclude it which would address part of the issues because within that bill there was a clause on water supply services and issues of sustainability. For instance, when the bill was keen on the influence , political influence because if the bill is so clear and has stipulated this is what is to be done then you see we are cubing the problem of political influence because there is a role of the community that it's supposed to play, if the community mismanages the money or the finances, then the penalties that are entrenched in the policy are supposed to take effect. Ideally as we talk now, there is a case in court about a water supply and it all emanated from the elections. So you see with a bill that won't happen because there is a procedure on how you are supposed to do things. Through that we'll be able to deal with issues of influence, there are issues that also come that affect sustainability like transfer of assets and that's a big problem. You'll pardon me some of them I'll somehow put them as problems but also as solutions. So if you want to transfer a machine from this community A to community B, we have seen a challenge yet these assets are held in trust by the communities. They are ideally County Government assets. 
Interviewer: What kind of assets are these?
Respondent: Like now we need to transfer a genset maybe we have done solar in this community A, and there is community B that can use this genset, when theirs has broken down and you are able to move this genset to here so that this community continues getting water while these are also getting water, but now there is solar, or there are two gensets within that community. One is seated idle but you cannot transfer it. So community B remains without water while we can transfer this genset here and both of them get services. That becomes a challenge which we wanted to address through the policy. The other thing is about issues of trust deed which we also did and which has also not seen the light of the day because ideally when we have a trust deed, it will help us within our budgeting cycles because there will be so many partners, government, putting resources together in one basket and this trust deed will help us solve problems of people going without service. For instance if there is a breakdown, we can get money from the trust deed. 
This is not going to depend on the budget cycle because it is like a special purpose account and then it will be accessible anytime there is an issue to be addressed on a certain water supply. The other thing is, it is good to encourage many players as much as possible not only to depend on O&M team, for the County which sometimes is overstretched and getting to point A, B every time becomes a challenge like what we have with FundiFix, Miambani that is something that we need to encourage so that people can be served but of course regulated so that people do not do their own things because when we encourage so many people that are not again regulated, you find that some go take the new things, they fix the old things and again you start another set of problems. Like we have one of us who retired and is doing a great job in the community. In fact they are calling him, they pay him and you see it's much faster for him to move not because we cannot move but where we cannot reach there are other people that can do the same job. 
That we are going to address those issues. The other thing is on accountability which I 've talked about. This one is just about continuous capacity building. Although we are saying we have trained them, remember we have new breed that is coming up, these committee members. We are also dealing with the new projects that are also coming up. We have project implementation committees, of course they are not trained at that level because ideally for PICs is just look at the projects they have done to the standard and to the quality. But after that we have to transcede them now to management committees which after that they need training and the kind of training that I'm talking about is not residential training because in the past there were residential trainings where just pick a chairman, the treasurer, the secretary, three people and you go train them in a residential place. 
Of course they spend a lot but the impact is minimal. It's community based training where they are. Reach them where they are, whether formal or informal because that way you are going to train more people so you are sure you have more resource persons and ideally even if they exit, there will be people still to support those who have come with ideas on how it should be run, and all that. So basically I think that's all. The other thing is about partners, I would say although I talked of challenge of partners doing their own things out there without maybe proper structure, they are doing a great job and it is upon us if we want to sustain these water supplies whenever we hear something is happening, we should not be resistant, we should be flexible and then we should reach them and see what they are doing. Whichever way they came in, they are doing a great job and they are supporting what we are supposed to do. So that flexibility can also address some of the challenges not to say this is my county, you cannot come in to my sub-county, do things I am not aware, try to see how you can reach out to them, be cohesive and see how you can move together. I think I would say basically that would be the way to go.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: One of the things that is key to me, is a policy and a bill which has been there for long, we started it, we've not seen through it, it has not been enacted and once that happens, I can assure you there will be proper coordination because there will be a framework that is set, on how things should be done, their entry points, what happens if a water supply breaks down, what happens if there are issues of unaccountability, what happens if there is undue pressure from the political class, even as an implementer, I'll also be covered. I don't want to lose my job, I don't want to be at locker heads with the political class because I'll be working within the set framework and to me that is very important and that would address most of the challenges that we are facing.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Several interventions that are happening, one, we are developing projects to address those areas that are not served because there are areas that are also experiencing shortage of water. And if you look at the way we are doing it, now we are doing like when we need to do mostly water development. Currently we are looking at projects that are big and that can reach so many people in places that are not served like now in Kitui Rural we have Kavisuni. Kavisuni didn't have a reliable water source but there is a sump-well that we have done that is serving some parts of Kavisuni. I am talking about the massive projects that can reach many people and I am just giving an example of that water supply which is solar powered initially that's a small market, which didn't have water and it is key to revenue collection to the County, it is a market for livestock for other products. But now they are able to access water so we are doing projects in tune of millions, 20's and above, 17-20 so that we reach more people and instead of doing pockets here and there, we look at one water supply and make it a supply that is going to take water to more places. Ideally that way more people will be served. 
That is one of them, secondly what we are also doing because as a department we have two sections, that is on water resource development, it is dealing with the earth dams, rock catchments, springs, name whatever water supply that can reach people and those sump-wells which are done in seasonal rivers that are overtime were not being tapped but we have seen it as a resource and which we are now developing to get water to people. So there is also the water supplies part whereby overtime we started what is called a framework contract so that we bridge this budgeting cycle. Framework contract is you get some people who are able to supply certain items, whether it is off season or in season, in season meaning that is we have not closed the financial year. Off season is when there is a break to the next financial year. That can bridge that gap through that framework contract. We have started it but again there were a few changes in terms of management and whoever was spearheading that now became the person in charge of procurement so we could not proceed with it but it is something that we are keen on. The other thing is we initiated purchase of items to put them in store so that whenever there is breakdown we are able to pick items from our stores and go address that issue. So basically those are the two types of interventions that we are doing. The other one is about the partners, that joint planning is also very important because initially one of the challenges why people are doing their own set of things, we did not have joint planning but now we are doing that joint planning so that we see we don't duplicate items and we leverage on the resources which is also very important.
Interviewer: Any other intervention?
Respondent:  I don't think there is any other.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent: WASH Forum has brought partners together and that's why I am talking about joint planning by virtue of us coming together I think now doing MoUs is not a big deal because of that coming together. There is what I would call like now we map Kitui and we know who is doing what where through that WASH Forum. So that if A is doing something, we either complement what they are doing or we leave that to that organization then we concentrate on another underserved area. Through that we also come together we learn we cross learn, we know what are the emerging technologies, even in terms of reporting, because ideally the way we report is different from maybe partners the way they do their reporting but through that WASH Forum, we come up with a common reporting structure so that everybody can feed into that, through that WASH Forum. In terms of the way we do things, for instance through this cross learning there are certain things maybe we have been doing them wrong. For instance Unicef took us for a training on smart reporting. Although we may not say it is an intervention but once an intervention is done and it is not reported, it is equal to as we are not doing anything. Through that WASH Forum, is where we cam up with an idea of the trust deed. So the way we do our own things and we see how to address certain challenges because we also share challenges within the WASH forum.
Interviewer: How else?
Respondent: There is integrated approach to our implementation because through the WASH forum we have seen that initially we were not keen on the environment, but it came out that environment is very important because ideally it's supposed to support the infrastructure that we are doing, it's supposed to support life so even as we do these interventions, then we should also be keen to the environment, how we treat the environment and I think that was very key.
