
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: Ok, what is coming out from this network is the issue of coordination, because we have so many actors within the County, so the issues of information sharing between the partners and sharing of best practices which each partner is working on, because now it avoids duplication of interventions and also what works well in the County in terms of water access to the communities. So, coordination and sharing of the best practices is working for the County.

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: From my own perspective, the sustainability of the water facilities is still a challenge for us even at ADSE and also at the County level. So the issue is on approach, because now, we have the government approach on contractual work approach whereby they give a contractor and after the contractor leaves the site, there is no handover to the community. There is no committee which has been set to run the project so, the approach we are embracing like the contractual approach is a bit challenging, because, even where I come from, I come from Kitui. We had even another site whereby we had no committee, the contractor has worked in the site and has left. So you see now there is no one who managing, because now they think the money that has been given to the contractor and has left. So that mentality of/syndrome of money issues is still a challenge. But for us, the aspect of… you see, once you…what is not working is known, the issue of the community not owning the project. Because when we work and we leave, so we need to stay longer in that community to coach them for a period of like…five years is still adequate for this work...for the roll-out of these interventions. Because now, there are some that won’t manage because the community-run projects is a bit challenging because of the ownership and also the maintenance of those water structures. So, we can think of simple ones that the community can manage. Because when you look at the boreholes and facilities, those ones are still collapsing because of the maintenance, because it is expensive to maintain a pump which is costing over 400,000, of which the community they do not have the money and yet you handover to the community and there is no committee collecting the money, although KITWASCO are still coming with taking up those projects, but now if it is left to the community without a strong committee to collect the funds to run the project, then it won’t take up, but once we have strong committees and mechanisms are put in place on water collection – the tariff issues, once have been sorted out and the community is able to collect funding to repair…to do minor repairs, even the government coming up to support with subsidies, it will...these issues won’t work unless those issues are taken into consideration.

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: From my perspective, because I have worked with communities for the last six years, once we work with the…work on the community approach, once we have a strong committee at onset of the planning of these water facilities, and then even before implementing the project we have to train these communities on…for them to own the project. But not the issue of letting the contractor do the work and then he leaves. Once we have a strong committee on site and then which has been capacity built to reduce the issues of the money…the management of the committee…you know those issues of once the money has been…they have begun collecting the funds, now the issues come at that stage. We need to form a strong committee on site and then we train that committee on issues of maintenance, the issues of money…management of funds, so after you handover to the community they are able to run the project. And even employing some simple…we need to undertake some of the activities which do not require money to manage, like the solar powered systems, they are still working because now there is no money required to manage that project because it is self-propelling once you leave it. But for some others like the…also, we need to…apart from working on underground water sources, we need to major on surface water harvesting. We have a lot of rain in this region which comes and goes within a span of 2 to 3 months. So we need to major more on surface water harvesting, because like now we have rivers along/within Kitui. And like a case I shared with you on harvesting water from a shallow well which has been dug up along the river section, you know, that water is there all through and once you do like issues of like the farm ponds and the sand dams which are self-sustaining, which do not require money to manage/to run, now that project will be…will peak. But now where money comes, to me, in terms of maintenance now it becomes a challenge. 

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee:  I would major more on surface water harvesting, because of experience in underground water.

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: I think the task now lies at policy level. We need to…it is more like enforcing once the main department which coordinates the water activities within the County like the Ministry of Water, the WRA (Water Resources Authority) and the NEMA (National Environment Management Authority) because of the EIAs (Environmental Impact Assessments) because they require a lot of…they require to do some…because surface water harvesting is affected by the rates of siltation, so once these bodies work to formulate a policy and also to plot some guidelines, so that whenever we are doing this… an activity, we have to follow these set guidelines. Now to me, to start at policy level, for them to influence and also to have authority on what scheme that needs to be developed in a given county. Apart from now…still, once we are working on others, but now surface, there needs some guidelines on how to roll it out and we need to have support from these other sectors because once water department does their task, and the farmers start planting their crops in the upstream parts, now the water component will be silted up and it won’t run. So we need collective effort from these actors and others have to come on board to support these initiatives.
