
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation / Water service sustainability in your woreda?  Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation / water service sustainability?

 Most of the sanitation problems in our institution are related to the two springs we are using as source of water for the public. We have about five boreholes that we use as source of water, too. One of the problems is that the springs do not discharge equal amount of water in the fall season and dry season. Less water is discharged in the dry season. The same thing is true for the boreholes, too. There is also sanitation problem on the water pipeline. The problem with hygiene is due to failure to clean the water tanks.  Not only the water tankers but also the water meter get dirty, and sometimes algae is observed in the water that comes mainly from the spring. This is due to lack of hygienic awareness in how to use the products properly. The people don't even have sufficient awareness about how to use the plastic pots they use for collecting water. When Water is not available for three to four days, the plastics become dirty and form algae. In this case, the people don't even wash them properly before using them. That is another sanitation problem.

 Interviewer:  Any other problem related to sanitation?

There is sanitation problem with regard to the water pipeline system we have in general. Weak safety plan system is the major one; this is lack of monitoring water flow all the way from the source to the end users.  The pipelines don’t get repaired timely and they are very old. So, even if we provide pure water, when it reaches at household level is not pure. So, we have such a weak safety plan in general. 

Interviewer: is the coverage adequate and fair? If not, why? 

Though we have ample underground water sources in Jinka, we are not fully utilizing it. The population size of Jinka town was 27000 in the last population census, in 2007 and now it is projected to be over 40,000. So, because of the population increment, the water service wouldn’t be matching the demand. Another challenge is management problem. There is frequent water interruption due to electric power disruption. Since the pipelines are also old, they get damaged frequently. As a result, lots of complaints are coming out from the beneficiaries. 

We have also a challenge related to system. We follow manual system. I know that IRC is doing on the water systems engineering. Our water billing system is manual and that is why beneficiaries don't settle their bills on time. There are lots good governance complaints from the people side. We don’t have also a laboratory that would be used to check water and its minerals continuously. We are using a laboratory borrowing from health office for the time being. 



2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

For the sanitation problem, we are doing a regular inspection, cleaning the tankers and applying chlorine from our side. We also clean the caps of the springs that form algae. When we   go out to the community, we also teach them about the possible ways of maintaining their sanitation. We believe that the community needs to have awareness creation schemes regularly.
 
Interviewer: What do you think is the solution to the safety plan?

We need to implement a proper safety plan. We need to provide training and awareness programs using our technical experts to the community and kebele residents regularly. There is only a theoretical strategy that is kept in office. We need to plan and implement it practically. The duty of awareness creation should be assigned to a different staff that can operate regularly. Recently, a woman has been assigned here to execute this work. And this has to be strengthened. 

There is no clear way out to solve the problem associated with the laboratory, but I think it is possible to make a difference by working with the different stakeholders. Regarding billing system, it is very important to focus on improving its program even if it is manual. I also believe that IRC will help on this if we plan and approach them. 

Interviewer: what is your plan a head to meet the demands of increasing population? 

We have planned to have four water projects to meet the demand. One of them is already going on with the help of an NGO known as World Vision. The biggest hope we have is on the newly planned water scheme supply that is going to be constructed on Ganery river. The study is being conducted by World Bank’s fund, and the first draft is submitted to the region and us. Based on the draft report, we have also provided feedback. The next work that is expected from the consult is bidding. Once this project is completed, I hope that the problem of water supply in Jinka will be solved. It is estimated that the project will take a budget of some 200, 000,000 birr. The time period is not clearly stated, but it may take three to four years to be completed. 

 There are plans to use standby generators to solve electrical interruption problems. Today, we were even discussing about maintaining generators that have become non-functional with some technicians who came from regional office. Using standby generators, we have planned to solve the problem coming due to electric power interruption. 

As I said, the water demand is higher than the water supply we are providing. The borehole project that was begun last year would create a huge impact on improving the water supply.  Again, the new water project that is being built on the river sooner will have contribution to minimize water supply shortage. 


3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs? 

Safety plan is the first thing to do if we prioritize solutions to our problems. The second thing is doing awareness creation work continuously. Third, establishing an automatic billing system will alleviate lots of problems. It helps the office to collect income on time, and it will help the people to save extra money that they are paying due to wrong manual water meter readings. This will also bring good governance.
