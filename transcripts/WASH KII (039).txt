Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability?
Respondent: One challenge is lack of community ownership. There is still that sense that the facilities are owned by the implementing organizations such that when there is a breakdown, there isn’t a lot of effort internally to do repairs and maintenance; they want to still call the organization or the water office to report breakdowns. Secondly, lack of technical skills in the community to do the operation and maintenance is a challenge. This is coupled with lack of spare parts being readily available in the local market. Like now, for very basic repairs, spare parts have to be bought from either Kitui or Nairobi. Spare parts are not available in Mutomo or in Ikutha towns. The other challenge is low of purchasing power due to lack of funds.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these challenges?
Respondent: On community ownership, on the issue of land there should be proper formal agreements. Sometimes, someone emotionally gives a piece of land but after the facility is set up, they say that they want to get the water for free because it is on their piece of land. Since there was no formal agreement then there are issues. 
I also think there should be frequent training, because like the artisans we trained we can’t say all of them are still around. Some of them have gone to look for jobs elsewhere so we need to keep updating in terms of the number of people trained as well as updating the knowledge and skills. 
The County government should think of having a policy on maintenance funds and creating a fund for that purpose. Actually, for quite some time, we have been here doing repair and maintenance of boreholes, we have not repeated or repaired one and repaired it again after months, but I know there have been breakdowns even after we have done the maintenance. As much as we are saying that the users contribute some money, they might not have sufficient funds to cover the full cost. In previous years we have done rehabilitation of boreholes by installing solar powered pumps to boreholes that have sufficient yield, so what I am saying in this regard is that even if the users contribute, in case of a breakdown of an expensive component, they might not be able to repair or replace it, for example in case of a motor which costs over 100,000 shillings. So, the government and the organizations should come up with a fund.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Training on technical skills because that would reduce the frequency of breakdowns. I would imagine that if there was to be training on technical skills, they would touch on issues of ownership in the training content. During the trainings, there could be mention or training on the formal land agreements and of course there would be the specific technical skills transfer regarding relevant facilities, whether the hand pumps or the solar power systems and I want to believe during those trainings there could also be training on financial management and therefore even if there was the fund I mentioned earlier, it would be well managed because of the training.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: My organization works in Kitui South sub-county of Kitui County. We are undertaking WASH projects with a few activities including repair of non-functional shallow boreholes that are handpump operated, we are also constructing sand dams-we have a target of 7 this year, January to December 2019. For the shallow boreholes, we have a target of 5 and also on increasing access to water we are supporting 10 primary schools with installation of 5000-liter tanks. We are also doing WASH in schools, supporting 10 primary schools in Kanziku Ward, with construction of 2 stand latrines, installation of handwashing stations, 6 in each of the schools, and establishment of hygiene clubs in the schools with membership of 30 pupils, 15 boys and 15 girls. We have two teachers in each of the schools to oversee the hygiene clubs, a male and a female and they have undergone a training on child hygiene and sanitation. Basically, that is what we doing.
The main reason for the interventions we are implementing is to increase access of clean water to the community. For example, the repair of boreholes was what I would call low hanging fruit, because the boreholes were already there but they were not functional so it made sense, instead of starting to drill others, we needed to ensure that the existing ones were functioning and if there was need for new water sources, that would come later. Regarding rehabilitation by installation of solar pumps, we realized that there were long queues and a lot of waiting time at those facilities some of which had good yields that could be pumped and stored and people could get water fast. This is what informed the rehabilitation, or the improvement. 
On the sand dams, we realized that they are very key water sources especially in Kitui South because there are dry river beds which have sand that is a prerequisite for sand dam construction. We realized that this would be a reliable source because the alternative to that would be the earth dams but we realized there is a lot of evaporation in the earth dams. For the sub-surface dams, there isn’t much evaporation because water is covered in the sand.
In school WASH, what informed was requests by the school community and the education officers on the need to support with latrines because there were some schools where the public health officers were intending to close because they didn’t have sufficient sanitation facilities. We went in and supported. Of course, they were identified through the public health office and the curriculum support office. When we constructed them and put up the hand washing facilities, we realized that the facilities were not being used because of lack of water. So that is what informed our decision of provision of water tanks to harvest the rain water.
