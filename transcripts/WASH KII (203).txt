
In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the woreda?

The first challenge to water service sustainability in the woreda is lack of budget. On top of this the woreda is very large compared to other woredas in the south omo zone, and its population size is more than 250 thousand. Thus, the budget allocated by the government could not satisfy the water demand of the population. Second, the government water projects that are implemented by regional bureaus have very poor quality and do not serve the community for a long period of time. The implementation process starting from the project bidding to final implementation of the project is problematic. As a result the water schemes developed by the government could not serve the community for longer period of time. Third, the community has poor awareness on the use and management of rural water schemes. The community do not keep the water schemes properly, for example, they do not fence the water schemes to protect from animals. And their participation on the development of water schemes is very low. 

What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

There is a budget problem at woreda and zonal level. In order to alleviate the budget problem at woreda level, we have to plan to diversify the revenue sources of the woreda. For example, we should improve the agricultural productivity so that farmers pay their taxes properly. Moreover, we should mobilize the people functioning in trade and service sectors to pay their tax duly. Thus, by doing these we can enhance the size our woreda’s budget. In addition to this, zonal and regional authorities should consider the woreda’s population size in allocating budget for the water sector.

As the woreda is very vast there is mismatch between the demand and supply of water. In this regard NGOs should collaborate with the government in alleviating the supply side problems. 

Regarding the quality problems of water projects implemented by regional bureaus, I suggest that the woreda administration and water offices should be mandated to supervise and monitor the implementation of the projects. We should also control the quality of inputs and materials that are being used by contractors. To this end, qualified quality control personnel should be assigned to supervise the implementation process of projects. There should also be appropriate quality controlling mechanisms during handover of completed projects.

Pertinent to community awareness, we should give intensive training to enhance the community’s awareness on proper use of water schemes. We should also introduce laws that enforce the community to keep water schemes properly. We should also support WASH committees in terms of training, community mobilization and finance. 

Of the solution you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

Of the solutions I mentioned earlier, alleviating the budget problem is the most important solution to ensure sustainable water service in the woreda. Moreover, coordination of NGOs with government bodies has vital role in solving problems related to water service. Besides, authorizing woreda administration to supervise and monitor government water projects is also very essential to ensure quality of water project implementation. If these suggested solutions can be implemented in the near future, we can alleviate the problems related to water service in the woreda. 

In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? 

The woreda water office is playing a leading role in coordinating stakeholders in the WASH sector. At woreda level we have regular meeting with the woreda stakeholders to evaluate action plans and activities of each stakeholder. Generally I can say there is good coordination between stakeholders functioning in WASH sector in the woreda. What could be improved related to the existing coordination? Even though there is good coordination between stakeholders in the woreda, the organization or the structure of the woreda WASH committee is not clear. That is each members of the committee do not have clear mandates or responsibility. Therefore, the WASH committee should be restructured and a clear mandate should be given to each member.
