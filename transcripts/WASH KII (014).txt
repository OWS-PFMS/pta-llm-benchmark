Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: There is the initial part where they used to run community borehole projects, a lot of water you find in Kitui is pumping schemes where there are borehole projects and some of them used to be powered by generators although the County is helping the people convert them to run on solar and running borehole water on diesel and selling it you would need to sell so that you are making some money to run the project by buying diesel so I think that was one of the issues. Another challenge would just be accountability I think, especially when it's handed over completely to the community whether it's through a committee, the issues of transparency, integrity or mismanagement of funds which they are more social issues than technical I think. So you find that every other time you visit a scheme they've changed the committee, they threw out the other one, they brought in another one primarily because they thought the other people were eating the funds that they were generating from the project. Can I come up with many challenges as I think through?
Interviewer: Yes.
Respondent: There are schemes where you find very saline water and then you find a donor has brought in an equipment to make the water edible for human use. But then running the equipment, the running cost may be too high and you need to sell this water at an affordable rate for the community. So like there is an example I can give, a  place called Mutomo, there is an NGO called Sweden Mutomo, they installed a desaliner. A desaliner is an equipment that reduces the salt levels in the water and then every other time they needed to buy the consumables you find that the volume of water sold does not raise the money to service the systems so at some point I thought that it's not sustainable they'll still keep coming to the donor to service the system for them.
Interviewer: So on that point are you talking about the appropriateness of the technology?
Respondent: The technology is okay, I'm talking about the cost at which you can sell and affordability and the general empowerment in terms of money, in terms of economic empowerment of the people. You can't sell the water at maybe more than Ksh.5 a jerry can. But the technology used would require that you sell it even at Ksh.50 a jerry can.
Interviewer: So the challenge there is the cost of the water?
Respondent: Running the equipment through selling this water.
Interviewer: What other challenge?
Respondent: Another challenge, I would also say the County has the financial year and it's always going in cycles. So at the end of the financial year, before they open a new financial year there is a gap. They close on the 31st of June and then they reopen but then until the budget is approved, what happens when there are projects that need to be implemented or there are breakdowns within the-- before the new financial comes up so that they can start advertising for new jobs, there is a gap and it's a big gap especially in a community where people don't have money to lift up broken down boreholes.
Interviewer: There is a financial gap that is what you are saying, between the county and the communities.
Interviewer: Any other challenge?
Respondent: I don't think I can come up with anything else for now. 
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Like the issue of the financial year of the counties, my thinking would be they can come up with something like a long-term contract with suppliers so that you can have them supply you with some of the spares before. It's a contract so it's not binding on the financial year ending. So that when the financial year is closed and you can't do any procurement you can still get things from suppliers and then you can service any broken down projects. That would be one of the solutions that I would think of or the alternative would be just buy spares during the financial year and you keep them in your stores assuming that whatever will breakdown you'll find something to repair it within what you have in your stores. 
On the issues of accountability on the communities and committees, I don't think you can do a lot more on that, you can just have them report to the sub-county water officer or keep books or have a bank account where they bank all this money. It doesn't go to the treasures house so that anytime this money is withdrawn the committee is aware and for what reason you are withdrawing this money. On boreholes powered by generators you can still keep the generators but then do a hybrid system. You just introduce solar and if the water needs are too much they can use the generator at night. So that you pump during the day you store water and if the needs are still way higher or at a time when you have overcast weather cloudy conditions and the pump is not doing well then you can use your generator. On the issues of running boreholes that have very high salt content, dissolved salts, let me not comment on that for now.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
 Respondent: I would pick that one for the county. And I would pick that one because the gap then at any one single time you would have fewer breakages and because of the accountability system where it's handed over to a community and they are reporting to a sub-county water officer then I would think they'd ensure a much more accountable system. 
Interviewer: So in summary which is the most important?
Respondent: The most important is accountability for the communities. I think that's more sustainable, if they can keep their money then the county can only be coming in to boost what from their savings and if people can learn to pay their bills, because you pay electricity at your place, why can't you pay for a broken down pump and it's serving you. If people can get that kind of discipline, then whoever comes in whether it's the county or it's the NGOs or the donors they'll be coming to assist on something that is already moving.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
 Respondent: I am in Business I am not doing interventions. Anyway, we sell water and energy solutions. We deal with boreholes that is borehole pumps, we do water treatment, we do solar powered solutions, that is solar pumping solar lighting and solar power back ups. We sell generators and we also do swimming pools. That's why I said water and energy solutions.
Interviewer: What informed your business?
Respondent: What informed our business or what informed our business coming to Kitui?
Interviewer: The business coming to Kitui.
Respondent: I would say primarily because of devolution, and with devolution we thought it wise to bring services closer to the people versus people coming to Nairobi. That would be the primary reason why there was a branch set-up in Kitui. The other issue is Kitui is under the arid and semi-arid regions of this country so they would need our solutions.
Interviewer: Are there certain spare parts or stuff that you are doing in Kitui that you are not doing in the other counties that are specifically for Kitui County because it's in the ASAL region?
Respondent: Yeah, like swimming pools we don't do a lot of swimming pools here but we do a lot of solar pumps, generators. Generators not so much but for schools yes. We also do water treatment although on a small scale but I'd say we do a lot of borehole pumps and solar equipment in Kitui.

Respondent: Other than the WASH forums we've also done specific trainings for County employees and other sector players and slowly they've adopted the technologies that are coming up. I've been trying to reach out to the chief officer to see whether he can consider converting some of the handpumps to run them on solar. It's something that he is considering although it hasn't happened yet. So I would say that there is a bit of influence from my side. The part on converting generator powered boreholes to solar powered boreholes, that has been a big success although I wouldn't say it's me.

So like in 2014-2015 the reception on solar pumping was not as big as it was. I would also attribute it to the change in governance when the new government came in in 2017 she came in with a different mindset. She was pro-solar. So I don't want to say it's exactly WASH. I think it's an idea that the time had come.
