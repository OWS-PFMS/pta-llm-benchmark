
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: I think there is good coordination between the actors and ourselves.  There is good interaction and the different actors are available when required, either for consultation or for discussions and dialogues.  

Interviewer:  My second question is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining rural water services in the county?

Interviewee: I can point out that one is lack of capacity in the community.  For instance, skilled persons to maintain and operate the systems.  And also ownership of the projects when they have been completed by partners – now the ownership by the community itself.  It’s like the community members sometimes still depend on the external actors to maintain the systems.  For now, that’s what I have.  

Interviewer:  And my third question is “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?”

Interviewee: The one I mentioned on lack of capacity, I think there needs to be more capacity building in regards to hands-on training on operations and maintenance.  There should also be contributions in terms of finances by those who are using the facilities, so that these finances can be used to cater for minor repairs whenever they come up.  There should also be a good reform system from the community systems to the government office in charge of water and the sub-county water office, so that issues that are difficult in the community then can be handled at that level.  

Interviewer:  You mentioned the issue of ownership, what do you think could be a solution to that?

Interviewee: I think that could be handled by first of all, identifying the needs of the community during the inception of the project, so that the projects that are intervened on are really those that are needed by the community.  And then there should be a discussion on the expectation of the actor to the community to own up after the project is over.  

Interviewer:  My fourth question is, “of the solutions you have listed, which is the most important?”

Interviewee: I think for now it’s capacity building.  

Interviewer:  If these solutions were to occur, what would happen next?

Interviewee: If capacity building was to be done, when a project is set up, people would be trained on good maintenance and operations, and in case of any breakdowns, there could be people skilled enough to handle the requirements.  The repairs and maintenance.  And in that way, the project would be sustained.
