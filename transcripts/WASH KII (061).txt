
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: Yes, as you have seen, our interactions with our seniors is free and open, so there are no bureaucracies when dealing with water issues. When we have a breakdown, I can call, even when I am in the field, to the director, the assistant director, and the process of procurement of materials needed for repair of the scheme starts, before I just come.

Another thing is even our MCAs (Members of County Assembly), we have no friction, we are lucky to have educated MCAs currently, who are knowledgeable, and when we are doing these water projects or when they go to their areas (their wards) when they see a malfunctioning scheme they can also consult us or instruct on things to be repaired and then they come back to us later.

And also with the department of devolution….that is coordination, I mean, we work so well with the Ward administrators, the village administrators, now that we do not have people…we do not have staff on the ground…at the sub-locational levels, we use them and they help us in making sure that the schemes are well managed.

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The biggest problem is mismanagement of funds because most of the committees after they have gotten the scheme when it is well-functioning, they swindle…they eat all the money. After they have finished the money, the project when it breaks down, they cannot manage it, they cannot repair the broken parts so it gets grounded. Then, and the worst part is that we are tied…..our hands are tied; we have not been given the Act to sue them. We only change the committees. We elect another team, they also eat the money, we remove them, we elect another committee and the thing goes like that. Those are the biggest challenges; mismanagement of funds.

Other challenges are if we elect a new committee, maybe we might have trained the previous committee in the management and repairs, the new committee is not trained. They do not know how to operate, they might mishandle the equipment. So it means we must keep on training and training. When we elect a new committee we must train the new artisans. Things like that. Because when we remove the committee, we remove together with the artisans because if it is a scheme and they have been swindling….mismanaging the money, we remove together with the artisan. So we need new artisans; we elect new artisans or we employ new artisans…like that. Those are our challenges. Yes. O&M, operation and maintenance, sustainability of the scheme. That is the biggest issue. 

Also, another issue is, if it is a pumping scheme, the maintenance might be very….because of fuel, consumption might be high, water is not very expensive; we are not supposed to sell it for more than three shillings, but they will still sell it above three shillings behind our back. But what we tell them is to sell the water between 2 to 3 shillings so that it can be affordable to every human being.

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: Solutions to these problems. There are many types of operations. If these schemes can be privately operated, I think the O&M sustainability can improve, because private-owned systems are well maintained, but public-owned systems, you know they know, if it breaks down, they will call the Governor, they will call the MCA so that it can be repaired. But if you know it is you and you…you will make sure that all the monies collected from the scheme is ploughed back. Nobody will swindle it or divert it to other activities, and if you divert it to other activities, the activities must be generating to go back to the scheme, maybe to do a pipeline. Yes.

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: Privately owned and operated schemes.

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: If it happens, it should be like this. After we have completed, because we do the scheme to completion, it is new, everything is new and then we hand over to the private manager, from there, he can manage the scheme well and it will not have problems. Yes. 

Interviewer:  Is there anything you would add?

Interviewee: I would add, if still they would be managed by the community, let us impose a penalty. If a community mismanages a certain scheme, they can be sued and their property attached. Let them not be left lose to think that government property has nobody to claim it. They should be sued so that the same scheme can stand on its own. If that is imposed, for sure nobody will eat the project money, because if you eat, your cow will go, your land will go, you will be jailed.
