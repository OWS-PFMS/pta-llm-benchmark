Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? 
Respondent: In my opinion, the main challenge is the source of water. Like right now the water table has gone down and the water demand has gone up so you would find it is a tricky situation now because we don’t have water and the demand is too high. Secondly is the operation of these facilities. Most of them were installed in places where there is no electricity so the operational cost is very high in terms of the human resource and the fuel for pumping is a major problem because you will find that at these facilities the population distribution there is very sparse so you will find from one to another there is a long distance and we have put the pipeline. So, at the end of the day you will need a human who will maintain that line. It is not like in the urban setting where there are many connections in one area. In the rural areas, if you have got like 10 connections, they are very sparse so you find that moving from one point to another we have to provide a motorbike. It is not like here in town where you can get like 100 in one place so that increases the cost of operating these facilities. Secondly where there is no electricity, we are using generators and the cost of diesel is also very high. Another challenge is that the quality of water is saline for most of the schemes we are running. It is a challenge because we are not providing water which is not of good quality and taste.
Climate change is becoming a challenge also, which we can probably link with reducing water yields and it also provides a challenge in selection of technology. Right now, we are in September. In Kitui some years back, around this time of the year the sun used to be very hot, but right now it is still very cold. So, you find now solar becomes a disadvantage when it used to be an advantage sometimes back. Now that one is also becoming a challenge. That is a conversation we are having and it is what is slowing down the changing of these systems. Some years back, August, September and October were hot months, but for the last two to three years there is a change of cycle. It is in September and people are wearing jackets. So, when we are pushing for solar system, we are also wondering whether it will work at this time. We are thinking of having two pumping systems, either diesel and solar or electricity and solar, which will make the cost of investment very high because you will need to have both systems operating at the same time. 

Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: One, we can reduce the cost of operation through introduction of clean energy, that is solar energy where possible. Secondly we can have a situation where we have the company and community partnership to reduce the cost of operation where we can train some of the community members who are the beneficiaries of the schemes and they can help in maintaining the pipeline and providing information on system performance and breakdowns, instead of having full time workers there. In regard to the salinity, we can adopt treatment technologies so that we provide clean water. I think that for reducing yields and climate change, we can think of catchment conservation and protection.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The use of solar because it will cut the operating costs. After we have installed a clean energy technology in a borehole, what will happen is that the cost of production will go down. Some of these facilities are not sustaining themselves and to sustain them we have to get some money from other projects-the ones that are doing well and making a bit of money. So, when they become fully sustaining after we reduce the cost of production and operation, we will also ensure that the pumping is more efficient. Just to add, on the issue of fuel, sometimes you may find that there is a gap between when the fuel is exhausted at the facility and when more fuel is procured. However, when we have got solar, solar energy is always there and the areas where the boreholes are located are in Yatta plateau where there is a lot of sun. So, you find that operating the facilities will be more practical using solar energy than using diesel. Additionally, maintenance of a solar facility is cheaper compared with maintenance of a diesel facility because the diesel-powered systems require a lot of servicing. Sometimes the oil filter is not working, pump filters are not working and such. When we go the solar way, you will find that the maintenance of the pumping system becomes very cheap and easy.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: We operate 15 boreholes which are spread across the county in the different sub-counties and they serve a population of approximately 100,000 people. There is a program we have started in Mutito where we are installing solar powered systems in the boreholes that we operate. We are also connecting some of the facilities with electricity, instead of using diesel. When some of these projects were done about 10 years ago, there was no electricity supply in the rural areas. So, where they were being established there was no electricity but the country has progressed and the electricity is almost everywhere now. Take for example in Ikanga, 10-15 years ago there was no electricity in the area so people used a generator to pump water from the borehole. The entire system was dependent on a generator. Now with the electricity being supplied by the government you find it is easy to apply to be connected to the electricity grid. These are the changes we are talking about and we have decided to connect the boreholes to electricity because of the development that has taken place and also to reduce operating costs. In regard to installing solar-powered systems, we have decided to take this approach in order to reduce the operation cost.
The other thing we are doing it taking over management of some boreholes. We have taken 100% management of three boreholes, that is Kilungu, Museve and Mamole. These three are part of the 15 that we are operating. As a water service provider, we adopt the systems, manage and operate them using our area manager. We do not agree to co-owning or co-managing the projects with the community. We either manage it fully or we leave it. The county and the community, they can co-manage. However, for us our operation is different from the county. We are supposed to generate revenue and maintain ourselves-pay for operation and administrative costs and such things. For the County, they have got allocation of money from the National Government whether they are generating any revenue or not and this is taxpayer’s money. 
Interviewer: How is the approach working?
Respondent: For us, because we are well established, we have got an operations office, we have got the technical and commercial departments so when we get in it is very easy for us to operate because when the fuel is exhausted, we just take the fuel to the facilities. If the service is not done, we take our technician there to do maintenance and general service. The challenge which is there is the transition from the community management to us. You will find that the way some of the schemes used to operate, they used to give water with a flat rate, for example, every house would pay around 500 shillings and they did not have a meter. It was like contribution to buy fuel for pumping the water. When we come in, we provide water bills and we charge the users as per their consumption. Now telling a person who was used to paying 500 shillings that their bill is say 700 shillings becomes a challenge. They say that we came in and water became expensive. Secondly, these community need to pay an administration and deposit fee to us so that they can become our customers which on a rough estimate is about 7,000 shillings, which is a lot of money for them. Another challenge with this approach is that we get schemes that have so many problems. There is no clear policy for this approach. The customers were told that once the schemes would be run by us as a formal water service provider, it would be cheap for them and they would get water constantly. Now, we come in and tell them to pay the fees and it is very expensive for them, when we bill per consumption it is also expensive. We have these problems. One recommendation I have for this approach is that when we have these transitions, it the process should be clear and well defined. The county can consider paying the registration fees for the customers or generally to take up the costs for taking over these projects. I have been trying to influence the county and suggested we sit and agree on the parameters to check before qualifying a scheme to be handed over to a water service provider. Generally, though, all water schemes qualify to be taken over by us because at the end of the day, our mandate is to provide water. What needs to be clear is in regard to which entity will bear the cost of the handover, because like it or not, there is a huge cost there.

In terms of the reasons for these interventions I have mentioned are basically the fact that we are an entity mandated to provide water. The main pipeline from Masinga is not adequate to serve the population, so we have to have other systems. Sometimes the cost is quite high but the fact that we represent the government, we have to focus on the mandate and make sure that we serve as many people as possible. We invest a lot in that. We focus on the unserved first before working on reducing the cost of water for those who are already served. If we get a hundred million, we think about putting pipelines, drilling another borehole for those people without water. Until we reach a point where now everybody has water we are operating at a very high cost to generate water, then now we can start thinking as a society how to reduce cost. But as long as there is someone who does not have water, the cost will go to that person first to ensure that they have water. About taking over management, the schemes were proposed to us by the county, which cited them as being poorly managed and therefore needing us and our formalized systems to support management.
