
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The question is not coming out clearly, because, if it is the focus to this exercise we have done, what I can see is if it were what shall it achieve or if it were in existence or is it the value of it in sustainability…in that perspective? We can say…in terms of what we have done we can see…what we do. The issue is, as we understand the network of the stakeholders, we can say it will go along with enhancing more focus to wherever they are undertaking their activities. Probably, it is also important for us because at moments we can say under specific activities are being undertaken in regions…there is always a bit of equity in where the stakeholders are, and then what kind of activities they are doing within the specific…can they be up-scaled…and all this. And therefore we see what can be adopted to see that our projects are sustainable because there are some specific projects which if an actor has already done and are still within the area of operations, we still link with them so that they can have continuity in understanding the communities need to be supported for sustainability of these schemes. Or in one way or the other we link where there is a possibility of doing it. Where there is no possibility, we know where …to what extend we can do the intervention, so maybe…the question might be in both the importance and current scenario.

In the current scenario, what is working well is that we know where all our stakeholders are and we can easily call them and understand the status of the schemes they are undertaking and probably this helps in sustaining the schemes they are operating on.

Interviewer:  You mentioned that you know where all your stakeholders are and what they are doing. Is there a way of them channeling that information to you, or do you have requirements or them to channel that information or they do it out of their own volition?

Interviewee: It is two way; there is when they demand it, they channel the information to us, there is what is also what is required; the best practice – that they submit on monthly basis…on quarterly basis. There are those who are under close link with our sub-county water offices whereby they can submit the reports on monthly basis, and the sub-counties can compile them and submit to us on a quarterly basis. What has been there is that we have not structured well the kind of reporting whereby to some extent, the sub-counties report basically what the government is undertaking and leaves out what the partners are doing. But we have been, out of the current WASH Forums we have already had, we have been emphasizing; let the reporting capture all the stakeholders – what they are doing, what the government is doing…this kind of reporting. That has been the case.

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The main problem is…I can say the main problem, the core problem is lack of monitoring. That is according to me. Because we have put a lot of assumptions on…most of the schemes are managed by the communities. Communities have no technical skills. They still lack governance skills and then we assume that they will manage the schemes perfectly. So now, we do not have a proper monitoring programme which we can now be identifying their gaps and weak areas so that we have continuous support with them. They require continuous training, coaching and support so that sustainability can be seen. What has been there, most of the people when they implement the projects, they assume the status quo will remain but due to depreciation, the project wearing out, operation and maintenance…there is a lot so maintenance and repairs that are required and which do not come out clearly and when you lack better systems of monitoring, communities still may not get to the…they may not be able to adhere to schedules unless they are clearly followed-up. So these are some of the major challenges to sustainability.

Just to add on that, apart from the monitoring, we do not have policies that give them a strict guideline; “you should do this, if you do not do this, this is the repercussion”. 

Repairs and maintenance has been left to the open market, so people go to all kinds of spare parts, which are either very cheap, others are counterfeit. So you find that when you go to buy you buy something that looks similar to reality but it takes just a short period and it is spoilt. So these are some of the challenges in regard to repair and maintenance.

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: There should be a systematic way of receiving information. That is one. Way of monitoring the schemes. Probably, if we can have a monitoring structure set, even at the click of a button you can get a link to the specific projects and know the status of the schemes at that point. That is one. 

The other one is we need to come up with good policies which can be adhered to. 

Then we need also have to…market links and awareness of maybe what is best for the…specific kind of equipment to be used within these water supplies. Or maybe link up with specific repair and maintenance service providers who we will be sure have quality products.

The other…you cannot leave out continuous training, and probably where we can say…we can think of continuous training so that we can give the communities a choice of management models they can adopt. We need also to bring either private partners in the management of these schemes. We need to bring all these kinds of options then maybe that one can be tailored and captured within the policy we are likely to develop.

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: The priority number one to address in these issues is if we can address the issues of monitoring. That one will enhance sustainability to a greater level; to a greater percentage. That one alone.

Then number two we come to development of specific policies for guidance and adherence to.

Number three. We have trainings and coaching to enhance the community to be able to…we do not want to say the community model has failed, but still we need to bring up this.

Number four is bringing up new stakeholders in management of rural schemes for competitiveness and comparison in sustainability.

Number five: To link them to the proper market and genuine service providers. 

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: If this happens in a systematic manner, giving priority to the factors as we have mentioned, I think we will be towards achieving a sustainable water service provision.

Interviewer:  Anything else that you would add to the information you have provided?

Interviewee: We are calling up for partnership because the budget we have is not adequate. There are some areas where we see the soft component of service provision is not highly budgeted for…or factored in our budget as a government. So, we still now need partners whom we can link with in addressing this soft elements of sustainability…when it comes to the government per se…it might not achieved within the span of time we are imagining. So, if we can have partners who are willing to partner with us, we upscale this and put up programmes which are in line with the new technologies…

I consider monitoring, mapping and other issues as soft components.
