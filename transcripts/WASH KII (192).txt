1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in your town?

As to our office, one of the major activities is water infrastructure development, maintenance and managing water associations and institutions. So, when we see from this point of view, one of the problems challenging the sustainability of water services is [Lack of community awareness and skill gap] The second problem is [lack of skilled man power] this is manifested when we are unable to maintain the damaged water schemes everywhere.  The third one is [shortage of spare parts] which means the limited capacity of we have to maintain the existing infrastructure. Regarding awareness, we have been observing gaps from the community to take responsibility and share the cost that would be used for maintenance of the water infrastructures. There are also extensive problems related to spare parts. From our side, we couldn’t give adequate training for community leaders because of financial capacity. For this reason, they have faced difficulties to manage at least minor maintenances around their area.  
In terms of water cost sharing, there are clear working declarations and rules at the federal, regional levels and it says that since water is an economic asset, the beneficiaries need to share the cost of maintenance and protect the service. But, in practice there is low level of initiative to execute the rules by the community.  We have already identified and evaluated the prevalence of this gap in all woredas. 
In general, from the maintenance and operation aspect, we have limited number of skilled man power at the zone level, particularly the limited number water mechanics cannot reach out to all districts. I am talking to the water service for an ordinary purpose, there is also a demand from large institutes that need motorized schemes and water in largest quantity, in this case, since we don’t have water cranes, we are requesting them from the region. From this perspective, I can say we have shortage of machines in general.  We need to work out to use the available water infrastructures, small matches and spare parts in the sense that they can be used for long

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
As to the zone water office, we can say that people are using pure drinking water when we make sure that we have fair distribution of water treatment chemicals throughout the zone, the can only be successful with the help and coordination of the region water bureau and other NGOs
Among the common challenges of water service sustainability that I mentioned earlier, community awareness should be given the focus of attention and the major areas of intervention. When we work on this, we are developing a sense of ownership and responsibility to water users.  When community develops a sense of ownership, they can internalize trainings that are provided to them to fill the gap in areas of small maintenance and operation of water infrastructure which can be done with their own resources.  By doing so, they can request woreda and zone experts regarding technical and other assistance when it comes beyond their capacity.  
Regarding shortage of man power, we need to capacitate our employees who are engaged in maintenance and operation areas starting from the woreda to zone. How could this work? We will request the region and NGOs working in the sector for their assistance. As I told you before, many of the water schemes in our zone are non-functional. One of the reasons for this could be over usage of the water services, particularly in low land areas like the Dasanech woreda, people will stay around dug walls for the whole day and consume more water both for themselves and their animals, and this increases the chance for the scheme to be damaged easily. Thus, in these areas, there is a need to have adequate spare parts to be reserved around woreda water offices, building the capacity of the water experts and providing awareness to the community to involve themselves in silly maintenance areas. If we work hard on awareness, the community can have the chance to collect money, buy spare parts and look for skilled experts by their own initiatives. 
As to water scheme maintenance, we have three types: very hard, medium l and minor level. The minor ones could be practiced by the community, the medium ones will be managed by experts from the woreda and zone and the very large ones will need usage of crane and tripod which requires call for other stalk holders and the need to build our financial capacity gradually.

3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs? 
Working on community awareness, in this regard, we have actions that we made from the government perspective and there are also steps performed by NGOs. From the government side, with the budget allocated from the region, we have trained about 104 water user associations and 10 federations through coordination of experts from the region, zone and woreda levels. NGOs, on their part have been practicing campaign on community awareness in close coordination with the woreda and zonal experts.  Not only, awareness creation, have they also made an intervention on capacitating the already established WASH cos. The trainings were provided in two different programs, in each of the schemes there were 7 water experts of which 5 are assigned to focus on the general management and institutional changes of the of the water service and the task of the remaining two was sharing their experiences on overall maintenance and operation of water schemes.  At this level, we were trying to attract more women, at least 50%, to be members of the WASHcos because we believe that women are closest beneficiaries of water services. This is part of the software development scheme. However, we are not saying that we have achieved our targets; our target is to attract more and more educated and experienced water experts to lead water schemes and bring about visible structural changes. In this way, we can make sure that the level we have now for water coverage and service sustainability would be changed drastically.  In fact, our institutional focus is working on sustainability of the established water infrastructures more than building the new ones. This is our greatest target and key work.  At this level, though there are geographical variations, we have identified gaps that need our further efforts. 
4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What should be improved?
As to the water office, we have stalk holders and NGOs are among them. Out of this, we have also government line offices starting from zone administration and up to now, we are working in a good coordination. Up to our maximum limit,, we have been working together with line offices on transferring the capital budget to water services, including building water schemes,  developing the capacity of and training the WASHcos. On the NGOs side, they have been filling the gaps through research and capacity building and maintaining and establishing old and new water schemes, respectively. They also participate in the provision of spare parts, updating the WASHcos through training and it was good so far. However, we are not saying this coordination is strong and adequate. We are below the standard in water coverage; our zone has 39.8 %, coverage this shows that there are still large gaps that needs holistic coordination with stalk holders. On top of that, many of the NGOs have limited budget for WASH. I think World vision and IRC WASH are better and we are expecting that they will have good insights to fill the gaps. IRC is working on rehabilitation program, they have also developed two water schemes at Muti and Kuber areas and they are still behind in building water infrastructures. On contrast, World vision has exceeded in this scheme, they have developed about 11 shallow walls and digging 6 more around bena Tsemay woreda. So, from the perspectives of actual infrastructural development, I can say we are all lagging behind but with the software aspect we have strong coordination. 

Probing: Why you told me information only about these two NGOs? What about others and what should be improved? Yes there are more, but the governments focus is on water infrastructure development and the number of NGOs operating in this scheme is limited. We are leading the coordination process and I think there is no gap on that. What we have to focus is actual work, including leading and forming the WASHcos.

5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how you believe this coordination would lead to more sustainable water service in the woreda? For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?

I believe that coordination will have great role in solving all the problems we mentioned before. For the first thing, if we have strong coordination, we can sense the water coverage shortage and propose common solutions as well. If we do this, we can look over alternative sources to solve this and other problems, including working on new infrastructures. As I told you before, you may dug and provide water for the community, but supplying pure drinking water is very important issue that should involve common discussions, imputes and efforts. 
We may not solve our problems 100%, but coordination will have its role in reducing the current state of water service problems. How about its role in solving skilled manpower? It has great contributions, because we are coordinating with administration, finance, education, health, and children etc. and these sectors have their role in decision making and budget allocating for the limited man power and facilitating trainings in water sector. There are no problems that will not be solved through discussion.
