1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the woreda?
In my opinion, there is no sustainable waters service in the woreda. The main reasons for this problem are: First, the budget allocated for the sector is very limited. It cannot enable to satisfy the needs of the community. Second, the topography of some of the kebeles in the woreda is not convenient to transport raw materials for the construction of water schemes. That is some of the kebeles do not have access to road so that they do not have water schemes. Even though there are considerable efforts to expand clean water supply both by the government and non-governmental organizations, still there is no sufficient and sustainable water service in the woreda. Are there any other problems related to water service sustainability? We have no other problems, our main challenge to water service sustainability is shortage of budget.
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
In my opinion, to alleviate the problem related to budget, there must be strong coordination between sector offices. For instance, at woreda level the water office, education office, health office and the administration office should coordinate to identify problems and to find solutions for the identified problems. If there is coordination between these woreda stakeholders and zonal offices as well, we might solve the problem related to budget. What other solutions do you recommend to solve the budget problem? It would be great if non-governmental (NGOs) provide financial support to alleviate the budget problem. At woreda level budget for water projects is approved by the woreda administration office. However, the woreda administration office do not have the capacity to allocate budget for more than one or two water projects per year. This could not answer the needs of the entire community. Therefore, NGOs should cooperate by providing either financial support or by directly constructing water schemes.
Regarding infrastructure problem, emphasis should be given for the construction of feeder roads in remote areas. Once we construct roads in remote areas, it is easy to transport raw materials for the development of water schemes in the area. To this end, both the government and NGOs should coordinate to alleviate the problem related to infrastructure (road). Have you discussed on the problems with the woreda administration office? Both the community and the sector offices raised the issue to the woreda administration office several times, however, the woreda on its own do not have the capacity to solve the problem.
3.Of the solution you listed, which is the most important?  Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs? 
To ensure sustainable water service in south Ari woreda, the most important solution is to create strong coordination between governmental and non-governmental organization.
4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved? 
There is WASH committee at woreda level, and this committee closely works with different sector offices. For instance, there is good coordination between water, health and education offices. These offices work together to solve any problems they face in WASH sector. Is there any gap related to the existing coordination? What could be improved? Honestly speaking the existing coordination among sector offices is good, and it is difficult for me to say there is gaps related to coordination at woreda level. However, at kebele level (the lowest administrative unit) there are coordination gaps in protecting water schemes from misuse. That is there no responsible body that regularly monitor water schemes and report to woreda water office when there is any problem.  
5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?
Coordination can solve the problems that I mentioned earlier. For instance, if we have strong coordination with various governmental and non-governmental organizations, we can change the community’s awareness on proper use of water schemes by providing intensive trainings.  Through coordination even woreda stakeholders can gain good lessons from those organizations that have enormous experience in the sector.
