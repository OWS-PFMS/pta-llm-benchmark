Question 1: In your opinion, what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui? Could you please explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: One of the challenges facing water services provision and especially rural water supplies, because those are the main facilities for water services at the grassroots, is governance. There is a huge gap when it comes to governance considering that these facilities are managed by water management committees. One of the reasons why there is a lot instability on the governance of these schemes is that there is a lot of political interference. You know water here in Kitui is a very emotive thing and therefore politicians will always find it easy to manipulate the electorate and in the process they end up interfering with the governance of the committees for the water schemes and in most cases they end destabilizing everything and eventually disrupting the services provision so this one in my view I think it needs to be addressed but now we don’t have a clear policy that separates the political activities and water services provision so we need to clearly demarcate the boundaries between politics and water service provision and now we are in a roadmap to developing a water policy for this county. I believe this one is going to be dealt with conclusively and therefore in future we will not have political interference with these schemes because it goes to even to an extend where politicians would use water schemes to reward their followers and now this one beats the logic for having scheme management; for the election of these water management committees. 
The other challenge we have is about financial management. The revenues that they collect from the water sales, that is for the services, you know water is free – they charge for the services. There is a very huge gap in the management of those finances. In most cases they are not accounted for and even if you try to make follow ups you don’t find anything simply because documentation is very poor and also the handling process. Most of them they keep the money in their pockets; they don’t have bank accounts and where they have accounts, they hardly use them to handle the money, so this is another field where we have a lot of challenges. This is why we have cases of schemes breaking down because of a minor breakdown but end up taking too long to be repaired simply because the money is not there and sometimes when the county government does not have some money on standby to replace these assets it becomes a big challenge. 
The other challenge with the water service provision is the technical capacity. We have a shortage of technical skills as far as water infrastructure management and maintenance is concerned and you realize that in most cases these schemes depend on electricians, plumbers and pipe fitters from the county headquarters and also from the sub county water offices and therefore because the schemes are so many, you realize that sometimes they will take a few days or weeks because of a minor breakdown. This one can easily be addressed if the county government together with our partners can come up with a program to delocalize these technical skills to the people – especially the basic technical and mechanical skills like servicing of the gensets, simple electrical procedures and plumbing works. This can be very good so that around every scheme there are these people who can support the schemes with the repair and maintenance – things like servicing the gensets, those are simple mechanical procedures and therefore if people can be trained they can definitely have people who can do it at the village level and this will really improve the quality of service delivery as far as water is concerned.


Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: I have mentioned that the policy is expected to handle the issues of political interference, and I have also talked about the county coming up with a program to train communities on simple technical skills. On the issue of governance and financial management, I think to some extend the model that we are using to run these facilities is not very effective; it is highly depended on government technicians who are in most cases not enough to attend to all the schemes, considering that we have over 500 schemes across the county. If we can find a way to come up with a model that is aimed at…a model that is going to ensure sustainability of these schemes depends on the resources available, including the human resource, I think that one will be better. We will cultivate a culture where these schemes are independent and self-sufficient. They will not depend on the government, because most of them as much as they run and they have committees and they collect revenue, they depend entirely on the funding from the government in case of a breakdown. If we can come up with a model that is going to transfer this dependency to themselves and then we cultivate that, I think it would do a big boost towards sustainability of the schemes. Having this model will cover all issues to do with governance, financial management and technical skills – an all-inclusive model.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: Of course, all of them are important but the most important one is to work on the financial management as a key component of the management model I have talked about earlier because if we clearly address this, you find that everything else will fall into place. With adequate funds the schemes can be able to source skills from far areas, get capacity to maintain the facilities and make sure that water is continuously available at the facilities. The financial management component of the model should ensure that there is circulation of money from the revenue to expenditure and that this is well monitored. There will be a clear structure which is known to the government and all the partners that the money from water sales should be dealt with in this way, even if it means we come up with a central kitty where these schemes can subscribe and then the money will be available for use in case there is a breakdown. That is what I am talking about so that we completely weed out all the wastage because there is a lot of wastage. Sometimes they put the money on wasteful activities and when it comes to a critical issue then the money is not there. So, if we can structure the utilization of this money, I think we will be moving towards a permanent solution to this.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: One of the major interventions we are implementing at the sub county particularly to our water schemes is that we have a hybridization program. We want to see to it that all our schemes use green energy and this is aimed at now achieving full cost recovery for the maintenance of the schemes. So we want to have our schemes equipped with solar pumping systems at least to bring down the cost of pumping. Previously we have used electricity and diesel gensets which have proved to be very expensive to maintain and also for the fuel. So, we think with the hybridization program we can bring this maintenance cost down and therefore the revenues that are collected now can be available for servicing and maintenance of the gensets, the pipelines and other equipment. So that one is a program we are having and where we have done it although we have not achieved 100%, the change where we have done it we have seen significant improvement. We have our partners who have supported us in this program like USAID through the KIWASH project, they have hybridized Musengo Water project and it was a huge investment and since the project was rehabilitated we have seen a lot of improvement and we have seen a lot of consistency in terms of water supply unlike previously when they were using electricity. It is proving to be a reliable solution towards addressing this. This one was informed by cost of operating the schemes which has been skyrocketing recently. You realize that over 50% of all the revenue collected from the water sales is going to purchasing diesel and therefore the remaining 50% is not enough to cater for maintenance and also for salaries considering that there is a lot of wastage of these funds through mismanagement. So with hybridization we can cut down the pumping costs so that if they are going to use diesel gensets it is only for back up or even to bridge the gap on demand so in most cases where there is a lot of demand, they can bridge it by pumping using the gensets at night and then during the day they can use the solar which is free and it has really worked.
The other one is that we have started a program to train water resource persons within every water scheme. We identified two of them, we trained them through the Kenya Water Institute, however we have not reached full actualization of this dream because we have not assimilated all of them to the schemes but we hope in the near future we will be able to accomplish that and we will continue to achieve this. The thinking around this kind of training was to disseminate these technical skills to the ground so that those repairs that don’t require specialized skills can be done by the communities, bearing in mind that those are people who are beneficiaries of the scheme and they can be easily reached. This cuts down the cost of getting a technician from very far away because it is very expensive to do so – they have to pay for fuel, for the driver and for other expenses. So, this was aimed at delocalizing these technical skills.
We also have what I would call mainstream interventions which are informed by our mandates such as extensions of pipelines to reach more people, drilling and equipping of boreholes and the like. We also have other interventions that are aimed at conserving the catchments. You know, recently we have experienced drying up of boreholes and this is because of the drought situations and so we have programs like construction of sub surface dams and earth dams along the catchments at least to try and conserve the catchments and to improve the rates of recharge for our aquifers. Other interventions are being done in areas where we have saline ground water. So, together with our partners we have been able to install reverse osmosis machines for desalinization although with some challenges because the output is not sufficient as far as the demand is concerned and also the maintenance of the machines is challenging because we have found it difficult to have the local people maintaining the systems. I can say that this has not picked well but now we are looking for ways on how we can improve it and customize it so that it can be usable and also operated by the local people without necessarily involving the technical people from the department.
