
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: I think it’s how we interact with the national and county government.  The national government in terms of the WASREB and the WARMA.  Yeah, the interaction between us and the national government, the county government and the regulatory bodies.  

Interviewer:  My second question is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining rural water services in the county?

Interviewee: Basically, the role of water supplies. The problem is about where to get that water.  It’s one of the starting points.  And again we supposedly have boreholes in those areas.  Management of the boreholes is a challenge to them because the moment it’s done and given to the community they end up eating all that money.  And they don’t have money in the case of breakdowns.  They mismanage their finances. 

Interviewer:  Anything else?

The lack of capital.  The same in the case of management, some of these people don’t have skills.  You find the gentleman is not educated and does not know the importance of the project.

Interviewer:  And my third question is “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?”

Interviewee: First of all, especially for the water service providers, It is high time the national government including the county government, to channel the resources that they have to us because we have the challenge of finance.  You find the national government and the county government want to do the projects, but we are the service providers, we are the people who give water to the community.  If you come to Kitui town and if there is no water, they will come to me and say “we don’t have water in Kitui town”, so we need to be given -  most of the resources should be channeled through us because it is us who know where the problem is.  We get like 90% of the water from Masinga dam.  When there is a breakdown in Masinga, it is we who look for money to do the repairs there.  Same case for the boreholes all the way from Masinga in the service provision area to Kitui town.  Kitui town needs a lot of water.  Most of the connections are in urban areas here in Kitui.  So we need to be given money, resources, including the meters.  The zonal meters and consumer meters so we can also measure the water we are producing and who is consuming that.

Another thing is about these community projects.  I know the county government is doing a lot to come up with these water projects in the rural areas.  Once they are done, they should not be given to the people at the community level.  They need to be handed over to us, so that we run them because we have the skills.  And actually, since we are a company, we can get the money to service, the little money we have and if we don’t have money we can borrow from the county government, to repair whatever is broken in those projects like the boreholes.

Interviewer:  If these solutions were to occur, what would happen next?

Interviewee: I can see more people getting water, that’s one.  We take care of our water because we are regulated.  The water we give to the people is treated.  So what we are giving to them has to be tested, to ensure it is safe water we are giving to these people.  With these community guys, we don’t know about the water they’re giving to the people.  We don’t know how they’ve treated that water, so you’re not sure what is being given to the people.  So [if the solutions are implemented], they will get safe, affordable because we charge very little.
