Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: One of the challenges that affect water service provision basically in Kitui and specifically in Mwingi where we have our operations currently I will say we the key stakeholders probably have a limitation in terms of how we connect with the common or the local people, the community themselves who are the primary beneficiaries of these water projects. Many times we sit in board rooms, we sit in higher level kind of meetings and make decisions that affect the primary user of these water resources without necessarily having to involve them. Now when we do those kinds of projects you realize that when it gets to the ground it lacks that part of ownership, it lacks that aspect of the communities walking together with that decision, they do not own. So many times when we roll out these kinds of interventions they do not support them and after one or two years you realize the water project has already collapsed.
 I strongly feel, for us to sustain these water projects in rural context we need to walk with the community, we need to walk with the primary beneficiaries of these projects and let them start owning the project right from the beginning. In that way they will be able to even support the projects even after the exit of the donors or whoever is funding the projects. So it is of primary importance to have these projects coming from the community other than the other way round. I really advocate for bottom top but with an intensive kind of discussions and deliberations, so that these people are able to be well guided so that they do not just come up with projects because they hear of them but they can come up with projects because they truly believe that they can work within their contexts because you realize that what works best in Mwingi probably will not work best in Mutomo. So we need to involve the primary user who is the community and let's have them walk together with the project and we will surely be assured of the success and sustainability. On the other hand we have also as ADRA Kenya encountered a challenge with issues to do with locating of these water projects especially the land. Many of the donors and many of support organizations that support the community projects do not buy land for installation of these projects. Most of these lands are either donated by the community especially when you are doing projects like the borehole, earth dams, sand dams and such things. And now when these communities donate the land there is this temporary agreements that they do make with the community that don't last long. After a short time, after the project has been installed, a lot of work has been done and the communities are already benefiting from the water projects. 
Many occasions we have witnessed some landowners turning against the community, chasing them away. They want to reposes their land. We have several cases in Mwingi, we have some other cases in Kitui Rural and when that kind of friction starts to occur within the community, people shy away and they let the project go away. When this person is left with the project, probably they may not be in a position to sustain that project which primarily the project was not done to benefit one person, it was done to benefit the larger community and now it becomes a series of frictions and collisions and people taking this up to the chiefs and in that way it lacks that assurance of that sustainability aspect of that particular project. In other occasions, you realize that the most vocal people in the society or the opinion leaders for that matter or the local leaders they tend to have a higher voice than the local people and in that many times they confuse the donor organization or even the government, they confuse the leadership and they try to be the first person to donate the land and when they do that, either land or whatever the resource that they do donate, when they do that and this project has already been installed, they take advantage of lack of information for the community, they misuse them, they try to monopolize that project and in the end the communities tend to detach from the water project and the aspect again of sustainability is not assured in that way.
Interviewer: Apart from those two, which other one?
Respondent: We still have a big gap on the issue of policy I think as we mentioned we were beginning this interview, in many occasions you realize the community does not know what they are entitled to do. The community does not know, there are no clear policy guidelines that guide especially implementation of water projects at the community level for other partners apart from the government. On the part of the government I may not speak much of that area but I know it is clear that there is from the new framework there is that community participation, there is that identification of the projects and how the project is rolled out. But with the NGOs we tend to rely more on community leadership and the government offices that are in place in that particular area where we are implementing and many times, sometimes we are not really I won't say misled but we don't do informed projects that have clear policy guidelines in terms of implementation, in terms of sustainability. When an NGO does, let's say a borehole or an earth dam or a sand dam or a pipeline after the exit of that project, say after one, two or three years, these people, it lacks how it is supposed to be transferred from the NGO to the community. 
Many times they have the water committees that we normally form that are not clearly stipulated in the law. There are no clear policy guidelines that guide the working of the water committees. Sometimes they rely on the local leadership like the chiefs, the assistant chiefs who may not constantly day after day monitor or supervise the running of these water resources and eventually some of those people embezzle the money that is gotten from these water projects and there is no clear policy, there are no clear laws that say whoever does this, needs to be done this. They just take you around in circles, there is no legal framework that guides how maybe the water committee that is manning a certain borehole when they get that one or two bob shilling that they collect how they are supposed to use it. Many times they embezzle that money, they cannot account to the community and there is no clear framework that guides on how action is supposed to be taken against such people. So we need to relook that area, bring together the stakeholders, especially through the government and agree on when these projects are put in place, which are really good and for the benefit of the community, how are we supposed to assure sustainability especially when we are working with the local leadership at community level.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Respondent: Well as I mentioned earlier, from our very first challenge that I mentioned, I mentioned about land ownership and how we need to have clear land ownership guidelines especially where we have community water projects installed. To limit or to discourage landowners from repossessing the land and frustrating the community in the name of expiry of lease, expiry of agreements and such things. In order to minimize again this community conflicts on the issues to do with land, there needs to be coordination. There is this body within the government that coordinates all the water actors within a certain area. We need to agree on very clear land ownership or land donation. The people who are donating land for water projects they need to have intensive land agreements which I propose needs to be registered with the lands' office and all other relevant government bodies. It needs to be to an extreme that if somebody provokes or disowns that particular agreement they are liable to a certain fine or penalty. That is issues to do with land. 
On the issues to do with us not informing the community I think I mentioned, we need to walk with the community right from the beginning of the project. And if need be, in fact from the beginning of identifying these areas of interventions. Let us use that bottom up approach whereby we walk with the community, understand their needs, let them talk about their needs, let's use that needs from the community to design the water projects, to design all the interventions that we may have to take so that whatever interventions that we do it becomes part of the community and in that way they'll be able to own it and walk with it to sustainability. We talked about the policy issues and I strongly feel that with now increased or enhanced representation, all the actors especially on water related issues, need to walk with the local leadership include now we have the County governments, we have the issues of water, is one of the devolved areas, we need to walk with them so that we can explore a possibility of formulating guidelines if not laws that will inform sustainability, implementation and also identification of these water projects so that we can have them like assured way of sustainability.
Interviewer: You spoke about the process of handing over the projects where we lack clear policy guidelines to handover the projects to the community and also there are no policies to prosecute or rather deal with the embezzlement of funds. Do you have suggestions on any management models for these schemes relating to handover and embezzlement?
Respondent: Many times whoever is implementing the water project, it is always at their own decision to decide how to handover their projects. We do not have a coordinated way or guidelines on how like for instance the Rural Focus are supposed to handover a certain water project in a certain area to the community. I propose that if the wing of the government that coordinates the working of the NGOs and all the players within the water sector can sit down together, develop a framework that will guide on what are the key water projects in the context of say Kitui County. We mention all the water projects, how can we ensure sustainability of these projects especially those that have been implemented by the Non-governmental organizations. Is there a standard way or rather come up with a standard of how these projects are supposed to be handed over to the community so that we can have a common working platform. Not like whatever ADRA feels like is how they hand over the projects. Rural Focus will feel is how they will hand over their projects. Others elsewhere we do not have a uniform way on how it's supposed to be done. 
That is why many times after the project or after the NGO has exited the area the project is basically left on the hands of the government. These governments sometimes they even don't know the existence of these projects. Sometimes they don't know the challenges because they've not walked with the project/ It is also clear in my mind that whenever we are implementing these projects we need to start walking with the government concerned office like say water department right from the beginning. Like what we do in ADRA Kenya, whenever we are starting any water projects in any area we must walk very closely in fact we normally walk like very close partners to the ministry of water for that matter for water projects. Now having that now whenever we exit from an area it becomes the property of the government and the water office continues to monitor this particular water project. So it becomes easier and sustainability in that way it is assured.
Interviewer: So in your opinion the problem is in the process of handing over and not the management of the facility after handing over?
Respondent: Well they are tied, they are interrelated, it is one and the same thing. How you hand over determines how management will be after the exit. If you have walked with these people, say it is a water committee manning a certain borehole or a certain water project or a pipeline, if you have walked together for like a year and then now you are stepping out you need to handover in a way that there is a clear guidelines on how these people are going to work in your absence. In that way, these people will be responsible to somebody or to a certain office and this way I'm proposing that the government water office in that area is supposed to take that role of supervising this water. But again, sometimes they are tied they can say that they are supervising, but again there is no clear policy that is guiding that if somebody does this, what are we supposed to do, if this happens what are we supposed to do. So sometimes they do not really offer that. They just offer supervisory role without any powers to like have a command of that particular water project.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: With the land ownership, if we can solve the problem of land ownership, once and for all, you can come up, I said earlier that we need to engage even the land office in this process. So that when somebody has donated a land for a certain purpose they donate it completely or what are the terms of that engagement. An din many cases I recommend that where possible let's have these projects in a community land or a public land. Let's use this land donation thing as a last resort but I understand for instance some projects may have challenges with that because if it's a borehole you have to do like water service to identify the sighting and necessarily sighting may not be where the public land is it may site in a place where it's a private land. That is why I'm saying, preferably let's have community water projects in a public land or in a government owned land and in the event that is not possible, let's have a very clear land agreement which has been signed by the lands office and it is punishable for everyone who revokes that particular agreement.
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: Kitui County has been a food insecure county for a very long time/ Kitui county has experienced perennial droughts and perennial food shortages and many households have been really struggling to access food especially during the dry seasons in year after the other. We have livelihood inclusive project in Kitui county specifically in Mwingi central where we are walking with the community to build their capacity to produce and not just to buy or to get from outside. This was informed by the fact that many times we've relied on rain fed agriculture for a long long time. People wait for the rains, they plant whatever crops and then sometimes rains are not enough, they do not harvest enough. They spend for like one month after the rain they have no food. We started a project that focuses on water fed agriculture. Water fed agriculture in the sense that we are looking at promoting high value crops that can be produced even in absence of rains with the little water that they get. We used approach that we are calling climate smart water capture for agriculture food production. This means that we tried to avail this because water has been a major challenge in Kitui. We designed sand dams which are environmentally friendly and of course soil conserving, etc. 
Once these water levels have come up, we are encouraging people to use water from whatever source is near them to do the vegetable production, to do like production of water melons but not only to rely on rains, but rely on the water fed aspect. The idea is to provide what we call production hubs for vegetable production because in Mwingi specifically the community has been getting most of the vegetables in Thika, Nairobi and other outside interventions. So we are focusing on production but we cannot produce without water. Now we decided we must bring in the aspect of water availability so that people may be able to produce and that is why we identified sand dams as the most appropriate avenues for water production. They are so many seasonal rivers in Kitui. In fact, with the county government had identified around 40 sites for construction of the sand dams in every ward. All the 40 wards of Kitui county and us we are actually supporting the county government with that intervention. We've done several sand dams whereby we are availing water for both domestic and farming. Now with also the aspect of sand dams has really encouraged, has really provided reasonable amount of access to water in the areas that we've installed these. Because it is integrating not only the aspect of crop production but also environmental conservation aspect. So when we are rolling out these interventions we are encouraging the communities, the beneficiaries to do soil conservation and such things with relation to the water aspect. It is like an integrated project where we are conserving environment, having water on board and also agricultural production. As ADRA Kenya we've also implemented before a number of water interventions including several boreholes. 
The need and the type of interventions has always been context driven. The area sometimes you realize that we may have good sighting for the sand dams, but again the issue borehole, they like compliment each other. Some places you realize that they have those for sand dam, we do the sand dam. Some other places they do not do good sighting so we are forced to do things to do with borehole. Other things that we have intendent to do before but we've not been able to do are things like the earth dams and the water pans. In fact we are encouraging the farmers to do on a small scale, the small water tank during the rain so that they can support them. Even if it is one month after the end of the rains, it is not equivalent to none.

Respondent: Well WASH forums have developed a great platform for information sharing and also sharing of experiences. The information that is shared in these forums, inform our planning and future engagements especially when we are doing new projects, new proposals and such things. The information that we normally get from these water forums, inform our interventions. The experience sharing through different challenges, experienced by different partners or actors in this water sector. They inform us to improve on our implementation so that we can ensure sustainability of these water projects that we are running. For the past few one year or so, having participated in these WASH forums, has given ADRA Kenya as an organization, a platform to learn, to share, to become better in terms of designing water related interventions in future. It is from these forums that we have been able to identify different gaps as identified by different stakeholders and it has really strengthened our engagement especially at community levels. Most of the partners or most of the organization that participate in the WASH Forums that we've engaged before, have done one or two different water projects within the county and other places. From the key lessons that they share from their experiences we are able to compile and come up with one hybrid kind of a project that has more impact and assure sustainability in a larger perspective. Of course the forums have been source of networking, which has been very key, we are able to know who is doing what where and through the forum again we've been able to avoid issues to do with duplication, sometimes you realize that if somebody is doing this we do not have like to do the same thing where they are doing it again and such things. Again through forums I think it has also provided an opportunity for the community themselves having been a part and parcel of those that participate in these forums to have their voice heard. We are able to hear what is the story from the side of the community, what challenges have they encountered and how best can we address them. Again through this forums we are able to agree as actors in the water sector together on how best can we address the challenges that are within...because most of the challenges they are so specific context driven challenges that you may get in Kitui county and you may not get in Kwale or any other place. So again it gives us an opportunity to learn from others, not only in Kitui County, or even from outside hoe have they been able to address which comes their way and how best have they been using that knowledge gotten from these forums to design better projects.
