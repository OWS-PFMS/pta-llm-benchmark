
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the Afar Region?

The first challenge to sustainable water service, as we observed from experience, is that there is [no adequate data], because of this, we don’t have enough dug boreholes, and even the existing once may fail for fluoride or excess salt content. These are all attributed to [lack of appropriate research]. The successful boreholes also get flooded and probability of failing is high. I remember different projects on different sites which failed this way. I don’t think there is adequate water sources data within the government, here in Afar region. The contractors and supporting stakeholders don’t have proper construction and hydrological data and they have technical gap. They don’t have the construction information they need.  The contractors should have well organized hydrological data. 
The other problem is linked to contractors: many of them have [skill gaps and lack support]. Also, the materials they use may limit them not to assure the quality. The other thing is that some NGOs depend on their past experiences and use data from other places which won’t fit the context of the area they are working currently. NGOs should use data from relevant governmental institutions. When there is consistent status/data, there will be consistent institutions. Because the institutions aren’t working this way, the bore holes are destroyed by runoff and the community would not use them for long. We have also [community owner ship gaps] challenging sustainability of water services. The conversion rate to fully fledged community ownership is very low relative to other regions. In other areas, I have been before; the community strictly supervises the water works starting from the beginning of the construction. Here, if the construction has minor failure, the whole project would probably fail. The community has no place on the supervision of the water projects. In other regions, but, the community builds fence and establishes committees. This requires much more efforts here in our region. These are the basic problems I know. 
2. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems you have mentioned?

06:35
Government officials should conduct feasibility study. Then they have to share the research findings with other NGOs and support them as per their specific program. The other solution is creating awareness in the community in order to make them feel a sense of ownership. Proper site selection and inspection would help in minimizing runoff and flooding. Supervising, monitoring and technical support to the contractors is the other solution expected from the contracting parties. If you let the contractors uncontrolled, they may ruin the project. Governmental and NGOs, at all levels, should supervise them from the beginning up to the completion. If these solutions are implemented properly, I think the problems could be solved.  The main thing is to create ownership feeling in the community. The other solution is applying consistent water construction design though out projects

What do you think should be done to improve community awareness? How can be approached to the community to feel ownership? First, projects should be based on the community’s need (need based). Starting from site selection, we should consult the community for site selection. Also, there should be a system to aware the community that it’s constructed based on their needs and for their own good. If the community accepts the projects as own, I think that could add value and bring sustainable water service. Establishing and creating awareness on WASH committees; supporting community and kebele leaders; would also help. 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? What would happen if the solution occurs?
Pre assessment and feasibility study is the first one followed by awareness creation of the community. 
4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved?
10:20

This is the primary task of the regional water bureau.  The water bureau is leading the coordination process in a good way at this time, but it has to be more improved. The water bureau has the responsibility of assisting other stakeholders and motivating them to work together. The water coordination office was playing its role in this regard in the past, but stakeholders should also be creative and initiative to improve the current coordination status. 

What could be improved?
11:45
Meetings should be more frequent. All the partners should participate in the meetings to share the lesson they have learnt. The coordination body have monthly meeting and if all partners participate and share their experiences in this meeting, I think the coordination system could be improved. If they don’t discuss on their challenges, it may be a problem for the next time. There might be some organizations here who don’t attend the meetings   and even the meetings may not be consistent. Therefore, making the meetings more continuous and participating all the stake holders would lead to improved coordination.
