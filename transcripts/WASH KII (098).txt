1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability in your town? 
Interviewer: As a town, what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability particularly regarding liquid waste disposal, public latrines, and communal latrines?
Respondent: The problem that we are currently facing as a kebele is buildings are not constructed according to the plan; absence of its own septic tank is one of the main problems. 
Interviewer: Explain it. What do you mean they do not follow the plan?
Respondent: When multi-floor buildings such as restaurants and hotels are built, they lack proper waste disposal facilities which in turn affect the community’s health negatively. Liquid wastes from such establishments have been leaking to the streets and public areas. This could have been prevented by including a septic tank in the design and construction of the buildings. 
Secondly, there is a lack of public latrines. We have around 700 rental kebele houses in which low income members of the community live. Due to the absence of quality latrine, many of these residents defecate and throw garbage in open areas. Latrine sludge is also released to the rain water drainage system, which is common among hotel owners. So, there should be a way to sustainably address these issues.
Thirdly, there is lack of awareness among our community members. We have still the problem of attachment to coexist with garbage. Lack of maintaining personal and environmental hygiene and sanitation by itself is another challenge to be mentioned here. 
Interviewer: What about waste dumping site?
Respondent: In the town, there is no appropriate waste disposal site, particularly solid waste landfill. We conduct community level cleaning campaigns three days a week. However, there is no place to dispose the garbage we collected during these campaigns.  


Interviewer: Is it for the solid or liquid waste?
Respondent: For both the solid and liquid waste. So, people who have the capacity to pay for vacuum trucks will get their latrines pumped. Otherwise, latrines of those who cannot afford to do so and communal latrines overflow. They are required to pay 2000 to 3000 birr for one service, and they cannot afford to pay for that. In relation to this, in the last winter in particular, many garbage trucks had been non-functional. We had managed to organize many volunteer youths and collected huge garbage; but we had had difficulties due to lack of trucks to dispose collected garbage and wastes. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Respondent: in both in the municipality and water utility offices, there are construction monitoring and supervision experts in our town. So, we need to use this expertise to conduct strong supervision when buildings are constructed starting from the very beginning. Many buildings in our town lack proper sewer lines and septic tank due to poor supervision and monitoring system. Therefore, consultants and construction supervisors should be able to make sure that buildings match the future growth plan of the town. 
Secondly, in collaboration with other partners, additional communal latrines should be built. Particularly, in densely populated areas, the town administration and donors should plan together to build communal latrines. 
For long-term solution, though, we should work on creating continuous awareness among youth, women, investors, residents in general. It is crucial to help them understand the detrimental effects of waste and improve their life style regarding hygiene and sanitation. Health extension workers in our kebele should play key role in this aspect. They should work to effectively implement the packages. 
Interviewer: What are these packages?
Respondent: There is a health extension package which includes progressive implementation of seventeen programs. The package includes hygiene sanitation and environmental health. So, the health extension workers should teach the community effectively. 
The last solution is legal measures. People who are not willing to apply procedures being implemented by the health workers should be punished. 
If hotel owners and households do not get their latrines pumped out when it is full, they should be penalized too. We have tried to take such measures in collaboration with the health workers. 
Interviewer: You have said that there are problems in public latrines in terms of quality and quantity. What should be the solution for this? 
Respondent: There are some bureaucracy issues in the municipality to acquire land and to construct public latrines. We selected and requested some locations but they rejected to provide the site with the justification that it is not aligned with the town’s plan. 
Interviewer: What about the issue of dumping site?  What would be the solution for this problem?
Respondent: Waste disposal site should be solved at the town level since many farmers have been affected negatively when urban dwellers are dumping waste on farmlands. Thus, the issue of disposal site should be resolved by the town administration. 
Interviewer: Isn’t it beyond capacity for the town to build a disposal site? I have heard that the project requires around two hundred million birr. Is this affordable?
Respondent: I think it would be great if they respond to it. The main point is we should address the issues that are within reach and capable. Due attention should be paid for this issue like the investment sector. 
Secondly, there are many youths who are willing to engage in the waste management industry as entrepreneurs. Therefore, we should be able to set up new enterprises. What they are asking for is land. As the community needs low-cost waste collection system, they need licensed piece of land. Our kebele has already issued them a letter of support and we will continue to support them as much as we can. If they obtain a positive response, it will be an employment opportunity for the use while solving the waste disposal problem in the community. 
Interviewer: You have mentioned that there are problems regarding the vacuum and garbage trucks. The trucks do not come on time and people should wait for a long time to get the service. What do you think is the solution for this?
Respondent: We don’t have them at kebele level since they are managed by the town administration. This is a common challenge across all kebeles, including ours. I think the town administration council has decided to purchase additional trucks. If that happens it would be helpful. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: The first practical solution should be creating awareness among our community members. 
Interviewer: What would happen if awareness is created?
Respondent: In Debrebirhan town, there are households that do not dispose their garbage even when the trucks are available. This is due to lack of awareness. This change can be materialized through the efforts of the management including us. The administration should work to improve the community’s lifestyle, hygiene, and sanitation status.
Secondly, problems related to input issues should be solved. When I say input, it is to mean things like trucks, disposal sites, and low-cost waste collection system, including establishment of enterprises. If we have all these at hand, we will be able to solve many problems. 
Actually, raising awareness would solve many more problems.  Many dump truck drivers who spend the nights in the town dump everything they collected on the streets. This is due to lack of awareness. These are some of the bad experiences discouraging the enterprises who are engaged cleaning the streets.
