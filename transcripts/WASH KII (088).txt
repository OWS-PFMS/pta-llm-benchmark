1)In your opinion, what are the main problems of sanitation service sustainability in your town? 
Interviewer: What I would be asking you is about public latrines, communal latrines, and waste disposal sites and problems have you observed that hinder the sustainability of sanitation services in your town?
Respondent: Problems that hold back town residents and believer from enjoying clean and beautiful town and carry out their church services is lack of public latrines. There are many homeless people around the church but we do not have enough latrines to serve these people. When people come to the church for Sunday service or a festive occasion, they do not get latrines nearby. Besides, many believers come from different part of the country to worship annual religious celebrations; however, we do not have latrines. We have a couple of latrines but we have been having difficulty to keep it clean. This is because we do not have the capacity and the location is not convenient. We have also been using they very poorly in a sense that it so filthy and difficult to use. 
Interviewer: Why is that? What is the problem?
Respondent: The public latrines are built for the local community too. So, the church could not take care of the latrine by itself. Also, we cannot prohibit other members of the community from using the latrine. 
On top of that, there is a market place near the church every Sunday. They just leave all the paper, plastic, and cardboard waste here in the market. The town municipality doesn’t follow up such things. There are piles of wastes in the marketplace near the church. 
Interviewer: What about dumping site? Is there any problem regarding that?
Respondent: There is no waste disposal site in our area there is a problem. There might be a landfill at a town level. But, the municipality does not pick the garbage we have. So, we do not have any other option other than burning even the waste we generate within our church.
Interviewer: Are you the one who pay for the vacuum truck to pump the latrines? 
Respondent: Yes, we are the one who pay for the vacuum trucks. There are local residents near one of the public latrines. We use the latrine with the residents together. But we are the one who pay for the vacuum trucks to empty it out if it is full.  
Interviewer: Do you pay for that?
Respondent: Yes. We do pay for that. The issue has been a bit difficulty and we are currently discussing about it in our church as how we should proceed on the matter.
Interviewer: You have not got any solution yet?
Respondent: We are trying to solve the issue with what we have. We are planning to hire a janitor who takes care of the latrines. 
Interviewer: Haven’t recruited yet?
Respondent: Not yet. We have one janitor who cleans up the temple and priest’s clothing.  However, because it is beyond capacity, we are planning to recruit additional cleaning worker. 
Interviewer: You have told me the problem regarding your latrines. What about as a town? There are many public latrines in the town. There is liquid waste from hotels. What are the problems related to these issues?
Respondent: From my observation people dump liquid wastes randomly everywhere. They do not use sewerage lines. I have seen this among residents and hotels too. We have found this risky for our health when we move around the city. 
Interviewer: What do you think is the source of that problem? Is it the problem of the leadership, the community? 
Respondent: It is not only the problem of leadership. It has also something to do with community awareness too.  Residents should have owned a latrine and garbage collection mechanism. But, instead of using these mechanisms people dump waste randomly everywhere which may be recklessness. The administration also lacks monitoring and follow-up. 
2)What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Let me go back to the first point you have mentioned. I have seen the latrines last year. They are limited in number. Even the currently existing ones are not being utilized properly. As a solution what do you think should happen?  
Respondent: The latrine has been serving the whole local community even if the church runs it. Not only church servants but also people come to the church from different places to the church use the latrine. Therefore, government and NGOs should pay attention to improve the latrines in terms of having water for washing hands and tank. This is not the responsibility of the government and NGOs not only of the church. 
Interviewer: So, not only the church, the entire community and NGOs should support, right? 
Respondent: Yes, it should be; because, there are few servants in the church, but the latrines serve the whole community around the church. People are practicing open defecation since the latrine dirty and difficult to use. This has caused many to experience different communicable diseases. So, both the government and NGOs should not neglect the issue, in Debrebirhan Sillasie in particular. 
Interviewer: Here, there are beggars. They use your latrines. Have you ever given any training or awareness creation sessions?
Respondent: I am appointed recently. I do not have the information what had been done in the past. There will be such things in the future. It is also appropriate to aware them that they should use the latrines properly. However, this only does not seem to be enough. We should create a conducive environment for awareness creation. 
3)Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: Lastly, you have mentioned many solutions but out of the solutions you have mentioned, which is the most important and should be implemented first? What would happen if the solution is implemented?
Respondent: Do you mean in the town? 
Interviewer: Yes. 
Respondent: Do you mean regarding the sanitation?
Interviewer: Yes
Respondent: Priority should be given to build of public and communal latrines in many places; because there is huge shortage in the town. I have seen many modern public latrines in Addis Ababa that have entertainment centers where you can read newspaper. Here, however, let alone that it is difficult to use the latrines. 
Interviewer: So, you are saying it is vital to work on creating awareness. 
Respondent: Yes. It is crucial to work on awareness creation. Besides, government and NGOs should build enough public latrines in selected places. This is what I would say should be given priority. 
Secondly, Garbage such as plastic and PET bottles are thrown all over the place in our town, so, there should be solid waste collection sites in the town. Whenever it is full, the garbage should be dumped to the sanitary landfill.  
Interviewer: Let me take you back to the main solution. If massive action is taken to create awareness, and people’s attitude is changed what changes would happen? And as you have said if entertainment centers are built alongside the public latrines like those in Addis Ababa, what changes would be made?
Respondent: If the community is taught about sanitation and hygiene and awareness is created, there will be huge changes in the town. 
Interviewer: For example what kind of changes? 
Respondent: First, the town will be beautiful and attractive city. Secondly, there will be a healthy community. Open defecation will be a shabby thing among the community.  
Interviewer: Is not there any penalty for people who practice open defecation?  
Respondent: No. penalizing such practices is not customary.  
Interviewer: That is the problem.  
Respondent: You might see banner posted in some places to prohibit open defecation. But people do not obey, and no one can punish them.
