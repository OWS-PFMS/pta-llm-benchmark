
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water service sustainability in the Afar Region?
We can have several challenges to water service sustainability in the region. The first main problem is natural disaster affecting the volume and availability of the water service. That is [drought and flood]. When we say drought, it denotes shortage of water in almost all water schemes, when the water in reservoir gets dry. Also, we can’t have reserve water without rainfall which means lack of rainfall may lead to lack of water in the reservoir. The first problem is caused by absence of expected rain and recurrent drought. The second one is [overflowing flood from highlands]; this can cause destruction of existing water schemes, defile water reservoirs, and the water to be contaminated. The overall effect of this action is water shortage. This will be challenging for water supplier institutions. The third problem is [lack of appropriate research on underground water resources]. Some organizations working in the water services dig boreholes without conducting relevant research and this is leading to failure in the started water schemes. The big failure for these kinds of organization is working with secondary consultants instead of focusing on direct beneficiary institutions like the regional water bureau. They just go on their own route without crosschecking the realities from water bureau. This will cause massive failure on the existing project and sustainability problem in the water service. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems you have mentioned?
03:53

Well prepared research and adequate plan would solve many problems. Absence of consolidated and organized plan within agencies, governmental and NGOs would result overlapping of water projects which are happening most of the time. We see that similar projects are being done in similar places. There is duplication of projects. Water trucking are done temporarily just for emergency cases like drought and not in well planned and organized way. There should be attention towards sustainable water supply system.
The second solution is that organizations operating in water sector should focus on own capacity building. Disorganized/loosely established institution can’t help the community unless it’s well organized. Wash committee have to be established, supported and refreshed with trainings which is very important. The other one is managers and leaders working on water service projects themselves should get capacity building trainings. Many of them may have the knowledge but lack capacity and technical insightfulness since our major goal is to bring sustainable water system in the region.  
Bore holes should be dug in advance and water have to be delivered for all. If we are concerned of emergencies (like water trucking) all the times, the community would get no sustainable water service. There are some woredas like Kueri and Ebidar (very serious water problems) which don’t have water services accessibility totally. This is because stakeholders usually prefer nearby woredas (like Dupity and Mille) and forget the woredas located far away from the center. Though the two woredas are very large in areas and have more than 17 kebeles, they use water trucking methods to access water.  
What is water trucking?
Water trucking is collecting water through vehicles which is very costly business. Vehicle rent is expensive and the cost could finance other long lasting and sustainable water service methods 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? What would happen if the solution occurs?

08:30 
Sustainable water supply through effective coordination is the first solution to me.
How could that be a solution?
Good beginnings are being implemented here by UNICEF and other NGOs. For example, bore holes are dug here and there but there is a coordination gap in the delivery, durability and handover system of water service to the community. There is lack of coordination. When some organization digs, the other could work on expansion or tanker installation, this way, time and resource could be saved. In our area bore holes are dug but there is a gap in sustaining the water service. What should get the priority is sustainable water service system. Durability problem is particularly worse in towns: for example, Awash town have no sustained water supply system. 

4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved?
10:45
As to my personal opinion, I think the coordination we have now is not successful. It’s because NGOs working in the water sector do have coordination problems. They are not eager to work with and consult the regional water bureau. They select places where they need to construct water points before properly consulting the region’s water bureau or they came to discuss to the bureau after they have identified the places that they are planning to work on. Also, they are not willing to attend meetings and send the monthly report even if they are invited for the meeting. There is WASH coordination meeting each month on Tuesday and every organization are cordially invited to attend it. However, only few NGOs come and participate usually. Even all the governmental stakeholders don’t participate in the meeting. In fact, water projects are being constructed but if there is no coordination it couldn’t be that much successful. Different NGOs should work in different area instead of concentrating in the area. There is almost no coordination related to projects that can benefit the community to sustainable water service. 
On our side, we are participating in the coordination meeting each month. We send minute for the meeting each month where action points of the minute would be revised and we try to discuss each stakeholders plan for the upcoming month. However, till now, report from the past month’s performance and plans for the next year are not collected and presented even though there were several reminders. Therefore, we can say there is no strong coordination even if there is a platform for that as a principle accepted by every WASH stakeholder. There are also other coordination meetings like Wash Task Force meeting, Flood Task Force Meeting and Emergency Task Force meetings. But still the participation and resource mobilization should get improved in all cases.
5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?

13:50

Coordination is very vital in solving all problems. The first thing is that coordination is power and it should be ready. It helps in avoiding resource overlaps. Secondly, it helps in identifying problems. The third one is that it helps to advocate when there are emergencies. Advocacy will help allocate resources properly and respond timely. Therefore, coordination is very important for resource mobilization, response and for sustainable water services. Coordination is important for each and everything that goes along with WASH services.
