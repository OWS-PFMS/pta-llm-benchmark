
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: Well, one is that pooling of resources. The resources each organization has. Bring it to develop the water services. The water and sanitation services. I think that is very important. Yeah. And also information sharing. Various organizations have different perspectives. They have different sources of information and knowledge. 

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The main challenge is the management of those projects. Sustainability has been a problem, and is attributed largely to management. 

Scarcity of resources is also a problem. Yeah.

Interviewer:  Water resources or?

Interviewee: There is water resources and also monetary resources. You know, they do not have a lot of money in other words, economic status is not very good.  So even if they wanted for example to sell water to a level where they are able to break even, maybe they wouldn’t be able to charge those rates that would make them able to break even.

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: Well, the solution is about to rethink the management model of the schemes and to think in terms of the kind of investment that should go into developing those resources and make them sustainable. The kind of investment that has impact, than piecemeal kind of allocation of resources.

Interviewer: Any other recommendation?

Interviewee: The other recommendation is sensitization and education. We need to every player in that field needs to be sensitized and given proper information, educated, so that the can be able to run those projects sustainably.

Interviewer: Can you explain a bit more about rethinking the management model? What would be a better management approach?

Interviewee: Well, you see, so far those projects have been developed by various players and usually they are handed over to the communities, but over time we have seen projects failing using that model. Probably if we could think of, for example, we have the water service providers like us. If you build the capacity of water service providers and they manage those projects, they could have, for example, scheme managers based in those areas, one scheme manager can be able to monitor a number of water schemes but then there is a central control point. I think that model might prove to be better than the model we are using currently. 

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: The issue of management. Yeah. Coming up with a workable management model.

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: First and foremost, those schemes would be brought to the mainstream management of the water sector. So the issue of regulation, everything will apply. The rules and regulations governing the formal water service providers will apply and therefore there would be a system which can be monitored and controlled.
