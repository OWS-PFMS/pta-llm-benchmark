
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The aspect that is working very well is the regular meetings where we meet and share experiences, share common problems and suggested way forward. That is working very well and all the actors when they come there they are very free, they give free opinion, the arrangement, although time is always a constraint allows people to express themselves and have a common goal. The quarterly WASH Forums. Those meetings are in fact very key eye openers for most of us.

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee: The main problems are that, one, there is lack of coordination. You find that there are many actors more or less doing the same thing but not in a coordinated manner. So, lack of coordination is a major issue. Then there is also the issue of some of the actors wish to intervene in some areas, but they do not have the data. So, although data in the recent past has been availed, but it could always be packaged better so that those who would want to intervene to make the schemes more sustainable are more informed. Then thirdly, there is the issue of ownership of the projects. Some of the projects which have been initiated are failing because the communities are not owning them up well and taking responsibility for those projects. So those are some of the areas that need to be addressed. 

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee: The solution first of all is to be very open-that all stakeholders need to inform their colleagues what they are doing, because this is not a competition, this is an area where we are collaborating each other’s’ efforts, so all organizations need to be very open on what they are doing, which interventions they are all involved in, and how, when there are gaps, how somebody else can come in and fill in those gaps- whether it is training, whether it is consultancy, whether it is funding, that is very key. Another area that can be addressed is this issue of the schemes. When you have management committees who are not well trained, it defeats the purpose of simply giving them a project. Sometimes a project is worth a lot but they have not been trained, they are simply given a project and it is handed over to them. So, the training should be done very well before handing over the project. Then there is need for monitoring and evaluation and follow up. This should be properly done. And you find that some of the… if we call them the…donor communities for example. They come and do a project but their project cycle ends so they are no longer on the ground. They need to handover the project to somebody else who is on the ground to make sure that there is proper monitoring and follow up and also repair and servicing of the plants, so that the project can be sustainable for long.

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee: I think for me, the most important is the one of empowering the communities; that first of all, there is need for that project and then they are trained on why they need it, how it will work and how it will solve their problems and after the project is completed, how to sustain it such that even if whoever constructed the project or who handed it over to them is not available, they can continue sustaining it. So, that to me is key, because, having traversed a big area of Kitui County, I have noticed that the number of projects are many. They are very many. The problem is that the ownership is lacking and the training…even when they own it, the know-how is lacking, so I believe that that is the most important.

Interviewer:  If the solution happens, what would occur next?

Interviewee: It will be…for example if you have a solar energy project, for example the one that is at Kabati, we have a community which understands why that project is there, because they can appreciate what they had before the project was in place; the problems they had before the project was in place. Then they understand that this is a project that is supposed to benefit the whole community. They will form the management committees. The management committees will have to be answerable to the members, which means they are accountable, they are able to explain what they do and why they do it, they have regular meetings, they have elections, the land on which the plant is put is duly recognized that it belongs to that scheme and not an individual so that later on there are no wrangles because of land ownership issues, and when there are breakdowns, it means that they have collected some money, they are able to undertake those repairs without even involving an external person. That way, then sustainability will be achieved and then the objective will also be achieved.
