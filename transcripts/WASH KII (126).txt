1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent 1: The existing water facilities of the town were built 20 years ago and their supply capacity cannot meet the current needs of the community. That is the water supply and demand are not balanced. The water supply lines are also very old and they are limited in some parts of the town. To alleviate the water supply shortage NGOs have developed boreholes in the town but the woreda administration cannot purchase water pumps and supply the water to the community due to budget constraint. About three boreholes left ideal (do not provide service) due to capital budget constraint. I think if these three boreholes become operational, the water supply shortage can be solved at least partly.
Respondent 2: I completely agree with what is mentioned by my colleague (Adam) about water supply shortage in the town. What I want to say is there is also serious sanitation service problem in the town. That is there are no drainage and sewerage lines or system in the town. The community simply dispose its liquid wastes in any open areas. This resulted in various public health problems.
Interviewer: Are there any other problems related to sanitation or water service in the town? 
Respondent 1: There is also serious water supply shortage in various rural kebeles of the woreda because of this the community cannot settle in one area. They migrate to other places in search of water for their consumption as well as for their livestock.
  
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
Interviewer: You mentioned that there is a serious water supply shortage both in urban and rural areas of the woreda. What do you recommend about solution to this problem?
Respondent 1: As water is life for any creature both the government and non-governmental institutions should provide remarkable emphasis for the development of water schemes both in urban and rural areas of the woreda. Constructing schools without water supply for students, is meaningless. Because both students and their parents needs water prior to any other community development interventions.
Thus, the government should allocate sufficient capital budget for the development of water facilities. NGOs should also give priority for water projects, as water supply is a serious challenge in our area.
Interviewer: You also indicated that there are no drainage and sewerage lines and/or system in the town and the waste management system of the town is very poor. What do you recommend to alleviate this problem?
Respondent 2: Developing appropriate sewerage system requires huge budget and it is a long-term plan for the town administration. However, as a short-term solution we should enhance the awareness of our community on waste management within their locality. To this end, the woreda health office in collaboration with other stakeholders should mobilize the community to clean their environment and minimize public health problems that will be caused by sanitation problems.
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent 1: We prioritize the solution to water supply shortage, as water is life for any creature. Thus, we need immediate help from the government as well as NGOs to find solution to this serious problem. 
If we can get sustainable and sufficient water supply, we can lead health life. That is the community can keep its personal hygiene, it can plant trees within its compound and mitigate the climate change problems.
Particularly the pastoral community can settle in one area if there is access to clean water both for his consumption and for the livestock.
