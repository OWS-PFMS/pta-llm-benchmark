1. In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to sanitation/water service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Shortage of potable water access: - There is a problem related to access among the different kebeles existing in the woreda. There is also a huge difference between the overstated percentage of water coverage report and the actual water access coverage in our woreda which is not more than 33% indicating how savior is the water supply problem
Awareness gap on water resource usage: - There is also lack of awareness on how to keep the existing water resource clean and how to use it properly by the majority of the community members. Instead of managing and maintaining water resource infrastructures by themselves, they refer to and complain every tiny problem to government officials. Due to this temptation, the community couldn’t fix failures happening in the different water infrastructures on time. This is affecting the sustainability of the service in the woreda. There is also awareness gap on how to use toilets, how and where to put water to wash hands/use it after toilet. 
In this regard, as we were trying to create an ODF woreda, we have done a lot on awareness creation and build community latrines everywhere. As a result of this, there are few ODF kebeles which received awards from the woreda and they have become models for others. This work is done mainly with the help of rural health extension workers. To speak it generally, there is a gap in sanitation and hygiene awareness, practice and usage of toilets both in the rural and urban areas especially by the hotels and restaurants.
Rehabilitation and maintenance problem: - This can also be caused by and related with shortage of budget. Absence of adequately trained experts that could at least be responsible for emergency cases to the problem is another issue. This, added with lack of monitoring systems from the woreda, is resulting in continuous damage of water pipes and other infrastructures. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 
Since there is no adequate water access in the woreda due to limited capital budget, we can’t talk about its sustainability. Accordingly, it requires more attention to the problem and additional budget from woreda administration. This also needs collaborative actions from private stakeholders, NGOs and other development projects running in the woreda such as the Agricultural Growth Program (AGP) and Sustainable Land Management (SLM)

 In order to minimize the awareness gap, it requires continuous capacity building and demonstrations on how to construct individual toilets and use a hand wash services after toilet. Health extension workers have a good opportunity to build a closer work relation with kebele administrators and the community in creating ODF kebeles
To solve the maintenance issue, we need to create unions and private actors who can fix problems happening in water infrastructures. Also, we need to provide technical trainings to the community itself so that they can manage the service themselves and build a sense of ownership. Gradually, the community members would start collecting income from the service itself and they can fix problems.  
Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Awareness creation programs take the first priority than other solutions.  This will have immediate and positive impacts on the life of the community and the service itself: for example, they can easily prevent communicable diseases, keeping their environment clean and protected.
