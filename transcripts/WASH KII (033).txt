Respondent:  Water service, we are talking about the service we give to people of Kitui in terms of water, I hope it also incorporates the sanitation part of it or it’s just water?
Interviewer: Yes it is both.
Respondent: Both?
Interviewer: Both yes.
Respondent: One, I’ll start with sanitation because if you look at Kitui County, we have the sewer, which was constructed but was not operational until can say one and half years ago when World Bank came in and they looked at the low income areas and they developed the sewerage system. And at the moment we have like 200 connections for sewer but is meant to be more than 3000 but it will be operational soon because we have the last mile connectivity which is coming through the funding from African Development Bank, to come and make sure that all the connections in Kitui  sewer is completed. That is on the sewer side. 
Interviewer: What about sewer, how is it a challenge to water services sustainability? 
Respondent: Of course now you know sewer works together with water. When I will talk about water you’ll see where they connect.  For water actually we have covered around 30-40% within our service provision area. With a population of almost 800,000 people, we cover only 35%. The challenge is because one, the main source of water for Kitui is in Machakos, Masinga Dam.  And from Masinga Dam to Kitui alone, is almost a 100 kilometers. The challenge is that the water is not in Kitui County but in Machakos. And the pipeline passes through the rural area. The challenges actually we normally experience are water theft, there are burst along the pipeline, and again the major challenge is we normally pump from the dam to a head of 270 meters.
We must use power, electricity power to pump water so that it can now flow by gravity to Kitui. There is a cost element there which is around 10 million per month. And since the company cannot break even when it is paying that operational cost alone of power, we are tempted to go back to the County Government to get a subsidy. You see? So is a challenge also in terms of pumping water up the Hill and then  flows here, passing through the rural area, there is water theft, there are bursts, there are leakages. The terrain again, some part towards Kitui, the terrain is not a good and because the pressure we end up having so many burst along the line.  Again, you know to have adequate and clean water you need to treat it. So the element of chemicals, we spend almost 800,000 actually, that is per month.
Interviewer: For water treatment?
Respondent: Yeah. Treatment, because we have to put the alum, we call it aluminium sulfate, we also put chlorine, and depending on the water, we also put soda ash. So that element of chemicals again. And then, we have also to monitor this water. When you release water for 100 kilometers away, you need transport to patrol the line. So there’s also the element of also cost of monitoring that we need fuel, we need motorcycles.
Interviewer: Okay. If I got you right, you mentioned about water treatment that is part of your roles as KITWASCO?
 Respondent: Yes.  Basically actually to understand our role, after the infrastructure is developed the relationship between us and the County and also the National Government is that Water Act 2016 moved us from the National Government, the Tanathi to the County Government. And before, the infrastructure used to be developed by the Tanathi who is a water board under the Ministry of Water National Government, but Water Act of 2016 transferred us from the National Government, Tanathi to Ministry of Water at the County level. So any infrastructure that is developed, the work of any water service provider is to do the distribution. Distribution means you pump water, you treat, and you sell that water and also you make sure that you sell at the rate that is given by the WASREB.  WASREB is the regulatory body. So you find we are semi-autonomous there. We work both in the County Government and regulated by the WASREB.

Pumping raw water, treating the water, it becomes clean water, we pump into the hill I was telling you about 270 meters and we also patrol the line where the water is passing all the way to Kitui and we bill customers for that water.

Respondent: One, let me tell you where we're coming from. As you pump this water, with all this cost you are losing a lot of water because of the water theft and bursts. And also in terms of the sewer, you find people want to use sewer but it was not operational. That is operating well it is flowing by gravity. So I’ll start with water and then I go to sewer.  Water theft, what we are doing I've come up with a strong team. We used to have a non-revenue water team. We call it unaccounted for water; we call it non-revenue water team where you bring in staff with good integrity. They come to you, they want to disconnect you they cannot be compromised. So I've come with a very strong team, to disconnect people including actually I’ve put a policeman there. So that if they want to come and arrest you because you stealing water, you cannot resist. That one major thing I’ve done.
I've also improved on buying the motorbikes. Over 20 motorbikes will be used to monitor, will be used for meter reading and for the emergency repairs. If it's a major burst, we run there. Where our vehicles cannot access, we have motorbikes to access there. But the motorbikes are not enough as I said so we need if we can be boosted in terms of the finance to buy more. When it comes to the sewer, the intervention that has come in and we are lucky actually because with this something called last mile connectivity. There is water line two. We have two lines running from Masinga to Kitui. One has offtakes where we give water from Masinga as we come to Kitui. There is another dedicated to Kitui. It was done five years ago, by Sino-Hydro the guys who were doing the road. And they are coming now; we call it last mile connectivity where they are coming to complete on the sewer. They laid the trunk mains they never connected to people. The people are connecting the sewer running here is because of the World Bank, but the last mile connectivity, which is starting any time from next week, it’s coming to connect over 2,500 connections free.
