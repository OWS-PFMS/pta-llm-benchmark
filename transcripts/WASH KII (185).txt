1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation/Water service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability? 
With regard to water supply service, Jinka University is a one service unit.  According to the information provided by Zonal Minerals and Energy office, rate of water service coverage is about 28% though this is a bit older data. The first problem associated with water, sanitation and hygiene is lack of access to pure water.  Utilization methods are poor in purifying springs and outreach the service   to the public.   About 70 % of people in the community have no access to clean water supply as per service standard. Different government and partner organizations promote supply of water service to the community. To help sustainability of WASH, various institutions are engaged in different water work operations such as drilling well activities and potable water supply. These partner organizations usually operate in pastoralist areas and several other attempts have been made by the government to make different institutions outreach the service to the people.  
In spite of all those operations, however, currently available clean water supply could not fulfill the demand for the increasing population. Various institutions are also established in order to meet the Millennium Development goal and sustainability. There is also a gap from the beneficiary side; they don’t perceive the existing water schemes as their own since they are constructed by the donors. Similarly, it is also questionable whether partner organizations and government are really committed to grant the completed projects to the public with the appropriate title and sense of ownership. The other issue is problem with maintenance support by the water and sanitation office since the water scheme demands regular inspection. 
There is also shortage of vehicles to travel to most of the rural kebeles.  Those kebeles located far from the town are not accessible by vehicles except through motor bikes. Low quality standard in the projects being developed is another major challenge. There is also delay of tasks being performed in the fields as the machineries breakdown in the long run due to lack of timely repair. 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

Government needs to give more weight to the issue of water and sanitation services. South Omo zone, accounting 23% land coverage in the SNNP region, is the largest of all the zones in the area but one of the zones with the lowest water coverage. Majority of the community in our zone are pastoralists and they are lowland areas which makes water access more difficult than those of the highlanders. So, this requires special attention to and more budget from the government and other partners working in WASH sector. 
The regional office should consider the climatic and environmental problems of this zone while allocating capital budget annually. It is not as simple as we speak out here. For instance, if you allocate some 5 million birr for a water scheme in this zone, you can’t develop other than a medium hand dug water well; but you can do greater than five boreholes if you use the same budget in the highland areas.  Most importantly, we should be able to produce adequately trained and technically fit human power in the sector. 

Trainings that focus on awareness creation should be provided to the community and students in schools regularly so that everyone can be aware of the effects of poor sanitation. It helps a lot if people learn about preparing latrine and get familiar to washing hand with soup and clean water. We observe lots of changes in towns, but that is not sufficient. The budget that is being allocated should also be sufficiently utilized for WASH purposes. The majority of our budget goes to salaries and other emergency expenses. It is still at the hands of the local and regional governments to properly allocate budgets and oversee its fruits. 

3. Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

The most important solution we need is to hire skilled technical manpower since we need them for timely maintenance and repair. We have only one expert in our zone that is accountable for any failure across all districts. With this resource, we can’t go far beyond the level we have now. 

The second most important solution should be extensive awareness creation programs to the community. Plumbing accessories are very expensive. One GI pipe with 2 and half inch is 200 birr, so the community needs to use water infrastructures wisely. They should also consider these infrastructures as their own property. Sense of ownership is necessary. The money we collect from the water service is not sufficient to cover the cost of maintenance.The question of water is equivalent to the question of basic human rights. Water service needs focus and attention by the government.  Almost 40% of the water schemes constructed so far are not well functioning because of lack of awareness and sense of ownership. Millions of birr were spent on those water schemes during construction. 

IRC is the only NGO operating on rehabilitation works in this zone. We are really grateful to their wonderful rehabilitation work that has been accomplished so far.  There are still a couple of water schemes needs attention from the government and other partners for rehabilitation process. Through coordinated actions involving both the local government and NGOs operating in the area, we need to have controlled water borne disease and a community with improved latrine and hand washing services. For this to be effective, waste collection sites and sanitary materials should be readily available.
