When we see the Afar region, the first series issue is water shortage. The recognized and prioritized problem of the community is water service. We know that it is series issue in the woredas we work in. The water service is less probable in the woredas we work. For example, when we do feasibility assessment for underground water availability, it has failed 5-6 times because of lack of accurate research. Do you mean lack of appropriate research? Yes it’s   because without doing [appropriate research] water service projects sustainability is half probable (50:50) most of the time. We have discussed this issue with the stake holders and we are informed that UNICEF, jointly with our donors, is planning to do feasibility study on 90% of the woredas, using new and latest technology, in order to identify potential water sources. It may start on the coming October and we think that it will minimize the problems we have now regarding appropriate water sources. Thus, there is [gap between the research and the real situation on the ground]. We released considerable cost in different times and at different places but there are situations that we couldn’t get water. We were in difference with the regional court for we had 6 deep water excavations but some of them have already failed. What other challenges are questioning the sustainability of water?
The second challenge is the recurrent [drought]. When there is recurrent drought, for instance, for up to 2 years in some woredas, the quantity of the underground water would decrease or get dry. This is the second problem. Do you mean that low volume of underground water because of drought? Yes, the drought will minimize the quantity of underground water and it will decrease the availability and distribution of the water in that specific area.
The other problem is that there are a number of water projects in the woredas we are working in but not effective and efficient. Frankly speaking, in the woredas like Adeab, Dewe and Telalak water is a very series issue, but both the number and quality of projects are below expected. I think there should be a project to develop big infrastructure construction. [The service is not adequate and the project work is not well coordinated]. It seems that individual engagements are more frequent in water works than coordinated actions. These are the problems that I know and there may be some others.

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to the problems you have mentioned?

As I told you, the first solution to the problems I have mentioned is, identifying improved, cost effective, modern and resource efficient research method. The UNICEF’s new approach, in collaboration with EU, will minimize the problem up to 90%. The second solution is the coordination between governmental and nongovernmental stake holders. The third solution is to have a more organized and well documented data system. Water potential areas and distribution schemes should be well documented. For example, we should get a compiled document that shows the woredas water distribution and potential sources. This will avoid the hassle that someone could have when working on water service projects. We so many water projects since water is equally important to the oxygen we breathe. We should overcome the physical foldaway of up to 80 to 100 kms, in some areas of the region that the people are travelling to get water. If all stake holders give priority to this problem and are well-coordinated, I think, we can overcome the problem gradually.

Among the problems I have mentioned, we have faced the absence of [feasibility study] several times. It helps us in identifying potential areas. I think feasibility study should come first as immediate solution. As to me, the second solution would be having organized and well compiled documentation system in woredas.
The third important solution is strong coordination between government, NGOs and other private partners and their positive attention to the sector. This would potentially solve the problem. 
What next steps would happen if the solution occurs? 
The society’s living system is entirely based on water services. If service is available for long, the community will start a sedentary life followed by other concomitant developments like schools, health posts etc. This would create a community satisfied with the basic needs and better living conditions than it has now. 




4.In your opinion, how effective is coordination in your woreda for sustainable water service? What could be improved?
Our association, lay volunteers’ international, is lead agency for the region’s WASH cluster. The association has started coordinated actions with the water office and all other stake holders in the sector. We have monthly meeting in this region to discuss, evaluate and update our performances. However, we could have done better than we have now. When we are thinking about coordination, it would be very good to be done jointly with other associations. For instance, assume that Save the Children or any other partners are collaborating in woredas we are working in, for projects like deep water excavations and if they are renting machinery for this activity, it would have been very good to share the task both from the resource and cost effectiveness perspectives. There was no such a strong trend so far. But we are discussing to make some changes. We had review meetings on a project called ‘RESET II’. It’s resilience building project in Ethiopia, funded by European Union. In this meeting, particularly with consortium members, we are working to bring about joint coordination efforts for projects that need common mission. In the past, the level of coordination that NGOs had with the government stalk holders was a bit poorer. From the government side, NGOs are being threatened and considered like they work just for their own benefit. But now, when we have some activity, for example, when we work on feasibility study in specific woreda, we initiate officials from all levels starting from the region and even at community level, including old aged people. 
So, what I am suggesting is that there is some sort of coordination now but that has to be improved. For example, we have WASH cluster and emergency prevention meetings every week here in Semera (in response to the current flooding) and with the community and water department office, every month. This has to be continued in a more coordinated way to bring about significant water service changes in the region.

5.In your opinion, can you walk me through how do you see improved coordination leading to more sustainable water service in the zone. For example, how could it solve some of the problems you mentioned before?

When there is improved coordination, there will be well organized information system and effective communication among stalk holders. For example, we may have strong experience in WASH issues but other may not. The other point is from resource allocation perspective, say for example, with coordinated effort we could have adequate machine that can dig as many bore holes as we need and that makes us cost effective. With coordination and information exchange, we would have better performance and develop positive attitude enabling everyone to be transparent and optimistic about others.
Coordination helps to make reasonable communication and experience sharing because problem identification is half a solution for a problem. We have common problems, (Save the Children, our organization and others) that are not practically possible to solve individually. It’s because of the beginning of coordinated actions that UNICEF (and EU on its side) is planning to start feasibility study, which is a result of the good coordination we have now.
