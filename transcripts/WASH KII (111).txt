
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to Sanitation/water service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
1.1The first challenging matter with regard to the sanitation problem in Debre Berhan town is closely associated with the growing urbanization process. Now, Debre berhan is quite different from what it looked like three years back. Its population size has dramatically increased; and following this, sanitation problem has also increased highly. 

1.2The second one is lack of adequate drinking water.  The town has completed its ten-year development plan within three years time. Due to lack of potable water as compared to high population density, the public is getting water based on shift program. In fact, the shortage is not only due to the increment of the population but also because of the expansion of the increasing number factories consuming this same source of water.  Well,   you can’t expand factories without using water, but the problem arises when the water scheme is limited. There is only one water point in the city.

1.3Expansion of factories: The factories are being built where these water schemes are located. Areas of water schemes should not be aligned with such factories. Since these factories started function using underground water, there comes depletion of water from time to time. If this continuous at the stage it is growing now, there will be severe shortage of water in the future both for the population and all other services that need water. The worst thing of these factories is their pollution. The liquid sewage of these factories is polluting both the public’s tap-water and the rivers flowing around the town. The other cause of pollution in town is the sewerage coming out of the residential houses carrying liquid wastes and flowing in the streets. 




I remember, recently due to blockage the drainage here around the university, there was bad smell and dirt coming and flowing all along the main street. In addition, solid wastes such as ‘plastics, ‘khat’ straw and grass have been left over all along the road.  This is one of the main challenges affecting the proper usage of the drainage. So, if this problem is not given attention right now, we will be confronted with more serious sanitation problems and severe shortage of water in the future. 

2. What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems? 

Optimum utilization of underground water: Since Debre Berhan town is located in a highland area and it has a higher altitude, investors are being attracted from time to time to construct lots of factories. One of the advantages that the city is proud of abundant underground water. Nevertheless, the resource will be exposed to pollution and depletion if it is not utilized wisely.  I personally feel unconfident that todays’s water utilization practice will lead to depletion of the resource in the near future since factories are expanding in an increasing rate. 

I believe that areas with good water source currently need to be reserved for future use. Areas with potential water source should not be allocated or given to people for constructing residential houses or factories. I don't want to mention names of factories now, but there are many factories that are creating lots of problems among the communities around Debre Berhan town. Farmers are always complaining about the problems they are facing because the environmental pollution coming out of these factories. These factories are highly polluting their environment.  Farmers who live around the town/peri urban dwellers/ basically use nearby rivers as a source of drinking water, washing cloths and for their animals.  However, since the source of water is being polluted with the sewage coming out of these factories, this is becoming a serious concern of our farmers, nowadays. 

 When we observe that this kind of investment is benefiting few investors but hurting the majorities of the people, I don’t see the benefit of having these factories. If these people hit a strike and come out here, it will definitely create a massive chaos and unrest. If these factories that are polluting water are beyond the control of government's capacity, it should be disclosed to the public so that they will give their own feedback or measures. But in reality, I don’t think it becomes beyond the government's capacity to react on it. It can’t be an issue above the law of the local government. 

While we are drinking pure water here in the town, we should not also forget that farmers in the rural areas are drinking water from the river which is not pure. Farmers also use the same water for their cattle, too.  I'm not claiming that all factories are creating these problems, but few factories are performing their production without keeping the interests of both the environment and the public. A few of them also showed improvements with regard to water pollution since they were positively treating comments from the general public. However, some of them are reluctant about the bad effects their factories are creating on the environment and the people living around. 

First of all, these factories are supposed to dig and construct their own well and get out of water. They should not share and consume water from the existing town’s potable water. Not only the factories, it is also a must for the University to have its own water source so that the share it is receiving from the city’s potable water could be potentially reduced. The university has already started digging water from its campus and the project is in a good progress at this time.  

Currently, there is a new project, ‘the green project’ that is going to be implemented in the near future. It is about planting seedlings which will consume additional water. This needs due attention ahead of the project from the project implements and town municipality.  

Instead of using potable water for watering plants, flushing toilets and all other secondary purposes, we can look for other alternatives of getting water. For example, we can use Besersa River for these purposes. 

Interviewer:  I understand that Beresa River is a potential source of water. Does the university have a department that takes initiatives and conduct research on the river and outreach its findings to the community?

Yes, of course. Not only Beresa River but also Chacha River, Angolela and other tributary rivers pollution are targeted to be the future potential water sources for the city. A research was conducted by the initiative of Law College with in the university. The study was supported with photos and videos that markedly showed the negative impacts of the factories on the lives of the population and being additional burden on water pollution. Responsible government entities from the federal and regional levels have also attended the workshop when the findings of the study were presented. It was found out from the study that the people in the rural areas needed justice with regard to the spoilage that is occurring to the rivers. Though the problems were disclosed in the workshop, no measure was taken so far. The people even expressed their grievance in such a way that their children had throat infections and skin irritations because of the contaminated water they have been using out of polluted rivers. 





3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?

The city municipality should find ways to dig more walls and increase water coverage. This will help the city absorb the increasing demand in water as a result of increasing population. 

The second most important solution could be encouraging industries to install their own water treatment plants and regularly monitor the implementation process. The factors can have one big treatment plant together so that it could be easy to disinfect the sewage. Recycled water can also be used for irrigation and many other purposes instead of releasing it out. Once the sewage is treated, it can even be carried to artificial lakes and reused for recreational purposes.
