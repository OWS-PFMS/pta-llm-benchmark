1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Interviewer: I have three general questions related to WASH service in your area. The first question is what are the main problems that affect the sustainability of water and sanitation service in the region? Please also explain how these problems affect the WASH service and the extent of the problems as well.
Respondent: The first major problem is the water we are using is a ground water and it has high content of fluoride. The other problem is the community settlement (they settled scattered) is not convenient to provide water service to them. You may find 4 or less households in one place. Because of this, they utilize surface water like Awash River. In addition, they are not aware of applying water treatment chemicals.  However, the main problem is the area is water resource deficient. Sometimes even by digging deep down you may not find a drop of water.
On the other hand, due to high content of fluoride we have closed 29 boreholes that were drilled between Logia and Assayta towns. The boreholes that developed around Dubti woreda are the one that provide proper service to the community.
Interviewer: You have told me about the problems related to water service would you please tell me about the problem related to sanitation or community personal hygiene.   
Respondent: Due to their religion, the community do not have observed personal hygiene problem. However, as they store water (drinking water) in very small water containers (that contain only about 3 to 5 liters) it is difficult to apply water treatment chemicals. Due to economic problems, they cannot afford to buy large water containers that are suitable for water treatment.  Mostly they utilize river water and flood water that contain large amount of dirt or mud because of this there is serious water sanitation problem. The housings are not also convenient for life and contribute for water contamination. Thus, the water that they utilize has high possibility of contamination from is source to end use. Due to this they mostly affected by Cholera (diarrheal disease). 
2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer:  Now tell me about the solutions to these problems.
Respondent: There is a geological service that is undergoing by the Regional Water Bureau. The main responsibility rests on this Bureau. However, we take sample for test when they develop boreholes and we decide together on the functionality of the water schemes.  The bureau is also currently conducting studies on those boreholes that contain fluoride to find means of treatments. 
It is also argued that there is advanced technology that detects the fluoride content of water during drilling thus this might be a solution not to invest capital on areas with non-functional water resources. We are looking to get this advanced machinery. Currently of 20 boreholes, only 4 are providing service to the community due to high content of fluoride. 
Interviewer: The other problem you raised earlier that affect WASH service sustainability is the settlement of the community (scattered settlement). What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to this problem?
Respondent: Currently the region has started implementing a rural community settlement program in which there is a strong effort to develop different infrastructures like school, health center and water schemes in one area. There is one unit that is responsible for monitoring of the implementation of this program under Pastoral Development Office. Particularly there is remarkable activity to settle communities in one suitable area that are currently living around the periphery Awash Basin as these people are at high risk of flood as well. Considerable effort is exerted in education, health, water and women developments in the region.
Interviewer: As the area is pastoralist, how much this settlement program is sustainable? How can we be sure that these people do not move to other place after the new settlement?  
Respondent: Even if they move to other place, they do not take their children and older family members. Only younger members of the family that are responsible for livestock herd management will migrate to other place in search of water and feed/grazing land for their livestock. Then after 8 months, they return to their home. Now health facilities, schools, water schemes and other infrastructures are under development in one area so we hope this will solve the problem.
Interviewer: You have also said the community lack awareness in application and utilization of water treatment chemicals. Why the community is not willing to utilize the available chemicals? What ideas or recommendations do you have about solution to this problem?
Respondent: There are 25 hotspot woredas mainly utilize river water (Awash River). Of these, 16 woredas have seriously exposed for water sanitation problems. To this end, we are requesting water containers support from different donors like CARE Ethiopia, Amref Health Africa and UNICEF. We supply the materials we obtained from these organizations to the community. However, it is difficult to reach these large groups of population under such problems. 
The other thing when there is incidence of Cholera (diarrheal disease) we provide education to the community but under normal circumstance we do not provide community education service. We only provide community education service when there is incidence of disease outbreaks due budget constraint. The government do not allocate sufficient budget to provide regular education service. This year is better than the previous years we only faced disease outbreak in one woreda but in earlier years, we fight against outbreaks in about 16 woredas at a time. The main reason for the occurrence of Cholera is absence of clean water. The community mainly based on untreated surface water. In the absence of proper water service (i.e. without facilitating access to clean water), the community is not willing to accept awareness enhancement advises. For instance, in one area a newly developed borehole has high content of fluoride and we have tried to advise the community not to use this water but they fight against us to use the water as the water looks like clean and safe and the community do not aware of its side effects.  Thus, the ultimate solution is to improve the access to clean water in the region. Parallel to this we should work on community awareness and mobilization to alleviate the problems. 
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Interviewer: The last question is that you have raised the problems related to water and sanitation service sustainability and you mentioned the solution to each problem. Of the solution, you listed which is the most important or which one is the first priority area? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: I think mobilizing the community and enhancing their awareness is the priority area. If we can do this, the community can develop personal latrines in their area. The second one is we should provide awareness enhancement training to the regional higher official or administrative members.  Moreover, any projects implemented by the Federal and Regional governments should be community centered. That is we should create sense of belongingness within the community so that developed water projects can provide sustainable service. The water service problem is more challenging for Health Bureau compared to the Regional Water Bureau, as our office is responsible to control water borne diseases. As I said earlier there are 25 hotspot woredas that has water service problems and of these, 16 has as seriously affected by water related problems. Thus, we have to coordinate with education, water and women and children affairs bureaus to provide aggressive awareness enhancement and community mobilization services.  By doing this we can ensure sustainable water and sanitation service.
