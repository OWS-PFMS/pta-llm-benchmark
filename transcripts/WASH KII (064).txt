
Interviewer:  My first question to you is, “In your opinion, what aspect of this network is working well in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:  The interaction of the map – what works well in sustainability?  As Tanathi, we are just infrastructure developers.  We develop the infrastructure as per the new constitution and the new laws.  So already we are not doing the services and we are not doing the management of the installed infrastructure.  We’re doing them and handing them to the county.  Our counties are still in the crawling they have not started walking.  When we handover to them, then they don’t do there’s.  We are not getting funding for rehabilitation, unless maybe a program comes in like drought funding.  Others maybe donors try to fund us for some rehabilitation.  Otherwise, we don’t get funds from the government for rehabilitation.  They are still rehabilitation, maybe some pipeline extensions, but not even 80%, I think the percentage comes in at 20%.  This now comes in as crawling, not yet walking.  We have that feeling of each one is doing the work of another.  They think tanathi is doing the work that the county is supposed to do because the water is devolved, and also we think they are trying to take the work from us. So that misunderstanding is bringing the sustainability of the project to go down, the way it never used to be.

When we talk about the Fundifix, if it can be implemented well is the best one.  WE’ve not started doing it with Tanathi, but according to the discussion in the meetings we’ve met with them, it’s quite a good one if it’s funded.  They’ve implemented the Fundifix technology and those projects are good.  But us we are infrastructure development, so we’re rarely given money for management, rarely given money for rehabilitation of projects which are not moving onward. 

KIWASH are doing good – if they can be given extension, more than what they’ve been given.  They are rehabilitating, they are training the communities.  They are doing boreholes for our projects and the county projects, so they are cutting across the county and Tanathi.

On the companies (WSP) they are just service deliverers – that is water flow.  Even rehabilitation they depend on the county and us.  There is water flowing to the point, none of the other (factors).  So if there is any major repairs to their systems, they rely on Tanathi  and they rely on the county still.  They are born of two mothers: they are born of tanathi and we’re supposed to hand them over to the county.  So we are systematically making them grow  and give them to the county so, now they are benefitting from us and from the counties.

County schemes, as we develop and hand them over to the counties, not unless we receive a program which is allowing us to rehabilitate totally, we hand over projects to them and we hand over the reports to the county.  If they get any problem they start working with the county.

Interviewer:  My second question to is, “What do you think are the main problems in sustaining water services in the county?

Interviewee:  Actually, the main problems is the politics.  Politics.  The much we understand is that the politics is part of us.  They are the main challenge.  We develop those projects from the ground and hand them over to the county, if the CBO (community-based organization) is not connected to the political link, they may fail to get the services.  Mostly, our communities use political lines, and political lines are not well distributed – it’s whom do you know.  So you might get the powers of politics giving the services on one side and the other sides are left without being given that.  Without getting their services being looked at, without being trained.  So the politics is major when we look about hazards.  

We depend on the central government, but before the budget and also the politics is inside this, so whatever we get is for the development of infrastructure and we tend to forget the sustainability part. Most of the projects we do is new, new, new, new, new,.  And tanathi has been doing new, new, new for 10 years.  The project we did in 2008 - is it functional? I may tend to forget it.  And this is when they are not linked well to the politicians.  If they are linked to them, they’ll still be serviced.  All the money will be diverted to them. Even between us and the national govnerment, the politicians will say let this money go to my constituency.  You might see us drilling a borehole in Kitui Central when we have not covered.. [names different areas].  Mwingi north is waiting [for] us and you can see us in Kitui Town drilling a boreholes there.  Strength of politicians – that is a challenge.

Other problems.  Still the counties are crawling.  The counties feel like Tanathi is doing their work, and wants us to be out.    We really want to work together.  Because the Act 2016 is very clear: we are the infrastructure developer, and they are the service provider.  The service is water flowing and management.  Ours is just the structure on the ground and leave them to take over.  If this Act is clear and everything is documented I think everybody will be doing his work and we will be well off because we will be directing them, and they’ll be knowing very well tanathi has done this work and now we have to pick pick it from there.   

I think we can be with the same good link with fundifix and the community, then community can report and that system can be linked to the offices and that is the county, not us because they are the service providers. Also I know the fundifix has not covered a large area, just Mwingi North.  

Interviewer:  My third question to is, “What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?

Interviewee:  I think solution comes with the so-called WASH Coordinator.  Linking all the stakholders together.  The way we used to meet, let it be in us that we know.  And have a calender, and in the office I know that July 15 we’ll have a meeting or August we’ll have a meeting, and everybody can understand.  Meeting continuously give our how we work and we keep on talking about the health and the law because everyone has a position within the water sector.  So this meeting will help us understand each other. The county will understand us.  Also the NGO will understand us and we will understand them.  This will give us the fair distribution of water resources within the county.  Which we are not yet there, where we want to be.  I think the WASH coordinator has a mandate on the solution of this issue.  With that discussion we won’t be seeing each other as an enemy.  Most of them we are working with them, but they think Tanathi is taking our work, county wants us to go home.  When we meet, because we are friends, so the community wouldn’t be having that much problems.  And also reporting, here we have a project.  And everyone has freedom of reporting. You know when I meet a project of the county which is stalled, let me have the easiness of reporting to them and they appreciate that.  

Interviewer:  Of the solutions you have mentioned, which do you consider to be the most important? 

Interviewee:  The WASH Forum.  The stakeholders meeting to be frequent and to be sort of planned, so that we don’t budget.  You know I could receive a letter, but will I attend? Maybe my colleague has planned to be somewhere.  If it’s budgeted, even the office will be knowing: this date and this date.  Because two of us are the ones who attend.  I am a community person and she is the one reports to the board.  So if it’s planned early I think it can help a lot.
