
1.In your opinion, what do you think are the main problems to water/sanitation service sustainability in your woreda? Can you please explain in detail how these problems influence sanitation/water service sustainability?
Respondent 1: Lack of sense of ownership from the community. This is the major challenge affecting sustainability of water services. As you know, majority of the schemes are developed by partner organizations, however, the problems arises when they left the area. Since the community is not formed in a way to own and protect the infrastructures, no one will be committed to be responsible to protect from damage and to maintain them when needed. 
The second main challenge emanates from the hierarchical management of the water institutions themselves. There is a gap in establishing WASHCOs in line with newly established water schemes from the government side though we know that there are successful WASHCOs which are being functional based on their internal bylaws. 
The third challenge is maintenance issue. We don’t give adequate technical trainings to the community representatives as far as our experiences from the majority of the areas where water schemes are found is concerned. 
Fourth, the WASHCOs themselves need incentive and motivation. Especially at their infancy stage, they need financing and capacity development and request the government to assist in this regard
Interviewer: Sorry, we will come to discussions on the solutions later, now let me ask other colleagues of there is anything left to add 
Respondent 2: As to me, the main problem is mismatch between the population size and the existing water service infrastructures, especially in urban areas.  This is because our infrastructures are  fixed which are not replaced for long time and this is creating a burden to the existing pipelines, generators and other power stations in the effort to satisfy the population 
The other challenge especially in rural areas is the climatic condition. Due to arid conditions, access to the water table is not an easy task which prevents development of boreholes and others everywhere. Instead, water schemes are being developed in areas where there is adequate water and after completion of the work, the water is forced to travel several Kms to provide services to urban people. This would have also another burden, maintaining damaged pipelines, for instance is not easy along its long way and coupled by lack of vehicles targeted for this service 
Respondent 3: As to me, the main problem is lack of quality of the schemes constructed which is eroding the trustworthiness of the beneficiaries. 
The second challenge is lack of spare parts in and around the existing infrastructures. For this reason, damaged scheme may take up to one to two years to get maintained. 
The third one is that people are not voluntary to pay for water service charges to WASHCOs simply because they thought that water services expense belongs to the government and other partners. 
Sometimes, we hear from the community that they don’t want to use water coming from places where there are graves. This is related to lack of awareness. 
Lack of skilled man power is another challenge. Even it is not possible to get skilled experts in kebeles since they are located in the zone and woredas. Added with lack of vehicles, an expert can’t travel, for example 47 kms from woreda to kebele, by walking to maintain damaged schemes. 

2.What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?
Interviewer: Let me come back to the you (respondent 1), I think you have mentioned  3 or more important challenges earlier and the first one is lack of sense of ownerships from the community. So, what ideas or recommendations do you have to this problems?
Respondent: Yes, I think 
The government and its partners should properly plan and work on project exist strategy including the details of ownership, management systems, maintenance of small and difficult damages, who should do this one, how to hire technicians, information exchange etc to be implemented after a partner project is phase out. This procedure should be discussed along with the community at the grass root levels to build a sense of ownership and to enable them maintain minor damages themselves and report difficult cases to the respective administrative levels 
Interviewer: Okay, you have also mentioned management problems and also challenges related to WASHCO administration, what do you suggest for this one?
Respondent: Yes, the solution for this is to establish strong WASHCOs on time having a clear bylaw on how to collect their incomes and discharge this for different expenses. For the success of this committee and the infrastructures, the government should take the initiative, manage and monitor the process regularly. 
Interviewer: what about solutions for the maintenance and skilled manpower related problems?
Respondent: Some projects provide training for at least two experts immediately after the establishment of water schemes but other may not do this and in this case the government should fill the gap. Theoretical and practical training should be given to all schemes developed around rural areas. Due to lack of skill training, minor problems on the spot may be interpreted as major failures causing delayed water services to the community.  
Interviewer: let me come to you (respondent 2) what should be done to get rid of limited access of water due to climatic problems? You have also mentioned challenges related to vehicle and limited capacity 
Respondent: awareness creation programs should be conducted widely to get the attention of the community towards soil and water management systems and environmental protections practices. This needs arrangement of continuous meeting plat forms including concerned stakeholders and community representatives since it is the foundation of all problems related to sustainability of water. 
Limited capacity is related to lack of motor bikes and finance. Government should take the lions share in attracting more partner organizations so as to fill this kind of capacity gap  
Interviewer: let me come to you (respondent 3), what could be the solutions for shortage of spare parts, poor quality of built infrastructures, community awareness etc ? 
Respondent: for the lack of quality, there should be monitoring and evaluation systems to oversee the standard of equipment during procurement and installation by the contractors. Adequate prior information about the areas where a scheme is to be constructed though conducting research is also vital for the investors to take action ahead of time. 
To solve the problem of skills gap, it needs regular TOT program from the region and zone to the experts located in the local level. Sometimes, we are doing the worst thing when a generator stops functioning, we request experts from the region to come and maintain which has both time and financial costs. If you see Bahirdar and Hawassa cities, they have a standby maintenance crew but we don’t have that system here. Incentivizing and motivation of people located in kebeles, especially for TVET graduates who have junior level skills is also very important. 
I always think that the government can establish a structure like health extension workers in rural areas, a team of skilled people based in rural areas but accountable to the upper office levels would bring a practical change if we are thinking about sustainability of the service. 
Recently, the government started a new structure and allowed for private people who are interested to organize themselves as an enterprise or federation to supply spare parts with reasonable price. Accordingly, they are linked to the rural WASHCOs and the water institutes found at different levels to provide spare parts and maintenance services up on request. This is a very important solution and has to be strengthened
3.Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps would happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: 
The most important thing is to work on continuous community awareness programs. When people are aware of the schemes and benefits and convinced well, they will start taking initiatives themselves, develop a sense of ownership. Due to lack of awareness, I know for instance that a community living around Ashekela area is now drinking river water while potable water is still available around.
