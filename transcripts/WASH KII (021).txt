Question 1: In your opinion what do you think are the main challenges to water service sustainability in Kitui County? Could you explain in detail how these challenges influence water service sustainability? 
Respondent: I think one of the main challenges in the sustainability is the issues relating to the management of the systems. Then there is also the element of financial. Because most of them are not able to sustain their operations from the water maybe they sell. Then we also have now the issue of catchment degradation because you find in some cases we have got some boreholes or shallow wells and springs which sometimes back they used to have a lot of water but currently they cannot be able to meet the demand because the yields have dwindled because of climate change. So I think those are the three major factors which I can say.
Interviewer: You have talked about management of the systems, as one of the challenge, financial issues and then catchment degradation, what else do you think is really affecting water sustainability?
Respondent: There is also the various stakeholders they are supposed now to work together. There we also have an issue because you find that it is as if there is no a coordinated way of how they are now supposed to work together, and if it is there, it is not implemented or effected. That's why you find maybe the water resources the other stakeholders they are supposed to work together with all agencies but you find that each of them does not even know how they are supposed now to relate when they are working together.
Interviewer: So you've talked about coordination, there is no proper coordination. Could you please elaborate? What do you mean coordination within the stakeholders?
Respondent: It is from the aspect of the consultation. You find that there are some issues which us as WRA we are supposed to work in close relation with the other stakeholders but you find that the stakeholders may be knowing that either we are supposed to do something with them, but you find that in most cases they do not contact us but when things go out of hand that is when they contact us.
Interviewer: What do you mean by out of hand?
Respondent: For example we had a conflict on a ... in a place at Mwingi. I think that conflict has been there for a long time but I think it is recent that we incorporated the team that went there. But the process of trying to solve the conflict has been going on for a long time. But I think once they realized later, I don't know whether they realized or they knew and they failed to inform us initially.
Interviewer: Who now?
Respondent: The County Government, NEMA (National Environment management Authority). That's why I said the issues of water management and water regulation lies with WRA (Water Resource Authority). So that's when we went there with them and we started coming up with a strategy on how to try address the issue. WRA being given the lead to spearhead that process. But it is an activity that has taken over a few years.
Interviewer: So could you please in summary talk about that point of consultation? This office being consulted, how other stakeholders as you've just said, in summary.
Respondent: I think what I would say, we are supposed to have a forum where maybe each stakeholder can be able to explain to the others what it's supposed to do, and how it expects to collaborate with the other stakeholders. So that each one of them can now take responsibility of ensuring they involve any stakeholder who is now relevant to what they are doing.
Interviewer: you said on the issue of having a forum where every stakeholder can say what they do, isn't that what the WASH Forum tries to do or you feel that you've not been involved well?
Respondent: That is what the WASH is trying to do but they should now try to stress. You know there is talking in the WASH Forum what is supposed to do and you are supposed to do this, you are supposed to do this. The County Government when maybe they are undertaking constructions of dams or sand dams they are supposed to consult WRA or NEMA but you find that on the ground that is not what happens.
Question 2: What ideas or recommendations do you have about solutions to these problems?  
Interviewer: If we can start with management of the systems, financial problems, catchment degradation and then the partners working together and emphasizing on what each stakeholder is doing.
Respondent: On management, you know most of the infrastructure are managed by the community, so I think we should empower the community and I think that is what is being stressed in the WASH Forum. Empowering the community to be able to manage now their systems.
Interviewer: What other ideas do you have?
Respondent: And also the trainings we involve even financial management because you find that most of the communities, 3/4 of the community projects they don't even have accounts. So when now there is a breakdown they have no money at hand to be able to cater for the repairs. They start now trying to look for money from other sources. The people that are using the infrastructure are the ones who are supposed to maintain, it is their responsibility to maintain the infrastructure. And also on the issue of catchment, the people at the grass root are supposed to manage the catchment so that they ensure there is water, water is available and of good quality. But you find that maybe in some cases, the community may not have the capacity to manage the catchment which means now they need to be trained on how to manage their catchments and this we try to do through the WRUAs but right now the funding of the WRUAs is very limited.
Interviewer: Any other idea, recommendation?
Respondent: There is the issue of training the stakeholders. I think they should be trained on say the relevant areas which may make them able to do their work properly. 
Interviewer: Strengthening the capacity of the stakeholders?
Respondent: Strengthening the capacity of the stakeholders.
Interviewer: Other than just the meetings?
Respondent: Yes other than just the meetings. Sometimes there were to be trainings on climate change, such things.
Question 3: Of the solutions you listed, which is the most important? Can you walk me through what next steps will happen if the solution occurs?
Respondent: So I think the most important is to have the resources to undertake the various activities. Because you have to have the finance because I don't know whether it will be enough, the said finance so that we can be able to organize trainings for the various projects on how they are supposed to manage their systems.
Interviewer: Just to clarify, you've proposed some solutions, like empowering the community to manage the systems, training to involve the financial management, then people at the grassroots to manage the catchments and then funding of the water resource users. Which you've said it's limited. So out of those which one is the most important?
Respondent: Empowering the community. 
Interviewer: After empowering communities?
Respondent: Because once they are empowered, they will be able to know that it is their responsibility to maintain their systems or their catchments. They will also be able to know even the resources, now before maybe they start looking for resources from somewhere else they have now to use the resources which they have.
Interviewer: So you mean the communities have not been getting enough trainings because--
Respondent: They have not been getting enough trainings. Because even when you go to the field you find even the simple things they are supposed to know maybe of the riparian areas, they are not aware of that.
Interviewer: Still under that, what do you think could be included in the trainings so that we could have water services sustainability now from the Water Resource Authority, what else do you think these communities need to be trained on?
Respondent: They should be trained on catchment management, climate change and the relevant rules and regulations governing the water resources management. 
Question 4: What kind of interventions are you implementing in Kitui and what influenced/informed the selection of these interventions?
Respondent: In Kitui what we are doing, in some places, some WRUAs have been formed so now through the WRUAs, we are trying to increase water availability through the construction of sand dams.
Interviewer: You are constructing sand dams?
Respondent: What happens, the donors now they find the WRUAs through us, through the WRA. So we have got some instances where some sand dams have been constructed, we had some NGOs who had funded some WRUAs.
Interviewer: You as WRA, Water Resource Authority, what are you doing, what are your interventions specifically?
Respondent: Interventions is to provide the technical knowledge on the construction of those systems to ensure the standards-they are constructed as per the laid down standards. We've got laid down standards for sand dams, so we try to ensure that the standards are adhered to.
Interviewer: What else are you doing as WRA?
Respondent:  We also try to enlighten the community on catchment management. That is why we have got WRUAs. And maybe in very few cases where we have got maybe some meetings organized by the chiefs, that one is very rare.
Interviewer: So what else is this office doing, Water Resource Authority, what else are you doing in Kitui?
Respondent: There was a time we were providing trees to be planted during the world water days in some areas.
Interviewer: Anything else that you are doing?
Respondent: There are many things but now they revolve to the regulations.
Interviewer: Just name them, what about regulations?
Respondent: The regulation, we are the ones who approve water abstractions. It is our responsibility to share the available water to the various water uses and users. So anybody who is abstracting has to get an approval from WRA.
Interviewer: What do you mean by abstraction?
Respondent: That is anybody who is abstracting water either from ground water using mechanical means or abstracting from a river using mechanical means.
Interviewer: Anything else?
Respondent: I think the others will come later.
Question 5: How has participating in the WASH forum influenced your organization’s interventions?
Respondent:  One, through the WASH Forum, we have been able to know stakeholders whom we should collaborate with. Because once we are there when the various stakeholders they say what they are doing now we compare what we are doing and we are able to know whom we can be able to collaborate with.
Interviewer: How else, you have been able to know who is who in the WASH Forum and how you can work with and who you can collaborate with, how else has that participation in the WASH Forum influenced your organization's interventions? 
Respondent: I think it has also somehow enhanced our collaboration with the stakeholders. For example after the few WASH Forums that we have held we have got some stakeholders who have started when they are doing anything connected with the water or the catchment they come to consult us here or they involve one of our officers.
Interviewer: How else has your participation in the WASH Forum influenced your organization's interventions?
Respondent: I think it has also helped the other stakeholders to know our position in the WASH.
Interviewer: What do you mean?
Respondent: You know, sometimes you find that for instance in service provision, the service providers or those people who are dealing with water supplies, they used not to consult WRA. So they used to go on site, do their things and they go. But some of them have started now consulting us before they do anything. They come to this office, a few of them.
Interviewer: Anything else you would want to add in that part of the interview?
Respondent: Like?
Interviewer: We've talked about the challenges, we've talked about the recommendation, and we’ve talked about the prioritizing the recommendations and then we have also talked about your interventions, could there be anything else because you said others will com later. Do you have something to add?
Respondent: I think they were preparing the policy, I don't know where it has reached so far, the Kitui Water Policy. And I think some of the things were captured in that policy and the Bill on how the various stakeholders in the County are supposed to work together. So we I think it has not yet been passed. That is what I think because I have not heard of it recently.
Interviewer: We are fast tracking that with the Ministry and we hope soon it will be enacted for implementation.
